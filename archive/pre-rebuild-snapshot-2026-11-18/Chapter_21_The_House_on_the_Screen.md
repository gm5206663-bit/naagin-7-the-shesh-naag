The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## The House in Mourning

The main hall of the Suri house at six in the morning, with the tube lights on and the garden doors shut against the reporters outside.

The television at the far end carries the news without sound.

A white flower lies on its side in the brass bowl beneath it, and the string of marigold over the doorway has begun to dry at the knots.

A tea tray stands on the low table with cups nobody has touched. The card beside it says *Shok Sabha* in black ink, and the lower corner has curled from the damp.

The house is full of people who come to mourn a woman they barely know. They sit in their good clothes, their voices low when a camera turns toward the gate, and their hands busy with the cups.

At the far end of the hall, Ravish bends over Swarna's body. He holds one of her hands in both of his and does not hear the guests behind him.

"Main tumhe kabhi nahin bhool paunga." His voice breaks once. "Kabhi kaise bhool paoge?"

The guest beside him touches the edge of her dupatta. "Apne rishtedaaron ko bhala koi kaise bhool sakta hai."

Parmeet stands by the closed doors. "Hum sab family jaise rehte hain." He says it to the room, and the room accepts it.

A reporter at the gate looks through the bars. "Sham ke telecast ke liye koi bite nahin hai."

"Bas ek final picture mil jaaye na, andar se. Maza aa jaayega."

The guard shakes his head. "Permission nahin hai."

"Peechhe hato."

At the side table, a guest lifts a cup and finds the tea cold. "Swarna tumhari saggi nahin thi, par family toh thi na. Thoda afsos toh dikhao."

"Unka fashion sense bahut achha tha," says another. "Unki skin itni glow karti thi. Daily skin-care routine follow karti thi."

"Roma, tumhe yaad hai inka woh parlour?"

"Parlour wali aunty ab bahut yaad karengi inhein."

A laugh escapes one woman and stops at the sight of the body. At the window, somebody says the minister loved Swarna, and the love killed her. The sentence passes from one person to the next without finding a place to sit.

A news voice comes through the television. "Minister Sameer ne apni patni ka qatl kar diya."

Parmeet looks once at the doors. "Hum sab dost ek family jaise hain."

The reporter outside raises his microphone, but the gate stays shut.

## Music Therapy

The upstairs sitting room at seven, with morning light caught in the glass and a speaker on the floor beneath the window.

The curtains lift and settle in the slow pull of the fan.

On the centre table, the fruit bowl has been pushed to one side to make room for a phone.

Ahana's phone lies beside the speaker, its screen showing the track's progress.

The tea tray from the hall stands by the door, every cup still full.

Ahana dances alone between the sofa and the open doorway. Her hands keep time; her feet do not touch the flower petals scattered from the hall below.

Dimple stands in the doorway with Pammi. "Yeh sab kya chal raha hai?"

Ahana stops the turn but not the music. "Main khush nahin hoon. Main dukhi hoon. Aur isiliye main dance kar rahi hoon."

"Dukh mein aise kaun naachta hai?"

"Amir log."

"Lekin amir toh hum bhi hain," says Dimple. "Aap toh aise nahin naachte."

"Yeh amiron ke bhi amiron ke bhi amir hain," Pammi says. "Shayad in logon mein dukh ko aise hi handle kiya jaata hai."

"Exactly." Ahana opens both hands. "Main music therapy le rahi hoon. Pain, stress relieve karne ke liye. Isse saara dukh, saara dard dheere-dheere khatam ho jaata hai. Aap bhi yeh therapy lijiye. Dil khol ke dance kijiye."

Pammi looks at Dimple. "Agar dance nahin kiya toh inhe bura lag jaayega."

"Ahana sad hai, toh woh music therapy le rahi hai," Dimple says, turning toward the stair. "Aur chahti hai ki hum bhi apna gham door karne ke liye music pe dance karein. Aap hi ne kaha tha na, iski har baat maan-ni padegi."

Parmeet comes upstairs with Baljeet. "Haan. Agar Ahana bitiya keh rahi hai toh humein bhi therapy try karni chahiye."

"Bilkul," says Baljeet.

Ahana turns the music up. "Kis baat ki deri hai, uncle? Chaliye. Saara dukh, dard, peeda gayab ho jaayega."

A chair shifts under Dimple as she lifts both hands, first awkwardly and then with the beat; Pammi follows, and Parmeet steps into the space between them while Baljeet begins to laugh before making a full turn.

At the bottom of the stairs, the hall keeps its white flowers, its card and its cold tea. The sound climbs down the stairwell and gets there before anyone does.

## The Backyard

The lane behind the Suri house at the same hour, with a streetlamp going yellow over the wall and the reporters' camera light bright against the leaves.

A folding ladder leans against the plaster beside the drainpipe.

The cable runs through a narrow gap in the gate.

"Ghar ke backyard mein chalein?" one reporter asks. "Kya pata wahan kuch mil jaaye."

"Kaise jaayenge?"

"Dhoondh, dhoondh. Wahan seedhi hai."

"Camera leke aa."

They take the ladder around the back, one man holding the bottom while another climbs high enough to see over the wall as the camera rises with him.

Inside, the dance continues. The reporters look down into the yard and find the whole family moving under the string lights. Their camera follows the turn from Ahana to Parmeet, then to the doorway where the guests gather.

"Yeh shok sabha chal rahi hai ya laughter-chef ka comedy episode?" the reporter says into his microphone. "Kehne ko dono families mein itni gehri dosti, lekin itne bade dukh ke baad bhi kaise hans-hans ke naach rahe hain."

Another reporter takes the camera from him. "Breaking news dene wale breakdance kamaal ka karte hain."

"Story is got talent."

The red light on the camera stays on as the song reaches the end and begins again.

## The Chain

The inspector's front room at ten that night, with the porch bulb throwing a hard square on the tiled floor and the bedroom door half open.

A small gold chain lies on the dressing table beside a tube of lipstick.

Its clasp points toward the mirror, turned up under the light.

Santosi comes in, sees the chain, and stops. "Itni halki chain? Yeh do kaudi ka gift laaye ho tum mere liye? Tum hi rakho."

The inspector puts his hands out. "Santoshi darling. Sirf naam ki Santoshi, tanik bhi santosh nahin. Itni achhi sone ki chain laaya hoon."

"Gusse mein andar gayi thi na?" she asks. "Gussa toh aayega. Chain laaye ho toh pehnao, uske baad hi romance."

"Pehle kehna tha na. Abhi pehnata hoon."

He lifts the chain and tries the clasp behind her neck, but it will not close; Santosi turns toward the mirror.

"Chain chhoti kyun ho rahi hai?"

"Sahi size ka toh liya tha."

"Lao, mujhe do." She reaches for it. "Chain bahut tight hai. Mere gale mein nahin aa rahi. Aao, tumpe try karti hoon."

"Arre, Santosi. Kya kar rahi ho?"

The inspector's hand closes around the back of his own neck. His face turns toward the mirror. In the glass, Santosi's mouth opens wider than the room can hold. Her shoulders lengthen; the woman in the mirror has Bharani's face now, and both hands close around his throat.

He tears himself free and runs into the bedroom. The door hits the wall. When he looks back, Santosi stands beside the dressing table, one hand on the chain.

"Nahin. Naagin. Naagin hai wahan."

"Naagin?" Santosi steps toward him. "Kya hai? Dikhao."

He turns. The mirror holds only his own face and the lipstick mark on his cheek.

"Yahin baithi thi. Tumhare roop mein aake chain se mera gala daba rahi thi."

Santosi touches the mark with one finger. "Yeh lipstick ka nishaan? Chakkar chal raha hai tumhara."

"Koi chakkar nahin. Main khud chakkar mein phansa hoon."

"Tumhari naagin ko toh kisi ne nahin dekha," she says, taking the chain from the table. "Par tumhara naagin poori duniya dekhegi."

He tries to follow her. "Nahin, nahin. Please."

The porch bulb flickers once above the chain hanging from her fist as she walks into the bedroom, leaving him where the mirror can keep him.

## On Air

The Suri drawing room the next morning, with the television light blue across the curtains and the sun still outside the east windows.

A newspaper lies folded on the arm of the sofa.

The remote is under a glass coaster, its dark buttons visible through the glass.

The clip runs again. On screen, the family dances at the mourning gathering. The camera catches Parmeet's raised hands, Baljeet laughing, Dimple turning, and Ahana at the centre of them.

Aryaman comes in from the hall. "Yeh sab kya hai?"

Parmeet turns from the television. "Kya hua?"

"Rival channels pe yahi news chal rahi hai. Aap sabne hamari family ka tamasha bana diya." He looks at the screen. "Log hamara nau baje ka show nahin dekh rahe. Woh yeh dekh rahe hain. In fact, hum bhi yahi dekh rahe hain ki hamari family kitni pagal lag rahi hai."

Dimple lifts both palms. "Hum khushi se nahin naach rahe the. Hum toh apna dukh kam kar rahe the."

"This is shameful," somebody says.

"No, it's shameful," another voice answers.

"But Arya—" Pammi starts.

"Hum toh sirf therapy kar rahe the," Dimple says. "Music therapy."

Aryaman looks at her. "Therapy? Kaunsi therapy?"

"Woh music therapy Ahana ne sikhayi thi."

"Usne? Kisne?"

"Ahana ne."

The clip begins again. A reporter's voice calls the gathering a spectacle, then the image freezes on Parmeet's face and the channel logo sits in the corner.

Vihaan stands beside the doorway. The light from the television stops on his sleeve, not his face.

The housekeeper carrying the tea tray sees him and lowers her eyes to the cups. Dimple's glance stops at his face before she turns back to the screen. The tray is steady in the housekeeper's hands.

"Yeh clip sirf yahan chal raha hai?" Vihaan asks.

Aryaman answers without looking away from the screen. "Har rival channel pe."

Vihaan takes the remote from under the coaster and sets it on the table, not in anyone's hand. "Toh is room mein kam se kam ek baar awaaz band kar sakte hain."

Parmeet looks at the remote. "Aur baahar?"

"Baahar woh apna kaam karenge." Vihaan turns to him. "Yahan humein apna."

He presses mute. The channel logo stays bright in the corner as the loop starts again without sound. Vihaan leaves the remote within reach and goes upstairs.

## The Feed Sheet

The fourth-floor room at three in the morning, with the desk lamp on and the rest of the house dark.

The open window admits the last traffic sounds from the lane below.

A grey envelope lies on the desk beside the account book.

The courier waits by the door while Vihaan opens it. His eyes lift to Vihaan's face, then fall to the envelope. Inside is the office copy of the Kumbh feed sheet, the same form that once hangs from a clip on an OB van door. Vihaan's signature sits at the foot of it. The new line beneath it carries no sender's name, only an instruction to keep the account open.

Vihaan places the sheet next to the account book. He does not call his father.

The phone rings once. "Verma." Vihaan keeps his eyes on the sheet.

"Sir, woh Kumbh account ka paper aaya hai."

"Kahan se?" he asks without looking up.

"Naam nahin hai."

"Line kis desk par daali?"

"Abhi kahin nahin."

Vihaan turns the sheet around. The lamp catches his signature and the empty space beside the new instruction.

"London file mein rakho."

"Par naam?"

"Mera."

"Sir, aapka sign pehle se hai."

"Is baar routing ka."

He writes his name in the receiving column, draws a line from the account to the London desk, and dates the entry. The number in the book does not change, but the route does.

Verma reads it back over the phone. "Kumbh account. London desk. Vihaan Suri."

"Haan."

"Phir agla paper bhi yahin bhej doon?"

"Bhej do."

The call ends. Vihaan leaves the copy in the folder with his name on its tab. The sheet no longer hangs from a van door, and the next instruction no longer has to cross his father's desk to reach the work.

## Impossible

Ahana's room at noon, with the sun square on the writing desk and the window open to the sound of the lawn.

A glass of water stands beside a hairbrush on the writing desk.

The bedroom door rests open against the wall, its latch in view.

The latch clicks once, then settles against the frame.

Aryaman steps in. Ahana looks up from the phone in her hand.

"Knock hua tha?" she asks.

He looks back at the door. "Koi awaaz nahin aayi."

"Ladki ke room mein jab aate hain toh knock karke aate hain. Basic manners."

"Therapy khatam ho gayi?"

"Ab batao, kya chahte ho?"

"Dekho, main in sab baaton mein nahin maanta. Lekin tum bhi jaanti ho, log maante hain ki ghar mein tragedy ho jaaye toh kuch din achhe kaam rok diye jaate hain. Warna log us insaan ko apshaguni kehte hain. Unki barbaadi ki wajah."

Ahana's thumb stops on the phone screen. "Kehna kya chahte ho?"

"Wahi jo tum bahut achhi tarah samajh gayi ho. Tumhari aur Vikram ki shaadi nahin ho sakti."

She taps the phone screen once with her thumb. "Kaun kehta hai?"

"Main, after all. Ghar mein death hui hai."

"Serious allergy hai na aapko shaadi se?"

"Haan."

"Kyunki shaadi se bada chhalaawa doosra koi nahin."

Aryaman looks toward the window. "Chot kaafi gehri lagti hai ki uske zikr pe bhi itna dil dukhta hai."

"Har cheez hi aisi hoti hai," she says. "Khushi kam, dukh zyada."

"Par tum kya jaano, jisne kabhi kisi se sachche dil se pyaar hi nahin kiya."

"Pyaar mein sirf dhokha hota hai."

She turns back to the phone. "Dekh rahi hoon. Dhokhe ko dekh rahi hoon."

Vikram comes in behind Aryaman. "Baby, kya hua?"

"Tumhare best friend ko lagta hai hamari shaadi nahin honi chahiye."

"What? Aryaman, maine tujhse kaha tha na, mere matter se door reh."

Parmeet arrives with Baljeet and Dimple, and the corridor fills before anyone answers.

"Ghar mein maut hui hai," Ahana says. "Log toh yehi sochenge ki main apshaguni hoon. Reason for your doom."

"Nahin, beta," Parmeet says. "Jo aisa sochta hai, woh pagal hai. Aur waise bhi Swarna ko hum log itna pasand nahin karte the."

Aryaman looks at him. "Dad, minister uncle jail mein hain."

"Jail hi gaya na. Phansi pe photo nahin latka."

"Usne jo kiya hai, uski saza honi chahiye."

The room shifts; someone looks toward the door while somebody else asks why the wedding has to happen so quickly.

"Pinky ko koi problem nahin," Aryaman says. "Uncle ko koi problem nahin. Tujhe kya problem hai?"

"Jab unhone maan liya ki main aur Pinky ek doosre ke liye nahin bane, toh tujhe kyun nahin samajh aa raha?" Vikram says. "Tu toh mera dost hai. Tujhe samajhna chahiye."

Ahana moves to Vikram's side. "Aryaman ko is baat se problem hai ki main tumse shaadi kar rahi hoon."

"Look, I don't know aap sabko kya ho gaya hai. Is shaadi ki itni aafat kyun padi hai? At best, yeh shaadi postpone honi chahiye."

"Magar kyun?"

"Because it is not right."

"Tum dono ke beech ka pyaar tumhari baat hai," Parmeet says. "Ghar ki baat ghar mein rahe."

Aryaman turns to Ahana. "Tumhara aur Vikram ka koi mel nahin. Mere Vikram ke saath pyaar ka naatak kyun? Mujhe tum dono ka rishta samajh nahin aata."

"Tumhe samajhne ki zaroorat bhi nahin." Ahana's voice stays level. "Yeh mere aur mere Vikram ke beech ki baat hai. Humein jaise deal karna hoga, hum kar lenge. Filhaal tum hum dono se door raho."

"Vikram sirf mujhse pyaar karta hai," she says. "Woh mandap pe nahin aayega. Tum dekh lena."

Aryaman steps out. Vikram follows him to the landing, then turns back toward Ahana.

## Pinky's Glass

The small sitting room at one in the afternoon, with the curtains drawn against the lawn and the table lamp on though the sun is high.

A glass of water stands at the edge of the rug.

A bottle of wine sits unopened beside the glass.

Pinky closes the door behind Vikram. "Mujhe lagta hai tumhe shaadi ke liye dobara sochna chahiye. Tumhare aur Ahana ke beech jo bhi hai na, woh sirf infatuation hai. Pyaar toh woh hai jo tum mujhse karte rahe."

Vikram reaches for the door handle. "Pinky, please. Mujhe is baare mein tumse koi baat nahin karni."

"Agar Ahana ne humein saath dekh liya toh use bahut bura lagega."

"Don't do this."

He leaves, and the wine stays unopened on the table.

Pinky lifts the glass of water instead, but Aryaman catches her wrist before she can drink.

"Stupid mat ban."

"Mat roko mujhe. Marne do."

"Tu pagal ho gayi hai?"

"Toh aur kya karoon, bhai? Aur kitne dhokhe khaoon?" She looks at the empty glass in her hand. "Mere dad, mere uncle us ladki ka bag pakad ke kheench rahe hain. Usne us ladki ne mujhse mera pyaar chheen liya."

"Main uske baare mein kuch nahin jaanta," Aryaman says. "Aur tu bhi nahin."

"Main aisi zindagi nahin ji sakti. Mujhe roz dekhna padta hai. Jo ladka mujhse pyaar karta tha, woh aaj kisi aur se pyaar kar raha hai."

"Pinky, meri baat sun." He keeps hold of the glass. "Nafrat zyada better hai. Yeh pyaar har kisi ke bas ki baat nahin hoti. It is a horrible thing. Yeh saansein chalne deta hai, lekin zindagi chheen leta hai."

"Par main tumhari tarah nahin hoon, bhai. Mere andar itni taqat nahin. Main andar hi andar mar rahi hoon. Aap mujhe rok sakte ho, mujhe suna sakte ho. Par kya aap mere liye Dad se lad sakte ho? Kya aap Vikram ko rok sakte ho?"

"No, you can't," she says. "You are so powerful and still powerless."

Aryaman looks at his sister. "Tu sahi keh rahi hai. Agar main us ladki ko saza de bhi doon, ghar se nikaal bhi doon, koi na koi use wapas le aayega. Lekin ek baat tune bilkul sahi kahi. Main tera bhai hoon."

"Ab chahe kuch bhi ho jaaye, yeh shaadi nahin hogi." He takes the wine bottle away from her. "Agar main aisa nahin kar paaya, yeh drink main khud tujhe pilaunga. Ab kuch aisa hoga ki ghar ke bade khud is shaadi ko rokenge. Varna mera naam Aryaman Suri nahin."

From the lawn, a worker calls that the renovation has to finish in two days. Parmeet answers from the veranda: "Hum hamesha ke liye yahan nahin reh sakte. Apna space chahiye. Aur yahan Aryaman use bhadkaane mein laga hai."

Later, Aryaman stands at the doorway to Vikram's room. "Sorry, no man. I'm sorry."

Vikram keeps his eyes on the papers in front of him. "Mujhe tumhari sorry mein koi interest nahin hai."

Aryaman stays for one beat, then leaves without another word.

Pinky finds Vihaan on the stairs to the top of the house, where the light falls through the stairwell window in one long bar.

His shirt sleeves are folded to the elbow, and there is ink on the side of his hand.

"Viju."

He stops on the step above her and waits.

She takes his face in both hands, the way she did the morning he came home. "Mera bhai kitna achha lag raha hai." Her thumb moves once across his cheek. "Aur meri kismat dekh."

"What happened, Pinky?" Vihaan asks.

"Vikram se baat kar." She does not let go of his face. "Woh teri sunega. Arya ki sorry usne suni nahin. Tu uska dost hai."

"What should I say to him?" he asks.

"Use rok le." Her voice breaks on it. "Yeh shaadi mat hone de."

Vihaan looks at her for a long moment. He does not say the wedding will stop, because it is not his to stop.

"Main usse baat karunga," he says.

Pinky looks at him, and nods, and lets her hands fall.

He goes down the stairs toward Vikram's room.

## Vikram's Door

Vikram's room in the afternoon, with the curtains half drawn and the desk lamp on against the grey light.

Papers lie spread across the desk, one sheet half off the edge.

The door stands ajar, and Vihaan knocks on it once before he pushes it open.

Vikram does not look up. "Aa gaya tu."

"Pinky ro rahi hai," Vihaan says.

"Toh main kya karoon?" Vikram puts the pen down. "Maine usse kaha tha, mere matter se door rahe. Sabse kaha tha."

"Usne mujhse kaha tumse baat karun." Vihaan stays by the door. "Karne aaya hoon."

Vikram looks at him for the first time. "Tu bhi yehi kahega? Shaadi mat kar?"

"Main yeh poochhne aaya hoon." Vihaan's voice stays level. "Uska ghar dekha hai tune? Uske papa se mila hai?"

"Woh apne kaam mein hain." Vikram picks the pen up again. "Shaadi ke baad mil lena. London mein reception hoga, sab hoga."

"London mein kahan?" Vihaan asks.

Vikram's hand stops on the paper. "Vihaan, tu yeh sab kyun poochh raha hai?"

The question sits between them a beat too long, and Vihaan lets it sit.

"Seedhi ladki hai woh," Vikram says. "In sab mein nahin padti. Kamre se bahar nahin nikalti."

Vihaan nods once. He files the sentence away with the pen stopping on the paper. The file stays shut.

"Soch le," Vihaan says. "Ek baar aur."

One low string is struck and left ringing under the quiet of the room.

Vikram turns back to his papers. Vihaan closes the door behind him and goes.

## The Four Rules

The small temple room at four in the afternoon, with the brass lamp lit in the niche and the rest of the room dim.

A glass bowl of water sits on the floor beside a mirror covered by a cloth.

The wick has bent toward the door, though the air is still.

Bharani stands opposite Ahana. "Aapka aks hi aapka aaina hai."

Ahana looks at the covered mirror. "Kaunsa rule?"

"Ek Sheshnaagin ke liye chaar niyam sabse zyada khatarnak hain. Pehla, Poornima ki raat. Chahe jo ho, naagin apni kechuli zaroor badalti hai. Jahan bhi kechuli gire, kisi insaan ki nazar nahin padni chahiye."

Bharani draws the cloth away from the mirror. Ahana sees the room, the lamp, and her own face.

"Doosra rule, sheesha aur paani. Jis pal naagin sheeshe ya paani ke saamne aati hai, uska asli chehra sabke saamne aa jaata hai. Jo bhi uska aks dekhega, woh pal mein pehchaan jaayega."

The lamp's flame wavers in the bowl, and Ahana turns her eyes away from it.

"Teesra aur sabse khaufnaak rule: naagin kabhi akeli nahin hoti. Uske sadiyon purane dushman uske saath rehte hain."

"Kaun?"

"Mongoose, mor, dragon. Yeh sadiyon se naagin ke khoon ke pyaase hain. Kahin se bhi aa sakte hain. Kabhi bhi."

Bharani keeps one hand on the back of the mirror. "Yeh ladaai kuch saalon ki nahin. Sadiyon purani hai. Naag Mandir ki kitab mein aur bhi niyam band hain. Jab bhi woh rahasyamayi kitab khulti hai, niyam khud saamne aa jaate hain. Har ichchhadhari naag-naagin ko unki aag se guzarna hota hai. Us haath se koi nahin bachta."

"Chautha niyam," Bharani says. "Har Sheshnaagin ka ek saathi hota hai. Aapka saathi Anantveer hai."

Ahana looks at her. "Woh kahan hai?"

"Naaglok mein koi nahin jaanta. Na uska chehra, na uska thikana. Aapko use dhoondhna hoga."

Ahana holds her gaze. "Toh main dhoondhungi."

She takes out her phone and types the name into its search bar. No result gives her a person she can verify, a face, or an address to follow. The first search leaves her with no usable lead.

"Naag Mandir ki kitab," she says. "Agar usmein koi nishaan hai, main wahin se shuru karungi."

Bharani's hand stays on the mirror. "Uske khulne par niyam saamne aate hain. Unki aag se koi nahin bachta."

"Toh taiyaar hokar jaaungi." Ahana saves the name in a note and adds beneath it: *no face; no place*. "Lekin dhoondhna nahin chhodungi."

Ahana looks at the water. "Aur aapko lagta hai main yeh sab bhool jaaungi?"

"Nahin." Bharani puts the cloth back over the mirror. "Main chahti hoon ki aap yaad rakhein."

## The Family Table

Pammi's dishes cover the table. A chair stands open at the end, beside an untouched plate.

Parmeet gestures Ahana toward it. "Pammi ne tumhari favourite dishes banayi hain. Aao."

Ahana does not sit. "Mujhe bhookh nahin hai. Mere pairon mein bahut dard hai, uncle. Mujhe foot massage chahiye."

"Tum waise hi dukhi ho," Dimple says. "Ahana tumhari chhoti behen jaisi hai na?"

Tina lowers her eyes. The family begins to eat; Ahana's plate stays untouched.

## The Report

The first-floor passage after the family meal, with one lamp burning by the stairs and moonlight at the far pane.

A brass bowl sits on the sideboard beside the tea tray.

Ahana stands in the open doorway, her phone face down in one hand. Vihaan comes up the stairs and stops outside the threshold.

The phone's dark glass catches the lamp, but its screen stays hidden from him.

One cup rests on the tray, a thin ring of tea drying at the rim.

She looks at him a beat too long, then looks at the lamp instead.

The lamp is on his face now: the line of the jaw, the sleeves still folded from the day's work.

Under the quiet, his low string comes in, and the been's second phrase over it — *Woh Do Log*, for Vihaan and the woman in the doorway, and it does not resolve.

He does not step closer, and he does not leave.

"Teen baje ki baat," she says. "Maine suni thi."

"Kaunsi baat?" Vihaan asks.

Beneath it sits the question he does not ask — *kahan?* — in a shut room, at three in the morning. He lets her answer the one he asked.

"Missing-person report. Sirf aapka naam. Ghar ka nahin."

"Wahi sahi tha. Report mein ek naam chahiye tha."

"Main jaanti hoon. Aapne uska naam nahin chhupaya."

"Woh aapka kaam nahin tha."

"Ab hai. Woh baat ab main bhool nahin sakti."

"Aapko bhoolne ke liye nahin kaha."

Somebody calls her name from below, and the stairwell carries it up.

Ahana sets the phone face down on the sideboard, then steps back into the room. The doorway stays open. Vihaan remains on the landing; the lamp keeps burning above the stairs.

The string goes out on the note it came in on.

## The Release

The police station at midnight, with a tube light over the corridor and the files stacked beneath the window.

A radio crackles on the desk beneath the corridor light.

One telephone rings, stops, then rings again beneath it.

"Woh minister aaj jail se chhoot raha hai," a voice says. "Police waale use Changistan bhejne ki taiyaari mein hain. Humein turant kuch karna hoga."

"God, mujhe bahut der ho gayi."

"Dhyaan se suno. Minister sahab ko lekar aa raha hoon. Sab kuch taiyaar rakhna."

At the next desk, the real inspector's wife steps into the station and puts a hand on his shoulder.

"Tum yahan kya kar rahe ho?"

"Tum mujhe sorry bolne aayi ho?"

"Kal raat maine tumhe bahut maara tha na."

The inspector looks over his shoulder. "Patidev? Tum mujhe patidev bol rahi ho?"

"Sorry, baby. Tum mere darling puppy dog ho na?"

"Main on duty hoon."

"Tum chahte ho main yahin tumhe sabke saamne sorry bolun?"

He glances at the corridor. "Bahar chalte hain."

The inspector and his wife step out, leaving the desk empty while the radio keeps talking.

At the lockup, Sameer waits with his hands on the table. The man in uniform opens the gate and takes him out. A second officer calls after them.

"Pichhle das saal ke saare cases ki file leke aao."

"Das saal ki?"

"Bees saal ka boloon?"

"Nahin, sir. Main leke aata hoon."

The station door opens again, and Sameer is led through it before the files reach the desk.

"Minister sahab," the driver says, "yeh gaana apne desh jaake gunguna lijiye. Jaldi kijiye. Isse pehle koi aa jaaye."

Sameer sits back in the jeep. "Dekha mere dost ka kamaal? Ek phone call aur main azaad. Ab main wapas jaaunga apne desh, apne logon ke beech."

The river runs beside the road. A man points to the dark on the other side. "Us paar teen sau kilometre par Megha Desh hai."

"Sabse bade chor, sabse bade dhokebaaz, sabse bade terrorist wahin hote hain. Gun banana, bomb banana, hum sab jaante hain." Sameer looks out the window. "Humare chhote se desh mein badi-badi baatein hoti hain. Tum log do-teen ghotale se darr jaate ho. Hum sar se paon tak ghotale mein sane hain. Phir bhi ghabraate nahin."

"Hum is desh ke dushman aaye kahan se hain?"

"Bharat se do sau kilometre door. Hamara thikana hai Changistan. Chhota sa desh. Andhera itna gehra ki roshni bhi andar jaane se darti hai. Kaal Nagri. Wahan bure kaam chhup ke nahin, parampara se hote hain."

He turns to the man beside him. "Insaan wahan ek cheez hai. Cheezon ki keemat lagti hai. Sabse sasta wahan khoon hai. Pashao ke mahal sone ke hain. Baaki log reth ke mahal mein rehte hain."

The jeep takes the bend. The window glass carries the river and the road over each other.

"Chhabbis saal pehle hamare Pashao ne humein yahan bheja tha. Kumbh ko khatam karne ke liye."

A voice from the front seat answers him. "Agar koi chhabbis saal apne desh se door reh kar is desh ko tabah karne ke liye aaya ho, toh kya uske paas ek hi plan hoga?"

"Nahin. Hamare paas teen plan hain." Sameer counts them on his fingers. "Pehla dragon, jise humne Kumbh tabah karne bheja tha. Doosra woh khufiya jagah jiske documents mil jaayen toh desh ko nist-o-naabood kar denge. Aur teesra hai Naagmani."

"Lekin haath toh teenon mein se kuch nahin aaya."

"Kaise nahin aaya? Naagmani kuch hi din mein hamare haath mein hogi."

The driver keeps both hands on the wheel. "Grenades, tanks, missiles. India jawaab dega."

"Hum bomb phenkenge, woh nuclear bomb phenk kar humein uda denge. Isliye hamare Badshahon ne badi tedhi chaal sochi hai. Gola-baarood hamara hathiyaar nahin. Naagmani hai."

"Woh mil gayi toh?"

"Jangstaan ka har raat ka sapna poora hoga. Bharat ka khaatma hoga. Kuch nahin bachega."

The man in uniform looks toward him. "Baby, tum kitne hot lagte ho jab tum aisi baatein karte ho."

The jeep slows. "Hallelujah."

## The Face

The empty road beyond the river at two in the morning, with the jeep's headlights cutting into dust and the water below the bank black.

A barrier stands open between the road and the bank.

At the far side, crocodile eyes move just under the black surface.

The phone on the dashboard shows no bars.

A glass of water sits in the cup holder; its surface trembles when the engine slows.

Sameer turns toward the woman beside him. "Tumhe pata hai na, tumhari yeh baatein mujhe kitni pasand hain."

The inspector's hand is on the door handle.

"Chup. Yeh nahin ho sakta. Tum zinda nahin ho sakti." Sameer steps away from her. "Tum mar gayi thi."

The woman looks at him from under the cap. "Par woh bhi bemaut."

"Tumhari itni achhi planning kharaab ho gayi." Sameer laughs once, sharp and thin. "Kahan maine socha tha buddhe minister se shaadi karke uski maut ke baad uske paise se aish karunga. Par ab dekho. Mar toh main hi gaya."

The light from the jeep catches the face under the inspector's cap. Swarna stands there.

"Amar hoke toh tum hi aa gaye."

Sameer backs toward the barrier and stops when the water moves; the crocodiles rise around the bank, one after another, leaving the road between them and the jeep.

"Bachao! Please! Peechhe—main naagin!"

The woman smiles. "Nahin. Main naaginon ki naagin hoon."

The road has no other light as Sameer's voice continues after the jeep's engine stops.
