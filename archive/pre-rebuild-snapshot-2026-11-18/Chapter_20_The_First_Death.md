# Chapter 20 — The First Death

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 10.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Cave

The cave wall is dark. A single pen lies on the floor before Mahasaphera. His fingers close around it. He does not look up as he writes. Shadow holds the walls, and the pen is the only light he needs.

The question from the earlier exchange stayed in the cave.

"Aapke paas mauka tha, to aapne ise jaane kyun diya?" Bharani had asked. "Mara kyun nahi?"

"Nahin," Ananta had answered. "Iske naseeb mein itni aasan maut nahi hai. Sone do ise aaj chain se."

The door opens. His assistant comes in sweating, his shirt damp at the collar, his breath quick. He stands at the threshold and does not move farther.

"Guruji, Guruji. Aap apni been bhool gaye the," the assistant says. "Maaf kijiye Mahasaphera ji," he adds. "Kisi ne mujhe gusal khane mein band kar diya tha."

Mahasaphera snatches his pen back. The nib scratches against the stone. He does not raise his eyes.

"Kahan mar gaya tha tu?" Mahasaphera says.

The assistant wipes his forehead. His hand shakes.

"Maaf kijiye, Gurujii. Pata nahi kisne?"

"Theek hai." Mahasaphera says. "Woh ichchhadari naagin. Anant kul ki rani. Use maine khud apne haathon se mara tha. Isliye wo ho hi nahi sakti."

"Ji Gurujii."

"Jao bahar jaakar. Maha sapera ji se kaho do maine use mara tha. Aur accha hi kiya maine mara tha. Warna wo hum sabko mar degi."

"Ji Gurujii."

The assistant steps back. His feet scrape against the cave floor.

"Aur suno. Tumne use khana khila diya tha na? Bhukha toh nahi rakha?"

"Nahi nahi. Maine use khana khila diya hai."

"Tumhe pata hai na ki wo pahadon ka janwar hai. Usse har koi darta hai. Har insaan kamptaa hai."

"Ji Gurujii."

Mahasaphera lays the pen down. The stone is cold beneath his palm. He picks up the been. The flute rests against his lips, dark and still, waiting.

"Jahan meri ye been shastra banakar kaam nahi aayegi, wahan ye astra banakar hamare kaam aayegi."

The score comes up — one low string, held too long, then nothing. The cave swallows it.

## The Engagement

The lawn at the Suri house at seven in the evening, with the sun still on the top of the boundary wall and the chandeliers not yet lit, and the guests standing about in the last of the light with their glasses and their good shoes in the wet grass. Two boys go along the edge of the lawn with a long pole, touching the paper lanterns alight one by one as the light comes down, and each lantern comes on under the pole and stays on.

Tina is in a sari the colour of peaches and she has Ahana's hand up between two fingers, turning it over the way a woman turns over a thing she has bought and is not yet sure of.

"Waise fashion sense to hai ladki mein." Tina turns the wrist once more. "Jo footwear isne pehni hai na — woh best luxury brand ka latest launch hai. Poora ek lakh ka joota, aur isne ghaas mein utaar diya."

"Ghaas mein aaya hai to ghaas mein hi jaayega." **Karan** says it at his own wife's elbow and laughs, and the laugh does not find anybody to land on. "Bhai ne jackpot maar liya. Vikram! Ahana Dhawan — ye kis khandaan ki bahu thi? Baap ka naam koi jaanta hai?"

**"Karan, tu apni shaadi ke chhe mahine baad muh utha ke bol raha hai." Nisha says it over her glass without turning her head, and Tina laughs into her sari, and Karan does not. "Humare ghar mein doosri shaadi ka hisaab hai. Tumhare ghar mein?"**

Nobody answers that, because in that house the money is the only thing anybody is certain of, and the money has been walking about the lawn all evening on a shoe.

Parmeet stands at the edge of the light with his glass held at the level of his chest where everybody can see it, and he is not drinking from it.

Vikram takes the ring out of the box. His hand is not steady, and the box lid comes down and back up twice before the ring is in the open air.

"Haath mat kapaao." Vihaan says it from the side of the group, without turning his head, in the voice a man uses on a horse. Vikram's hand steadies and he laughs, and the laugh is real this time, and he gets the ring clear.

He is moving it toward Ahana's finger when the noise at the gate goes wrong. A car door, and then a second car door, and then heels on the gravel at a pace that nobody wears to an engagement.

From the edge of the light, Parmeet says, "Ruk jao."

Bharani comes up the lawn. She is in white and carries a folder flat against her forearm like a waiter carries a tray, and she walks straight through the guests, who part for her because she does not slow down.

"Suzy." Ahana says it before Bharani is within speaking distance, and the name is a warning and a welcome at the same time. "Tum yahan?"

"She is my personal assistant." Ahana says it to the lawn, and to Tina's dropped hand, and to Karan, who has gone quiet. "Suzy, kya hai?"

"Mr Dhawan ne letter bheja hai, madam." Bharani holds the folder out with both hands. "Aapke liye. Aur unhone kaha hai ki sabke saamne padha jaaye."

The lawn goes quiet in a way that a lawn does not go quiet by accident. Something in the middle of the crowd has stopped, and the stopping runs outward from it.

Vihaan is at Ahana's shoulder before the envelope is open, and nobody has sent for him.

"Madam." He says it low, and puts his hand out flat, palm up, and waits. "Letter mere haath se jaayega. Aap padhiye, par pehle mujhe dikha dijiye ki seal toota hua hai ya nahi."

Ahana looks at his hand for a moment. Then she puts the envelope into it, in front of the whole lawn, and the lawn watches a man it does not know very well take a woman's letter off her and turn it over.

He turns it once. The wax is whole, and the crease is the crease of a thing that has been folded by one pair of hands and not two, and the seal has the impression of a signet ring and not of a thumb.

"Naya hai." He gives it back to her. "Kisi ne ise khola nahi hai. Padhiye."

Only then does he step back half a pace, to where he can see the lawn and not be in it — and the lawn, at that moment, tells him everything. Parmeet's hand has gone to the inside of his jacket before the seal is broken. Karan's mouth has closed on the joke he has not been allowed to make, and Karan's mouth is never closed. Baljeet, by the drinks table with two men, has put his glass down and is already looking at the gate.

**The letter is new. The house is not.** **The lawn has been told what is in the letter before the letter has been opened.**

Ahana takes the envelope. She does not look at Bharani, and she does not step back out of the light. She opens it there, in the last of the sun, with all of them watching, and she reads it out in her own voice, in the voice of a woman reading off a shopping list.

"'Paise ke bhookhe hain.'" She holds the paper at the height of her chest so that she does not have to look up. "'Mujhe sunkar hairani hai ki tumhe kisi sadak-chhaap actor ke bete se pyaar ho gaya hai. Sadak-chhaap — jo mere joote din raat chamkaate hain. Aur tumne aise logon mein uthne baithne waale ladke ko chun liya.'"

Somebody on the lawn makes a sound and turns it into a cough. Vikram's face has gone the colour of the lanterns. Parmeet has still not drunk from his glass.

"'Badi sharam ki baat hai.'" Ahana turns the page over, finds nothing on the back of it, and folds it along its own crease. "Bas. Papa ka poora message yahi hai."

Vihaan looks at her for the length of one line of that letter, and not longer, and looks away before she has finished folding it. He has seen a great many people read bad news off a page. He has never seen one read it without their face doing anything, and he does not have a word for what that is, and the evening does not give him time to want one.

Tina's hands have gone back to her own sari. Karan is examining the lawn, and Nisha is not, and Nisha is watching Ahana.

Parmeet puts his glass down on the tray of a boy who has to turn to catch it. "Ab main bolunga."

He crosses the grass and puts both hands up, palms out, at the level of his own chest, as though the whole lawn has started arguing with him and he is the one calming it.

"Bade sahab ko poora haq hai." Parmeet says it warmly and says it loudly. "Beti ka baap hai. Koi baap apni beti ko sasta nahi bechta, aur koi baap apni beti ke haath kisi ke haath mein dene se khush nahi hota. Hum naraz nahi hote. Hum samajhte hain."

He turns so that he is talking to the whole lawn and to the letter and to the father who is not on it.

"Aur mera jawab yahi hai — ye shaadi hone se koi rok nahi sakta." He puts an arm out toward them both. "Aur main kahunga muhurat nikal raha hai. Aaj hi ring badal lo."

The applause starts where the drinks table is and comes across the lawn in a wave, and it is loud and it is relieved, and under it **Dimple** says something to the woman beside her that makes them both laugh, and it is about the shoe.

Vikram puts the ring on Ahana's finger. His hand does not shake now, and he is smiling, and he says something to her that the applause eats.

"Ye asli heera hai." Tina is back at the hand before the applause has finished, holding the ring up to the lantern light. "Ye asli hai na?"

"Of course asli hai." Ahana looks at the ring, and not at Tina. "Nakli cheezon ki jagah meri life mein nahi hai."

Bharani steps back with the folder under her arm and her eyes down. Nobody on that lawn has asked her a single question, and she has been in the house four minutes.

Aryaman claps twice, and stops, and stands with his hands at his sides in the middle of a lawn where everybody else is clapping. Across the grass, at the corner of the veranda, Pinky stands with her glass held in both hands, and her face has nothing on it at all.

Then the lanterns come on properly, all the way down the lawn at once, and the light changes and the evening becomes the party it has been waiting all day to be.

Vihaan does not clap. He takes Vikram by the elbow and walks him three steps out of the applause, and he does not let go of the elbow while he speaks.

"Attari ki file kal subah kholi jaayegi." He says it with his face turned away from the lawn. "Usme jo hai, usme mera naam aur tumhara naam nahi hona chahiye. Tumhari shaadi ho rahi hai. Isliye ek baat tay kar lo — us file ko haath mat lagaana."

"Tumhe kya lagta hai, main—" Vikram starts, and the elbow comes under his hand and the sentence stops.

"Main kuch nahi soch raha. Main tumhe bata raha hoon." Vihaan lets go of the elbow. "Aur ek baat. Tumhari hone waali biwi ne aaj raat is lawn pe ek letter apne haath se khola hai jismein uske baap ne tumhe sadak-chhaap likha hai, aur uske chehre pe kuch nahi aaya. Main yeh kisi ko nahi bataunga. Par tum yeh soch lena ki tum kis ghar mein shaadi kar rahe ho."

Vikram stands there with his glass. Over his shoulder, at the corner of the veranda, Bharani is walking backwards off the lawn into the dark at the side of the house, and her eyes are on the two men by the drinks table and not on the door she is leaving by.

Vihaan watches her go, and does not follow her, and goes back into the light.

## The Attari File

By ten the lawn has been abandoned for the inside of the house, and the house is full: the chandelier on, the furniture pushed back, the veranda doors open so that the party can spill. The four dark rectangles of the gate screens stand in their case by the door to the security room, and nobody has looked at them all evening.

Vihaan catches Aryaman between the drinks table and the door.

"Tumhare paas file hai," Vihaan says.

"Kaunsi file?"

"Attari ki." Vihaan does not move out of the doorway. "Woh mine jiski photo upload nahi ho rahi. Do din se tum us file ko apne table ki drawer mein rakh rahe ho aur mujhe bata nahi rahe."

Aryaman looks at him for a moment longer than the sentence needs. "Kahan se pata?"

"Kyunki tumne mujhe teen mahine pehle woh drawer dikhaya tha jab usme kuch nahi tha." Vihaan holds his eyes. "Ab usme kuch hai. Photo upload nahi hoti, aur tum phone band kar dete ho jab Roma ka naam aata hai. Main aankhon se dekhta hoon, Aryaman. Yeh mera kaam hai."

"Report aaj raat likhni hai." Aryaman takes his own phone out. "Kal subah."

"Attari ki file pe Vikram ka naam nahi jaana chahiye." Vihaan says it quietly and does not make it a threat. "Aur na Parmeet ke haath se. Woh file kis ke paas se gayi, wohi baad mein batayega ki gaadi kahan khadi thi. Chhoti si baat hai. Bas ek naam."

Aryaman studies him. "Tumhe kis baat ki fikar hai?"

"Us cheez ki jo file mein hai." Vihaan is already turning. "Kal subah chhah baje. Main tumhare table pe hoon."

He is two steps away when Parmeet crosses the floor with a glass in each hand and puts one into Vihaan's without asking whether he wants it.

"Beta." Parmeet's voice is warm and it does not travel past the two of them. "Attari waali file tumhare paas hai? Chhoti si baat hai. Bas ek naam."

Vihaan looks down at the glass in his hand, and then at Parmeet.

"File mere paas nahi hai," he says. "File office mein hai, aur office kal khulega."

"Office kabhi band nahi hota, beta." Parmeet smiles at him. "Jaanta hoon main. Naam badal jaate hain, kagaz wahi rehte hain."

"Papa." Vihaan puts the glass down on the nearest tray, and it is the same tray the boy has been carrying all evening. "Aaj raat main kisi ka naam nahi badalunga. Engagement hai. Ghar mein khushi hai."

He goes. Parmeet watches him go, and the smile stays on his face a beat after it has stopped meaning anything.

At the buffet, Sameer is working through a plate with both elbows out. Vihaan stops behind his chair.

"Sameer." Vihaan's hand comes down once on the back of the chair and no further. "Chal."

"Main yahan hoon."

"Tum wahan nahi ho." Vihaan says it flatly. "Tum Swarna ke aage-khoob drama kar rahe ho, aur tumhare haath kaanp rahe hain, aur tumhari plate mein chhe cheezein hain jinmein se ek bhi tumne banaya nahi. Chal. Baahar hawa khaale."

**"Plate rakh."** Vihaan says it at the level of the table, not the room, and Sameer puts the plate down and stands up, and the two of them go out through the veranda doors together, and neither of them says anything else.

At the door, as they pass it, the lamp gutters and comes back, and Vihaan stops for half a second, and looks at it, and goes on.

Across the room, Ahana has watched the whole thing: the chair, the plate, the two men going out through the veranda doors. She has not moved, and she has not stopped smiling, and the smile is not for Vihaan.

## The Dance Floor

The hall light falls over the crowded dance floor. Ahana is with Vikram among the dancers. Aryaman stands apart at the edge.

A little later, Vihaan comes in from the veranda alone. His jacket follows the lean line of his shoulders; when he turns, the cuff draws over a forearm strong and supple, never heavy. The chandelier catches his face, and two people at the edge of the floor look up.

Ahana lets go of Vikram's hand and crosses to Vihaan. No one bumps her into him. She chooses the place at his side, in Vikram's line of sight, and smooths a fold at his cuff that is not there.

"That cuff is losing its argument," she says. "I see why people look twice."

Vihaan looks at her fingers, then at the ring on her hand. "Vikram is waiting."

"I know."

The drums blur at the edges; his answer is low and even. Ahana's shoulders drop a fraction and her breath comes level. The touch is for Vikram's eyes. The ease that finds her is not.

The face before her is the one she believes killed her family. In her reckoning, Vihaan is a murderer and a powerful Suri figure implicated in the rest of the house's wrongdoing. The ease in her breath changes none of that.

Vikram has stopped dancing. Ahana leaves her fingers on Vihaan's cuff until she feels Vikram's eyes land there; then she takes them away.

Vikram leaves the dancers and goes to Aryaman.

"What's wrong with you, Aryaman?" Vikram asks.

"Dekh, main jaanta hoon tu gusse mein hai. Teen chaar baar mera munh tod chuka hai, maine kuch nahi kaha. Lekin ab bas. Vikram, just listen to me, okay? Abhi tujhse Pinky ka bhai nahi, tera dost baat kar raha hai."

The smile goes off Vikram's face.

"Tu jaanta kya hai is ladki ke baare mein?" Aryaman asks. "Main nahi chahta jo mere saath hua woh tere saath bhi ho. Main bhi kuch nahi jaanta tha us ladki ke baare mein."

"Chhote bhai, woh ladki alag thi. Ye alag hai."

"Ye ladki galat hai," Aryaman says.

Vikram holds his gaze.

He walks back to Ahana. Aryaman stays at the edge of the floor as the music starts again.

"Did Aryaman tell you to be careful?" Ahana asks.

"He said he doesn't want me hurt."

"And Vihaan?"

"He only said I was waiting."

"I could see you." She turns the ring once with her thumb. "Why did he need to say it for you?"

"He was being polite."

"Then let him hear you speak for yourself."

Vikram looks across the floor at Vihaan and goes to him.

"Don't answer for me again," Vikram says.

"I didn't."

"You told her I was waiting."

"Because you were." Vihaan says it flatly.

"Then let me say it."

Vihaan holds Vikram's gaze for a moment, then looks past him to the hall. He does not argue.

Vikram returns to Ahana. "I don't want you fighting with him for me," she says.

"I'm not. I'm telling him not to make this his decision."

She takes his hand. "I trust you."

"He can stay out of our engagement."

Ahana lets the promise sit between them without asking for it. Vikram's hand closes around hers. Across the floor, Vihaan's jaw works once; he turns away before either of them looks back.

At the drinks table, Sameer is occupied with Swarna and a pastry. The conversation near them turns when the ring is mentioned: the precious stone they have been trying to reach is not the one on Ahana's finger.

"Naagmani ke baare mein socho," one of the men says, keeping his voice low. "Woh bas hum chaaron ki hi honi chahiye."

"Arre tu fikar mat kar, Sameer. Woh Naagmani hum chaaron ki hi hogi."

There is laughter, and then the admission that the crocodile still comes in dreams. Once the Naagmani is in sight, someone says, they can take it and leave. The glasses rise again as the music starts.

By eleven the party has moved out onto the terrace and the hall has the look of a room that has been danced in: the floor grey with the marks of shoes, a strip of gold foil off a glass in the corner, and the hired lights going round and round over it and finding nobody.

On the landing above, Swarna has her phone up in both hands, and her thumb on the screen, and she reads what she has written twice before she sends it. Down on the terrace, past the pillar, Ravish has his phone out of his jacket and he reads it with his glass halfway to his mouth, and he smiles at it before he has finished the sip.

"You scared me, Swarna. What's wrong with you?" Ravish asks.

"Kya keh rahe the tum us ladki ke baare mein?" She comes down as far as the fourth step and stops with one hand on the banister. "Is she hotter than me?"

"Jealous?" He grins. "Let me tell you one thing. Shayad tumhe kisi ne bataya nahi, par tum jab gusse mein hoti ho na, to aur bhi zyada khoobsurat lagti ho. Hot."

Swarna looks at him without smiling. Below them the hired lights move over the floor.

Because the woman in Swarna's sari is already dancing.

She is out on the terrace with the men, and she is dancing well, and it is the sari that everyone sees first: the tasseled end of a black dupatta in the lantern light, a wrist going up, the pale green of it, and the men close round her and the men are laughing. Her laugh comes back off the house and it is a laugh that carries.

The woman on the stairs hears it. She stands where she is on the fourth step with her hand on the banister, and the colour goes out of her face, and she is looking at her own green sari out there on the terrace with somebody else's shoulders under it.

Sameer sees it too, and he sees it before he has worked out what he is seeing.

He is at the drinks table with **Arjun** and a paper napkin in his fist, and his eyes go out past the pillar and stay there, and then the napkin comes down onto the table and his glass goes over on its side, and Arjun rights it, and Sameer does not notice that either.

"Tina." He says his daughter's name as if he is looking for a witness. "Ye dekh. Ye kya ho raha hai?"

Tina, who is nineteen and whose father is the only man on that terrace she is not allowed to be angry with, says nothing at all, and that is the worst thing she could have done to him, and he goes.

He goes across the terrace at a pace that makes people move out of his way without knowing why, and the dancing woman is saying something over her shoulder to the man beside her, and the laugh is still going, and he takes her by the wrist.

"Swarna." His voice comes out of him at a size that stops the music. "Music band karo!"

The hired lights keep turning and there is a scrape and the song dies in the middle of a line.

"Behayawani." He holds her wrist up at the height of both their chests so that the terrace can see the arm. "Saare ke saamne, is ghar mein, meri izzat — tumhe koi farq nahi padta. Maine tumhe kabhi nahi roka. Aaj rok raha hoon."

The woman in his hand is not fighting him. She stands there in the middle of that terrace with her wrist up and her face turned half away, and the men from the dance have gone back three steps and are finding something to look at.

**"Aur main tumse poochhna chahta hoon—"** Sameer's voice cracks and comes back bigger. "—ki tumhe kaun saandh raha tha? Kaun saandh raha tha, Swarna? Aur kya main tumhare liye itna hi hoon?"

His hand comes up. It comes up the way a man's hand comes up when a thing has been building in him since before he entered the terrace, and everybody on that terrace sees it come up.

**Swarna's car has been called.**

It is the only sensible thing anybody does all night, and he does it at the drinks table before he moves: two sentences into his phone in a voice smaller than the music has just been — *"Shyam. Swarna madam ki gaadi. Abhi. Peeche ka darwaza, aur chabbi driver ke haath mein"* — and then across the terrace at a pace that is not fast.

"Sameer." Vihaan comes in from the side, and he does not put his body between them, and he does not touch him. He stands at his elbow at an angle that makes the two of them a conversation instead of a scene. "Tumhara glass gira hua hai. Aur tumhare haath mein apni biwi ka haath hai, aur poori duniya dekh rahi hai."

The hand stays up for one more second, and then it comes down, and it comes down onto her wrist again, and lets go of it.

"Woh—" Sameer's mouth works. "Tumne dekha nahi, woh kis tarah—"

"Main sab dekhta hoon." Vihaan says it quietly. "Aur main jo dikha hoon aaj raat, woh main kal bhool jaunga. Tum bhi bhool jaana."

Parmeet arrives then, and he arrives the way he does, straight into the middle of it with both hands up.

"Ruk jaiye." Parmeet's palm comes flat against Sameer's chest and stays there. "Sameer sahab, ghar mein mehmaan hain. **Ahana dekh rahi hai.**"

Sameer turns his head and looks, and Ahana is standing at the top of the three steps into the hall, in a green sari, with her hands folded in front of her, and her face is exactly as sorry as it should be.

"Aur Swarna ko — abhi." Parmeet does not raise his voice. "Gaadi aage laao. Do teen din ke liye. Aur Sameer sahab ko andar le chalo. Music band rahega. Party khatam."

Two men take Sameer away and he goes with them, walking on his own feet and talking the whole way, and the terrace empties out, and the hired lights go round and round over the hire chairs and find nobody, and a boy starts stacking them with a sound like somebody dropping a set of plates one at a time.

Vihaan does not go in. He goes to the bottom of the terrace steps, where Swarna is standing with her sari caught on one heel and her eyes down, and he stands there with his hands in his pockets and says nothing at all for a while, which is the only thing anybody did all evening that had no purpose in it.

"Gaadi aati hogi." He looks at the drive, not at her. "Woh do teen din ka koi matlab nahi hai. Parmeet sahab mehmaan hote hain to narm ho jaate hain. Kal nahi aayege, parso aa jayenge. Is ghar mein theeking fast nahi hoti."

"Izzat ki baat nahi thi." Swarna's voice is very small and very even. "Woh jo hai woh main nahi thi."

"Pata hai."

She looks up at him.

"Yahan kis ne kya kiya, woh mujhe pata hai." Vihaan says it to the drive, and does not smile, and does not make it a kindness. "Aur kis ne kya dekha, woh mujhe pata hai. Main kisi ko bataunga nahi. Aur kal subah aapka car aapke darwaze pe hoga, aur aap woh car chadhegi, aur aap yeh raat kis ke ghar mein guzri hai woh kisi ko batane ki zaroorat nahi."

The car comes. The driver holds the door. She gets in without saying anything to him, and looks back once through the back glass at the house, at the lit terrace and the hired lights and the man standing at the bottom of the steps with his hands in his pockets.

Parmeet finds him on the way in.

"Beta." His father is smiling and the smile is not for him. "Tum bahut de rahe ho aaj raat. Car, haath, aadmi ka peecha—" He puts his hand on Vihaan's shoulder, and it stays there a moment longer than a hand needs to stay. "Ye ghar tumhare naam par chal raha hai. Par ye ghar kabhi kisi ke saath khada nahi hota jab mehmaan andar hote hain. Samajh rahe ho? Kal se tum sirf apni gaadi dekho. Baaki ye log khud dekhenge."

"Ji." Vihaan says it, and his father's hand comes off his shoulder, and his father goes in laughing at something the man on the door says.

The hall is not empty. Up at the top of the stairs the woman in the green sari starts coming down — the same green sari Sameer has just had by the wrist out on the terrace, and a different woman inside it. Vihaan stands at the bottom of the stairs and looks up. She looks at him and keeps coming, and the shoe that comes onto the step into the hall has a buckle on it that no sari at this wedding brought with it.

At the top of the house, above all of them, a light is on in a room at the end of the fourth-floor corridor, and it will still be on at three, and the house will not ask why.



## Sameer's Door

The lights at Sameer's house catch the car as it pulls in. The guard looks up at the cameras above the entrance: two are not working. He watches the minister go past and decides to tell him in the morning.

Swarna follows Sameer inside. He will not wait for her.

The bedroom door swings against the wall, and her hand catches it before it closes.

"Baby, ruko to. Meri baat suno."

"Main tumse itna pyaar karta hoon aur tum mujhe hamesha hurt karti rehti ho." He turns on her. "Shakal nahi dekhna chahta main tumhari."

"Theek hai. Tumne meri baat nahi suni na, to mat suno. Ab mujhe bhi tumse koi baat nahi karni."

She goes into the bedroom and shuts the door. A little later, a voice calls her name.

"Jaanu."

Sameer is in the doorway. Swarna comes forward, relieved to see him.

"Baby, mujhe pata tha tumhara gussa thanda ho hi jaayega. Main jaanti hoon tum mujhse zyada der tak naraaz nahi reh sakte."

The talk turns to the family they killed, and then to the country they intend to destroy and the one to which they mean to go afterwards. The man in the doorway watches Swarna as the words come out.

"Par usse zyada maza to swarg mein hai," he says. "Hai na? Oh, no. I'm so sorry. Tum to narak mein ja rahi ho."

The face changes. Ahana stands where Sameer stood.

## The Murder

The bedroom light falls across the open door. Swarna backs away from it. She runs downstairs and finds Sameer drinking. For a moment she looks from him to the staircase, trying to make the two sightings fit.

Ahana is between them. Swarna runs again, out of the house, and falls. When she looks up, the face above her is changing.

"Tum — tum naagin ho?"

"Haan, main ek ichchhadhari naagin hoon. Par tu mujhse badi aur zehreeli naagin hai. Main to apne dushmanon ko dasti hoon, lekin tune apnon ko hi das liya. Apne hi desh walon ko."

"Please! Koi mujhe bachao!"

"Bhaag jitna bhaagna hai. Aaj maut tere peeche nahi padi hai. Khud tere saamne khadi hai."

Swarna scrambles for a hold on the ground. Ahana catches her and pulls her back towards the house.

"Mera parivaar to tumhare liye sirf ek shuruaat tha. Tumhara asli nishana to ye poora shehar, poora Bharat desh tha. Yahan laashein bichhana chahte the tum, hai na? Par shayad tum ye bhool gaye ki Bharat sirf zameen nahi, ek naag bhoomi bhi hai."

"Mat maaro mujhe."

"Maine maara hi kahan hai tumhe?"

Inside, Ahana takes Sameer's shape. Swarna sees her husband's face as the knife strikes. When the sound ends, Ahana leaves the body where the camera can record it.

Elsewhere in the house, Sameer puts down his drink.

"Zyada hi chilla diya apni jaan par. Jaake use mana leta hoon."

He calls for her as he goes. "Sona? Swarna, baby?"

He finds her on the floor.

"Abhi bhi gussa ho mere se? Dekho, main bahut possessive hoon tumhe leke. Tumhe kisi aadmi ke saath dekhta hoon to — Sona?"

He drops beside her. There is blood on his hands when the guard arrives, and Sameer pulls the knife out, calling her name.

"Sona. Sona!"

Ahana has her first victim. The camera will speak, and the minister will be trapped.

## The Arrest

The police lights wash over Sameer's house and gate. The guard tells the officers what he heard from the bedroom and what he found when he came in.

"Jab maine minister sahab ke bedroom se kuch girne ki awaaz suni, maine dekha ki minister sahab khoon se lathpath the. Yahi hai khooni."

"Maine use nahi maara!" Sameer says. "Main usse pyaar karta tha. Main use kyun maarunga?"

The officers take him away.

The station register lies open on the van's bonnet with the pen still in its spine, and nobody has shut it against the night.

The knife remains with the police. A phone rings by the door.

At the Suri house, the television carries the news. The family gathers around it while the reporter describes the allegation. Someone remembers the quarrel at the party and Sameer's threat in front of the guests.

"Sameer sach mein jail gaya to hamare liye mushkil paida ho jaayegi," Parmeet says.

The broadcast returns with footage from Sameer's house. His bloodied clothes and hands fill the screen. Vikram stares at it; Aryaman turns towards the stairs.

At the station, Sameer insists again that he loved his wife. Then Swarna appears before him, and her face changes to Purvi's.

"Maana ki tumne Swarna ko nahi maara," she says. "Magar mujhe to maara tha na?"

Sameer cries out for help. The police hear him and tell him to stop making excuses.

"Gagan, tu to mera aadmi hai. Kuch kar. Yaad rakh, tune mera namak khaya hai."

"Yes, sir. Par abhi maine kuch kiya na, to aap meri naukri kha jaayenge. Sorry, sir."

At the Suri house, Vikram asks where Ahana is. Aryaman says she is probably planning her exit. Vikram goes to her room.

## Ahana's Door

The first-floor gallery at ten past two, with the bulbs down the wall each in their own shade of yellow, and a tray on the carpet outside Ahana's door with two glasses on it and one of them still wet inside.

Vikram comes up the last stair at a run. Aryaman comes behind him at a walk that is worse.

**"आना! आना, ओपन द डोर।"** Vikram has both hands on the panel. **"आना! दरवाज़ा नहीं खुल रहा। कहीं उसे कुछ हो तो नहीं गया — हमें दरवाज़ा तोड़ना होगा।"**

He is already back a step with his shoulder turned when the door comes open, and she catches him with both hands on his arms before he goes past her.

"Kya hua, baby?" Ahana says it, and her voice is the voice of a girl just woken out of an evening, and her hair is down and wet at the ends.

**"तुम ठीक हो?"** Vikram is holding her shoulders. **"तुम्हें पता है मैं कितना डर गया था? वो न्यूज़ देख के तुम घबरा गई होगी ना?"**

**"कौन सी न्यूज़, baby?"** She says it kindly. **"मैं तो बाथरूम में रिलैक्स कर रही थी।"**

"Kaan to khule the na." Aryaman says it from behind Vikram, and it is not kind at all. **"Ye itni zor-zor se darwaza peet raha tha. Sunai nahi diya?"**

**"नहीं।"** Ahana lets go of Vikram's arms. **"म्यूज़िक ऑन था।"** She opens the door a little wider, so that both of them can see the room behind her — the speaker on the shelf, the dressing table, the bed with the sheet turned down. **"और वो इतना सूदिंग था ना, कि baby, नॉक-नॉक की आवाज़ में बैकग्राउंड में चला जाता है। सॉरी, पता नहीं चला।"**

"Koi baat nahi." Vikram says it too fast. He is smiling at her the way a man smiles when he has decided to be relieved.

**"मुझे ना बहुत थकावट सी फील हो रही है।"** She puts one hand flat against the door frame and leans there. **"थोड़ा सोना चाहती हूं।"**

**"सो जाओ।"**

**"यार, sure."** Vikram steps back. **"हम कल बात करेंगे। एंड यू नो ना — आई लव यू।"**

**"आई नो।"** Ahana smiles at him. **"सो जाओ।"**

The door goes to. The two men stand in the gallery with the tray between them.

And then Aryaman turns to go, and stops, and his hand comes off the banister.

## Two Places

The gallery at the top of the second stair, one bulb in the long corridor and the rest of it in its own dark.

Ahana is standing at the far end of the gallery.

Not behind a door. Not in a room. **Standing in the lit part of the corridor at the top of the other stair, in the sari she wears on the lawn, with her hands folded in front of her, looking at him.**

Aryaman's whole body goes still in a way Vikram's does not.

"Tum —" Vikram looks at the door and then down the gallery and then at the door. **"ये यहां अभी तो ये कमरे में थी। फिर इतनी जल्दी चेंज करके यहां कैसे आ सकती है?"**

Aryaman walks. He walks the length of the gallery at a pace that has nothing in it but the question, and he stops in front of her, and he speaks quietly, and every word of it is a man taking hold of the only thing in the room he is certain of.

**"ऐसा कैसे कर सकती हो? अभी तुम वहां थी। अब तुम यहां हो। कैसे?"**

Ahana waits. She does not answer.

A door stands ajar two rooms down with no light behind it, and none of them looks at it.

**"बकवास बंद करो।"** Aryaman's voice comes down instead of up. **"और चेक तो मैं करूंगा। अगर तुम यहां हो — तो उस रूम में कौन है?"**

No answer comes. The question stays in the gallery.

## The Sisters

The room is dim, its light falling across the floor. Ananta finds Purvi and speaks her name.

A lamp burns low on the bracket by the door, and the wick needs trimming.

"Purvi. Main samajh sakti hoon, use itna kareeb apni aankhon ke saamne dekhna aasaan nahi hoga. Par ye mat bhoolna usne tere saath kya kiya."

Purvi meets her sister's eyes.

Her glass of water stands untouched on the floor by the bed, and the curtain does not move.

"Main jaanti hoon, di. Ab kuch saabit nahi kiya jaayega. Laashein bichha kar jawaab diya jaayega. Aur woh kehlaayega ek naagin ka inteqaam."
