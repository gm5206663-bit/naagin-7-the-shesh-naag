The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## The Invitation

The Suri breakfast table on the wedding-eve morning, with sweet boxes stacked by the door for the evening's distribution and Pinky's tea going cold in front of an empty chair.

Vihaan reads his paper and does not read it. His eyes keep going to the far end of the table, where his sister sits with her eyes down. He lets her be; the morning has Aryaman in it, and Aryaman is the thing to watch.

Pinky sits at the far end with her eyes down, quiet since last evening, and nobody at the table asks her to be otherwise.

Her cup sits untouched where the servant set it, the tea skinning over.

Parmeet finds Aryaman by the sideboard. "Baby." He claps his shoulder, fond and firm at once. "Tumhein pata hai main kab se tumhein dhoondh raha tha? Miss kar raha tha. Aur tum kuch nahin bologe. Kal tum bahut kuch bol gaye ho. Aaj shaadi hai. Mujhe koi gadbad nahin chahiye."

"Dad, you are right." Aryaman picks up his cup. "Mujhse galti ho gayi thi. Aaj main kuch nahin karunga. And I have to say — everything is looking perfect. Bas ek cheez missing hai."

Vihaan looks up from his paper. Aryaman feels the look and smiles through it a beat too long.

"Ahana ke dad?" Aryaman looks around the table. "Mr. Dhawan. Vikram, yaar, tune unhen invite nahin kiya? Ahana ki family se koi aayega to kitna achha lagega na."

"Haan. Unhen invite karna chahiye." Parmeet nods slowly. "Sahi baat hai, Parmeet," Baljeet says from his end, and sets his cup down like a full stop.

"Ahana, beta." Parmeet turns to her. "Tum kya kehti ho?"

"Mujhe lagta hai iski zaroorat humein nahin hai." Ahana keeps her eyes on her plate. "Vaise bhi woh India mein zyadatar rehte nahin hain, aur jab hote hain to aksar kaam mein hi busy rehte hain."

"Ahana, you don't worry about that." Aryaman smiles at her. "Maine already Mr. Dhawan se baat kar li hai. Mere kal ke behaviour ke liye compensation. Dad, woh abhi apni mines pe hain, Delhi ke paas."

Already. Planned yesterday. Vihaan folds the corner of his page down, and waits for the rest.

Vihaan folds his paper once and sets it down. "Main bhi chalta hoon." Vihaan says it the way he says everything at this table — flat, decided, no reason offered. Aryaman showed him the footage yesterday and said *tu dekh*; today he goes to see.

Nobody expected the quiet one. Aryaman covers first — a grin that does not reach past his brother.

"Mines?" Parmeet looks up. "Jahan woh naya heera mila tha?"

"Exactly." Aryaman spreads his hands. "Phir to humein unhen invite karna chahiye," Parmeet says, and pushes his chair back.

"Chalo. Haan haan haan. Chalo chalo. Bilkul." The table breaks into movement — chairs, shawls, car keys, Pinky slipping upstairs without a word.

Three cars pull out of the gate one after another, and the Delhi road takes them past fields and brick kilns while the wedding noise of the house falls away behind. Vihaan takes the second car, with Aryaman's shoulders in his sightline.

## The Mines Gate

The mines gate at noon, with a loaded truck waiting at the weighbridge and the appointment ledger lying open in the gate cabin.

Dust hangs over everything. The office block stands beyond the wire fence, and men with helmets cross between the sheds without looking at the cars.

Vihaan is out of the second car first, and waits while the family sorts itself.

The family piles out. "Chalen." Vikram turns, and Ahana has stopped three steps from the gate. "Kya hua, baby? Chalen andar. Hum chalo chalo. Chalo na. Ruk kyun gayi?"

"Nahin jaana mujhe andar." Ahana does not move. "Kyun kar rahe ho ye sab?"

Aryaman comes up beside her, close enough that only she hears. "Tumne apne hisse ka fun kar liya. Ab meri baari hai. Sabke saamne tumhari asliyat laani hai."

Vihaan hears it from three steps behind, where he has put himself without being asked, and files it with the footage.

Vikram hovers between the bride and the gate, helpless. "Use do minute de." Vihaan does not raise his voice. "Darwaze pe khade hokar mines andar nahin aayengi."

Vikram lets out a breath and nods, grateful for anything to do, and turns back to her. Behind them, Vikram coaxes Ahana through the gate at last.

His keys turn once in his pocket and go still.

At the cabin Aryaman leans in. "Bhaiya, Mr. Dhawan se milna hai."

The guard does not look up from his register. "Dhawan sahab kisi se bhi bina appointment ke nahin milte hain."

"Appointment hai." Aryaman taps the counter once. "Unhen inform keejiye, please."

Vihaan notes the appointment the way he notes everything about Aryaman today — called ahead, planned yesterday, compensation with a date on it. Between the cars and the cabin, Aryaman has stopped meeting his eyes.

Inside the outer office a voice carries over the partition, sharp with habit. "Aap bas quality check keejiye. Mujhe grade one quality chahiye."

A worker comes through with something cupped in both hands. "Sir, sir, ye mining mein mila hai, sir."

They pass the stone from hand to hand while the office fan turns it over in the light, and nobody speaks until Dhawan waves it off.

"Bahut chhota hai." Dhawan does not lift his eyes. "Mere kisi kaam ka nahin hai. Jisne dhoondha hai usi ko de do."

"I think yahi Mr. Dhawan hain." Vikram straightens. "Mr. Dhawan! Mr. Dhawan!" Parmeet calls, and Aryaman with him, and the man at the desk turns at last.

## The Mines Office

Dhawan's office in the afternoon, with sample trays along one wall and a framed map of the galleries behind the desk.

The room smells of dust and machine oil. Dhawan looks the crowd over once, slow, and waits.

Vihaan takes the wall by the door — the place from which the whole room can be seen — and counts the exits in it while the room still stands.

"Dhawan sahab." Aryaman steps forward. "Main Aryaman hoon. Aur maine aapko phone isliye kiya tha kyunki main chahta tha ki aap isse milen." He points. "Is fraud se."

"Ye kya bakwaas kar rahe ho?" The shock goes around the room before Vikram finds the words.

Nobody sits. The sample trays rattle once as someone backs into them, and a glass paperweight rolls to the desk edge and stops.

"Chill, bro." Aryaman holds up a hand. "Main chahta hoon ki tum ye sab apni aankhon se dekho. Dad, aap Vikram ki sagaai isliye todna chahte the na, kyunki aap chahte the ki Vikram ki sagaai Mr. Dhawan ki beti se ho?"

"At least," he looks at Ahana, "ye to yahi kehti hai."

Vihaan watches Dhawan's hands on the desk — steady, ringless, a working man's hands — and finds nothing in them to file.

"Mr. Dhawan." Dhawan rises from his chair. "Ye meri beti hai hi nahin. Ye sab kya bol rahe ho aap log?" Vikram looks from one face to another, lost.

Vihaan watches Aryaman watch the shock land. The room reels; he holds his wall.

"Sahab." Parmeet finds his voice first. "Ye kaisi baatein kar rahe hain aap?"

"Wahi jo aap sun rahe ho." Dhawan does not sit. "Ye meri beti nahin hai."

"To aap kaun se mere dad hain?" Ahana steps into the middle of the room. "Dad aise karte hain kya apni beti ke saath?"

"Aur kya karoon?" Dhawan's voice rises to meet hers. "Jis din tumne apne baap ki marzi ke khilaaf jaake ek do-kaudi-ke-actor ke bete ke saath shaadi karne ka faisla kiya, usi din se tum meri beti nahin rahi. Maine tumse kaha tha ki mujhe un kangalon ke saath koi rishta nahin rakhna."

Ahana's dupatta slips half off her shoulder, and she does not fix it. The room holds its breath around the two of them.

"To mujhse bhi koi rishta mat rakhiye." Ahana does not step back. "Kyunki main apni marzi ke ladke ke saath shaadi karoongi. Aur aapko koi haq nahin banta mere in-laws ko aise beizzat karne ka."

"Maine tumhare liye billionaire chuna tha." Dhawan spreads his hands at the room, asking it to witness the waste. "Aur tumne kya chuna? Na class, na status — struggling actor ka parivaar hai woh. Aur us ladke ke baap ke doston mein kaun aata hai? Ek average news channel ka maalik. Aur ek aisa minister jo apni biwi ka kaatil hai."

The insult is aimed at Parmeet's circle, and a son of that house stands by the door. Vihaan goes very still. Dhawan does not notice; Aryaman does, and looks away first.

Parmeet's jaw works once. Behind him Baljeet studies the floor tiles like a man reading bad news in them.

"Dhawan sahab." Parmeet keeps his voice level with visible effort. "Maana ki hum aapki barabari nahin kar sakte, lekin society mein hamara bhi rutba hai. Hamari izzat hai. Puraani baatein bhool jaaiye na."

"Ab Dhawan sahab, kal in donon ki shaadi hai." Parmeet presses on while the door is open. "Aap aakar aashirvaad denge to bachchon ko bahut achha lagega. Maaf kar deejiye, bachchi hai."

"Main maafi kyun doon?"

The fan turns overhead, useless against the heat. Vikram leans forward with his whole heart in his face.

"Sorry, Dad." Ahana's voice drops to something small and true-sounding. "You know I love you. I am sorry."

Dhawan looks at her for a long moment, and the anger goes out of him like a switched lamp. "Dekho na, hamare chhote se jhagde ke beech mein aise faltu ke log fayda uthaane aa gaye."

"Aur tum?" Vikram turns on Aryaman, sudden and bright. "Yaqeen ho gaya? DNA test bhi karwana hai?"

"Nahin nahin." Aryaman shakes his head, cornered and smiling both. "Ye sab karne ki zaroorat nahin hai."

"I am sorry, bachcha." Dhawan opens his arms. "Mujhse bhi galti ho gayi."

"It's okay, Dad." Ahana goes into the hug, and somebody laughs with relief. "Bhai bhai wah! Papa khush to sab khush!"

Dhawan's pen rolls off the desk in the crush, and nobody bends to pick it up.

Vihaan bends and picks it up. "Sahab," he says, and holds it out; Dhawan takes it, and looks at him properly for the first time.

"Tum kaun ho?"

"Vihaan," he says, and steps back to his place. "Aryaman ka bhai."

Dhawan studies him a moment longer, then the relief-noise takes him back.

The room beams around the hugging pair. Vihaan's eyes never reach them — they stay on Aryaman by the door, counting what the hug costs him. He files it with the footage, with the threat at the gate. The file stays shut.

## The Gift

The assay room in the afternoon, with washed stones drying on felt trays and the strongbox standing open against the wall.

The family crowds in behind Dhawan. Parmeet hangs back a moment at the door, and then comes in too. Vihaan files in at the back, where the trays can be seen over shoulders.

"Sahab, main aapse milne ki koshish bahut dinon se kar raha tha." Parmeet folds his hands. "Maine suna hai in mines mein ek nayab heera mila tha, jiski wajah se mazdooron ki aankhen kharab ho gayi thi."

"In khaanon mein aaye din heere milte rehte hain." Dhawan waves at the trays. "Kuch hi din pehle ek nayab heera mila tha. Maine plan kiya tha ki woh heera shaadi ke baad main Ahana ko gift karoonga."

A pencil lies across the nearest felt tray, keeping some assayer's place.

"No, Dad." Ahana touches Vikram's arm. "Actually mujhe Vikram dilwa dega."

Vikram covers her hand with his for a second, and the room pretends not to see.

"Vikram se nahin mile na?" Ahana turns, bright. "This is Vikram. You will love him, Papa. He is the best. Nahin nahin nahin nahin nahin nahin."

"Matlab Vikram achha nahin hai?" Somebody laughs — Baljeet, from the back.

"Nahin, Vikram achha hai." Dhawan smiles for the first time. "Heera aur bhi achha hai."

"Dikhaoge nahin?" Vihaan asks it from the back, flat. "Nayab heera — dekhne ka mann hai."

Dhawan laughs, delighted to be asked. "Abhi nahin. Shaadi ke baad. Vaada hai."

"Mera matlab ye tha, beta ji." Parmeet steps in smoothly. "Papa itne pyaar se chhoti si gift de rahe hain. Le lo na. Sahi baat hai."

An envelope of assay slips waits on the strongbox, sealed and initialled.

"Theek hai, Papa." Ahana folds her hands back at him. "Woh nayab heera aap shaadi ke baad mein gift ke taur pe de dena. Aur aap dil se de rahe hain isliye main mana nahin karoongi."

Dhawan's ring flashes as he folds his hands — one stone, modest, a miner's vanity.

"So kitni sanskari bachche hain." Parmeet beams around the room. "Jeet gaya, beta."

The room beams with him — Vikram fit to burst, Baljeet nodding, Dhawan wiping his eyes. Vihaan's face does not join them. His eyes sit on Aryaman in the corner, who smiles with the rest and does not mean a word of it. Two seeings in one frame, and his stays shut.

"Haan, hai na." Dhawan clears his throat. "Chalen?"

"Baby, mujhe Dad ke saath to time spend karna hai." Ahana keeps hold of Vikram's sleeve one moment longer. "Tum jao, welcome later. Haan haan, baap-beti ko saath mein time spend karne do na."

"Chalo chalo, beta ji." Parmeet herds them toward the door. "Aap apne papa ke saath achha time spend karna. Haan, chalo, sweet."

"Theek hai." Vikram looks back once. "Main Aryaman ke saath jaata hoon. See you."

Vihaan clocks it: Vikram takes Aryaman. The watched gets a watcher of his own, and he lets them go.

The door shuts on the father and daughter. Outside, the afternoon heat comes back like a held breath let go.

## Outside the Office

The veranda outside the assay room in the afternoon sun, with the yard falling away below and a dumper grinding up the far track.

Parmeet walks Baljeet to the rail, out of the doorway's hearing. Vihaan walks out with the family, and his habit puts him at the edge of their hearing — family standing where family stands.

"Maine kaha tha na." Parmeet keeps his voice low, but triumph carries. "Hamara naya heera wahin pe milega. Wahin, usi jagah. Hamara naya heera — hamari Naagmani."

The word lands in Vihaan's file without meaning and without weight, shaped a little like the temple book's name and signifying nothing. And filed with it, the mismatch: triumph on Parmeet's face, and none on Baljeet's. He does not break stride. He walks on past them to the yard steps, and the file shuts on one more unopened letter.

Below, Vikram catches up with Aryaman by the parked cars. "Ye kaise kiya us ladki ne?" Aryaman stares at the dust. "Maine jitna socha tha usse zyada shaatir nikli woh."

"Ye sab kya tha, bro?" Vikram turns him around. "Tu Ahana ka peechha chhod kyun nahin deta? Suna na uske dad ne kya kaha? Usne apna sab kuch chhod ke, apne dad ke khilaaf jaake mujhe chuna hai. To tu kyun chahta hai ki woh apni choice mein pachtaaye? Tu mera dost hoke uske saath dushmanon jaisa behave kyun kar raha hai? Tu sun raha hai na?"

Vikram holds his eyes. "I love her."

Vihaan hears that too, from the yard steps: his cousin loves the girl his brother hunts. The triangle sits in the file with the word.

"Ya love, right?" He does not look at Vikram at all. "What? I am sorry."

"Tu baar-baar sorry bolke wahi karta hai." Vikram's voice hardens. "Kyunki main nahin chahta ki tere saath dhokha ho —" Aryaman cuts in, fast. "Lekin ab jab sab clear ho gaya hai to mujhe is shaadi se koi problem nahin hai."

Vikram studies him, then nods once and walks back toward the office. Aryaman stays by the cars, alone with the dust.

Problem mujhe us ladki se hai. Us Ahana se. Kuch bhi karke mujhe is shaadi ko rokna hoga. Woh ladki bharose ke layak nahin hai. Kuch bhi kar sakti hai woh.

Vihaan comes down the yard steps and stops beside him. "Asliyat laani hai sabke saamne." He gives the gate's threat back to its owner, word for word. "Ye plan hai ya zid?"

Aryaman puts the phone away without looking at him. "Tu ghar chal."

"Main yahin hoon." Vihaan does not move. "Jab batana ho."

Aryaman walks off past the sheds — not toward the office, not toward the cars. Vihaan waits three breaths. Then he follows.

## The Second Meeting

The back of the sheds in the afternoon, with stacked crates narrowing the passage and a godown door standing ajar.

Vihaan follows at three breaths' distance, and stops where the crates end. Ahead, past the door's black slit, two voices trade low sentences — one young and urgent, one older and flat.

A tin sign creaks on the next godown's wire, rusted past reading.

"Mr. Dhawan, mera naam —" the young one says, and the rest garbles against the tin. "Meri aapse phone pe baat hui thi. Aapki beti Ahana ke baare mein."

"O haan." The older voice takes its time. "Main bhi us — se milna chahta hoon, jo meri beti hone ka daava kar rahi hai. Aap please mere saath chaliye."

"Aa —" A foot scrapes. Hinges complain. The slit of black narrows to nothing, and then there is only the tin ticking in the heat.

He keeps his hands off his keys.

Vihaan does not go closer, and he does not look. Looking gets remembered; hearing is only his.

The godown door opens again. Aryaman comes out alone, and stops when he sees his brother standing in the open with the sun on him.

"Tu yahan kya kar raha hai?"

"Tujhe dhoondh raha tha." Vihaan does not move. "Andar kaun tha?"

"Koi nahin." Aryaman walks past him toward the sheds. "Chal."

Vihaan lets him pass. Aryaman lied to his face without breaking stride. He files the lie with the voices — faceless, and his brother's. And the older voice, promised along, never comes out.

## The Rescue

The mine mouth over the pit in the late afternoon, with the cage standing idle and the alarm bell rope hanging by the notice board.

Vihaan walks the yard while the office sits behind its doors — he cannot go back to calm yet — and reaches the mouth as the first cry comes up.

"Ab bas ye Aryaman koi naya drama shuru na kar de." The words are hardly out — Ahana's, said under her breath as she steps out of the office — when the first cry comes up from below.

"Bachao! Bachao! Bachao! Bachao! Bachao! Koi hai kya? Bachao! Koi hai? Bachao! Bachao!"

Men run from the sheds. "Are bhai sahab, kya ho raha hai? Are woh andar ek aadmi fansa hua hai." The cries go on under the running feet. "Bachao! Koi hai kya? Bachao! Bachao! Bachao! Koi —"

"Bachao!"

"Bachao!"

Vihaan gets to the rope first and hauls it, and the bell takes over — brass over dust, on and on — while helmets crowd the cage and the foreman shouts the cage down.

"Jao!"

The cage drops. The bell stops mid-swing. For a long minute the mine mouth holds nothing but ticking tin and held breath.

"Baja jao." The foreman sends the second cage after the first, and the dark swallows it whole.

Down in the dark, past the cage lights, two shapes go where the men cannot — fast, low, silent — and the trapped man's terror finds a new pitch and then steadies. No trumpet announces it. The rescue plays near-silent, the way deep things do.

"Guru —" The word comes up the shaft with the first cage, half prayer and half disbelief, and the crowd surges forward as the man is lifted out, shaking, held up on both sides.

"Rasta do!" Vihaan shoulders through and takes the shaking man's arm over his own shoulder, and walks him clear of the cage mouth into the thinning dust.

The foreman looks once at the stranger with city shoes, and lets him.

The mines give back their man, and Vihaan never sees how — his eyes are on the man's feet, finding ground. How the dark gives him back, nobody says.

Behind the sheds, away from the crowd, Ahana catches Bharani's sleeve. "Aapne use bacha liya."

"Humne use bacha liya." Bharani's voice stays low. "Aur humein kal ki shaadi bhi bachani hai."

Ahana's praise warms one degree. "Tumhari acting — mines mein, Dad banke. Kaamal kar diya."

"Behosh kiya tha use." Bharani says it plainly, worker to worker. "Asli Dhawan ko. Pehle. Phir uska roop le liya."

## Wedding-Eve

The Suri courtyard in the evening, with strings of marigolds going up along the veranda and the dhol players rehearsing in the corner.

Vihaan has been watching the stairs all evening. Pinky slipped up them at noon and has not come down for the marigolds.

"Kal ka din Suri parivaar ke liye faisla banke aayega." Parmeet stands in the middle of it all, counting on his fingers. "Koi gadbad nahin honi chahiye. Khaane-peene-rehne ke intezamaat behtareen hone chahiye. Behtareen se behtareen."

"Hum — don't worry." The arrangements man folds his hands and backs away into the marigolds.

A tray of marigold heads crosses to the stairs on a boy's shoulder.

"Maine hamare honeymoon ke liye Europe trip plan kiya hai." Vikram swings an arm around Ahana's shoulders.

Europe. His ground. Vihaan's face does not move.

"Kya baat hai. Oh seriously, I love you, bey." Ahana laughs up at him. "I love you too. Aaj main bahut khush hoon."

"Aaj mere iklaute bete ki shaadi ho rahi hai." Parmeet's voice goes thick with the evening. "Sheetal — Vikram ki pagdi to taiyaar hai na? Kyunki iski pagdi pe sehra main khud apne haathon se bandhoonga."

"Kya baat hai. Wah!" The aunties clap. "Abhi lekar aayi." Sheetal hurries inside, laughing. "Aao aao, beta ji, aap jaldi se taiyaar ho jao. Aapke papa aa rahe hain. Aap to khush ho?"

"Bachchon, aap bhi taiyaar ho jao." Pammi claps her hands over the dhol. "Ye lo, mehendi artist bhi aa gayi."

Somebody's abandoned cup steams on the low wall by the stairs.

"Are chal." Ahana takes the mehendi woman's elbow. "Main mehendi waali ko apne saath upar hi le jaati hoon."

At the stairs Ahana stops with the mehendi woman, and beckons Bharani close. The dhol covers them from the courtyard. "Bharani, main upar jaati hoon. Tum yahin ruko. Is Aryaman pe nazar rakhna. Iske dimaag mein zaroor kuch na kuch plan hoga shaadi rokne ka. Aur main nahin chahti ki apne plan mein successful ho."

Pinky comes down the stairs with her eyes down and goes past him without a word. He lets her pass.

Vihaan crosses to Parmeet while the courtyard rearranges itself around the dhol. "Pinky se do baat kar leejiye," Vihaan says, and keeps it flat and short. "Meri nahin sunegi ab."

Parmeet looks at him for a moment, then nods once and turns toward the stairs. Vihaan does not follow. The counsel is Parmeet's to give.

## The Counsel

The passage outside Pinky's room in the evening, with one lamp burning and the wedding noise rising from below.

Parmeet knocks once on the open door. "Pinky, beta."

She sits on the edge of the bed with her face in her hands, and does not look up.

A glass of water stands untouched on the bedside crate.

"Kismat mein jo likha hota hai use koi nahin badal sakta." Parmeet sits beside her, careful, the way men sit who have not done this in years. "Vikram teri kismat mein nahin likha. Is baat ko accept karo and move on karo."

Below, the dhol misses a beat and finds it again. Somebody laughs — Pammi, timing everything wrong on purpose. "Is bande ka bhi timing kamaal ka hai. Chalo."

## The Search

Ahana's room at night, with the mehendi drying dark on her hands and the lamp turned low.

The artist has come and gone. Bharani closes the door and bolts it, and the wedding noise drops to a hum.

The Naag Mandir book lies on the bed between them, shut, its brass clasp catching the lamplight. It came to her room with the wedding gifts, unwrapped among them, unopened.

"Kholo ise." Ahana sits down across from it.

"Khola to phir band nahin hoga." Bharani does not touch it yet. "Aag se koi nahin bachta. Ye taiyaari ek raat ki nahin hai."

"Phir bhi kholo."

Bharani opens the book. The pages lie empty a moment — then she takes Ahana's fingertip and presses it to the clasp's point, and one drop of blood falls on the page and spreads.

Ink rises on the empty page, one line, in a hand neither of them owns: *Jahan aag bandhegi, wahan woh hai.*

Ahana takes out her phone and opens the note. Three lines under the name: no face; no place; parson ke baad.

She cuts the third line. In its place she types the book's line, whole: *Jahan aag bandhegi, wahan woh hai.*

"Pehle shaadi." She puts the phone away. "Aur shaadi ke saath ye."

"Aag se koi nahin bachta." Bharani closes the book on the drying blood, and the clasp clicks shut like a small jaw.

## The Airul Call

Parmeet's study at night, with the curtains drawn and the video screen throwing cold light across two whisky glasses.

Baljeet sits forward in his chair. Parmeet answers the call, and Airul's face fills the screen — all teeth and good humour.

"Kya baat hai, Parmeet bhai." Airul beams at them. "Aaj to bade achhe mood mein lag rahe ho. Tumhare ghar mein shaadi. Hum bahut khush."

"Lekin tumhare paas ginati ke sirf chaar din baaki." The smile stays; the eyes change. "One two ka four. Sirf chaar din."

Baljeet's glass sweats a ring onto the desk, and he does not wipe it.

"Bas aap ye samajh lo." Parmeet leans into the camera. "Ki Naagmani humse kuch palon ki doori pe hai."

"Kitni doori pe?" Airul tilts his head. "Jitni doori pe tumhari maut."

The video stutters. "— Bharat desh —" Airul's voice breaks on the word. "— na hi toota — na hi kata —" Then the picture clears, and he goes on as if nothing broke.

Parmeet's pen rolls off the blotter and falls without a sound on the carpet.

"Suna tumhara dragon fail ho gaya." Airul examines his nails. "Are, 24 saal se tumne kya kiya? Naam kamaya, paisa kamaya. Thoda good luck bhi kamana tha."

"Wahan paisa hai." Airul pats something heavy off-screen, fondly. "Lekin yahan hum."

The curtains breathe once with the cooler draught and settle.

"Bhai sahab." Parmeet clears his throat. "Aapka bachcha bhi hai."

"Bhai, hum bhi milna chahenge Baba se." Baljeet manages it from the edge of his chair.

"Haan haan, zaroor, kyun nahin?" Airul turns, delighted. "Lo, mera bachcha aa gaya."

"Aa gaya mera Baba." The tiger pads into the frame behind him, huge, unhurried, and Baljeet stops breathing.

The tiger breathes on the far side of the screen, and the study air thins around it.

"Mera bachcha." Airul does not take his eyes off the screen. "Mere bachche, is duniya mein insaan bahut hain, lekin wafadaar sirf jaanwar. Isiliye to humne inko apna beta bana liya."

"Lekin mera bachcha bhooka hai." The tiger's tail moves once. "Aur bhooka pet jaanwar dushman aur dost nahin dekhta. Woh poora ka poora kha jaata hai."

A file lies shut under the lamp, and nobody opens it.

The screen goes dark. For a moment the study holds nothing but the two glasses and Baljeet's breathing.

"Kuch bhi karke humein Ahana se Naagmani leni hogi." Parmeet pours with a steady hand. "Kal shaadi hai. Uske baad Mr. Dhawan humein Naagmani de denge. Woh hum Airul ko de denge. Uske baad hum apne desh wapas chale jaayenge."

"Nahin nahin nahin nahin, bhai sahab." Baljeet shakes his head all the way through. "Mujhe nahin banna. Na magarmachh ka, na bhooke sher ka."

## London Tickets

The upstairs passage at night, with gift boxes stacked along the wall and Ahana on her phone in the middle of them.

Rishabh and Baljeet hover near the stairs, wanting her attention. The phone keeps it.

"Mr. Mathur, maine aapko clearly bola tha ki mujhe kal subah ke tickets chahiye." Ahana turns half away from them. "London jaana hai. Main, mere husband, aur — what do you mean? Unki ID mein kuch issue hai? Wait, main aapko phir se bhejti hoon. Please check karna. Mujhe kal subah urgently chahiye. Okay? Thank you."

She lowers the phone. "Uncle, aapne Daddy ko kahin dekha?"

"Beta, woh to seedha shaadi mein aane waale hain na?" Rishabh blinks.

"Opho, mere Daddy nahin, uncle." Ahana almost smiles. "Vikram ke Daddy. Unka ID chahiye tha mujhe tickets ke liye."

"Kal hum log London ja rahe hain." She counts it off for them. "Papa bhi wahin aane waale hain. Woh apne London ke friends ke liye reception wahin rakh rahe hain. Aur kaha ki gift bhi mujhe wahin denge. That's so-called naya heera."

"Beta, never mind." She is already walking. "Main khud dhoondh leti hoon."

She goes down the passage without looking back, and the two men are left standing between the gift boxes with nothing said of what they came to say.

## The Double-Cross

Parmeet's study late at night, with the desk lamp burning low over two whisky glasses.

Baljeet cannot sit still. He stands by the lamp while Parmeet pours, and the words come out of him all at once.

"Bhai sahab, iska matlab ye hai ki Parmeet ka bachcha humein double-cross kar raha hai." Baljeet grips the desk edge. "Shaadi ke baad woh akela mani leke London bhaag jaayega. Aur humein Airul ke magarmachh ko aur uske tiger ko khilwa diya jaayega. Ye hum hone nahin de sakte."

"Dil to karta hai ki iski drink mein zeher mila doon." Baljeet looks at Parmeet's glass and away. "Na iski subah hogi, na hi London bhaag paayega."

"Bhai sahab, agar Parmeet ko kuch ho gaya to shaadi ruk jaayegi." Parmeet does not raise his voice. "Shaadi ruk gayi to Naagmani hamare haathon se phisal jaayegi. Aur agar Naagmani hamare haathon se phisli to wohi humein apne bachchon ka naashta bana dega."

"Chalo chalo." Baljeet gestures at the whole game. "Ye asli hai ki nakli hai? Aajkal Ahana ke gehno ke alawa mujhe to kuch bhi asli nahin lagta. Vaise Sameer bhai sahab ne apni will to likhi hai na."

"Mujhe pata nahin, bua ji." Tina answers from the doorway, where she has been hovering unasked.

Dimple looks up from her knitting by the lamp. "Excuse me." She stands. "Aap log ye kya kar rahe hain? Uncle ke jaane ke baad jo bhi unka tha, woh sab unki betiyon ko to milega na. Par kaun se vaade ka zyada hoga, mujhe ye jaanna hai."

"Mom, chill." Tina turns to her mother, seated silent beside Dimple. "Pata nahin kya ho gaya hai ghar ke saare badon ko — jisse dekho Ahana-Ahana karne mein laga hua hai. Okay?"

The lamp burns on over the two glasses after they go, and the house settles under the wedding hush.

## The Signing

His room on the fourth floor late at night, with the desk lamp on and the wedding hush lying over the house below.

A knock, soft. The servant holds out a courier envelope at the door. "Chhote sahab. London se courier aaya hai. Aur sahab — kal shaadi hai. Aap soyenge nahin?"

"Rakh do." Vihaan does not look up from the ledger. "Shukriya." A breath. "Subah dekhte hain."

The door shuts. He opens the envelope and takes out the renewal page — the corrected line, sent again as asked — and reads it the way he reads every page, to the last line.

The last line holds. One figure once, and the month is the same month.

London's noon window is gone with the day; the corrected page is in his hand anyway, and his signature goes back the same night. He signs under the last line, the pen moving once and stopping.

He scans the signed page and sends it into London's evening. Then he writes the signing in the diary under tonight's date, and caps the pen.

One low string is struck and left hanging over the quiet.

## The Mandap

The Suri courtyard on the wedding morning, with the mandap standing under marigold strings, Vikram seated beneath it in his sehra, and the main door of the house thrown open behind them.

The dhol has steadied into its wedding pace. The family gathers in wedding clothes, and the morning smells of marigold and ghee lamps.

Vihaan stands with the family, close enough to the gate to see who comes. Dhawan is expected, and he wants to see the man's face in daylight.

"Baithiye, jajmaan." Rishabh settles Vikram straighter on his cushion, fussing, borrowing the pandit's word. "Baithiye."

Vikram's phone lies face-down on his cushion, forgotten for once.

A car sounds at the gate, and Rishabh goes forward with both hands out. "Dhawan sahab, welcome, welcome, welcome!"

Across the courtyard an old man's head comes up like a hound's, scenting something over the marigolds. Mahasapera. He goes very still, and the welcome noise goes on without him. Vihaan sees the head come up.

"Ji." Mahasapera reaches Parmeet's side without seeming to cross the space. "Ye hamein dhokha dene ki koshish karega."

The old man drifts back into the crowd, and Vihaan crosses to Parmeet before the dhol swallows the moment. "Ye kaun hain?" he asks under the dhol.

"Shaadi ka ghar hai." Parmeet smiles at the gate, already moving. "Sab aate hain."

Vihaan files the non-answer with the stillness — something in the air turned the old man's head, and he does not know what. The file stays shut.

## Powder

The upstairs passage on the wedding morning, with the house loud below and Ahana's door standing ajar.

Mahasapera comes up the stairs without hurry and without sound. He stops at her door. He looks in.

The mirror faces the door. In it, for one held breath, the naagin looks back at him — green and gold where the bride should be.

*Ichchadhari.*

The word lands in him like a stone. He steps back from the door, takes a paper twist of white powder from his sleeve, stoops, and pours a white line across her threshold, end to end.
