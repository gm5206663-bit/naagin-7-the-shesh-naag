# Chapter 30 — The Warning (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 19, 28 February 2026.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Miracle

The night keeps the first images to itself — an old temple behind her sleeping eyes, and a pull under her ribs when she wakes, as if something far off has taken hold of a thread tied to her.

"Kya ho raha hai mujhe." Ahana sits up in the dark. "Us din wo ajeeb sa sapna — ek puraane mandir ka. Aur aaj ye ajeeb sa khinchaav. Jaise ki mujhe kuch — "

"Aakhir Naagrani gayi kahaan?" Bharani's voice goes thin down the corridor, searching rooms that do not answer.

Across town the night belongs to Tina, out of the house after weeks and telling her phone all about it. "O guys — finally. Itne time baad main ghar se baahar nikli hoon party karne ke liye. Warna hamesha us ghar mein kuch na kuch chalta hi rehta hai."

Her friends crowd the frame. "But we heard ki tum to uski seva and all kar rahi thi."

"No no no." Tina waves it off. "Main uski koi seva-va nahin kar rahi thi — us Ahana ki. Okay, hogi wo koi Mr. Dawan ki beti. Par main bhi kuch kam nahin hoon. Mere papa bhi bahut bade minister hain."

"I'm sorry, yaar." A friend goes quiet. "But wo tere dad ne — teri mom ke saath — I mean, tere stepmom ke saath — bura hua, yaar, wo sab."

"Come on, yaar." Another cuts in. "Tina kitne time baad hamaare saath aayi hai. Chhodo ye saari baatein. It's party, man."

Tina's phone light trembles in her grip.

The car climbs the hill road with the music up, Mandy at the wheel, Tina laughing — and then the laughter turns.

"Mandy — Mandy." Tina screams at the wheel. "Bachao. Bachao, do something — bachao."

"Please — koi bachao." The headlights swing over empty dark. "Please koi hai. Ye kya ho raha hai. Ye gaadi upar kaise jaa rahi hai."

The keys shudder in the ignition.

A fender hangs over the drop, and the valley waits below.

The car rises. Slowly, impossibly, the metal lifts back onto the road as if the hill itself has changed its mind, and the engine ticks in the sudden quiet.

"Ye kya ho raha hai." Mandy stares at her own hands. "Koi miracle hai kya."

"Guys — guys." Tina is out of the car, staring back down the road at nothing her friends can see. "Aap upar kaise — my god."

A shape moves at the road's edge where no shape should be — scale-gleam, serpent-length, gone before the second look.

"Mujhe aisa kyon laga." Tina does not take her eyes off the empty road. "Ki wahan Ahana thi."

## The Book

The temple holds its lamps low, and the old book lies open where Bharani has led her.

"Ye aap kya karne jaa rahi thi." Bharani does not let go of her wrist. "Mujhe kuch pata nahin, Bharani. Mujhe ajeeb-ajeeb se khayaal aa rahe hain. Wo kitaab jo naag mandir mein hai — aur phir wo Sheshnaag. Ye sab mujhe kya kehna chahte hain? Chaliye mere saath."

"Kya hua, Bharani?" She follows him in. "Aap chaliye mere saath."

Dust moves in the lamp-shaft over the open page.

"Ye sab kya ho raha hai." She looks from the page to him. "Maine aapko pehle bhi bataaya tha." Bharani's voice drops to doctrine. "Ki bhavishya mein hone waali ghatnaaon ke poorvaabhaas aapko pehle hi hote rahenge. Naag mandir ki is pauraanik kitaab ke maadhyam se Mahadev humein samay-samay par chetaavni dete hain. Aur jab bhi koi badi vipda aane waali hoti hai — to aapse poorv ki chhah Naaginein aapko sanket deti hain."

The painted Naagin stares up from the page, and her eyes seem to burn.

"Is baar bhi." Bharani does not blink. "Is kitaab ke maadhyam se humein chetaavni mil rahi hai. Kuch bahut bura hone waala hai."

The diya gutters beside the open page.

"Naaginein hamesha desh ko bachaati hain." He holds her eyes. "To is baar bhi koi nayi museebat aa rahi hai."

Her ring clicks once against the page's edge.

"Mujhe nahin pata kya hone waala hai." Ahana closes her hand over the page's edge. "Lekin main us dushman ko khatm kar doongi."

## The Resolve

The den's low room keeps its maps unrolled under a smoking lamp, and its patience gone.

"Ye Hindustan hai." Airul looks at the marked country a long moment. "Jisko humne bees-pachees saalon se barbaad karne ki koshish ki. Par unfortunately — is Hindustan mein Kumbh ke mele ke din hamaara dragon aur hamaara baarood dono hi naakaamyaab hua."

Parmeet shifts. Baljeet does not.

"Par hum thodi na haar maan-ne waale hain." Airul turns.

Parmeet's glass sweats untouched beside the map.

"Ab hum aisa kuch karenge — jisse inhein pata chalega ki dehshat inse kitni door hai. Arre inka ye Delhi — uska dil, uski soch, uski bhakti. Ye bhakti mein hi inki asli shakti hai. Agar Hindustan ko todna hai na — to pehle Hindus ko todna hoga."

A snake crosses the floor between them, and Parmeet flinches back hard.

"Previous plans flopped." Airul does not look at the snake. "To ab naya plan execute hoga. The real game begins — ab se. Is baar sirf aur sirf meri marzi chalegi."

"2025 ke Kumbh mein humne haath badhaaya tha." His voice goes low. "Lekin waqt ne hamaara haath hataa diya. Inke paas ab waqt hi nahin bachega."

"Par ye hamla shuru kahaan se hoga?" Parmeet finds his voice. "Which place is the target?"

"Jab taakat mutthi mein hoti hai." Airul closes his fist where they can see it. "To poori duniya ek khilauna ban jaati hai. Aaj yehi khilauna hamaare haath mein hai. Taareekh likhne waale to bahut hote hain — lekin taareekh badalne waale kam. Aur aaj ye dono kaam hum karenge."

The lamp gutters over the marked map.

"Pehle galti ki gunjaaish thi." Airul lays his palm flat on the country. "Kyonki taakat adhoori thi. Lekin ab taakat poori hai. Aur jab taakat poori hoti hai — to ek hi cheez bachti hai. Nateeja."

## The Empty Room

Vihaan wakes to a dark room gone quiet in the wrong way.

The lamp burns low by an unslept bed, and the house beyond the door has nothing to say for itself.

"Ahana." Vihaan checks the balcony, the corridor, the stairs going down. "Ye ladki itni raat ko kahaan chali gayi."

He searches the floor room by room, and finds no one awake to ask, and comes back to wait — and then the door moves, and she is there, too composed by half.

"Tum itni raat ko kahaan gayi thi." He does not sit. "Kahaan jaaogi."

"Paani peene." She does not look at the bed. "Kitchen tak."

"Kitchen mein paani." He looks at the bed instead. "Aur wo bed pe pillows — wo aise kyon rakhe the. Jaise wahan pe koi so raha ho."

A pillow still holds a sleeper's dent with no sleeper to own it.

"Kyonki mere sone ka tareeka wahi hai." She meets his eyes. "Main waise hi soti hoon."

He holds her look a beat longer than comfort allows — the wife who arranges pillows like alibis and drinks water like a story — and files it with the rest of what the night has not explained. "Tum — " he starts, and the house phone rings downstairs before he can finish, and Aryaman's voice answers it two floors below.

## The Loyalty

Ravish's room smells of the party still on him, and his phone glows against his ear under the low lamp.

"No no no — you listen. Listen to me." He swirls the glass. "Main kya bol raha hoon — listen to me. Tum us party mein bahut zyaada khoobsurat lag rahi thi. Sachchi. I promise — maine thodi si daaru pi hai, thodi si — par tum bahut zyaada khoobsurat lag rahi ho. Tumhaari aankhein. Tumhaare khoobsurat-khoobsurat baal. Aur tumhaari wo — naagin jaisi chaal."

"Ek second — hold karna." He squints past the glass. "Kya — "

A snake slides in through his doorway and stops where he can see it.

"Saanp." The glass lowers. "Saanp — nahin. Main — "

"Can you hear me?" His girlfriend's voice goes small in the phone. "Hello — "

"Tum sirf — tum hi tum to ho yahaan." Ravish stares at the snake, then past it. "Aur koi mujhe dikh to nahin raha. Hai na — beautiful?"

"Excuse me." The phone crackles. "Tum mujhse baat kar rahe ho ya kisi aur se?"

"Baad mein baat karta hoon." He cuts the call. "Sorry."

The snake is gone; Bharani stands in its place, and the doorway stands empty behind her.

"Sorry sorry sorry." Ravish backs a step with both hands up. "So I am — I am very very sorry. I didn't mean to do that. Main — please mujhe maaf karna. Mujhe maarna mat. Dekho mujhe waise hi bahut ladkiyaan gaaliyaan deti hain — but maine kabhi abhi tak maar nahin khaayi."

"Aaj ye — kal koi aur." Bharani studies him. "Ye tumhaari loyalty hai."

His glass sweats a ring onto the side table.

She kisses him on the cheek before he can answer, and his apology dies where it stood.

Her bangle clicks once against the doorframe as she turns to go.

Ise zaroor kuch pata hoga, she thinks, watching his face. Hamaare desh par kaun sa khatra aa raha hai.

## The Call

The house phone has barely stopped when Aryaman's voice fills the stairwell in the dead of night.

"Haan — Saxena. Bolo." A pause. "Aryaman — main tumhaara source isliye hoon kyonki main jaanta hoon tum India se kitna pyaar karte ho. Aur ye news achchi nahin hai. Aryaman — koi India ke saath bahut bura karne waala hai."

"Dad — Ramesh — Karan — Arjun. Jaldi aaiye." Aryaman is already halfway down. "Mom — most stupid. Kya hua? Dad kahaan hai?"

"Wo to kisi kaam se baahar gaye." His mother hurries out. "Par hua kya?"

"Humein office jaana hoga." Aryaman takes the stairs two at a time. "Abhi. Abhi-abhi India ke liye threat aaya hai."

"What? India pe?" Karan stops where he stands. "Yes. Par bro — hum to safe hain na?"

"Hum rahe na rahe." Aryaman looks back up the stairs — at Vihaan on the landing, at Ahana a step behind him, at Tina hovering past them with her eyes fixed on Ahana. "Desh ko kuch nahin hona chaahiye. Isiliye humein abhi office jaana hoga — aur army aur police ko inform karna hoga. So get ready. Quick."

"Ab ye Ravish kahaan hai?" He grabs his keys. "Whatever. I don't have time for this."

The keys ring once against the bowl as he takes them.

"Main bhi chal raha hoon." Vihaan comes down with his wife's water-story still unsolved between them, and it is not a question. Ahana's nod is a beat too quick — mujhe Aryaman ke saath rehna hoga, she is already thinking, kyonki jis jagah se ise khabar mil rahi hai wahin se khatre ka sach bhi milega; uske baad hi to main us khatre ko rok paaoongi — and Tina watches her think it.

Across town, Ravish's phone lights with his brother's name. "Hello — haan haan — aa rahi hoon — Man, bhai, bolo na jaldi." He listens, and his drunk haze clears all at once. "Office pahunch — bahut urgent. What — bhai — just — just give me one minute. Main — haan, main aa raha hoon. Okay okay okay — guys, hurry up, hurry up, come on."

"Tumhaare bhai ka call tha." Bharani is already stepping back. "Tumhein jaana chaahiye. Parmeet sir aur Baljeet sir bhi ghar pe nahin hain."

"Yeah — you know what — wo kya hai ki — " Ravish grabs his jacket. "Kya bolo — kya plan hai? Plan plan plan — listen babe, I have to go. Main — main tumse baad mein milta hoon. Okay — I have to go. I'm sorry, bhai — hello."

The door shuts behind him, and Bharani stands alone in his rooms.

Iska matlab kitaab ka ishaara sahi tha, she thinks.

Aur Naagrani ka bhi — that warning has not closed.

## The Office

The channel floor runs on cold light and ringing phones, and the night staff moves like a disturbed hive.

"Sir — mere paas national attack ki tip aayi hai." Aryaman holds the officer's eyes across the desk. "Arre man — ye same alert abhi humein kuch der pehle aa chuka hai. Hum already iski jaanch mein lage hue hain."

A file cart rattles past toward the studio.

"That's great." Aryaman does not sit. "Mere paas abhi aur koi information nahin hai. Lekin main source contact mein hoon. Humein kuch pata lagta hai to hum aapko bataate hain."

"Hamaari taraf se har jagah alert jaa rahi hai." The officer stands. "I will keep you updated."

Cold air hums from the dead vents.

"Yahaan Aryaman ko itni andar ki khabrein mil kahaan se jaati hain." Karan watches them from the floor's edge. "Ki kahin kuch hone waala hai."

"Inke khabri ka network bahut strong hai." Dinesh does not look up from his screen.

Dinesh's screen fills with red tickers. "I'm sure Dad aur taauji ko bhi kuch idea zaroor hoga ki yahaan par kya ho raha hai."

A red tally lamp burns over the dark studio glass.

"Any update?" Aryaman crosses to the desk. "Abhi tak kuch nahin. You guys on the news. Done, bhai — hum floor pe milte hain."

"Sir — Roma kahaan hai?" He stops. "Wo aaj pehli train nikal rahi hai na — Kashmir se Amarnath ke liye. Roma wo cover karne gayi hai."

The floor holds its breath between two rings.

"Arre wo to mere dimaag se nikal hi gaya." Aryaman rubs his face. "Is tip ne mujhe side-track kar diya tha. Kaun si tip, sir? Okay, sir."

"Dinesh." Ahana finds him at the huddle's edge. "Dinesh — "

"Sir — sorry — aapko mera naam kaise pata?" Dinesh stares.

"Oh god — Purvi yahaan kaam karti thi." Ahana smiles. "Tu nahin — yahaan aana — of course pata hoga na, Dinesh. Arre — Aman har waqt tumhaari hi baat karti rehti hai. Dinesh aisa, Dinesh waisa — Dinesh jaisa koi nahin. Kabhi-kabhi tumse door se jalan hone lagti hai."

"Kya, ma'am?" Dinesh flushes. "Haan — aur kya. Achcha sun."

She lowers her voice, and the smile stays exactly where it is. "Secret hai. Bahut important hai. Jaise threat ke baare mein tumhein pata lage — sabse pehle. Main — "

"Main sabse pehle aapko bataaoonga." Dinesh straightens. "Aryan sir se pehle bhi."

"Very good." She turns him toward the floor. "Tumhaari promotion pakki hai. Jaao."

Vihaan has watched the whole exchange from the desk's far side without moving. "Jaldi se kaam karvaao." He says it to the room at large, and the room moves. "Chalo — pata karo. Hamaare paas time nahin hai. Jaldi jaldi jaldi."

His eyes find his wife's across the moving floor — the woman who buys newsroom loyalty with promotions between one breath and the next — and hold them one beat too long.

"Bas." He looks away first.

## The Launch

Delhi station holds its breath under the floodlights' glare, and Roma's camera burns steady in the crowd.

"Aaj ek historic journey shuru ho chuki hai." The studio voice rolls under the pictures. "Delhi se Kashmir jaane waali ek khaas train — ek naye bridge ke raaste se — Anandnath tak pahunchegi. Wo bhi record time mein. Is train se Amarnath yatra hogi zyaada aasaan — zyaada surakshit. Hazaaron shraddhaalu Bholenath ke darshan ke liye ravaana ho chuke hain. To aap aur hum yahaan kya kar rahe hain? Chaliye chalte hain — Delhi railway station — jahaan hamaari reporter live hai."

"Delhi se Amarnath tak — ye superfast train ki pehli yatra hai." Roma walks the platform with the crowd parting around her. "Jismein har ek chehre pe utsaah aur shraddha ki chamak saaf dikh rahi hai. Haathon mein tiranga — aur Har Har Mahadev ke jaikaare se mahaul goonj raha hai."

A child's paper flag snaps in the engine's hot wind.

Roma's camera finds the whistling engine.

"Bachche, boodhe aur javaan — sabhi is pavitra safar ka hissa banne ko beqaraar hain." Roma turns with the moving train. "Ye safar sirf ek yatra nahin — aastha, ekta aur romaanch ka anokha sangam ban chuki hai."

The whistle goes, and the devotees answer it.

"Har Har Mahadev." The chant rolls down the platform as the wheels turn. "Har Har Mahadev."

## The Reveal

The den's low room fills with the screen's glow, showing Roma's live pictures back to them, frame for frame.

"Ye — ye tumhaara hi telecast chal raha hai." Airul does not take his eyes off it. "Live telecast. Tum poori duniya ko dikha rahe ho — aur tum yahaan par andhe bane hue ho."

"Lekin Pasha — " Parmeet falters. "Humein aapki baat samajh nahin aayi. Hamaari baat samajh mein nahin aayi."

"Ye — ye Hindustan ki khaas train." Airul turns up the volume, and the chant fills the den. "Delhi se nikal kar Ambernath tak jaa rahi hai. Ismein hazaaron shraddhaalu — bachche, boodhe — sab ek saath jaa rahe hain. Aur inke saath baith kar jaa rahi hai — inki maut."

The screen's glow crawls across his face.

Parmeet's ring twists round his finger.

Baljeet's phone stays dark in his fist.

"Aate jaao — aate jaao — bharte jaao — marte jaao." He counts the compartments off on his fingers. "Jitne zyaada honge — utna zyaada mazaa aayega. Is baar koi nahin bach paayega. Koi bakhsha nahin jaayega. Arre — is baar nateeja aayega."

"Ye train jo hamaara guroor hai." His voice drops under the chant. "Ye — ye inka guroor toot jaayega. Dhu-dhu karke sab jal jaayega. Humko bahut mazaa aayega. Bahut mazaa aayega."

On the platform, the last doors slam. "Abe dhakka kya de raha hai be?" A scuffle flares and dies. "Dhakka diya nahin — lag gaya, yaar. Chalo chalo chalo. Jaan de — dhakka maara na. Maine kuch nahin kiya. Zyaada bakwaas mat kar." — "Kya jhagda ho raha hai ye? Apni jagah pe jaake baitho. Chalo." — "Theek hai, sahab."

Back on the channel floor, the feed fills the wall — and Aryaman goes still in front of it.

Vihaan steps closer to the wall, the wrong angle running under his eyes. "Ruko. Ye footage — ye to hamaara coverage nahin hai."

Aryaman turns — "Kya?" — and Vihaan does not blink. "Ye kisi baahar ke camera ka point of view hai. Hamaare bande pe laga hua camera — ye uska POV hai."

Aryaman is already moving. "Dinesh — feed nikalo. Abhi. Trace karo — kahaan se aa raha hai ye."

The wall shows the faithful waving flags at a lens that is not the channel's — and the brothers stand in its light, one who read it, one who moves on it.
