The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Same Day, Twenty-Four Years Later

One in the morning, at the Arora house, and the terrace is the only place in it with the light off. Sheshnaag's hood is not shown, but the terrace feels like a temple that has forgotten its god.

Gurinder Arora stands at the parapet with his phone in his hand and his spectacles in the other, and he is not talking to anybody.

Twenty-four years. He does the sum again, because the sum is the part that does not fit. A family that comes out of nowhere with a surname half the district shares. A channel that owns a basement. A boy who prints cards in the middle of the night. And a wedding in the morning that is the only reason any of them sit in the same house.

"Kya yeh log Mahakumbh mein bahut badi dahshat phailana chahte hain?" He says it aloud. "Pichhli baar jab yeh log aaye the to Mahakumbh tha. Aur aaj, chaubees saal baad, wahi din."

The wind comes over the parapet and turns the edge of his kurta.

"Shayad yeh log kuch bahut bada plan kar rahe hain."

He looks out at the roof across the street, where a water tank sits and nothing else happens.

"Nahin." He puts his spectacles on. "Main inhein inka maksad kaamyaab nahi hone doonga. Kisi bhi haal mein nahin."

The door to the terrace opens. Ananta stands in it with her dupatta round her shoulders.

"Papa? Aap yahan?"

"Yes, main sahi tha." He says it to her, and then to the stairwell, where his wife is coming up. "Ananta, Purvi ke office mein zaroor kuch ajeeb chal raha hai. Mujhe uske saboot khojne honge."

"Papa, abhi?"

"Abhi." He takes his keys off the parapet. "Tum dono so jao."

## The Sisters' Room

The girls' room has two beds in it and one of them is covered in clothes. The dressing table has a tin of powder open and a lamp angled wrong, and the ceiling fan makes a sound on the second turn every time.

Ananta is on the near bed with her knees up and her hands folded on them. Purvi is at the dressing table with her back to the room and her hair down.

"Jab pyaar ho jaye to kya hota hai?" Purvi asks it of the mirror.

"Kya?"

"Jab pyaar ho jaye to kya hota hai, di?"

Ananta sits up. "Ek second." She points. "Madam, tum yeh sab kyun poochh rahi ho? Pyaar to nahi ho gaya meri behen ko?"

"Pata nahi." Purvi turns round. "Shayad yeh pyaar hi hai jo mujhe usse pehli nazar mein ho gaya. Jab se usey dekha hai, dil kahin kho sa gaya hai."

The fan goes round twice.

The tin of powder on the dressing table is open and there is a print of a hand in it.

On the far bed the clothes are piled high enough to slide off the edge, and one bangle has already gone to the floor.

"Kahin tere is dil ka chor Vihaan to nahi?"

"Nahin." Purvi says it at once. "Woh kyun hoga?"

"Purvi." Ananta comes forward. "Camera-man type ke ladke na, are terrible. Yeh sirf dil todna jaante hain. Tujhe pata hai kaisa ladka chahiye?"

"Kaisa?"

"Koi bahut seedha-saadha. Pyaar karne wala. Bahut hi achha sa aadmi. Pyaar nibhane wala banda." Ananta's chin comes up. "Vihaan jaisa nahi."

"Toh kaisa?" Purvi puts the comb down. "Aisa ladka Dilli mein kahan milega, di?"

"Dilli mein nahin milega." Ananta sits up against the wall with her arms round her knees. "Isliye to main keh rahi hoon."

"Kya keh rahi ho?"

"Ki jo mile, use dekh ke samajhna." Ananta's chin stays up. "Aaj raat sangeet mein mera gaana chal raha tha. Beech mein band ho gaya."

The fan goes round.

The window is open on the lane and a scooter goes past and does not slow down.

"Di, woh—"

"Poora hall chup ho gaya. Do sau aadmi. Aur woh aadmi sound table pe khada tha, haath fader pe." Ananta says it slowly. "Usne band karwaya. Kisi ne poochha nahin. Kisi ne kuch nahin kaha."

Purvi does not answer her.

The dressing table mirror has a photograph pushed into the corner of its frame, and the photograph faces the wall.

The fan's cord hangs down over the bed and the switch on it is at the bottom of the swing.

"Uske baad maine dekha." Ananta's voice stays where it is. "Tu kaanch mein ghutnon pe thi. Tere paer ke neeche toota hua glass. Aur Parmeet Suri ka beta teen sau logon ke saamne ghutnon pe baith kar tujhe uthata hai. Apne baap ke ghar mein. Apne baap ke mehmaanon ke saamne."

"Woh—"

"Aur ek bhi aadmi usse rokne ke liye nahin uthta. Ek bhi nahin." Ananta's hand comes down flat on the bed. "Yeh ghar ka badmaash nahin hai, Purvi. Yeh ghar ka chhota maalik hai. Aur jo us ghar ka maalik hai, woh Papa se aise baat karta hai jaise naukar se."

"Di, bas—"

"Usne tera naam liya." Ananta does not look away. "Teen sau logon ne suna. Main ne suna."

Purvi's hand comes off the powder tin and leaves the print of it in the powder.

The bedside lamp is on and its shade has a scorch mark on one side.

"Woh mujhe naam se bulata hai." She says it quietly, to the tin. "Yeh galat hai?"

The fan goes round twice. Ananta looks at her sister for a long moment, and then she lies back down and puts her arm over her eyes.

"Main tumhe kaam se nahin rokti." She says it to the ceiling. "Main tumhe us ghar se rokti hoon. Kal main usi parivar mein ja rahi hoon. Main jaanti hoon main kya keh rahi hoon."

"Of course, di." Purvi picks her comb up. "Aur mujhe bhala Vihaan se kyun pyaar hoga?"

"Bas." Ananta's arm stays over her eyes. "Yeh baatein raat ko nahi karni chahiye. So jao."

"Ruk na."

Purvi opens the slim diary beside the lamp and turns it to the page the list is on, and reads it down with her finger.

"Do Bareilly ke liye cars, teen Kapurthala ke liye, aur agar woh Chachi aa rahi hongi na to unhein zaroor bolungi ki woh sattu zaroor leke aaye."

"Tumhein sattu kyun padi hai?"

"Dekho." Purvi turns the page round. "Yeh poori list hai unke naam ki. Sab same hai." She waits, and then she says it. "Iska matlab yeh log ghuspaitiye hain. Jo hamare desh mein chori-chhupe ghusse hue hain. Maine unke kamre mein Mahakumbh ki cuttings bhi dekhi hai."

Ananta looks at the page for a while.

Down in the house somebody is running water into a bucket and the noise of it comes up the stairwell.

"Aap so jaao." She says it gently. "Mujhe lagta hai aapko so jana chahiye, kyunki kal shaadi hai aur pheron ke liye aapko raat tak jaagna hai."

Purvi closes the diary and puts the light out, and in the dark, from the other bed, her sister says one more thing.

Outside the window the lane's lamp goes out and comes back and stays on.

"Purvi."

"Hmm."

"Kal Mummy ke saamne main kuch nahin bolungi."

"Kuch hua hi nahin, di."

"Haan." The fan goes round twice in the dark. "Wahi to."

## The Cake

Half past one, and the house has gone quiet the way a house does after a party, with one light on in the passage and the kitchen still smelling of the catering.

"Chalo." Ananta is at the door of their room with her hand on the switch. "Ab so jaana chahiye. Kal pheras hain, raat tak jaagna hai."

"So to jaana chahiye." Purvi is sitting on the bed with her knees drawn up. "Lekin apni behen ko birthday wish kiye bina kaise so jaaun?"

Ananta stops with her hand on the switch.

On the table the plates are stacked and the fork on top of them has been licked and put back.

"Aapko yaad tha?"

"Haan." Ananta comes and sits down. "Tumhein laga main bhool gayi."

The door opens without a knock and the man of the house comes in with a cake box in both hands.

The box in his hands has a ribbon on it that has been tied badly and tied again.

"Hello, girls. Aa jao."

"Arre, hello ke bachche." Purvi has her hand on the mirror. "Ladkiyon ke room mein knock karke aaya jaata hai."

"Exactly." Aryaman puts the box down on the bed. "Yeh sab formality main nahi karta. Aur specially tab jab cake mere paise ka aaya ho."

"Mummy, Papa, aa jao." Ananta is at the door with her hands cupped round her mouth. "Ab Purvi ka cake kaatna hai."

"Yeh rahi. Kitchen mein thi." Their mother comes in with the knife and the plates and her dupatta over her shoulder. "Thank you."

"Purvi." Aryaman sits on the floor by the bed. "Tera birthday hai na? Tere birthday ke liye maine tera naya naam rakha hai — Baby Penguin."

"Tune phir se bakwaas kiya na?"

"Aaja, aaja, birthday girl."

"Arre mummy." Gurinder is in the doorway with his arms out. "Toh itna bada ho gaya na, lekin akal abhi bhi ghutnon mein hai iske. Yeh dekho, sab gira diya."

Purvi goes down on her knees with the plates and picks them up and says, to the plates, "Main theek hoon. Sab theek hai."

A slice of the cake goes over on the plate and nobody rights it.

"Ek beti ki shaadi aur ek beti ka birthday." Their mother says it with her hand on the back of Purvi's head, and then again, because she likes the sound of it. "Dono ek din."

"Sahi baat boli aapne." Aryaman is laughing now. "Humare kitne paise bach gaye. Agle saal se dono ki party ek hi din mein nipata denge."

"Ek minute." Ananta holds her phone up. "Cake katne se pehle ek selfie le lein."

They get in together, four heads and a cake, and the flash goes, and nobody in that photograph is looking at the thing that is not in it.

The camera is on the bed between them when the flash goes, and it stays there afterwards.

"Chalo." Gurinder is at the door again. "Abhi ho gaya na sab kuch. Chalo, jaldi so jaate hain."

"Arre suniye." Their mother catches his sleeve. "Aap kahan ja rahe ho itni jaldi mein?"

"Mujhe zara bahar kaam hai."

"Bahar is waqt?"

"Tum sahi keh rahi thi." He has his spectacles in his hand. "Humne kitne logon ko cards nahi diye hain na? To socha ki extra card chhapwa ke baant denge. Main cards print karke le aata hoon."

The door shuts behind him. In the room the four of them sit on the floor round a cut cake, and the fan makes its sound on the second turn.

The knife lies on the cake box with the icing still on it.

The window in that room is shut and the room has gone warm with the four of them in it.

## A Man Who Is Not In It

Two in the morning, and the hall comes down around the last of the family.

Chairs on their sides. The sound table gone to the van. A boy going along the wall with a bin bag and the marigold going into it.

Vihaan is on the lawn with the light-and-sound vendor's final bill in one hand and his phone in the other, walking the line of the tent.

A man comes past with a coil of cable over his shoulder.

"Chhote sahab." He stops. "Woh log aa gaye hain. Gate pe gaadi khadi hai."

"Kaun log?"

"Woh." The man nods at the drive.

At the gate a car stands with its lights on, and four people are getting into it, and one of them has a case on her shoulder and one of them is being helped in by the elbow.

"Yeh sangeet mein aaye the?" Vihaan looks up from the bill.

"Ji. Subah se." The man shifts the coil. "Aapke Tauji ke mehmaan."

"Mehmaan." Vihaan looks at the gate. "Kis kaam ke?"

"Pata nahi, sahab." The man goes. "Hum kaam waale hain. Humse kaun batata hai."

Vihaan stands with the bill in his hand and watches the car go out through the gate and turn left.

"Ek baat." He says it before the man with the coil is out of hearing. "Tauji ke mehmaan teen din se hain. Khaana kahan khate hain?"

"Chhote sahab, woh apna khud ka laate hain." The man stops. "Aur, sahab—"

"Bolo."

"Humare ladke terrace pe nahin jaate raat ko. Do saap hain. Kale. Ek to kal raat se wahin hai, tanki ke neeche." The man shifts the coil to his other shoulder. "Maine Parmeet sahab ko bola tha. Unhone kaha jo hai so hai."

Vihaan looks up the side of the house at the terrace and the water tank against the sky.

"Kisi ko kuch mat bolna." He turns back to the bill. "Aur kisi ko bhi terrace pe mat bhejna. Main dekh lunga."

The man nods and goes.

"Oye." One of the uncles comes out of the hall with a bottle in one hand and two glasses hooked in the other, and he is the cheerful one at every function in that house. "Chhote, aa. Aaj to peeni padegi. Kal tera bhai shaadi kar raha hai."

"Main nahin peeta, chacha."

"Tu nahin peeta?" The uncle puts the glasses down on the wall. "Poora London, poora London, aur tu nahin peeta? Kya karta hai phir tu wahan, doodh?"

"Kaam." Vihaan puts the phone in his pocket. "Aur chacha, aapke do glass hain."

"Ek tera tha."

"Ek mera nahin hai." He picks one glass up and puts it back on the tray of the boy going past with the crates. "Aap apna peeiye. Main andar hoon."

"Arre, ruk." The uncle is laughing now, and it is the laugh of a man who has had four. "Sun. London mein ladkiyan bhi hain? Ya wahan bhi tu kaam hi karta hai? Kyunki teri umar mein—"

"Chacha." Vihaan stops at the step with his back to him. "Aapke bete ne aapko bataya nahin?"

"Kya?"

"Kuch nahin." He goes up the steps. "Subah chaar baje uthna hai. Aapko bhi."

The uncle stands with his bottle and looks after him, and says something to nobody that the lawn takes away.

Inside, the hall is empty and the marigold is down off the chandelier and somebody's dupatta is over the back of a chair by the far wall, and he looks at it for a second and does not go near it, and goes up the stairs past it.

In the morning the small book that the gate keeps has four names in it in Shyam's slow hand, and all four are crossed out, and beside them the time of the going: ten past two.

## The Guests Are Cancelled

Seven in the morning, and the Suri front room is full of people who are not guests.

"Mummy." Tina is in her robe with her hair half done. "Ab bataogi bhi? Sab mehmaanon ko cancel kyun kiya hai?"

"Baby—"

"You know na Tina ki wedding ko leke main kitna excited thi." She has her phone in her hand and it is going. "Infact mujhe to laga tha ki is shaadi mein poora shehar aane wala hai."

"Arre baby, kya karein?" Vihaan comes in from the veranda in the middle of it with his coffee.

"Aur tum, tum kya kar rahe ho? Tumhare bhai ki shaadi hai."

"Main dekh raha hoon, Mummy." He does not raise his voice. "Sab dekh raha hoon."

Dimple has her hand on her own forehead. "Us ladki Ananta ki haalat dekhi thi na aapne sagai mein? Woh ladki ultiyan par ultiyan kiye jaa rahi thi."

"Mummy—"

"Agar logon ko pata chal jaata ki woh pregnant hai isliye shaadi ho rahi hai, to hamari bahut badnami hoti." She looks round the room. "Maine Bollywood ke saare bade producers aur actors ko invite kar diya tha. Ab call karke mana karna padega."

"Please, relax." Roma has her arm round her from the other side. "Come, sit here."

She sits, and she looks up at all of them, and her chin comes up.

The tray of tea that has been brought in stands on the table with every cup on it full, and nobody reaches for one.

The chairs in the front room have been pulled into a half-circle and not put back.

"Main aap logon se pehle hi keh rahi hoon." Her voice carries. "Mere Vikram aur Pinky ki shaadi mein yeh sab nahi chalega."

"Baby." The elder of the house is at the sideboard. "Aap jaisa chaahoge, bilkul waisa hoga."

"Bhabhi, aap chinta mat kijiye." Roma's hands are up. "Hum log bada reception karenge. Usmein jise chahe bula lein."

"Filhaal." Dimple's hand comes down on the arm of the chair. "Filhaal humein guest cancel karne padenge."

On the lawn the workers are taking the chairs down off the stacks and putting them back up again in a different order, because there are three hundred chairs and forty people coming.

Out on the drive a stack of chairs goes over and nobody picks it up.

## The Print Room

Half past twelve at night, and the channel's office has four windows with light in them and the rest of it dark.

The month-end is on the fourth floor, and the month-end is his. Two screens, a printout with a ruler across it, a cup with the tea gone cold at the bottom, and the whole floor dark behind him except the strip that the lift throws up the wall.

He is on the phone with the London end when the lift comes up and Shyam brings a man down the corridor.

"Sahab." The guard's cap is in his hand. "Yeh Ananta ji ke papa hain. Cards print karwane aaye hain."

Gurinder Arora is in the same cream kurta, and he has a folder under his arm, and he is tired the way a man is tired at the end of the day his daughter marries.

"Namaste ji." He puts his hands together. "Main woh Ananta ka father. Kal meri beti ki shaadi hai, Ravish Suri ke saath."

"Main jaanta hoon." Vihaan puts the phone down on the desk and stands up. "Vihaan."

"Haan ji." Gurinder looks at him for a second longer than the introduction needs. "Cards kam pad gaye the, to print karwane the. Parmeet ji ne kaha ki yahin print karwa lo office mein, kyunki ab is waqt kahin koi shop khuli nahi milegi."

"Hamare paas printing machine hai." Vihaan comes round the desk. "Yeh aap mujhe de dijiye."

He takes the folder, and he does not go. He turns the first page up and looks at it.

"Ek minute." He puts it down again. "Aap yahin rukiye. Main kar ke laata hoon."

"Thank you."

The fourth floor is dark and the machine is two floors down, in the room behind the print desk, and the room is locked, and the key is in the drawer of the empty desk outside it, and that takes four minutes.

Gurinder Arora waits where he is left. Then he walks a little way along the corridor, because a man who has been standing all day will walk a little way along any corridor.

The board at the end of it has the sangeet prints pinned across it in rows, still up because nobody has taken them down: the hall, the aunts, the child with the garland, Tina's father asleep in a good chair. And in the third row, one picture of a pair dancing with a lamp behind them, and the girl's face half turned away, and the man's hand flat on her back.

Gurinder takes his spectacles out of his kurta and puts them on and stands in front of that picture for a while.

"Ji." The guard is at the end of the corridor. "Woh chhote sahab neeche gaye hain. Chaar minute."

"Haan." He folds the spectacles. "Yeh ladka kaun hai?"

"Vihaan sahab. Chhote sahab." The guard says it easily. "Parmeet sahab ke chhote bete."

Gurinder looks at the picture again, at the eyes and the set of the shoulders, and he puts the spectacles away and does not say anything else about it.

When he comes back up with the carton, the corridor is empty and the print desk has a page on it that has not been there before, and the page is a list of names.

He stands at the desk and reads it, and his hand is flat on the counter.

"Chhote sahab." The guard is at the end of the corridor. "Woh sahab chale gaye. Bola ki subah aayenge."

"Kitne bajay gaye?"

"Abhi. Do minute pehle."

Vihaan looks down the corridor at the lift.

The indicator above it is not on 4. It is on **B**, and it stays on B for a long moment, and then it goes out.

Then he takes the pen off the print desk, and he opens the visitors' book, and he writes the time in the margin, and under it, in his own hand, one line: *lift B — barah baje ke baad — Ananta ji ke father gaye nahin, doosri gaadi se gaye.*

Vihaan closes the book. He puts the pen back in the holder and turns it so that it lies the way it lay.

## The Basement

The lift stands in the corridor in the dark with its door open and its button panel lit, and the panel has four floors on it and no fifth button under them.

Gurinder puts his spectacles on and looks at it properly.

"Bhai." He has his phone at his ear. "Bhai, basement to jaati hi nahi hai."

He presses what there is to press. The doors stay open. He steps back, and his heel catches on the skirting, and the small hinged box set into the wall beside the lift — the one the painter has painted over twice — swings out on its hinge.

Inside it there is a keyhole and a key.

"Us nagri param ke roop se chaabi mili hai." He takes the key out and turns it over in his fingers. "Lekin yeh chaabi lagti kahan hai?"

He puts it in. The panel goes dark, and then a fifth button comes up under the row of four, lit, with nothing written on it.

"Ismein sirf kaun sa button hai?" He presses it with one finger.

The door closes on him. The floor numbers count down to 1 and stop, and the lift goes on going.

The basement is a store room the size of the channel's whole floor, and it is not a store room. There are crates along the far wall with a generator's name stencilled on them and a generator's weight written on the side in a shipping line's hand. There is a bench with four empty clips on a shelf above it. There is a map of the city on the wall with three of the Kumbh's ghats marked in red chinagraph and a fourth mark that is not a ghat.

And at the end of the bench, in an open crate with the straw pulled back, there are sticks of RDX, packed the way people pack something they mean to use.

"Yaar abba." A voice comes down the lift shaft from the corridor above, thin and far away, and it is not a voice he knows. "Yeh yeh sab yahan—"

Gurinder has his phone out. He is photographing the crates, the map, the bench, the sticks, one after another, with his hand not quite steady.

"Yeh sab main—" He says it to nobody. "Yeh sab saboot mein—"

The lift gives its chime. He gets in and holds the fifth button down with his thumb until the door shuts on the basement, and he does not breathe until the numbers come back up.

## The Thana

Twelve minutes past two, and the car stands at the side of the sector road under the one lamp that is working, with the engine off and the phone face-up on his knee.

"Haan Shalini, bolo."

"Kahan reh gaye aap?" His wife's voice is small and close. "Itni raat ho gayi hai."

"Bas thode se cards bache hue hain." Gurinder looks at the folder on the seat beside him. "Unhein baant ke aa raha hoon."

"Cards." A pause. "Aap ghar aa jaiye. Subah sab kuch hai."

"Main aa raha hoon."

Gurinder puts the phone in his pocket and sits for a moment with both hands on the wheel. Then he gets out and walks to the gate of the thana, which is lit, and goes in.

The road is empty in both directions, and the car's light is the only one on it.

Three in the morning, and the police station at the corner of the sector road has two men in it awake.

The duty officer is behind the desk with his register open and his spectacles on the end of his nose, and the man in the cream kurta is on the stool in front of it with his hands on his knees.

"Sir." Gurinder's voice is steady. "Mujhe complaint likhwani hai."

"Kiske khilaaf?"

"Parmeet Suri." He says the name and then the rest of it. "Uski poori family ke khilaaf."

The officer's pen stops.

The register on the desk is open and the carbon under the page has gone through to the page beneath it.

"Parmeet Suri?" He takes his spectacles off. "Woh business tycoon, jiska khud ka news channel bhi hai? Aapko pata hai na woh kitne izzatdaar log hain? Kahin aapko galatfahmi to nahi hui?"

"Sir, woh to is desh ke bhi nahi hai." Gurinder leans forward. "Woh bahut badhiya hai — chaubees saal pehle humare padosi desh Changistan se aaye hue jaasoos hain woh log. Sir, yahan apna naam aur pehchaan badal ke reh rahe hain. Humare desh ke khilaaf bahut bada plan kar rahe hain, sir."

"Beta—"

"Ek minute." Gurinder opens his folder on the desk. "Yeh dekhiye. Maine news channel ke basement mein yeh sab dekha."

The officer looks at the page. Then he turns it over, and turns it back.

"Aur sir, maine Mahakumbh ki cuttings bhi dekhi." Gurinder's hand is flat on the desk now. "Sir, yakeen maaniye, woh log kuch bahut bada plan kar rahe hain."

The officer sits back in his chair and looks at him for a while.

Behind him the board on the wall has three notices pinned to it and one of them is hanging by a corner.

"Agar yeh sach hai," he says slowly, "to humein raid daalna hoga."

"Sir, uske liye itni raat ko apply nahi kar sakte." Gurinder is up. "Kal subah hi yeh kaam ho paayega. Kal subah sabse pehle warrant nikaalo."

"Kal subah."

"Sir, please." He puts both hands together. "Please, unhein arrest kar lijiye. Kal us ghar mein meri beti ki shaadi hai, sir."

The officer says nothing.

"Lekin desh ke saamne koi rishta bada nahi hota." Gurinder's chin is up and his eyes are wet and his voice does not go. "Main woh shaadi rok doonga, sir."

"Aap is shaadi ko mat rokiye." The officer picks his pen up again. "Kyunki woh log wajah jaanna chahenge, aur alert ho jayenge. Kal sabse pehle humein raid daalne dijiye — unke ghar pe bhi, aur office pe bhi. Phir aap apni beti bhi bacha lenge, aur desh bhi bacha payenge."

"Lekin sir." Gurinder's hands come down on the desk. "Rasmon ke pehle aa jaiye please. Warna bahut bada anand ho jayega."

"Aap nishchint rahiye."

The register shuts. The man in the cream kurta goes out into the dark of the sector road and stands on the pavement for a moment with his hand on the bonnet of his car.

## The Storm

The sky over the city at four in the morning is the colour of a bruise, and the only light in it is the sodium glow off the road, and the wind has changed.

In the hall of stone the lamps are lying over on their sides and the bell rope is standing out from the door.

## Two Weddings, One Day

"Hmm."
