# Chapter 20 — The First Death

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 10.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Cave

The cave wall is dark. A single pen lies on the floor before Mahasaphera. His fingers close around it. He does not look up as he writes. Shadow holds the walls, and the pen is the only light he needs.

The door opens. His assistant comes in sweating, his shirt damp at the collar, his breath quick. He stands at the threshold and does not move farther.

"Maaf kijiye Mahasaphera ji," the assistant says. "Kisi ne mujhe gusal khane mein band kar diya tha."

Mahasaphera snatches his pen back. The nib scratches against the stone. He does not raise his eyes.

"Kahan mar gaya tha tu?" Mahasaphera says.

The assistant wipes his forehead. His hand shakes.

"Maaf kijiye, Gurujii. Pata nahi kisne?"

"Theek hai." Mahasaphera says. "Woh ichchhadari naagin. Anant kul ki rani. Use maine khud apne haathon se mara tha. Isliye wo ho hi nahi sakti."

"Ji Gurujii."

"Jao bahar jaakar. Maha sapera ji se kaho do maine use mara tha. Aur accha hi kiya maine mara tha. Warna wo hum sabko mar degi."

"Ji Gurujii."

The assistant steps back. His feet scrape against the cave floor.

"Aur suno. Tumne use khana khila diya tha na? Bhukha toh nahi rakha?"

"Nahi nahi. Maine use khana khila diya hai."

"Tumhe pata hai na ki wo pahadon ka janwar hai. Usse har koi darta hai. Har insaan kamptaa hai."

"Ji Gurujii."

Mahasaphera lays the pen down. The stone is cold beneath his palm. He picks up the been. The flute rests against his lips, dark and still, waiting.

"Jahan meri ye been shastra banakar kaam nahi aayegi, wahan ye astra banakar hamare kaam aayegi."

The score comes up — one low string, held too long, then nothing. The cave swallows it.

## The Engagement

The lawn at the Suri house at seven in the evening, with the sun still on the top of the boundary wall and the chandeliers not yet lit, and the guests standing about in the last of the light with their glasses and their good shoes in the wet grass. Two boys go along the edge of the lawn with a long pole, touching the paper lanterns alight one by one as the light comes down, and each lantern comes on under the pole and stays on.

Tina is in a sari the colour of peaches and she has Ahana's hand up between two fingers, turning it over the way a woman turns over a thing she has bought and is not yet sure of.

"Waise fashion sense to hai ladki mein." Tina turns the wrist once more. "Jo footwear isne pehni hai na — woh best luxury brand ka latest launch hai. Poora ek lakh ka joota, aur isne ghaas mein utaar diya."

"Ghaas mein aaya hai to ghaas mein hi jaayega." **Karan** says it at his own wife's elbow and laughs, and the laugh does not find anybody to land on. "Bhai ne jackpot maar liya. Vikram! Ahana Dhawan — ye kis khandaan ki bahu thi? Baap ka naam koi jaanta hai?"

**"Karan, tu apni shaadi ke chhe mahine baad muh utha ke bol raha hai." Nisha says it over her glass without turning her head, and Tina laughs into her sari, and Karan does not. "Humare ghar mein doosri shaadi ka hisaab hai. Tumhare ghar mein?"**

Nobody answers that, because in that house the money is the only thing anybody is certain of, and the money has been walking about the lawn all evening on a shoe.

Parmeet stands at the edge of the light with his glass held at the level of his chest where everybody can see it, and he is not drinking from it.

Vikram takes the ring out of the box. His hand is not steady, and the box lid comes down and back up twice before the ring is in the open air.

"Haath mat kapaao." Vihaan says it from the side of the group, without turning his head, in the voice a man uses on a horse. Vikram's hand steadies and he laughs, and the laugh is real this time, and he gets the ring clear.

He is moving it toward Ahana's finger when the noise at the gate goes wrong. A car door, and then a second car door, and then heels on the gravel at a pace that nobody wears to an engagement.

Bharani comes up the lawn. She is in white and carries a folder flat against her forearm like a waiter carries a tray, and she walks straight through the guests, who part for her because she does not slow down.

"Suzy." Ahana says it before Bharani is within speaking distance, and the name is a warning and a welcome at the same time. "Tum yahan?"

"She is my personal assistant." Ahana says it to the lawn, and to Tina's dropped hand, and to Karan, who has gone quiet. "Suzy, kya hai?"

"Mr Dhawan ne letter bheja hai, madam." Bharani holds the folder out with both hands. "Aapke liye. Aur unhone kaha hai ki sabke saamne padha jaaye."

The lawn goes quiet in a way that a lawn does not go quiet by accident. Something in the middle of the crowd has stopped, and the stopping runs outward from it.

Vihaan is at Ahana's shoulder before the envelope is open, and nobody has sent for him.

"Madam." He says it low, and puts his hand out flat, palm up, and waits. "Letter mere haath se jaayega. Aap padhiye, par pehle mujhe dikha dijiye ki seal toota hua hai ya nahi."

Ahana looks at his hand for a moment. Then she puts the envelope into it, in front of the whole lawn, and the lawn watches a man it does not know very well take a woman's letter off her and turn it over.

He turns it once. The wax is whole, and the crease is the crease of a thing that has been folded by one pair of hands and not two, and the seal has the impression of a signet ring and not of a thumb.

"Naya hai." He gives it back to her. "Kisi ne ise khola nahi hai. Padhiye."

Only then does he step back half a pace, to where he can see the lawn and not be in it — and the lawn, at that moment, tells him everything. Parmeet's hand has gone to the inside of his jacket before the seal is broken. Karan's mouth has closed on the joke he has not been allowed to make, and Karan's mouth is never closed. Baljeet, by the drinks table with two men, has put his glass down and is already looking at the gate.

**The letter is new. The house is not.** **The lawn has been told what is in the letter before the letter has been opened.**

Ahana takes the envelope. She does not look at Bharani, and she does not step back out of the light. She opens it there, in the last of the sun, with all of them watching, and she reads it out in her own voice, in the voice of a woman reading off a shopping list.

"'Paise ke bhookhe hain.'" She holds the paper at the height of her chest so that she does not have to look up. "'Mujhe sunkar hairani hai ki tumhe kisi sadak-chhaap actor ke bete se pyaar ho gaya hai. Sadak-chhaap — jo mere joote din raat chamkaate hain. Aur tumne aise logon mein uthne baithne waale ladke ko chun liya.'"

Somebody on the lawn makes a sound and turns it into a cough. Vikram's face has gone the colour of the lanterns. Parmeet has still not drunk from his glass.

"'Badi sharam ki baat hai.'" Ahana turns the page over, finds nothing on the back of it, and folds it along its own crease. "Bas. Papa ka poora message yahi hai."

Vihaan looks at her for the length of one line of that letter, and not longer, and looks away before she has finished folding it. He has seen a great many people read bad news off a page. He has never seen one read it without their face doing anything, and he does not have a word for what that is, and the evening does not give him time to want one.

Tina's hands have gone back to her own sari. Karan is examining the lawn, and Nisha is not, and Nisha is watching Ahana.

"Ruk jao." Parmeet puts his glass down on the tray of a boy who has to turn to catch it. "Ab main bolunga."

He crosses the grass and puts both hands up, palms out, at the level of his own chest, as though the whole lawn has started arguing with him and he is the one calming it.

"Bade sahab ko poora haq hai." Parmeet says it warmly and says it loudly. "Beti ka baap hai. Koi baap apni beti ko sasta nahi bechta, aur koi baap apni beti ke haath kisi ke haath mein dene se khush nahi hota. Hum naraz nahi hote. Hum samajhte hain."

He turns so that he is talking to the whole lawn and to the letter and to the father who is not on it.

"Aur mera jawab yahi hai — ye shaadi hone se koi rok nahi sakta." He puts an arm out toward them both. "Aur main kahunga muhurat nikal raha hai. Aaj hi ring badal lo."

The applause starts where the drinks table is and comes across the lawn in a wave, and it is loud and it is relieved, and under it **Dimple** says something to the woman beside her that makes them both laugh, and it is about the shoe.

Vikram puts the ring on Ahana's finger. His hand does not shake now, and he is smiling, and he says something to her that the applause eats.

"Ye asli heera hai." Tina is back at the hand before the applause has finished, holding the ring up to the lantern light. "Ye asli hai na?"

"Of course asli hai." Ahana looks at the ring, and not at Tina. "Nakli cheezon ki jagah meri life mein nahi hai."

Bharani steps back with the folder under her arm and her eyes down. Nobody on that lawn has asked her a single question, and she has been in the house four minutes.

Aryaman claps twice, and stops, and stands with his hands at his sides in the middle of a lawn where everybody else is clapping. Across the grass, at the corner of the veranda, Pinky stands with her glass held in both hands, and her face has nothing on it at all.

Then the lanterns come on properly, all the way down the lawn at once, and the light changes and the evening becomes the party it has been waiting all day to be.

Vihaan does not clap. He takes Vikram by the elbow and walks him three steps out of the applause, and he does not let go of the elbow while he speaks.

"Attari ki file kal subah kholi jaayegi." He says it with his face turned away from the lawn. "Usme jo hai, usme mera naam aur tumhara naam nahi hona chahiye. Tumhari shaadi ho rahi hai. Isliye ek baat tay kar lo — us file ko haath mat lagaana."

"Tumhe kya lagta hai, main—" Vikram starts, and the elbow comes under his hand and the sentence stops.

"Main kuch nahi soch raha. Main tumhe bata raha hoon." Vihaan lets go of the elbow. "Aur ek baat. Tumhari hone waali biwi ne aaj raat is lawn pe ek letter apne haath se khola hai jismein uske baap ne tumhe sadak-chhaap likha hai, aur uske chehre pe kuch nahi aaya. Main yeh kisi ko nahi bataunga. Par tum yeh soch lena ki tum kis ghar mein shaadi kar rahe ho."

Vikram stands there with his glass. Over his shoulder, at the corner of the veranda, Bharani is walking backwards off the lawn into the dark at the side of the house, and her eyes are on the two men by the drinks table and not on the door she is leaving by.

Vihaan watches her go, and does not follow her, and goes back into the light.

## The Attari File

By ten the lawn has been abandoned for the inside of the house, and the house is full: the chandelier on, the furniture pushed back, the veranda doors open so that the party can spill. The four dark rectangles of the gate screens stand in their case by the door to the security room, and nobody has looked at them all evening.

Vihaan catches Aryaman between the drinks table and the door.

"Tumhare paas file hai," Vihaan says.

"Kaunsi file?"

"Attari ki." Vihaan does not move out of the doorway. "Woh mine jiski photo upload nahi ho rahi. Do din se tum us file ko apne table ki drawer mein rakh rahe ho aur mujhe bata nahi rahe."

Aryaman looks at him for a moment longer than the sentence needs. "Kahan se pata?"

"Kyunki tumne mujhe teen mahine pehle woh drawer dikhaya tha jab usme kuch nahi tha." Vihaan holds his eyes. "Ab usme kuch hai. Photo upload nahi hoti, aur tum phone band kar dete ho jab Roma ka naam aata hai. Main aankhon se dekhta hoon, Aryaman. Yeh mera kaam hai."

"Report aaj raat likhni hai." Aryaman takes his own phone out. "Kal subah."

"Attari ki file pe Vikram ka naam nahi jaana chahiye." Vihaan says it quietly and does not make it a threat. "Aur na Parmeet ke haath se. Woh file kis ke paas se gayi, wohi baad mein batayega ki gaadi kahan khadi thi. Chhoti si baat hai. Bas ek naam."

Aryaman studies him. "Tumhe kis baat ki fikar hai?"

"Us cheez ki jo file mein hai." Vihaan is already turning. "Kal subah chhah baje. Main tumhare table pe hoon."

He is two steps away when Parmeet crosses the floor with a glass in each hand and puts one into Vihaan's without asking whether he wants it.

"Beta." Parmeet's voice is warm and it does not travel past the two of them. "Attari waali file tumhare paas hai? Chhoti si baat hai. Bas ek naam."

Vihaan looks down at the glass in his hand, and then at Parmeet.

"File mere paas nahi hai," he says. "File office mein hai, aur office kal khulega."

"Office kabhi band nahi hota, beta." Parmeet smiles at him. "Jaanta hoon main. Naam badal jaate hain, kagaz wahi rehte hain."

"Papa." Vihaan puts the glass down on the nearest tray, and it is the same tray the boy has been carrying all evening. "Aaj raat main kisi ka naam nahi badalunga. Engagement hai. Ghar mein khushi hai."

He goes. Parmeet watches him go, and the smile stays on his face a beat after it has stopped meaning anything.

At the buffet, Sameer is working through a plate with both elbows out. Vihaan stops behind his chair.

"Sameer." Vihaan's hand comes down once on the back of the chair and no further. "Chal."

"Main yahan hoon."

"Tum wahan nahi ho." Vihaan says it flatly. "Tum Swarna ke aage-khoob drama kar rahe ho, aur tumhare haath kaanp rahe hain, aur tumhari plate mein chhe cheezein hain jinmein se ek bhi tumne banaya nahi. Chal. Baahar hawa khaale."

**"Plate rakh."** Vihaan says it at the level of the table, not the room, and Sameer puts the plate down and stands up, and the two of them go out through the veranda doors together, and neither of them says anything else.

At the door, as they pass it, the lamp gutters and comes back, and Vihaan stops for half a second, and looks at it, and goes on.

Across the room, Ahana has watched the whole thing: the chair, the plate, the two men going out through the veranda doors. She has not moved, and she has not stopped smiling, and the smile is not for Vihaan.

## The Dance Floor

The chandelier throws its light over the crowded floor. Ahana comes against Aryaman, and his hands close around her arms before she can fall. She looks up at him.

"Nice physique. Tum aur main saath mein zyada achhe lagte hain."

"Tumhari sagai ho gayi hai," Aryaman says, "aur tum mere saath flirt kar rahi ho?"

"Ise healthy flirting kehte hain. Sehat ke liye bahut achhi hoti hai. Tum bhi kiya karo." She glances at her ring. "Aur waise bhi meri shaadi hone waali hai. Marne waali nahi hoon main."

She leaves him and the music rises. Vikram finds Aryaman at the edge of the floor.

"What's wrong with you, Aryaman?" Vikram asks.

"Dekh, main jaanta hoon tu gusse mein hai. Teen chaar baar mera munh tod chuka hai, maine kuch nahi kaha. Lekin ab bas. Vikram, just listen to me, okay? Abhi tujhse Pinky ka bhai nahi, tera dost baat kar raha hai."

The smile goes off Vikram's face.

"Tu jaanta kya hai is ladki ke baare mein?" Aryaman asks. "Main nahi chahta jo mere saath hua woh tere saath bhi ho. Main bhi kuch nahi jaanta tha us ladki ke baare mein."

"Chhote bhai, woh ladki alag thi. Ye alag hai."

"Ye ladki galat hai. Tu jaanta hai, woh mere saath flirt kar rahi thi? Usne kaha ki hum dono saath mein zyada achhe lagenge. Ke uski shaadi hui hai, woh mar nahi rahi. Woh mujhse flirt kar sakti hai."

"Really?" Vikram takes out his phone. "Usne mujhe sab sach bata diya. Tune do-teen baar usse kaha ki tum dono saath mein achhe lagte ho. Woh irritate ho gayi, isliye usne tang aake tujhse woh sab kaha. Use tujhse chhutkara chahiye tha aur tu uska haath nahi chhod raha tha."

"That's just not true, man. Main uska haath kyun pakadunga?"

"Ruk. Ye dekh."

The photograph on the screen shows Aryaman holding Ahana's arm. Vikram keeps the phone where he can see it.

"Usne chupke se ye photo click karwaayi. Use pata tha tu mera bachpan ka dost hai. Shayad main uski baat pe yakeen na karoon. Dekh, saaf saaf haath pakda hua hai tune uska."

Aryaman looks from the screen to the floor where it happened.

"Lekin usne ye photo kab li? Mujhe pata kaise nahi chala?"

"Tujhe kaise pata chalega? Tu to uski aankhon mein khoya hua tha." Vikram puts the phone away. "Aryaman, I know you don't like her. Waise bhi auraton pe zyada trust nahi hai tera. Par ye ladki alag hai. She is lovely, and she is mine. Isliye meri bandi se door rahiyo. Samjha tu?"

He walks back to Ahana. Aryaman looks after him.

"Mera dhyan kahan tha?" he says under his breath. "Is baat ko ghuma ke apne favour mein kar liya isne."

Ahana holds out her hand when Vikram reaches her. He takes it.

"Baby, are you okay?" he asks.

"I don't know, baby. Pata hai main kitni ghabra gayi thi? Tum to kehte the woh tumhara bahut achha friend hai. Phir bhi usne mere saath aise."

"I know. Woh usually aise behave nahi karta. Pata nahi tumhare saath aisa kyun. Shayad use chadh gayi hogi. Par jo kuch bhi hua, I'm sorry."

"Tum kyun sorry bol rahe ho?" She tightens her hand around his. "Par kuch bhi karke us insaan ko mujhse door rakhna."

"Ab woh tumhare saamne nahi aayega."

At the drinks table, Sameer is occupied with Swarna and a pastry. The conversation near them turns when the ring is mentioned: the precious stone they have been trying to reach is not the one on Ahana's finger.

"Naagmani ke baare mein socho," one of the men says, keeping his voice low. "Woh bas hum chaaron ki hi honi chahiye."

"Arre tu fikar mat kar, Sameer. Woh Naagmani hum chaaron ki hi hogi."

There is laughter, and then the admission that the crocodile still comes in dreams. Once the Naagmani is in sight, someone says, they can take it and leave. The glasses rise again as the music starts.

By eleven the party has moved out onto the terrace and the hall has the look of a room that has been danced in: the floor grey with the marks of shoes, a strip of gold foil off a glass in the corner, and the hired lights going round and round over it and finding nobody.

On the landing above, Swarna has her phone up in both hands, and her thumb on the screen, and she reads what she has written twice before she sends it. Down on the terrace, past the pillar, Ravish has his phone out of his jacket and he reads it with his glass halfway to his mouth, and he smiles at it before he has finished the sip.

"You scared me, Swarna. What's wrong with you?" Ravish asks.

"Kya keh rahe the tum us ladki ke baare mein?" She comes down as far as the fourth step and stops with one hand on the banister. "Is she hotter than me?"

"Jealous?" He grins. "Let me tell you one thing. Shayad tumhe kisi ne bataya nahi, par tum jab gusse mein hoti ho na, to aur bhi zyada khoobsurat lagti ho. Hot."

Swarna looks at him without smiling. Below them the hired lights move over the floor.

Because the woman in Swarna's sari is already dancing.

She is out on the terrace with the men, and she is dancing well, and it is the sari that everyone sees first: the tasseled end of a black dupatta in the lantern light, a wrist going up, the pale green of it, and the men close round her and the men are laughing. Her laugh comes back off the house and it is a laugh that carries.

The woman on the stairs hears it. She stands where she is on the fourth step with her hand on the banister, and the colour goes out of her face, and she is looking at her own green sari out there on the terrace with somebody else's shoulders under it.

Sameer sees it too, and he sees it before he has worked out what he is seeing.

He is at the drinks table with **Arjun** and a paper napkin in his fist, and his eyes go out past the pillar and stay there, and then the napkin comes down onto the table and his glass goes over on its side, and Arjun rights it, and Sameer does not notice that either.

"Tina." He says his daughter's name as if he is looking for a witness. "Ye dekh. Ye kya ho raha hai?"

Tina, who is nineteen and whose father is the only man on that terrace she is not allowed to be angry with, says nothing at all, and that is the worst thing she could have done to him, and he goes.

He goes across the terrace at a pace that makes people move out of his way without knowing why, and the dancing woman is saying something over her shoulder to the man beside her, and the laugh is still going, and he takes her by the wrist.

"Swarna." His voice comes out of him at a size that stops the music. "Music band karo!"

The hired lights keep turning and there is a scrape and the song dies in the middle of a line.

"Behayawani." He holds her wrist up at the height of both their chests so that the terrace can see the arm. "Saare ke saamne, is ghar mein, meri izzat — tumhe koi farq nahi padta. Maine tumhe kabhi nahi roka. Aaj rok raha hoon."

The woman in his hand is not fighting him. She stands there in the middle of that terrace with her wrist up and her face turned half away, and the men from the dance have gone back three steps and are finding something to look at.

**"Aur main tumse poochhna chahta hoon—"** Sameer's voice cracks and comes back bigger. "—ki tumhe kaun saandh raha tha? Kaun saandh raha tha, Swarna? Aur kya main tumhare liye itna hi hoon?"

His hand comes up. It comes up the way a man's hand comes up when a thing has been building in him since before he entered the terrace, and everybody on that terrace sees it come up.

**Swarna's car has been called.**

It is the only sensible thing anybody does all night, and he does it at the drinks table before he moves: two sentences into his phone in a voice smaller than the music has just been — *"Shyam. Swarna madam ki gaadi. Abhi. Peeche ka darwaza, aur chabbi driver ke haath mein"* — and then across the terrace at a pace that is not fast.

"Sameer." Vihaan comes in from the side, and he does not put his body between them, and he does not touch him. He stands at his elbow at an angle that makes the two of them a conversation instead of a scene. "Tumhara glass gira hua hai. Aur tumhare haath mein apni biwi ka haath hai, aur poori duniya dekh rahi hai."

The hand stays up for one more second, and then it comes down, and it comes down onto her wrist again, and lets go of it.

"Woh—" Sameer's mouth works. "Tumne dekha nahi, woh kis tarah—"

"Main sab dekhta hoon." Vihaan says it quietly. "Aur main jo dikha hoon aaj raat, woh main kal bhool jaunga. Tum bhi bhool jaana."

Parmeet arrives then, and he arrives the way he does, straight into the middle of it with both hands up.

"Ruk jaiye." Parmeet's palm comes flat against Sameer's chest and stays there. "Sameer sahab, ghar mein mehmaan hain. **Ahana dekh rahi hai.**"

Sameer turns his head and looks, and Ahana is standing at the top of the three steps into the hall, in a green sari, with her hands folded in front of her, and her face is exactly as sorry as it should be.

"Aur Swarna ko — abhi." Parmeet does not raise his voice. "Gaadi aage laao. Do teen din ke liye. Aur Sameer sahab ko andar le chalo. Music band rahega. Party khatam."

Two men take Sameer away and he goes with them, walking on his own feet and talking the whole way, and the terrace empties out, and the hired lights go round and round over the hire chairs and find nobody, and a boy starts stacking them with a sound like somebody dropping a set of plates one at a time.

Vihaan does not go in. He goes to the bottom of the terrace steps, where Swarna is standing with her sari caught on one heel and her eyes down, and he stands there with his hands in his pockets and says nothing at all for a while, which is the only thing anybody did all evening that had no purpose in it.

"Gaadi aati hogi." He looks at the drive, not at her. "Woh do teen din ka koi matlab nahi hai. Parmeet sahab mehmaan hote hain to narm ho jaate hain. Kal nahi aayege, parso aa jayenge. Is ghar mein theeking fast nahi hoti."

"Izzat ki baat nahi thi." Swarna's voice is very small and very even. "Woh jo hai woh main nahi thi."

"Pata hai."

She looks up at him.

"Yahan kis ne kya kiya, woh mujhe pata hai." Vihaan says it to the drive, and does not smile, and does not make it a kindness. "Aur kis ne kya dekha, woh mujhe pata hai. Main kisi ko bataunga nahi. Aur kal subah aapka car aapke darwaze pe hoga, aur aap woh car chadhegi, aur aap yeh raat kis ke ghar mein guzri hai woh kisi ko batane ki zaroorat nahi."

The car comes. The driver holds the door. She gets in without saying anything to him, and looks back once through the back glass at the house, at the lit terrace and the hired lights and the man standing at the bottom of the steps with his hands in his pockets.

Parmeet finds him on the way in.

"Beta." His father is smiling and the smile is not for him. "Tum bahut de rahe ho aaj raat. Car, haath, aadmi ka peecha—" He puts his hand on Vihaan's shoulder, and it stays there a moment longer than a hand needs to stay. "Ye ghar tumhare naam par chal raha hai. Par ye ghar kabhi kisi ke saath khada nahi hota jab mehmaan andar hote hain. Samajh rahe ho? Kal se tum sirf apni gaadi dekho. Baaki ye log khud dekhenge."

"Ji." Vihaan says it, and his father's hand comes off his shoulder, and his father goes in laughing at something the man on the door says.

The hall is not empty. Up at the top of the stairs the woman in the green sari starts coming down — the same green sari Sameer has just had by the wrist out on the terrace, and a different woman inside it. Vihaan stands at the bottom of the stairs and looks up. She looks at him and keeps coming, and the shoe that comes onto the step into the hall has a buckle on it that no sari at this wedding brought with it.

At the top of the house, above all of them, a light is on in a room at the end of the fourth-floor corridor, and it will still be on at three, and the house will not ask why.



He is still standing there when the boy comes past with the chairs.

"Sir, andar?"

"Andar." Vihaan does not move. "Teen minute."
## Sameer's Door

The corridor on the first floor is dark at the far end and lit at the near end by the bulb above the service stairs. Of the two cameras over Sameer's door one hangs dead, its little red eye out. The other stands beside it with its eye lit and its glass polished, and it points at that door, and it has pointed at that door all evening, and nobody has looked at it once.

Vihaan stands under the near bulb with his hands out of his pockets, looking at them.

He has spent six months learning this house. He knows which of its walls are old and which have gone up in the year of the channel money, which doors sit two inches out of true because the frame came cheap in 1998, and which man on which gate takes the morning bribe. He knows that the west stair's lights have a fault that shows as a dead screen in a room twelve metres away. What he does not know, standing under the bulb with his hands out of his pockets, is what a camera does when it dies: whether the eye goes black or the eye stays red and the tape goes blank, because a screen can lie about a corridor in two different ways and only one of them leaves a record.

"Chhote sahab."

The boy from the chairs has come up the service stairs with a folding table on his hip and stopped at the top of them.

"Woh ek band hai," the boy says, and nods at the dead one, and does not come any further into the corridor. "Subah se. Bijli ka kaam chal raha tha, to bijli wale ne bola tha ki —" He does not finish it, and shifts the table on his hip. "Par doosra chalta rehta hai. Woh chalta hi rehta hai. Kisi ne kabhi poochha nahi."

Vihaan looks at the lit one for a while.

"Yeh doosra kahan likhta hai?"

The boy blinks at him. "Sahab?"

"Iska jo dekhta hai. Woh kahan jaata hai."

"Pata nahi, sahab." The boy shifts the table again, and then, because he is fifteen and being asked something by a man who does not usually ask, he tries: "Shayad Shyam ji ke kamre mein? Woh sab cheezein rakhte hain."

"Shyam ji ke kamre mein." Vihaan says it back slowly, and it is the first thing all night that he has had to be told.

Six months in this house. The gate, the books, the four cars, the man on the west stair who takes the morning bribe — and he has never once asked where the cameras write. **The one thing in this house that tells the truth runs all evening and points at a door, and he has to learn it from a boy with a folding table.**

"Tumhara naam?"

"Sohan."

"Sohan." Vihaan says the name once and lets it stand. Vihaan takes his phone out. "Tum abhi neeche jaao, aur ye batana kisiko nahi hai. Aur yeh —" he turns the phone's face to the boy, "— kis ko dena hai, pata hai?"

The boy reads the name on the screen and his face does the thing a face does when it is being given something to hold.

"Ji," he says. "Woh Shyam ji ka aadmi hai."

"Woh Shyam ji ka aadmi hai." Vihaan puts the phone away. "Table jaati hai neeche. Aur sun."

"Ji."

"This corridor is not yours tonight." Vihaan says it without heat. "Tum chaar minute mein neeche nahi aaye, to Main aage bhaagta hoon, aur is ghar mein main kisi ko nahi chhodta."

The boy goes down the service stairs with the table on his hip, and the sound of him goes away, and the corridor is the corridor again.

Vihaan does not knock. He takes the key down off the hook by the door — the tag on it blank, the brass worn smooth at the bow — and he weighs it in his hand and puts it back on the hook.

Then he turns the handle.

It is locked, and it takes him a moment to find out that it is locked, and that moment is the only mistake he makes all night.

"Sameer." He says it at the level of the handle, into the wood. "Main hoon. Kholo."

Nothing.

"Sameer." He knocks twice now, flat of the hand, not a fist. "Tumne teen minute pehle mujhe dekha tha. Main wahi hoon. Darwaza kholo."

Inside the room there is a sound: a glass going down on something hard, once, not found at the first attempt.

"Sir, aap —" It is Sameer's voice, and it comes out of him in pieces, and there is a wetness in it that the wood takes away. "Aap jaaiye. Please. Main aapke saamne aana nahi chahta."

"Kyun?"

"Meri haalat." A laugh, short and bad. "Aapne mujhe neeche dekha tha. Main nahi chahta ki aap mujhe —"

"Main tumhe neeche dekh chuka hoon." Vihaan says it into the wood and does not step back from it. "Us se bura kya hai?"

And then the handle moves under his hand.

It moves the wrong way — out, not in — and it moves when nobody has turned it, and Vihaan's hand comes off it and his arm comes round to show him how many steps there are to the stairs, and there are six, and he counts them, and the counting takes no time at all.

The corridor light at the far end goes out.

"Kaun hai wahan?" Vihaan says it to the far end, and his voice does not go up. "Kaun hai?"

A woman laughs in the room, and the laugh is not Swarna's, and it is not the laugh of anybody who is on the other side of a locked door.

Vihaan puts his shoulder to the door once. It is a good door, the kind this house buys, and it moves a quarter of an inch and comes back at him. He has one hand on the frame to hit it again and then he does not, because inside the room something has begun to speak in a voice he knows and does not know, and the words are not words he can hear through oak.

He has a phone in his pocket and one number in it that will answer at one in the morning.

He does not take it out.

A boy is on a service stair with a table under his arm and a name in his head, and a car is coming round the back of the house with a driver in it and a key in the driver's hand, and Vihaan does none of those things. He stands in the corridor with his ear to a door in his own house and lets a woman be killed behind it, and knows that he is doing it, and does it, and that is the thing the house will never be told and he will never say.

Swarna's voice goes up inside the room — not a word, just the pitch of a throat.

"Swarna." Vihaan says it to the wood, at the level of the handle, in a voice that does not go through doors. "Andar aa raha hoon. Sunai de raha hai?"

The pitch stops.

The corridor is very quiet, and the bulb above the service stairs is humming the way old bulbs hum in an old house, and down the well of the stairs somebody is still playing a song. Vihaan stands with his hand flat on the door and his forehead almost against it and does not turn the key, and there is no reason he can give for it afterwards. Not fear. Nothing in this house frightens him, and nothing has frightened him here since he turned eleven. Something else — the flat, cold, certain thing he has been feeling since two in the afternoon, that says: she is in there and you are here and this is the line, and if you cross it the six months are over and the file is not and your father is not.

The line is a door.

And then the door opens.

## The Murder

It opens inward and there is nobody behind it, and the room beyond is dim, one bulb above the bed, the quilt pulled half off and lying where a man has got out of it and not made it.

Vihaan looks at the room for one second, and in that second he sees all of it, and it is the reason his hands do not shake afterwards and the reason he does not sleep. The bulb above the bed has a strand of hair across it, lifting and settling in the draught.

The room is empty. It is not tidy, and it is in no state that a woman is killed in. A glass stands upright on the side table with a thumb's width in the bottom of it. The wardrobe hangs open six inches and the green sari on the door of it is still on its hanger. Sameer's shoes are under the bed in the position a man steps out of them, and one of them is on its side.

"Swarna?" He takes a step in, and the floorboard under his foot speaks, and nothing answers it. "Sameer?"

The window at the end stands open on the dark of the service yard, and the curtain lifts out from it, and comes back, though there is no wind.

Then the sound starts, and it is not the sound of fighting.

**"मत मारो मुझे।"** A woman's voice, out in the corridor or below it or in the wall, and there is no breath in it. **"मैंने मारा ही कहा है तुम्हें।"**

And then, from the same direction, a voice that is not frightened at all:

**"आज मौत तेरे पीछे नहीं पड़ी है। खुद तेरे सामने खड़ी है।"**

Vihaan stands with his hand flat on the door frame and does not move.

**"तुम हो?"** Swarna's voice, going up. **"नागिन हो?"**

**"हाँ। मैं एक इच्छाधारी नागिन हूँ।"** And then, without heat, like a woman reading a bill: **"पर तू मुझसे बड़ी और ज़हरीली नागिन है। मैं तो अपने दुश्मनों को डसती हूँ — लेकिन तूने तो अपने ही देश वालों को।"**

Vihaan closes his eyes.

*Ahana.* It is not a guess and he does not like how little it costs to make. The girl at the gate with her chin up, the sari that is not hers, the tiled roof of her voice when she is frightened and the flatness under it when she is not. **He has been in a room with that voice for six weeks.**

**"मेरा परिवार तो तुम्हारे लिए सिर्फ़ एक शुरुआत थी।"** The voice goes on, and there is something in it now that is not the woman threatening a servant. **"तुम्हारा असली निशाना तो ये पूरा शहर, पूरा भारत था। यहाँ लाशें बिछाना चाहते थे तुम, है ना? पर शायद तुम ये भूल गए कि भारत सिर्फ़ ज़मीन नहीं — एक नाग भूमि है।"**

And then he hears Swarna say the one sentence a dying woman gets.

**"तुमने तो मुझे मार डाला।"**

**"नहीं, मैंने नहीं मारा।"** A pause in which a house and a man standing in a corridor both hold still. **"माना कि तुमने स्वर्णा को नहीं मारा — मगर मुझे तो मारा था ना?"**

Vihaan's hand comes off the frame.

He knows that sentence. He has watched it be true for six weeks — the girl with the calendar round her neck, the sister who is not her sister, the family that took her in, the wedding that is not a wedding. **Somebody in this house killed her, and she has come back wearing their skin, and nobody in Delhi has any idea.**

"Kaun ho tum?" His own voice, suddenly, in a corridor he cannot get out of. "Andar — kaun ho tum?"

**"भाग।"** Not to him. **"जितना भागना है।"**

A door slams somewhere. A body goes down a service stair and out, and out is good, out is the garden — and then all at once the pitch of the whole night changes, and there is nothing in the house any more but a woman screaming the way a woman screams when she is not going to be saved.

**"कोई मुझे बचाओ!"** It comes up through the floor and through the window and through the open corridor, thin and total. **"प्लीज़ कोई है? कोई मुझे बचाओ!"**

Vihaan gets three steps to the stair. He is a man in his own house and he is already moving and his hand is on the banister — and then he stops, and he stands on the landing with his hand on the banister for the length of four of his own breaths, and lets it happen, and hears himself do it.

**"भाग जितना भागना है। आज मौत तेरे पीछे नहीं पड़ी है। खुद तेरे सामने खड़ी है।"**

And then, after it, a sound he does not expect: **"सोना, मेरा पहला शिकार ख़ामोश हो गया।"** Something in the voice is satisfied, and something under it is torn. **"अब ये कैमरा बोलेगा, और मिनिस्टर फँसेगा।"**

The camera.

Vihaan turns his head and looks — past the open door, down the corridor, at the small lit glass standing over Sameer's door that he walks under forty minutes ago and never once looks at.

**The one thing in this house that has been telling the truth all evening, and it has been telling it about this corridor, and he never asked it a single question.**

Then the corridor is quiet, and below the house is not.

**"सोना।"** A man's voice now, coming up the stairs, cracked open. **"सोना, कहाँ हो?"**

Vihaan goes down.

## The Arrest

The landing at one in the morning, with the tiled floor still wet from the last of the evening's mopping and one bulb doing the work of three.

The guard is in the doorway with a torch in his hand and he has not got the torch on, and past him there are three more men on the stairs and one of them is on his phone.

**"Woh bhaag rahi thi,"** one of them is saying into the phone, and he is saying it in the flat voice a man uses when he has decided the story and is only filling in the words. **"Unhone hi kiya. Main ne dekha — dono ladd rahe the, aur unhone —"**

"Ruko."

The men on the stairs stop.

**"Daarwaza band karo,"** Vihaan says, and he comes down the corridor past the guard, and the guard takes a step back without deciding to. **"Aur koi andar nahi jaata, koi bahar nahi jaata, aur kisi ko nahi bolte —"** He holds the guard's eye. **"— ki tum ne kya dekha. Samajh aaya?"**

"Ji — ji chhote sahab."

He takes the man with the phone by the wrist and lowers it without changing his face and says, very quietly, "Tumhari baat abhi police se hogi. Abhi se pata nahi, abhi se pata hai." Then he picks out the two whose faces he knows off the gate, because he has watched them take a bribe and knows which way they lean. **"Tum do — neeche jaao. Darwaza wahi rahega jahan main bolunga. Koi bahar nahi."**

"Ji."

He goes down the stairs at a pace that is not running and is not walking either.

The house's whole evening is standing at the bottom of them on the lawn end of the hall, and it has gone white. The television is showing a stain on a floor that this house owns. Ahana stands where she has stopped in the middle of it, straightening her sari, and her face is the face of a girl who has been frightened by a sound — and it is good, it is very good, and Vihaan looks at it for a moment and then at her hands.

He takes the measure of the room in one pass and it is wrong, all of it. Sameer is at the bottom of the terrace steps with the knife still in his hand and his shirt out of his waistband. The guards stand round him not holding him and not touching him. Swarna's car stands at the back door with its interior light on and nobody in it, the hired lights still go round and round the empty terrace floor, and one lantern lies at the bottom of the steps on its side with its own flame halfway up its glass.

**"Hathiyar neeche."** Vihaan does not come near him and does not make it a kindness. **"Abhi. Sameer — neeche."**

The knife goes down on the step. It rings.

"Uska pichha karo. Andar. Kuch nahi chhiye — haath nahi, kanpati nahi, baat nahi." He turns to the man off the gate and his voice does not go up at all. **"Aur jo Swarna ji ki gaadi ka driver hai — woh yahan se nahi jaata. Koi bhi ghar se nahi jaata. Koi bhi."**

By the time the police come, the drive is lit, the gate is shut, no phone is in anybody's hand, and every man off those stairs has been told that he saw nothing, twice, by a man who does not raise his voice.

Everything is in order and Swarna is dead on the first floor.

One of her shoes is at the bottom of the steps, on its side, the heel snapped off it.

Vihaan stands at the bottom of the terrace steps in his wedding guest's clothes with his hands at his sides and lets the world look at him, and he is the calmest person in that house and it is the coldest he has ever been in it.

## Ahana's Door

The first-floor gallery at ten past two, with the bulbs down the wall each in their own shade of yellow, and a tray on the carpet outside Ahana's door with two glasses on it and one of them still wet inside.

Vikram comes up the last stair at a run. Aryaman comes behind him at a walk that is worse.

**"आना! आना, ओपन द डोर।"** Vikram has both hands on the panel. **"आना! दरवाज़ा नहीं खुल रहा। कहीं उसे कुछ हो तो नहीं गया — हमें दरवाज़ा तोड़ना होगा।"**

He is already back a step with his shoulder turned when the door comes open, and she catches him with both hands on his arms before he goes past her.

"Kya hua, baby?" Ahana says it, and her voice is the voice of a girl just woken out of an evening, and her hair is down and wet at the ends.

**"तुम ठीक हो?"** Vikram is holding her shoulders. **"तुम्हें पता है मैं कितना डर गया था? वो न्यूज़ देख के तुम घबरा गई होगी ना?"**

**"कौन सी न्यूज़, baby?"** She says it kindly. **"मैं तो बाथरूम में रिलैक्स कर रही थी।"**

"Kaan to khule the na." Aryaman says it from behind Vikram, and it is not kind at all. **"Ye itni zor-zor se darwaza peet raha tha. Sunai nahi diya?"**

**"नहीं।"** Ahana lets go of Vikram's arms. **"म्यूज़िक ऑन था।"** She opens the door a little wider, so that both of them can see the room behind her — the speaker on the shelf, the dressing table, the bed with the sheet turned down. **"और वो इतना सूदिंग था ना, कि baby, नॉक-नॉक की आवाज़ में बैकग्राउंड में चला जाता है। सॉरी, पता नहीं चला।"**

"Koi baat nahi." Vikram says it too fast. He is smiling at her the way a man smiles when he has decided to be relieved.

**"मुझे ना बहुत थकावट सी फील हो रही है।"** She puts one hand flat against the door frame and leans there. **"थोड़ा सोना चाहती हूं।"**

**"सो जाओ।"**

**"यार, sure."** Vikram steps back. **"हम कल बात करेंगे। एंड यू नो ना — आई लव यू।"**

**"आई नो।"** Ahana smiles at him. **"सो जाओ।"**

The door goes to. The two men stand in the gallery with the tray between them.

And then Aryaman turns to go, and stops, and his hand comes off the banister.

## Two Places

The gallery at the top of the second stair, one bulb in the long corridor and the rest of it in its own dark.

Ahana is standing at the far end of the gallery.

Not behind a door. Not in a room. **Standing in the lit part of the corridor at the top of the other stair, in the sari she wears on the lawn, with her hands folded in front of her, looking at him.**

Aryaman's whole body goes still in a way Vikram's does not.

"Tum —" Vikram looks at the door and then down the gallery and then at the door. **"ये यहां अभी तो ये कमरे में थी। फिर इतनी जल्दी चेंज करके यहां कैसे आ सकती है?"**

Aryaman walks. He walks the length of the gallery at a pace that has nothing in it but the question, and he stops in front of her, and he speaks quietly, and every word of it is a man taking hold of the only thing in the room he is certain of.

**"ऐसा कैसे कर सकती हो? अभी तुम वहां थी। अब तुम यहां हो। कैसे?"**

Ahana tilts her head.

**"यू आर ऑब्सेस्ड विद मी।"** She says it almost gently. **"कहीं प्यार श्या तो नहीं हो गया? ऐसा ही होता है — मैं तुम्हें यहां भी दिख रही हूं, मैं तुम्हें वहां भी दिख रही हूं। प्यार में तो अक्सर ऐसा ही होता है। प्यार की सूरत सब जगह नज़र आती है।"** She folds her hands again. **"अपने दिल को भी चेक कर लो। वो तो ठिकाने पर है ना, या उस पे भी मेरा ही कब्ज़ा है?"**

**"बकवास बंद करो।"** Aryaman's voice comes down instead of up. **"और चेक तो मैं करूंगा। अगर तुम यहां हो — तो उस रूम में कौन है?"**

**"हाथ छोड़ो मेरा।"** Ahana pulls back. **"आओ, हाथ छोड़।"**

He takes her wrist. And Ahana turns her head down the gallery and screams it — **"विक्रम! विक्रम, please help me! ये लड़का मेरा हाथ पकड़ रहा है!"** — and every bit of the fright in it is right, and it is the same fright she wears ten minutes ago at a door with a tray outside it.

Vikram comes up the gallery like a man who has heard this before, and the tray at the other end of it lies where it fell, one glass on its side and the ring of it still wet on the carpet.

**"जस्ट शट अप। शट अप, आर्यमन।"** He gets between them. **"तुझे प्यार में धोखा मिला है, इसका मतलब ये नहीं कि — मूव ऑन कर, आर्यमन। अपने ट्रॉमा से बाहर आ। हर किसी की लव स्टोरी तेरी लव स्टोरी की तरह सैड नहीं होती।"**

The gallery is quiet.

Aryaman looks at his friend for a long moment. Then he looks at the girl, who has one hand on Vikram's arm now and is looking at the floor the way a woman looks at a floor when she is being defended.

**"मैं जानता हूं ये लड़की गलत है।"** He says it to Vikram and he says it quietly. **"और ये मैं साबित करके रहूंगा।"**

"Sorry," Vikram says to her, at the door. "I'm sorry."

**"इट्स ओके।"** Ahana pats his arm. **"मैं मिलती हूं तुमसे।"**

## The Sisters

The service lane behind the house at twenty to three, with the fence's shadow lying flat across the concrete and a single sodium lamp making everything the colour of a bad photograph.

Ananta is standing where the yard meets the wall, with her sari over her head, and she has not been crying.

"Purvi."

Ahana stops.

**"समझ सकती हूं।"** Ananta says it without moving. **"उसे इतना करीब अपनी आँखों के सामने देखना आसान नहीं होता।"**

**"मैं तो अपना प्यार हार गई।"** Ahana's voice is flat and complete. **"और अपना दी। मैं जानती हूं कि अब कुछ नहीं बचा है।"**

**"मुझे कुछ नहीं भूलना है, पूर्वी।"** Ananta steps out of the fence's shadow and into the lamplight, and she is not the girl who cried in a passage two days ago. **"तुम भी मत भूलो। आर्यमन ने हमारे साथ क्या किया था —"**

**"नहीं भूली हूं।"**

Ananta waits, and when her sister does not go on, she comes the last two steps herself.

**"तो कब तक, पूर्वी?"** She says it without heat, which is worse than heat. **"कब तक इस घर में उनकी रस्में करती रहेगी? कब तक इस आदमी का हाथ पकड़ेगी जो —"**

**"जब तक बारह नहीं हो जाते।"** Ahana's voice does not change at all. **"तेरह थे। मेरा परिवार, और मैं। बारह बचे हैं। जब तक बारह नहीं हो जाते — मैं यहीं हूं, इसी घर में, उनके बीच।"**

"Purvi —"

**"और वो सिर्फ़ आदमी नहीं है, दीदी।"** Ahana turns her head a little, and the sodium light does something bad to her eyes. **"वो उसका भाई है। और उसका भाई है उस घर का बेटा। और अगर मैं उस घर में रहनी है —"** She stops herself. **"मुझे रहना है।"**

Ananta looks at her for a long moment.

**"मुझे कुछ नहीं भूलना है, पूर्वी।"** She takes her by the wrist now, and holds it the way a sister holds it. **"और तुम भी मत भूलो।"**

**"ये नागिन का बदला है।"** Ahana says it to her sister's face, in the sodium light, with the house asleep behind them. **"ये तो सिर्फ शुरुआत है।"**

## The Leaving

The lawn at half past one, with the hired light still turning over the wet grass and the media's headlamps coming up the drive to stand in a row at the gate, and a man sending the last of the guests' cars round the back without asking anybody's leave.

The police take Sameer away in the second car, and the media get the gate before the third, and the lawn is a set of white lights pointed at a family.

Parmeet has not moved from his chair on the veranda. **"Woh jail nahi ja sakta."** He says it to no one and to the whole lawn. **"Meri izzat ka sawaal hai. Baljeet — sun."** He does not look round. **"Tu ise dekhta hai."**

Baljeet comes round the chair and stands where he has been told to stand.

"Nahi."

Vihaan says it into the taste of cold coffee and it stops the lawn.

**"Jail jaayega. Woh jaayega, aur woh jayega abhi, aur tu kuch nahi karega —"** He puts the cup down on the rail. **"— kyunki agar hum use nikaalne ki koshish karenge to agla program yeh hoga ki *yeh ghar* news par hoga. Aur us ke baad, Papa, hum sab news par honge, aur uske baad hamein koi nahi puchhega ki woh kya hai."**

Parmeet looks at his son for a long moment. "Tum bahut daud rahe ho aaj."

"Main daud raha hoon." **"File kal subah chhah baje khulti hai —"** He says it to the lawn, and it is the first time all night that he has said the word file in that house. **"— aur us file mein woh cheez nahi honi chahiye jo aaj raat main kar sakta tha. Aur main nahi karunga, kyunki agar doosra koi karta hai, to file kholne se pehle woh band ho jaayegi, aur hum sab ke naamon se."**

"Tum dhamki de rahe ho."

"Main hisaab de raha hoon." Vihaan's glass comes down on the rail again, and the second sound is smaller than the first. "Aapne mujhe teen mahine pehle is ghar ka hisaab diya tha. Main aaj raat woh hisaab de raha hoon."

The silence after that is the longest of the night, and it is broken by Aryaman, who has come across the lawn with his jacket under his arm and his phone in his hand.

"Vihaan. Teen mahine pehle —" Aryaman does not raise his voice either. It is worse that he does not. "— us file mein kya tha? Aaj ki raat mein kya nahi tha?"

"Kal subah chhah baje." Vihaan turns his glass once on the rail. "Us pehle ek shabd bhi na kholna. Yeh meri request nahi hai."

"Yeh kya hai?"

"Yeh ye hai ki main tumhe woh dikha raha hoon jo abhi tak kisi ko nahi dikhaya." Vihaan looks at him. "Aaj raat is ghar mein ek aurat mar gayi hai aur us ka shauhar gaadi mein baitha hai. Ab talak kisi ne ye nahi poochha ki kaun kaun ghar mein tha. Main chahta hoon ki ye poochha jaaye — kal subah, chhah baje, aur ye poochhne se pehle, mujhe tumse sachai chahiye."

"Kaisi sachai?"

"Woh sachai jo tum abhi tak mujhe nahi bata rahe. Isliye kal subah chhah baje. Us pehle, agar tum mujse kuch bhi poochho —" Vihaan picks the cup up, "— main jhoot bolunga. Main tumhein bata raha hoon, isliye poochhna nahi."

He goes in through the veranda doors with the cup in his hand and he does not look back at any of them.

## Doosri Manzil

The lawn empties the way it filled, except faster, and under the porch light the family goes up the front stairs one at a time the way families do when they have all agreed not to say anything until the doors are shut.

The corridor on the second floor at two in the morning, one tube burning at the near end of it and the far end gone into its own dark, and Swarna's door standing open on a room nobody has been allowed to touch, and the light from inside it lying out across the tiles in a long yellow shape.

A man from the local station waits outside it with a torch in his belt and a register under his arm, and the look of a duty nobody asks for. Somebody has told him what a house like this is. Nobody has told him what this one is.

"Sir, aap andar nahi ja sakte," he says, and he says it before he has seen who he is saying it to.

**"Koi andar nahi jaayega,"** he says, and he says it before he has seen who he is saying it to.

Vihaan stops in front of him. **"Yeh mera ghar hai. Aap mere ghar mein meri amma ke kamre ke saamne khade hain. Aap andar nahi jaayenge —"** He turns, and the man from the station sees his face, and stops talking. **"— koi andar nahi jaayega. Ek doctor kee zaroorat hai, ek saaf chaadar kee zaroorat hai, aur ghar ke andar ki jo bhi cheez hai, woh wahi rahegi jahan hai. Meri baat samajh rahe hain?"**

The man's jaw works once. **"Sir, yeh thana ka case hai —"**

**"To thane wale se mujhe milaiye. Abhi. Aur uske pehle —"** Vihaan's voice comes down instead of up, **"— aap do baatein likh lijiye. Pehli: do CCTV camera teen din se band hain aur kisi ne report nahi ki. Doosri: is kamre ka darwaza andar se band tha, aur yeh —"** he nods at the door, **"— yeh tuta nahi hai. Kisi ne ise khola hai."**

"Sir —" the man starts, and then writes it instead. His pen goes slow and then fast, and he makes a small sound with his mouth as he writes the second line, because he has understood what it means and does not want to have understood it.

Below them, on the landing, Aryaman has stopped halfway up with his hand on the banister. Neither of them looks at the other. Vihaan goes past him and down, and the last thing the house hears him say to a man from the station is **"Aur mujhe kal subah chhah baje ke pehle koi phone nahi karega."**

## The Gate

Vikram's car is at the gate with its light on and Ahana is not in it.

Ahana comes down the drive in the last of the lights, stopping to be looked at twice, and she passes Vihaan with her chin up and her hands folded in front of her, and she says, on her way past, in a voice pitched for one person only:

"You're very calm."

**"Main calm hoon."** Vihaan does not turn round. **"Aap ke liye ek car hai, Ahana. Aap us mein baith jaaiye."**

"Aap puchhenge nahi?"

**"Main puchh raha hoon."** He turns round now. **"Aap ne Swarna ji ki sari pehni hui hai?"**

The whole drive is quiet for one second.

"It's a **sari**, Vihaan." She says it perfectly, and the perfection costs her. "There are forty in that house."

**"Aap ke paas kal subah chhah baje tak ka waqt hai,"** Vihaan says. **"Us ke baad main jo poochhunga, us ka jawab nahi hoga. Sirf ek hisaab hoga."**

She looks at him for a moment longer than she means to, and then she gets into the car and the car goes, and the gate shuts behind it, and the gate is the only thing in that house he told the truth to all night.

Inside, on the landing where the light from the hall does not reach, Aryaman is still standing halfway up the stairs with the phone in his hand, and the landline in the study begins to ring for the fourth time, and nobody answers it.

Vihaan stands at the bottom of his own stairs with the whole house behind him in the dark, and he thinks about a door he could have opened, and he takes out his phone.

"Verma." It is two in the morning. "Camera. Pehli manzil, Samir ke kamre ke bahar wala. Jo chal raha tha. Woh kahan likhta hai, aur us mein aaj raat ka kya hai — mujhe woh chahiye, aur mujhe woh subah tak chahiye." He listens for a while, and then he listens without saying anything at all.

**"Kab se?"**

He stands very still at the bottom of his own stairs.

**"Kaun bheja?"** A pause. **"Kis ne poochha tha — ki us kamre mein kya hua?"**

The line goes on. Vihaan's thumb finds the edge of the stair rail and stays on it.

**"To phir woh bahar kaise hai?"** He says it quietly. **"Maine darwaza band kiya tha. Maine gaadiyan roki thi. Maine har aadmi se kaha tha ki phone neeche rakhe — aur footage us waqt bahar ja chuka tha, jab main lawn pe khada tha aur poora ghar mere order maan raha tha."**

He listens again.

**"Nahi. Tu galat nahi hai. Main tha."** He closes his eyes. **"Maine is ghar ke har darwaze pe aadmi lagaya aur ek kamre ke andar ki cheez ko bhool gaya jo pehle se bahar ja rahi thi. Ab sun."** His voice comes back to its own level. **"Subah chhah baje. Woh jo bhi hai — jo bhi ne bheja hai — main us tak pahunchunga. Aur Vihaan Suri ka naam us list se hat jaayega, chahe mujhe us list ko hi jalana pade."** He puts the phone away and goes up.

At the top of the house, above all of them, the light at the end of the fourth-floor corridor is still on at three in the morning, and the house does not ask why.


The man on the second floor does not sleep.
