## Teen Baje, Teen Manzil

Three in the morning, and every light in the Suri house is on, and the house has the look a house has when nobody in it has been to bed. The tube in the porch buzzes. The gate stands open on the drive, and the gravel has been walked over from the road to the steps and back again. Behind the house the generator runs at its one note, and in the hall the marigold under the chairs has begun to brown at the edges.

The hall has been washed twice. The chairs still stand in their two rows with the marigold under them, and the carpet at the third step has a dark place in the pile that has dried brown, and Vihaan is on his knees beside it with the camera in both hands, jaw tight, hands steady on the camera, voice flat.


The flash goes up the stairwell and puts the banister on the wall for a moment and takes it away.

"Shyam." Vihaan does not look up from the small screen at the back of the camera. "Tum yahan the."

"Ji, sahab." Shyam stands at the end of the passage with his cap in his hands and his eyes on the floor.

"Aaj dopahar, teen baje." Vihaan turns the camera over and looks at the step again, smiling small and broken at nothing. "Gate pe kaun aaya tha?"

"Koi nahin, sahab."

"Koi nahin." Vihaan stands up. "Aur bhabhi? Aur Purvi?"

"Bhai sahab ne kaha tha—"

"Main bhai sahab se poochh raha hoon ya tum se?"

Shyam's cap goes round in his hands once. It is blue-black wool with a badge on the front, and the badge has been polished so often that the lion on it has no face left.

"Kitaab lao." Vihaan puts the camera down on the step and holds out his hand. "Aaj ka panna."

Shyam goes down the passage and comes back with the cloth-spined register and puts it in Vihaan's hand and does not let go of it at once.

The page for the day has four cars on it, all four crossed out. The white police car is the third line. Under the fourth line the page is empty to the foot of it, and empty on the page after it, and empty on the page after that.

"Poora din khaali hai." Vihaan turns the book round in his hand, thumb on the page edge. "Chaar gaadiyan, aur poora din khaali."

"Hukum tha, sahab."

"Kiska?"

Shyam does not say it.

Vihaan closes the book and puts it under his arm and goes up the stairs past the photographs, and the passage light stays on behind him.

The photographs go up the wall in their frames, three sizes of them, and the glass on one near the top has been cracked across and left cracked.

At the top of the stairs the door of the room at the back is open, and a man is sitting on the edge of the bed in the dark with his shoes on, and his shirt is half out of his trousers, and there is a smell of the cloth that someone held over his mouth and nose.

"Aryaman."

Aryaman lifts his head. His eyes go to the camera in Vihaan's hand and stay there.

"Kya hua?" His voice is not right yet. "Main— mujhe kuch yaad nahin hai. Main utha aur—"

"Bhai." Vihaan sits down on the chair by the door, and does not come closer. "Aaj raat, gyarah baje ke baad, tumne kisi ko dekha?"

"Kisi ko?" Aryaman looks at him. "Tum kya keh rahe ho?"

"Purvi kahan hai?"

The clock in the passage goes on ticking. Aryaman's hands come up to his own face and stay there.

"Woh chali gayi." Aryaman's voice comes out through his fingers. "Papa ne kaha— woh dono chali gayin. Bhabhi aur—" He stops there.

"Purvi ka phone do baje se band hai." Vihaan says it plainly. "Gate ki kitaab mein aaj chaar gaadiyan hain, aur chaar ke baad panna khaali hai. Agle teen pannu bhi. Ghar mein chaudah log hain, aur tumhare gate se aaj kuch nikalta kisi ne nahin dekha."

Aryaman's hands come down off his face.

"Bhabhi kahan hai?"

Vihaan does not answer that. He sits with the register on his knee and lets the question stand in the room, and it is Aryaman who looks away first.

"Kal subah aath baje inspector mere desk pe hoga." Vihaan stands up. "Tum mere saath chaloge. Jo yaad hai, jo yaad nahin hai— dono batana. Main tumhara naam kisi kagaz pe nahin likh raha. Abhi nahin. Aur ek baat—" He stops in the door. "Parmeet se akela baat mat karna. Jab baat karni ho, mere saamne karna."

He goes out and pulls the door to behind him, and on the landing he stops and holds the sill and puts his head down for a moment, and then takes out the phone.

"Verma. Teen baje ka phone hai. Sun." His voice is level. "Missing person. Purvi Arora, teis saal, channel mein kaam karti hai. Aaj dopahar se ghar pe nahin hai. Inspector ko bol do ki file subah aath baje mere desk pe ho. Aur ek baat—" He looks at the stairs below him. "Us file mein ghar ka naam nahin aayega. Sirf mera."

## Mitti

Six in the morning, and the grey light that comes before the sun is properly up. The sheds stand in a row with their shutters down, and the last one has its shutter half up with a truck inside it. Behind the sheds the ground is broken open, and the tyre tracks go round and round the same circle in it, and at the end of the tracks there is a pit with the earth going back into it on top of a sheet. Two crows sit on the wire over the yard and have not moved since the men came.

"Oye iska haath hila yaar." The tall man in the black shirt is up out of the pit and back a step. "Oye!"

"Abey paagal hai kya tu?" The second man has the shovel in both hands. "Aisa hota hai kya? Mar gayi hai woh."

The shovel goes in and comes out and the earth lands and slides down the side. Behind the sheds a pump is running, and the sound of it goes on under everything they say.

"Har baar ko sehne ke liye bani thi woh." Nobody in the pit says this. It comes down over the sheds and the gravel and the grey sky, and it is a woman's voice and it is old and it is not for them. "Amrit ko paane ke liye vish peena padta hai. Yeh baat woh achhe se jaanti thi. Phir unki jaan kyun chali gayi? Kahan hai woh saari shaktiyan, aapka vardaan, aapki kripa jo aapne hum naaginon ko di thi."

The two men in the pit look up at the sky and then at each other.

"Abey tu ne zyada chadha li hai kya?" The second man puts the shovel down. "Maa kasam isne aankh kholi hai."

The sheet is the colour of the inside of a hospital, and where it has been dragged it has taken the red dust of that ground and kept it.

The sheet has come away from a face at the end of the pit and the eyes in it are open.

"Chal mitti daal." The tall man's voice has gone thin. "Daal, daal, daal—"

"Ruk." The second man goes down on one knee at the edge and looks, and then he is very still. "Abey iski aankhon mein—"

"Kya?"

"Chehre." He says it without lifting his head. "Iske dono aankhon mein chehre hain. Ek, do, teen— maine tera gina. Saat, aath—"

"Kisi naagin ki aankhon mein—"

"Sirf naagin ki aankhon mein." The second man's hand comes up. "Aur jo bhi koi un par hamla karta hai na, uski tasveer naagin ki aankhon mein chhap jaati hai. Yeh sabko pata hai. Yeh sabko pata hai!"

"Abey ye naagin thodi na hai." The tall man kicks the earth back in with the side of his boot. "Bhabhi thi. Humare saath chaar din roti khaayi hai."

The second man goes down on his other knee in the dirt at the edge, and the dirt goes into his trousers, and he counts under his breath, and the count goes past ten and past eleven and stops at thirteen, and then he is on his feet and walking backwards from the pit.

"Terah." His mouth is dry. "Terah chehre. Tera chehra. Mera chehra. Sabke chehre."

"Mitti daal." The tall man has the shovel up.

"Zameen ke neeche se—"

"MITTI DAAL."

The earth goes in over the sheet. It goes in fast and it goes in wrong, and one corner of the sheet stays out of it, and the second man puts his boot on the corner and pushes it down.

Behind them the last shed has its shutter up and a truck standing in it with the back doors open, and the truck is not going anywhere. Its bonnet is dull with the dust of that ground, and on the shelf under its windscreen there is a garland gone brown and a paper god with one corner curled.

The pit's edge has the marks of two boots on it, and the shovel stands in the earth where it has been left, and the wire over the yard is empty now.

"Aapka vardaan vyarth nahin ja sakta, Mahadev." The old voice is not finished. "Yeh anarth nahin ho sakta. Aapko humari Maha Naagrani ko wapas laana hi hoga. Humare liye, is desh ke liye, is desh ko bachane ke liye, aapko unhein lautana hi hoga."

The invocation goes up over the sheds at six in the morning, thin and sure of itself, and the two men stand beside a filled-in pit with the shovels in their hands and do not say one word to each other.

"Jagiye Mahadev." The voice is not in a hurry. "Jagiye."

## Aadha Ghanta

Seven in the morning, and the sun is over the sheds now, and the yard behind them is full of it, and the dust the forty men have walked up stands in it in a cloud to their knees. The truck is in the middle with its tarpaulin off and its back doors open, and three more stand against the wall, and the men are round the first one the way men stand round a fire.

"Mere aadmi pehle wahan pahunchenge." The man with the phone is walking along the line of them. "Wahan bomb aur visphot ka plan ban raha hai hamara. Bas aadha ghanta aur ye poori jagah dhamake se ud jayegi."

"Har har har Mahadev." One of the forty says it, and two of them laugh, and the laugh goes down the line and stops.

By the truck's back wheel there is a coil of wire, a roll of tape, and a pair of rubber gloves that have been pulled off and left where they fell.

The phone rings in the walking man's hand and he takes it and his face changes.

"Kya matlab hai gaadi paani mein?" He stops walking. "Hello? RDX laga diye, aur phir—"

He listens, and the forty watch him listen.

"Nashay mein tha?" His voice has gone quiet. "Saara RDX paani mein gir gaya. Kuch kaam ka nahin raha. Sab barbaad ho gaya." He puts the phone down against his leg and stands there. "Tu ek baar idhar aaja. Tujhe to main hi maarunga."

"Sir—"

"Gaadi ka number?" He says it to nobody. "Kis ki gaadi thi?"

The man beside him has a page in his hand, and he looks at it, and he holds it out.

"Channel ki gaadi hai, sir." The man's finger goes along the top line. "OB van, teen mein se ek. Pontoon pass laga hua hai. Pass pe—" He stops.

"Bol."

"Pass pe chhote sahab ka signature hai."

The man with the phone looks at the page for a long moment, and then he folds it and puts it in his inside pocket.

"Toh ab Plan B ka waqt aa gaya hai." He looks down the line of forty. "Aur woh gaadi paani se nikalni chahiye. Aaj raat. Kisi ko pata nahin chalna chahiye ki gaadi kahan se aayi thi, kiske naam pe chali thi, aur usmein kya tha."

## Naam Kya Hai Tera

Eight in the morning, and the lane behind the Naag Mandir with the temple wall on one side and an open drain on the other, and the sun coming over the wall in one slab that stops halfway across the lane. A girl lies face down in the wet grass at the edge of the slab, and her kurta is dark down the back of it, and her spectacles are in the grass a foot from her hand with one lens out of the frame.

"Arre kya sahi ladki hai yaar." A man comes round the corner of the wall with a scooter's handlebar in his hand. "Utha le kya isko?"

"Yeh ladki chalegi kya?"

They turn her over. Her eyes are open and they do not go anywhere at once, and then they do, and they come round to the two of them.

"Naam kya hai tera?"

"Ananta."

"Ananta." The man with the handlebar looks at his friend and grins. "Ananta."

"Kaun hai tu?"

"Naagin hoon."

"Kya bakwaas kar rahi hai ladkiyan?" He laughs, and the laugh is honest, and it is the worst thing in the lane. "Naagin. Arre, aaj kal sab naagin hi hain. Sun, tera ghar kahan hai? Hum tujhe chhod dein, humein Kumbh nikalna hai."

The girl on the grass looks past him at the temple wall.

"Anant kul ki Naagrani." She says it to the wall, and her voice is a girl's voice and the words are not. "Ichchhadhari naagin."

The two men stop laughing, and the song is already up over the lane, out of nothing, the way that song arrives: *na nar na naari, na insaan na jaanwar… Shiv vardani, Naaglok ki rani…*

"Bhai—"

"Chhod isko." The handlebar comes up in front of him. "Chhod, chhod, chhod—"

The drain runs. Somewhere behind the wall a shop's shutter goes up and stays up, and a bicycle goes past the mouth of the lane and does not stop.

The lane is quiet, and then the bell in the temple behind the wall moves, and there is no wind in that lane at all.

Two women come round the corner of the wall from the other end, one old with a lamp and one young in green, and the men are up and running before the second one's foot is on the grass.

"Maharani." Uttara goes down on her knees in the wet and puts the lamp beside her. "Maharani, hum aapko utha rahe hain."

The girl on the grass closes her eyes, and opens them, and the eyes are a girl's eyes again, and she says the only thing she has said all morning that a girl would say.

"Paani."

## Do Baje Ki Khabar

A quarter to two in the afternoon, and the channel's floor with the sun on its glass wall so that the meeting round the assignment desk stands in its own reflection. The big screen is on with the sound low and the ticker going under it, and the light off the screen comes and goes on their faces while they talk.

"Kumbh se do package aaye hain." The boy at the desk has both of them up on the big screen. "Pehla, ghat pe hungama. Doosra, woh cheez jo ud rahi thi."

"Kya cheez?"

On the big screen the ghat is on again with the sound off, and the crowd is a wall of backs, and the water behind it is the colour of tin.

"Koi cheez nahin hai, sir." Roma puts her cup down. "Main ghat pe do ghante khadi thi. Aadmi bola ki ud raha tha. Aurte bol rahi thi ki aag ugalti thi. Aur jab maine poochha ki footage kahan hai—" She lifts her hands. "Kisi ke paas nahin hai. Ek aurat ne mujhse poochha ki main kaun hoon. Maine kaha— bas kijiye. Yeh news channel hai, koi TV serial nahin."

"Ek camera bhi nahin?"

The ticker goes round under the screen. On the desk by the boy's hand there are four cups, a plate with biscuit crumbs on it, and a phone lying face up with a call waiting on it.

"Ek camera bhi nahin." Roma looks at the screen. "Sir, jo aadmi wahan sabko bacha ke gaya— woh koi tha. Logon ne dekha. Humare paas kuch nahin hai."

"Toh hum kya chalayein?" The boy at the desk has his hand over the mouse. "Sir, main kya likhun?"

"Likh." The man from the channel's top floor is at the back of the group. "Do minute. Uske baad ghat ke darshan, uske baad PSA."

The packages go up on the screen one after another: a reporter holding a microphone in the middle of a crowd, a man waving his arms, an old woman saying the word *dragon*, and the channel's own voice over it saying that the dragon belongs to the stories of Changistan.

At the back of the floor Vihaan stands with his earpiece in and his hands in his pockets and watches it twice.

"Roma." He takes the earpiece out. "Ghat pe kitne camera the?"

"Humare do. Do OB vans. Aur ek jo kabhi chalti nahin—"

"Generator van."

"Ji." Roma looks at him. "Woh apne aap chalti hai, sir. Uska camera subah chhe baje se record pe tha. Kisi ne bandh nahin kiya. Hum sab woh tape dekhne hi nahin gaye."

Vihaan stands very still for a moment.

"Tape kahan hai?"

"Neeche, van mein. Aur us van ka card—" Roma stops. "Sir, woh card aapke paas hai. Aapne kal raat sab cards apne paas rakhe the."

Vihaan takes the camera bag off his shoulder and puts it on the assignment desk and opens it. The cards stand in it in their plastic sleeves in a row, and on the outside of each sleeve there is a date in his own hand in blue.

## Looteri Dulhan

Four in the afternoon, and the same floor, and the sun has gone round to the far side of the glass wall, so that the light now comes in low and yellow across the desks and the monitor has to be turned up to be seen through it. The PSA runs with the sound low, and the floor works under it, and two of the young men at the far desks have stopped working to watch.

*Aajkal bahut naye-naye scam chal rahe hain. The digital scam. Identity scam. Online banking scam. Lekin in sab ke beech ek purana scam phir se saamne aaya hai.* The card on the screen turns over. *The looteri dulhan scam.*

Then the house.

Parmeet Suri sits in the chair in the hall of his own house with the fire out behind him and his spectacles in his hand, and he says it to the camera the way he says a date.

"Mere ghar mein do shaadiyan hui. Ek ne ghar basaya, aur ek ne mere ghar walon ko choona lagaya. Aisi looteri dulhanein kisi pandemic se kam to nahin hain. Ab sawaal yeh hai ki hum unhein pehle din se pehchan kyun nahin paate? Aankhen kholo. Jaano. Pehchano."

"Can you believe that?" The voice over the card is a woman's, and it has been to the channel's own edit and back. "Well, you should. Kyunki kal ye aapke saath bhi ho sakta hai."

The monitor goes to black and then to the channel's logo, and the floor goes on with its morning.

Vihaan stands in front of the monitor with his hands at his sides.

"Kisne book kiya?"

"Sir?" The young man with the tablet comes up beside him.

"Yeh PSA." Vihaan turns round. "Yeh paid hai. Kisi ne slot book kiya, kisi ne creative diya, kisi ne paisa diya. Kisne?"

"Sir, woh maine hi—" The young man stops. "Sir, woh aapke daftar se clear hua tha. Booking order pe aapke signature hain."

The floor goes quiet in the way this floor goes quiet.

"Lao." Vihaan holds his hand out, and the order comes. It is a page and a half, and the second page has the client's name at the top and the amount in a box and, at the foot, an initial in small blue biro, and the initial is his.

He reads it standing up, all of it, and the client's name above it is not a client's name: it is the house's.

"Kitna?"

"Do lakh, sir. Do mahine ka package. Ghar ki taraf se."

"Ghar ki taraf se." Vihaan puts the page down on the desk and looks at the monitor again, where the channel's logo is going round and round. "Is channel ka kagaz mera desk se clear hota hai. Yeh PSA usi kagaz pe gaya hai, mere initial ke saath, ghar ke paise se. Mera hi ghar apni shaadi ka ishtihaar chala raha hai, aur kagaz pe mera naam hai."

"Sir, main cancel kar doon?"

"Nahin." Vihaan picks the page up. "Agar cancel karoge to woh samajh jaayenge ki kisi ne dekha hai. Chalne do. Aur teen hafte ka bill unke ghar bhejo, poora, advance ke saath."

He puts the page in his folder and goes to the lift, and at the lift he stops.

"Aur ek kaam karo. Is PSA ka master copy mere paas rakho. Original. Jo aaj chala, woh bhi."

## Chauthi Manzil

Six in the evening, and the last of the light is going off the tops of the buildings outside, and in the room on the fourth floor the curtains are half shut and what is left of it comes through them in two bars across the bed. There is a dress still in its plastic on the bed, and a woman in front of the glass with her jewellery already on, and a man at the window with a glass in his hand.

"Live? Are you ready?" She turns round. "Ek aur peeche. You want to see it?"

The man at the window has a glass in his hand and his back to her, and he watches the road below.

"Maine humare liye dress mangwai thi." She holds the plastic up against herself. "Pasand nahin aayi?"

On the bedside table there is a tray with one plate on it, a bottle, and two glasses, and the ice in the bucket beside it has gone down to water.

The man puts the glass down on the sill.

"Tum bade log ho." She sits down on the edge of the bed with the dress across her knees. "Sab kehte hain. Tumne kabhi mana bhi nahin kiya."

At half past seven the corridor outside fills up with the sound of a lift arriving, and the woman's hand stops on the plastic, and the man turns round from the window at last.

## Manohar

Half past seven in the evening, and the tube lights in the fourth-floor corridor are on and the carpet's pattern runs away to the lift, and one door stands open with the room's yellow light coming out of it into the corridor. Inside, a woman sits up in the bed with her jewellery still on, and a man stands at the window with his back to the room and his cuffs undone.

"Manohar, maine socha bhi nahin tha ki tum mere saath aisa karogi." The woman has her hands over her face. "Manohar, I swear— maine kuch galat nahin kiya. Main toh bas— ye mujhe khud le kar aaye the." She does not point at the window. She does not have to.

"Main just shut your mouth." Manohar is in the doorway with his bag still on his shoulder, and he says it to the window. "Aap jaante ho na meri wife hai. Main aapki itni respect karta hoon. Aur aapne mere saath aisa kiya. Main ye job abhi isi waqt chhodta hoon."

"Manohar, meri baat suno. Manohar—"

Her jewellery is on the tray on the bedside table and not on her, and one earring has rolled off the tray and stopped against the lamp. The room's yellow light is on the ceiling. Down the corridor a trolley goes past, and the sound of it comes in and goes away.

"Arre bhai, ye sab kya tha?" The voice comes from the corridor behind him, and Aryaman is there in his shirt sleeves with his arms out. "Kya kiya aapne? Kya tha?"

"Sir, aap—"

"Ek second." Aryaman puts a hand up. "Agar aapko uska offer pasand nahin aaya to aap usey seedha-seedha mana bhi to kar sakte the. Ye sab naatak karne ki kya zaroorat thi?"

"Maine kya kiya?" Manohar's voice is going. "Maine kya kiya, sir?"

"Bhai, mujhe pata hai ki aapne kya kiya." Aryaman leans in the doorframe and smiles at the room. "Is kamre mein aap Manohar ki biwi ke saath the. Right? Phir bhi aapne mujhse kaha ki main Manohar ko yahan bhejoon, taaki woh aapko pakad le."

The woman on the bed has gone still.

"Come on, bhai." Aryaman's hands come out of his pockets. "Mujhe pata hai ki aap auraton se kitni nafrat karte ho. But please— Manohar to aapka favourite tha na? Uske saath aise kyun kiya? Ab woh to naukri chhodega, hisaab se. Uski biwi bhi usey chhod ke chali jayegi."

Manohar stands in the hotel corridor with his bag on his shoulder and does not move at all, and the door frame is warm where his hand is on it, and out of the window at the end of the corridor the street below has its lights on and its traffic going nowhere.

"Maana ki uski biwi ne aap par try kiya

"Maana ki uski biwi ne aap par try kiya. Aapko approach kiya. Lekin aap usey seedha-seedha mana bhi to kar sakte the. Baat wahin pe khatam ho jaati." Aryaman pushes himself off the doorframe. "Agar aaj main usey na keh deta, to kal koi aur usey haan keh deta. Aisi ladki ki yahi problem hoti hai. Unhein mana karne se koi farq nahin padta. Aur haan, aaj Manohar mujhse nafrat karega. Mere saath kaam nahin karega. Par tu dekhna, kal woh khud aake mujhe thank you bolega."

"Sir." Manohar's voice is very quiet. "Sir, meri biwi—"

"Maine koi shaadi nahin todi." Aryaman says it to the corridor. "Maine Manohar ko tootne se bachaya hai. Kyunki ab apni life mein woh aise dhokhe nahin khayega. Agar aaj yeh saamne nahin aata, to woh usey aise hi dhokha deti rehti, aur us shaadi ke saath-saath Manohar bhi doobta."

He walks away down the corridor with his hands in his pockets, and the lift doors take him, and the brass of the doors goes on shaking for a second after they have closed.

In the room the woman has her hands over her face again, and the man at the window has still not turned round, and the dress in its plastic lies across the foot of the bed where she has put it down.

At half past eight Manohar comes up to the third floor with his bag still on his shoulder and a sheet of paper in his hand, and he puts the paper on the desk in front of Vihaan.

"Sir, resignation."

It is a company sheet with the channel's mark in the corner, two lines typed in the middle of it, and a space at the bottom for a signature that is still empty.

Vihaan reads it standing up, all of it, the way he reads an invoice.

"Kaam kyun chhod rahe ho?"

"Sir—"

"Kaam kyun chhod rahe ho, Manohar?"

Manohar's mouth opens and shuts.

The office on the third floor has its lights on and the floor outside it is dark, and the cleaning man is going along the desks with a bin and does not look up.

"Main aapki jagah hota"—Vihaan puts the paper down and turns it round and pushes it back across the desk—"to main is kagaz pe aaj kuch nahin likhta. Main kal office aata, aur main kaam karta, aur main us aadmi ke saamne har din baithta. Yeh kagaz unke haath mein jaayega, aur usmein aapki jagah koi aur hoga."

"Sir, meri biwi—"

"Ghar ki baat ghar mein." Vihaan's voice does not change. "Kaam ki baat yahan. Aap kal nau baje aayenge. Aur agle mahine se aapki salary teen hazaar badh rahi hai, kyunki jo aadmi is office mein sabse zyada jhelta hai, uska hisaab main rakhta hoon."

Manohar stands there with his hands at his sides.

"Sir, aap unke bhai hain."

"Ji."

"Phir aap—"

"Main unka bhai hoon." Vihaan picks up his pen. "Aur main is desk pe hisaab rakhta hoon. Dono baatein sach hain. Aap kal aayenge."

## Do Hazaar Card

Nine at night, and the Suri hall is still dressed for a wedding that has not happened, and the chairs stand in their two rows with the marigold under them going brown, and the chandelier is on at full. The long table has been brought in under it and covered with printer's samples, and two of the kitchen staff are clearing the sideboard behind the family, quietly, and one of them keeps looking round.

"Abhi chhe mahine pehle hi shaadi ka itna bada drama kiya." Pinky has a sample in each hand. "Aur ab phir se ek aur nayi shaadi."

## List

Ten at night, and the study on the first floor has one lamp on it and the rest of the house dark behind the shut door, and the list is on the desk in four columns, and there is a glass on the desk and a plate with something left on it that nobody has cleared.

"Aaj main Aryaman ko disturb nahin karna chahta." Parmeet turns the list round. "Kaafi der se drink pe drink piye jaa raha hai."

"Yeh hai list humare saare investments ki." His finger goes down the first column. "Gaadiyan, bangle, karobaar. Sabki list hai ismein. Oonche se oonche daam lagao aur bech kar yahan se niklo."

The list is a ruled sheet in Parmeet's own hand, four columns, and the fourth column has three lines in it and then nothing.

Ravish goes down the list with his finger. Karan stands at the window with his phone face down in his hand.

"Tauji, yeh—" Karan turns round. "Yeh teen gaadiyan bech dein? Woh nayi hain."

"Gaadiyan bech do." Parmeet takes his spectacles off. "Aur ye." His finger comes down on a line at the foot of the second column. "London office. Isko sabse pehle."

The room goes quiet.

"Papa." Vihaan is in the doorway with his folder under his arm and his tie off, and the lamp on the desk puts his shadow forward into the room before the rest of him. "London office mere naam pe hai."

"Tumhare naam pe kaam karta hai." Parmeet does not look up. "Kagaz pe woh Suri family ka hai. Aur paper mere paas hain."

Vihaan comes into the room and stands at the end of the desk and looks at the list, and turns it round with one finger, and reads it all the way down.

"Channel ka Kumbh feed." His finger stops. "Yeh bhi list mein hai."

"Woh feed Kessler ko ja raha hai. Uska paisa wapas aayega aur hum bahar nikal jaayenge."

"Woh feed do hafte pehle Kessler ko gaya." Vihaan looks up. "Us kamre mein saat aadmi baithe the aur mujhe bulaya bhi nahin gaya. Kagaz pe mera naam hai. Aur teen pontoon pass usi feed ke saath gaye, mere haath se." He turns the page. "Aaj subah pehla pass jis gaadi pe laga tha, woh gaadi paani mein hai. Sardar Patel Marg ke pull pe. Aapne mujhe bataya nahin."

Nobody moves.

"Main poochh raha hoon." Vihaan's voice stays level. "Us gaadi mein kya tha?"

"Bombay ka meeting bhool jao." Parmeet takes the list back and folds it. "Aur ye list se apna hissa katwa lo. Tum apna paisa le kar ja sakte ho. Ghar chhod ke."

The phone in Parmeet's hand has its screen lit, and the light of it is on his jaw and not on the rest of his face.

"Ji." Vihaan puts his folder on the desk. "Main jaanta hoon ki main ja sakta hoon. Main poochh raha hoon ki us gaadi mein kya tha."

"Verma?" Vihaan says it at the same time into his own phone, from the door. "Haan, abhi. Gaadi number se. Aaj subah ka accident, Sardar Patel Marg. ## Message

## Magarmach

Midnight, and the industrial sheds with their shutters down, and a yard behind them with a concrete tank in it and a single bulb on a pole over the water with a tin shade nailed over the bulb. The insects come to it and the bats come for the insects, and both go on and off the light while the men wait. The yard is mud at its edges where water has been thrown.

Muddy water, and something long in the water turning over, and a man at the edge of it in a white kurta with a bucket beside his foot and rubber sandals on.

"Mere bachche." He puts his hand down into the tank and touches the ridge of something's back. "Mere magarmach. Ye gusse se bhaunklaaye hue kyun hain? Inhein khana nahin khilaya kya?"

Three men stand at the edge of the yard and do not come all the way across it.

"Khilaya tha, sir." Ravish's voice is careful. "Par inke liye to khana hi kam pad jaata hai."

The man with the bucket looks up.

"Aur mangao." He drops something into the water and the water goes over. "Mere bachche bhookhe nahin rehne chahiye. Achha— kya khilaya tha inhein? Inka pet hi nahin bharta?"

The three of them stand still.

"Chaubees saal." The man wipes his hand on his kurta. "Bharat desh. Saapon ka desh, saperon ka desh, naagmani ka desh. Pichhle chaubees saal se tum logon ne woh naagmani dhoondhi. Aur naagin marne ke baad bhi tumhein woh nahin mili."

"Sir, humein pata hai ki—"

"Mil jayegi." The man's voice is mild. "Milni chahiye. Warna tum logon ko wapas bulane ki taiyari shuru karni padegi."

The water goes still and the light on the pole hums, and a ripple goes out from the wall of the tank and comes back again.

"Chaubees saal se ye naagmani tum humare liye dhoondh rahe the." He picks the bucket up. "Lekin ab ye naagmani tum apne aap ke liye dhoondhoge. Apni jaan bachane ke liye dhoondhoge. Aur socho— agar nahin mili, to?"

"Sir—"

"Nayab heera mila hum dhoondh rahe hain." He drops the last of it into the tank and the water comes up over the ridge of a back. "Bahut jaldi mil jayega. Par pehle— mere bachche abhi bhi bhookhe hain."

"Jaao." The man does not turn round.

## Faisla

Six in the morning, and the Naag Mandir with the lamps lit in a line down the hall and the smoke of them going up flat to the ceiling, and the stone floor polished by feet so that the flames stand in it upside down. The cloth is off the seat at the end of the hall and folded on the step below it.

"Ab kya karna hai, Naagrani?" Uttara's hands are folded. "Kya faisla hai aapka?"

The girl at the door has her sister's sari folded in her arms, folded in three with the gold border turned inside so that it does not show, and her own kurta still stiff down the back. She looks at the seat, and at the cloth, and at the three women standing in a line at the side of the hall.

"Woh mitti mein hai." Her voice is a girl's voice. "Uski aankhon mein chehre hain. Tumne kaha tha ki is kul ki naagin ke aankhon mein har hamla karne wale ka chehra chhapta hai."

"Ji."

"Kitne?"

"Terah." Uttara does not move. "Ananta ki aankhon mein terah chehre the. Terah aadmi. Humne gine hain, Maharani."

"Terah." The girl says the number once, plainly, in the voice she keeps for facts. "Ek chehra, ek aadmi."

"Ji."

The lamps do not move. The smoke of them goes up flat. On the step below the seat the cloth lies folded, and the gold thread in its border takes the light and gives it back in one thin line.

She stands at the door of that hall with her sister's sari in her arms and the lamp light on her face.

"Toh terah." She says it to the hall. "Ek ek karke. Main unke ghar jaungi, unke apne ghar, jahan woh apne bachchon ke saath baithte hain. Aur main unhein wahi dikhaungi jo unhone meri behen ko dikhaya."

The lamps do not so much as move.

"Aapko pata hai"—Uttara's voice is low—"ki aapko rasta Naagraj se milta hai. Aapko Anant kul ke raja se milna hoga, aur woh kahin nahin hai. Koi jaanta nahin hai ki woh kahan hai."

"Toh main akeli chalungi."

Behind the three women there is a long table with the things of the mandir on it — a bell, a conch, a copper pot, a heap of marigold that is not fresh — and the dust of the hall lies on all of it, and the light of the lamps makes it stand up and drift.

"Nahin." Uttara's lamp comes up. "Aap akeli nahin chalengi. Hum aapke saath hain. Par ek baat—" She looks at the door of the hall, where the light of the morning is coming in. "Woh aadmi, jo gaadi paani se nikalne aayega. Woh aapko dhoondh raha hai."

The girl with the sari in her arms says nothing at all.

"Uske ghar mein aap kaise jaayengi?"

"Chadhai ke raste se." She turns for the door. "Jahan se main aayi hoon. Aur woh—" She stops. "Woh mujhe dhoondh raha hai. Toh woh mujhe dhoondh lega. Aur jab milega, tab batayenge."
