# Chapter 24 — The Wrong Groom

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 13, "Bride Swap and Bean Battles".*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## Pinky's Morning

The small room at the top of the house holds the wedding morning at arm's length, and the light through its one window falls in a clean bar across the desk.

Vihaan sits at the desk with the laptop open and one earbud in, and Bombay talks in his ear about a feed that must run clean through fifteen days of dust. On the desk the diary lies open at today's page, and today's page says the wedding first and the money after, in his own hand.

Pinky sits on the bed with her knees up, still in the suit she slept in. She has not dressed for the wedding, and nobody has come to dress her.

"Bombay ko bolo," he says into the phone. "Feed aaj shaam paanch baje tak locked chahiye. Uske baad mela ground ki light mere haath mein nahin." He listens. "Haan. Meri zimmedari."

He takes the earbud out. The room goes quiet except for the house below, which drums like a dhol it cannot put down.

"Bhai," Pinky says. "Aaj woh shaadi kar raha hai."

"Haan," he says.

"Us ladki se. Jise woh jaanta tak nahin."

He looks at her. Her face does its best to be still, and fails the way his own never does.

"Pinky," he says. "Tum taiyaar ho jaao. Neeche mat aao. Ye tumhara din nahin hai, aur ghar ko aaj tumhari himmat dekhne ki zaroorat nahin."

"Kya karun, bhai?" she asks. "Kamre mein baith kar dhun sunun?"

"Haan," he says. "Kaan band karke nahin. Khol kar. Jo sunai de, mujhe bataana. Main neeche rahunga."

A steel glass of water stands on the desk beside the diary, untouched since morning, with the spoon still in it.

On the bed's edge her wedding-guest suit lies folded, the paper price tag tucked under the collar so no one sees it.

Downstairs somebody tests the mandap mike, and the whine climbs the stairwell and dies.

Someone knocks once and goes past without waiting — feet running on the stairs, a girl's voice raised and breaking, and then Aryaman's voice after it, sharp. Pinky lifts her head.

"Ye Tina hai," she says.

"Haan," he says. "Uske bhai sambhaal lenge. Tum apna darwaza band rakhna."

"Darwaza band rakhne se shaadi ruk jaayegi?" she asks.

"Nahin," he says. "Par tum tooti hui kisi ko nahin dikhogi. Ye bhi kuch hai."

## Tina Breaks

The guest corridor on the wedding morning runs loud with the house below, and the tube light at its end buzzes over the stairs.

Tina stands in the corridor with her back to the wall, and her voice carries down to the hall. "Aap kar kya rahe hain?" she demands. "Humne kaha tha ye shaadi nahin hogi. Shaadi toh ho rahi hai, aur mera pyaar Ahana ko mil raha hai. Iska matlab meri zindagi toh bematlab hai na."

Aryaman comes up the stairs two at a time. "Tina," he says. "Neeche chalo. Abhi."

"Nahin," she says, and laughs the laugh that frightens the servants. "Pinky — paki Pinky — tu pagal ho gayi hai. Am I mad? Haan, main pagal ho gayi hoon. Humaare khoon mein hai pagalpan."

She runs. Her door slams, and the bolt goes home, and something glass breaks inside against tile.

Aryaman puts his shoulder to the door once, twice, and the bolt holds, and then the key turns from the inside of the next room — the servant's pass — and he goes through the bathroom and comes out of her door with blood on one knuckle and her wrist in his hand.

"Are you mad?" he asks her.

"Haan, main pagal ho gayi hoon," she says, trembling. "Aap bhi toh us Radhika ke pyaar mein abhi tak pagal ho. Aur main Vikram ke — " Her voice breaks clean open. "Main nahin jee paungi uske bina. I can't live without him. I love him, bhai. Please listen to me."

A hairbrush lies on the floor with three teeth missing, and the mirror above the dresser carries a crack from one corner halfway down. On the bed the bridal-guest favours sit in a row, unopened, each tied with the same gold thread.

"Tere bhai ne tujhse kaha hai na?" Aryaman asks. "Vikram ki shaadi us Ahana se nahin hogi toh nahin hogi. Trust me."

"Trust me," Tina repeats, and the fight goes out of her all at once, and she sits down on the floor where she stands.

## The Accusation

The side room off the hall stays dim through the wedding morning, and the light from the hall door cuts one bar across the carpet.

Parmeet shuts the door and turns on Rishabh. "Tumhari himmat kaise hui humein dhokha dene ki?" he asks. "Tumhein kya laga? Humein pata nahin chalega. Itne saal ki dosti bhool gaya tu?"

"Parmeet, lekin maine kiya kya hai?" Rishabh asks.

"Naagmani ke saath London jaane ka plan bana raha hai," Parmeet says. "Puchte hain — kiya kya hai? Ye kaun sa plan? Rishabh, jhooth mat bol. Humne khud suna hai Ahana ko tere London ke ticket ke baare mein baat karte hue."

"Tum logon ka dimaag kharab ho gaya hai kya?" Rishabh asks. "Yaar, mera aisa koi plan nahin tha. Na London ka, na Naagmani ka."

Outside the door the corridor stands empty, and then it does not: Mahasapera comes down it without hurry, slows at the sound of the Naagmani word, and stops where the door's bar of light does not touch him.

"Naagwani?" he says under his breath. "Iska matlab hai andar jo naagin hai, woh koi aam naagin nahin hai."

Rishabh's voice rises inside. "Kya kar raha hai tu?"

Mahasapera smiles at the shut door. "Woh Anant kul ki Naagrani hai," he says to no one. "Ab main inhein kuch nahin bataaunga. Ab main is Naagmani ko apne saath le jaaunga. Phir woh naagin bhi meri, aur Naagmani bhi."

A brass key turns slowly in the lock of the side room from the inside, and stops halfway. On the wall the framed photograph of the house's groundbreaking holds its dust undisturbed, eleven men with shovels and no smiles.

## The Basket

Ahana's room on the wedding morning stands sideways with light, and the mirror faces the door with the bride half-dressed in it.

*Ichchadhari.* The word has landed in him already, at the threshold, in the held breath. Now Mahasapera comes back along the passage with the been in his hand, and he sets his mouth to it before the door.

The been's note goes through the door like a needle through cloth.

Inside, Ahana stops with one earring in her hand. The note finds the spine and climbs it. She dances two steps she does not choose, and the third step is not hers at all, and where the bride stood the naagin pours down shining and coils.

Mahasapera lifts the basket's lid. "Aa ja," he says.

The earring lies on the dresser where her fingers left it, one of a pair, winking. Across the threshold the white line of powder lies end to end, unbroken, keeping nothing now.

He settles the lid, takes up the basket's rope, and goes down the back stairs with it, and the house below drums on and knows nothing.

## The Empty Room

The upstairs passage by noon holds heat and noise in equal measure, and the light from the stairwell window falls across Ahana's shut door.

Vihaan comes up with the search because Parmeet's voice sent for every son of the house, and he stands at the back of the crowd while Tina goes from room to room saying the bride is nowhere. Below, at the mandap, Vikram waits with the sehra in his lap, asking the air.

"Kahan ho, baby?" Vikram calls up the stairs. "Kitni der kyun laga rahe ho? Ahana apne room mein nahin hai. Kya tumne har jagah achche se check kiya?"

"Ji," somebody answers.

Bharani pushes through the crowd with her face white. "Naagrani apne kamre mein nahin hai toh woh hai kahan?" she asks the passage at large.

They open the bride's door. The room stands wrecked — the dresser swept, the bed stripped to the mattress, a chair on its side.

"Is kamre ka aisa haal kisne kiya?" Parmeet asks.

Dimple wrings her hands. "Bhai sahab, mujhe Ahana ki chinta ho rahi hai. Kahin use kuch — humein fauran mister Dhawan ko bataana chahiye."

"Haan, chal," Parmeet says.

Vihaan looks at the wreckage the way he looks at a tampered page: once, whole, and without touching. His eyes stop on the washroom door, shut, with no light under it.

"Papa," he says. "Main gate par khada hota hoon. Dhawan sahab aayenge toh seedha upar nahin aayenge. Pehle aap se baat hogi."

Parmeet looks at him for one beat, and nods. "Ja."

He turns to go — and the washroom door opens.

"Itne saare log ek saath mere room mein," Ahana says, blinking. "Dara diya mujhe."

"Beta ji, dar toh hum gaye the," Dimple says. "Humne aapko sab jagah dekha. Aap kahin nahin mili. Aur ye room ka aisa haal kaise hua?"

"Mere na earring ka set nahin mil raha tha," the bride says, smooth as poured cream. "Usi chakkar mein ye sab ho gaya."

Vihaan stands still. The answer came too fast and too complete, like a total that has been worked backwards from, and he files it where he files everything the house says too smoothly: shut, and dated today.

"Ye sab chhodo," somebody says. "Humein muhurat ke liye late ho raha hoga na."

"Par maine is room mein already check kiya tha," Tina insists, staring. "Par tum yahan nahin thi."

The bride smiles at her. "Tumne room mein check kiya na, Tina. Washroom mein nahin."

"Ye saari baatein hum baad mein karenge," Dimple says. "Pandit neeche tumhara intezaar kar rahe hain. Let's go. Chal, chal, sab log chal."

The crowd pours down the stairs with the bride in its middle. Tina stands where she left her, shaking, half a step behind them all.

Vihaan offers her his arm, the way the house's son does for a guest who shakes. "Chalo, Tina," he says. "Neeche pandit intezaar kar rahe hain. Aur tumhaari tabiyat theek nahin lag rahi."

She takes it. He walks her down behind the rest, and his face gives the stairs nothing.

A single bangle lies broken on the landing where the crowd's feet missed it, red glass in two clean halves. The stairwell window throws its bars across them both as they go down, light and dark, light and dark.

## Snow and Verdict

The hideout's stone room stands deep in snow-glare, and the cold comes off the walls in sheets that the one fire does not dent.

Ahana lies human-shaped on the stone, shaking, her breath coming short. The been plays on without pause, and each note takes a little more of the warmth out of her fingers.

*Cold.* The word in her is her mother's voice. *Cold, and the ground, and the men with the torches.*

She remembers the night the family died — the fire, the shouting, the ground going dark under her feet — and the memory comes up through the cold like a hand through ice.

"Anant kul ki Naagrani hoon main," she says, sitting up. "Apne dushman se haar jaaun — itni kamzor nahin."

Mahasapera lowers the been a finger's width. "Agar tu naaginon ki naagin hai," he says, "toh mujhe bhi anek siddhiyaan praapt hain. Taap bhi nahin — aur bhi jaanwar hain mere bas mein. Woh tera sabse puraana dushman. Tu shayad usse nahin mili. Barf ki gufaaon mein in ichchadhari naaginon se mera saalon se rishta hai. Woh kab se tera in gufaaon mein intezaar kar raha hai. Haara tha woh ek naagin se. Is baar humne usko apni siddhi mein aur bhi shaktishaali aur khatarnaak bana diya hai. Wakt aa gaya hai uske intekaam ka."

The snow at the cave's mouth stands up and walks: the Yeti comes down the slope with its arms wide, roaring.

What follows is fast and shine-bright and terrible. She meets it head-on, and the snow flies up in walls, and when the walls come down she stands and it does not.

Mahasapera backs against the stone with his hands up. "Maaf kar do," he says. "Mujhe maaf kar do. Mujhe maaf kar do. Daya karo. Mujh par daya karo."

"Naagin ki adaalat mein gunahgaaron ko maafi nahin," she says. "Sirf maut milti hai. Tujhe bhi wahi milegi."

"Sharanaagatam," he whispers.

She laughs once, short and bright. "Chand tandav."

The ice on the wall cracks in one long line from floor to ceiling, and holds. The been lies where it fell, split at the mouthpiece, silent for the first time that day.

## The Pheras

The mandap stands dressed in marigold on the swept ground, by afternoon light, and the wedding crowd sits three deep with the sun on its shoulders.

Vihaan sits in the family's row with his hands empty — the phones left upstairs, the diary shut, the day given to the house. Pinky's absence sits beside him like a second person; nobody asks after her, which tells him Parmeet's orders went ahead of him.

The bride sits beside the groom with her eyes down. The groom's sehra hides his face.

Vikram's phone rings. "Haan, bolo," he says into it. "Main sun raha hoon." He half-rises. "Shaadi chhod kar kahan ja rahe ho? Phere poore hon, andar, phir jaana."

He sits. The bride leans toward him. "Vikram," she says, low. "Washroom jaana hai. Abhi."

"Jao," he says. "Pandit ji — paanch minute ke liye mantra rok dijiye. Ahana aa jaayegi, phir shuru karte hain."

The pandit folds his hands. "Kaisi baat kar rahe hain, jajmaan? Shaadi ke phere beech mein nahin roke ja sakte. Apshagun hota hai."

"Inke yahaan kuch bhi karo, sab mein shagun-apshagun hota hi hai," somebody mutters.

"Arre pandit ji, kya baat kar rahe hain aap?" Rishabh says. "Jaane dijiye bachchi ko. Jao. Hatiye. Please."

She goes. The crowd shifts and murmurs, and the sun moves one bar's width across the marigold.

Then the lights go out — every string, every bulb, the mike's red eye — and the courtyard drops into brown dark.

"Ye light ko kya hua?" somebody calls.

Vihaan rises half out of his seat in the dark. His first thought is upstairs — Pinky, alone, in a dark house on the day of all days — and he takes two steps toward the stairs before the weight of the crowd holds him.

The lights come back. The bride sits beside the groom with her eyes down, the same and not the same, and the crowd settles, and he sits.

"Shri Ganeshay namo namah," the pandit says, folding his hands. "Ab var-vadhu phere shuru karein," the pandit says. "Pehle phere mein var aage aur vadhu peeche rahengi. Mangalam kundaliksham, mangal tano hari."

Round by round they go, and the mantras rise, and the marigold nods in the heat.

"Sarv mangal maangalye," the pandit sings over the fire. "Shive sarvaarth saadhike. Sharanye. Gauri Narayani namostute."

"Isi ke saath ye vivaah sampann ho gayaa," the pandit says at last. "Aaj se aap dono pati-patni hain."

The mike stands dead through it all, its red eye dark, and nobody troubles to wake it. Seven rounds of rice lie scattered where the couple's feet turned, white on the red cloth.

"Vikram," the pandit says. "Tum bhi apna sehra hata sakte ho."

## The Sehra Lifts

The mandap on the swept ground holds its breath under the afternoon sun, and every face in three rows turns to the groom's hands.

The groom lifts the sehra.

"Vikram — oh god." Pammi's voice goes up like a kite. "Ye Ahana Vikram ki dulhan bante Aryaman ki dulhan kaise ban gayi?"

Aryaman stands in the groom's sherwani with the sehra in his hand, smiling.

"Kya hua?" he asks the hall. "Surprise pasand nahin aaya?"

The hall erupts. And under the eruption, an aunt two rows back whispers to her neighbour, loud in the way of whispers meant to carry: "Us din dance floor par jodi bani thi. Aaj dekho — dulha hi badal gaya."

Vihaan hears it. The pair stays public — three hundred witnesses at the dance, his hand held out, Parmeet's reading of her character with the whole floor watching — and now the floor's jodi sits married to his brother. He files the whisper with the day: the house has begun reading the wedding against the dance, and there is no unread-ing it.

He looks for Vikram and does not find him. He looks at Aryaman, smiling in the stolen sherwani, and at the bride, whose shock sits on her face like jewellery put on for the occasion.

*Pinky.* Upstairs, alone, with the hall about to tear itself open below her.

He slips out along the wall while every eye sits on the mandap, and takes the stairs two at a time, and nobody sees him go but the servant at the landing, who flattens himself to let him pass.

A fallen marigold garland lies trampled by the stairs' foot, orange petals ground into the carpet, with a crushed paper cup beside it.

Above, the landing window burns white with the afternoon, and the corridor's shut door stands dark by contrast.

## The Hall Turns

The hall runs white-hot under the wedding lights, and the crowd stands now, every chair empty.

Ahana rises from the mandap with sindoor in her maang and fury in her face. "Vikram kahan hai?" she demands. "Vikram kahan hai? Vikram — Vikram!" She turns on Parmeet. "Uncle, mujhe Vikram se shaadi karni thi. Is — is se nahin. Dekha aapne? Dekha kaise is ladke — is bijli ke khambe ne mujhse dhokhe se shaadi ki hai? Main is — main is shaadi ko nahin maanti. Hum abhi ke abhi yahaan se chalenge. Chaliye."

"Nahin, beta," Parmeet says. "Lekin hamaare yahaan is shaadi ko bahut maante hain. Agni ko sakshi maankar — "

"Saaf-saaf kya naam hai?" an elder intones. "Saat pheron ka bandhan. Saat pheron ka bandhan saat janmon tak saath rehta hai. Beta, ye sindoor, ye mangalsutra — ye sirf nishaani nahin hai. Janm-janmantar ka rishta hai."

"Haan, beta," Pammi says, beaming through the smoke. "Aryaman beta, pata nahin kab tumhein hamaari pyaari bahu se pyaar ho gaya, aur tumne itna bada decision le liya."

"Dhokha!" Rishabh shouts. "Dhokha kiya hai tum logon ne. Ye meri bahu banne waali thi. Tumhaari nahin — mere bete Vikram ki patni. Ye shaadi ek dhokha — " The mike is dead, so he shouts it raw. "Ek fraud hai. Mister Dhawan, aap — aap mere samdhi banne waale the."

"Ye mere samdhi hain," Parmeet says, "aur mere samdhi rahenge."

"Main is shaadi ko nahin maanta," Dhawan says. "Tum logon ne mazaak bana ke rakha hai meri beti ki life ka. Chalo, beta. Hum ghar jaayenge. Jab woh shaadi valid hi nahin hai toh main shaadi ka tohfa — woh naayaab heera — kisi ko nahin doonga."

"Kaise nahin doge?" Baljeet asks. "Kaise tod sakte hain aap ye shaadi? Aap bhi shaadi nahin tod sakte. Ahana hamaare ghar ki bahu hai. Aryaman ki biwi. Ab Ahana bitiya hamaare ghar hi mein rahegi."

"Bhai sahab sahi keh rahe hain," Dimple says. "Aap hamaare ghar ki bahu ko aise nahin le ja sakte."

Rishabh catches Parmeet's sleeve and hisses low, for his ears only: "Agar woh money humein nahin mili na, toh Erul hum teenon ko magarmachch ko khila dega. Money tabhi milegi jab Vikram aur Ahana ki shaadi hogi. Shaadi karwaao."

"Tum bhool rahe ho, ye Hindustan hai," Parmeet murmurs back. "Humaara Changistaan nahin. Yahaan — " He stops. "Naagmani par dhyaan do. Warna hum mein se koi nahin bachega."

Two chairs lie overturned by the mandap steps, and a brass tray of rice lies scattered beside them, and nobody rights either.

The crowd presses back from the mandap steps by a pace, all at once, the way water draws back before it comes on.

The dead mike stands at the hall's edge with its cable beside it, and a paper plate lies face-down in the cable's loop.

## The Aside

The corridor behind the hall runs cooler and dimmer, and the wedding lights reach it only as spill through the door.

Dhawan has his daughter by the wrist. "Daan sahab, mujhe kuch nahin sunna hai," he says over his shoulder. "Main apni beti ko lekar ja raha hoon toh ja raha hoon. Chalo."

"Ek minute, father sahab. Ek minute." Aryaman steps into the doorway. "Kya main bas ek minute apni wife se baat kar sakta hoon?"

"Wife? My foot." Ahana shakes her father's hand off and turns on him. "Tumse baat karna toh chhodo — main tumhaari shakal dekhna bhi pasand nahin karti. Dafa ho jaao yahaan se. Chaliye, baba."

"Main toh tumhaare faayde ki baat karne waala tha," Aryaman says. "Lekin agar tumhein khud ka nuksaan karwaana hai toh rehne do."

She stops. "Dad — one second, bolo," she says, and walks back to him.

"Yahaan pe na koi Oscars nahin bat rahe," Aryaman says, low. "Toh itni acting kyun kar rahi ho? Restaurant mein tumne kaha tha ki tumhein shaadi isliye karni hai kyunki uske baad tumhein tumhaare dad ka business mil jaayega. Toh lo — ho gayi shaadi. Ye Vikram ke liye itni concern kyun dikha rahi ho? Wow. Kaafi manipulative ho tum. Kuch-kuch meri tarah. Same same, but different-different."

She studies him for one long breath. Then she turns to her father.

"Dad, sorry," she says. "Par ye log sahi keh rahe hain. Shaadi ho chuki hai meri. Meri maang mein Aryaman ke naam ka sindoor hai, dad. Toh ab yahi mera ghar hai. Aur main nahin ja sakti aapke saath."

"Teek hai," Dhawan says. "Phir tum yahin baitho. Khelo yahaan ghar-ghar. Main ja raha hoon."

"Arre sahab — Dhawan sahab, Dhawan sahab!" The elders flutter after him down the corridor, and he goes through them like weather. "Arre bhai sahab, ye toh chala gaya. Aur Naagmani bhi nahin dekhe kya?"

"Ladki ka baap hai," Parmeet says, watching him go. "Thoda wait karo. Thoda nakhra dikhaayega, phir maan jaayega."

A tray of untouched sweets stands on the corridor table, going soft in the heat, with one fly walking the rim.

Dhawan's car starts below with a roar, and the sound hangs a moment and is gone, and a dropped envelope lies by the corridor door where the elders' rush left it.

## Vikram's Vow

The hall stands half-empty under the wedding lights, and the marigold looks tired now, browning at the edges.

Vikram comes through the door with a bruise rising on his temple and his sherwani half-torn at the shoulder. The remaining crowd goes quiet the way a field goes quiet before hail.

"Vikram!" he shouts. "Teri himmat kaise hui ye sab karne ki? Tu toh mera dost hai na! Aur ye — ye meri mangetar thi!"

"Ab ye meri biwi hai," Aryaman says. "My wife."

"Par main tumse pyaar karti hoon, Vikram," Ahana says quickly. "Isne mujhse dhokhe se shaadi ki. Tumhaari aadh mein."

Vikram does not look at her. "Maine kaha tha na — ye kabhi chaahta hi nahin tha ki hamaari shaadi ho. Aur isne wahi kiya." His voice rises till the walls throw it back. "Tujhe kya lagta hai? Tu ye sab karega aur main Pinky se shaadi karunga? YOU ARE WRONG. Ab tu suffer karega. Just wait and watch, Aryaman. JUST wait and watch."

"Vikram," Ahana says.

He walks out without looking back, and the door bangs, and the hall breathes again.

"Ab jo jo ho gaya, so ho gaya," Parmeet says heavily. "Ahana Aryaman ki biwi hai. Is ghar ki bahu hai. Bilkul sahi. Tum dono dekh kya rahi ho? Bahu ko apne kamre mein le jaao."

"Arre wah! Itni jaldi kamra taiyaar bhi ho gaya," somebody says, too brightly.

The torn shoulder of Vikram's sherwani left three gold buttons on the floor by the door, scattered where no foot has touched them. The hall's big clock ticks on above the empty chairs, past five, into the wedding's wrecked evening.

## Outside Pinky's Door

The upstairs corridor runs dim behind the wedding's noise, and the evening light through the landing window falls long and amber across Pinky's shut door.

Vihaan stands in front of the door with his back to it. He has stood there since the sehra lifted — through the eruption, through the shouting, through the car that went away roaring — and he will stand there till the house sleeps or breaks.

Inside, Pinky sits on the bed with her hands in her lap. The door between them stands ajar a finger's width, because he promised her the truth and the truth needs a door it can come through.

"Bhai," she says through the gap. "Neeche kya ho raha hai? Bataao na."

"Sehra utha," he says. "Dulha Aryaman nikla."

The gap goes silent.

"Aryaman?" she asks. "Vikram kahaan hai?"

"Pata nahin," he says. "Mandap mein nahin tha. Parmeet uncle aur Baljeet uncle ne — " He stops. What he does not know, he will not build for her out of noise. "Pata nahin, Pinky. Sach yehi hai."

Another roar comes up the stairwell — Dhawan's voice, Parmeet's, the elders' — and then a car, and then quieter, meaner shouting.

"Ye Dhawan sahab ki gaadi thi?" she asks.

"Haan," he says.

"Ahana didi ruk gayin?"

"Haan," he says. "Usne kaha — yehi uska ghar hai ab."

A pause. "Aur Vikram?"

No honest answer comes to him, so he gives her the plainest thing he holds. "Vikram abhi tak saamne nahin aaya," he says. "Jab aayega, gusse mein aayega. Tum neeche nahin jaaogi. Ye meri baat hai, aur ye aakhri hai."

"Main dekhna chaahti hoon use," she whispers. "Ek baar. Bhai, please — "

"Nahin," he says, gently, and does not move from the door. "Tum use aise nahin dekhogi. Aur woh tumhein aise nahin dekhega. Jo tootna hai, ye darwaza uske beech mein rahega."

Then Vikram's voice comes up the stairwell, raised till the walls throw it back — and with it, clear as a struck bell through the floorboards, her own name.

*Pinky.*

She goes white. "Bhai," she says. "Usne mera naam liya. Kya keh raha hai woh?"

Vihaan listens to the voice below wrench itself hoarse — the refusal, the wrong, the vow — and every word of it reaches the corridor whole.

He could soften it. He could tell her Vikram spoke in anger and anger passes and mornings change men. He does none of it.

"Usne kaha," he says, steady, "ki woh tumse shaadi nahin karega. Sabke saamne kaha. Gusse mein kaha — par kaha."

The gap goes silent for a long time. He hears her breathe, once, twice, the way a person breathes when the air has turned against them.

"Theek hai," she says at last, very small.

"Pinky," he says.

"Darwaza band kar do, bhai," she says. "Aaj ke liye."

He shuts the door between them with his own hand, softly, and stands outside it with his back to the wood, and does not leave.

The amber bar from the landing window climbs the corridor wall as the sun goes, slow as a held breath. Below, the hall's noise thins by degrees into the clink of servants clearing a wedding that married the wrong man.

## Taken Up

The upstairs corridor by evening holds the wedding's tired light, and the lamps come on one by one down its length.

Dimple and Pammi bring the bride up with a small tired procession, and the house's noise stays below. Vihaan stands outside Pinky's shut door where he has stood all evening, and steps aside to let them pass.

Ahana walks between the elders with her eyes down and her shoulders shaking — the picture of the wronged bride, played to the last finger.

"Sorry, Ahana," Dimple says. "I know you are upset. Lekin beta, jo bhi hota hai na, achche ke liye hi hota hai."

"Haan," Pammi says. "Abhi tum aaraam karo."

They reach her door. Vihaan opens it for them — the house's son, his hand on the wood — and holds it while they bring her through.

"Hum — ya. Chalo. Nice," Pammi says vaguely, settling her on the bed.

For one breath, as the door swings, the bride's eyes lift — and meet his.

The distress in them is perfect. Too perfect: it sits on her face the way jewellery sits, put on for the occasion, with the true accounts kept underneath. He has read tampered pages for two years, and a performed total has a shine no true one carries.

He files it shut, with the earring answer from the morning, and lets the door close.

"Bhai sahab ke bete ne darwaza khola," Pammi murmurs to Dimple, pleased, as they turn away. "Kitna tameezdaar bachcha hai."

"Chhote sahab," the servant says, coming up with the bride's milk. "Dheere se," Vihaan says, and takes the glass from his hand and sets it inside the door himself. The servant stares after him all the way down.

The glass of milk stands inside her door, untouched, catching the lamp. The corridor's lamps burn steady now, each with its small halo, all the way to the dark stairwell.

## The Moon Night

Her room by night holds lamplight and moonlight both, and the window stands open to the full moon climbing over the city.

Ahana sits alone on the bed with the day's jewellery still on, and the girl in her goes back a year — the fire, the shouting, the ground going dark — and comes forward with her jaw set. *Khatm. Un sab ka ant shuru ho chuka hai.*

She rises and goes to the window. The moon fills it whole.

"Aaj poornima ki raat," she breathes. "Yaani meri kechuli badalne ki raat." Her hands go to the latch. "Nahin, nahin, nahin, nahin. Aaj nahin. Nahin."

The moonlight touches her arm, and the skin there prickles and shifts colour at the edge of seeing.

The door opens. Aryaman stands in it with a glass in his hand and wedding-night mischief on his face.

"Ready for first night?" he asks.

"Apni dost ki hone waali biwi se shaadi kar li," she says, turning from the window. "Jabki tum toh kisi aur ke pyaar mein the na. Kya hua uska? Kahaan hai ab woh? Shaadi ka zikr hote hi nashe mein doobe rehte the. Jeena nahin hai mujhe — yahi kehte-peete the na. Par jaise hi hot ladki saamne aayi — chaahe woh tumhaare childhood best friend ki fiancée hi kyun na ho — niyat palat gayi. Use apna mauka bana liya. Sirf date nahin — usse dhokhe se shaadi kar li. Wah. Apne dost ka dil, bharosa, mujhse shaadi karne ka sapna — sab tod diya. Itni berahmi ki. Mere saath itna bura karke bhi — soch kya rahi thi tum? Khair, meri chhodo. Apni us ex-girlfriend ka hi soch lete. Kya sochegi woh — ki maine is insaan se pyaar kiya. Aur soch ke kitna pachtaayegi. Waise naam kya tha uska? Kuch P se naam tha. Radhika — "

"Don't. Say. That. Name." Aryaman sets the glass down hard. "Uska naam mat lena."

"Radhika," she says. "Kyun? Badnaami ho jaayegi? Tumse pyaar karne ki sazaa — aur ho bhi kya sakti?"

"Shut it." His voice goes flat. "Tumhein kya lagta hai? Tum bahut doodh ki dhuli ho? Bahut mahaan ho, bahut great ho? No, madam. Tumne bhi kisi se uska pyaar chheena hai. Ek saal — ek saal se meri behen Pinky aur Vikram pyaar mein the. And I am sure tumne unke beech mein aane se pehle ek baar bhi nahin socha hoga. Bolo. Socha tha?"

"I HATE YOU," he says. "Jitni nafrat main zindagi se nahin karta, usse zyaada nafrat main tumse karta hoon."

He walks out and slams the door. She locks it behind him, and stands with her back to the wood, breathing.

The window stays open, and the moon climbs higher, and the lamp gutters once in the draught. On the dresser the milk stands untouched beside the earring's empty half, white on white.

Then the air by the window thickens — and Ananta steps out of the curtain's shadow, human-plain, her sister's face stripped of company manners.

"Ye tune kya kiya, Purvi?" Ananta asks quietly. "Tu pyaar karti hai — aur pyaar ko dafn karke Aryaman se shaadi kar li, use mitaane ke liye?"

Ahana's face does not flinch. "Maine wahi kiya hai jo sahi tha," she says. "Jo mere badle ke liye sahi tha. Apne badle ko poora karne ke liye main yahaan har ek ko mita dungi. Aryaman ko bhi."

## Radhika's Door

The street outside Radhika's house runs night-empty under the lamps, and the bottles in Aryaman's hands catch the light as he walks.

He is drunk past steadiness and short of collapse, and he throws the first bottle at her gate. It shatters.

"Radhika!" he screams at the dark windows. "Please — please mujhe chhod ke mat jaao. I LOVE YOU. I LOVE YOU SO MUCH. Tum jo bologi main karne ko taiyaar hoon. I will do ANYTHING. Please. Please don't leave me. I know you love me."

No window lights. He throws the second bottle, and the blood starts on his hand where the glass comes back.

"Magar ab tumhaare saath nahin ho sakti," he says in her voice, to himself, swaying. "Meri aadhi zindagi toh tumne barbaad kar di thi. Par jo bacha tha na — aaj maine khud barbaad kar li. Ab Aryaman poora ka poora khatm ho chuka hai. I am — " He laughs. "I am sab — khatm."

Headlights swing round the corner, too fast. The brakes scream. And a woman's hand hauls him backwards by the collar out of the road, hard enough to tear the seam, and the car goes past with its horn tearing the night.

He hangs on her shoulder, breathing paint and dust. "Radhika," he mumbles. "Tum aa gayi. Mujhe pata tha — tumhein ab bhi fikr hai."

She gets him to her car somehow, pours him into the passenger seat, and fastens his seatbelt with hands that do not shake.

The broken glass glitters across the road in the lamp-light, green and brown, in a wide fan. Her car starts on the first turn, and the headlights take the road home.

## The War Room

India's special war room runs cold and blue underground, and the big screens throw their light across the officers' faces.

"This is Bharat ka special war room," the briefing officer says. "Ye woh jagah hai jise saalon pehle hamaare scientist Vishal Puri sir ne banaaya tha. Desh ka progressive defence cell — jahaan special agents aur bade officers milkar Bharat ko apne dushmanon se bachaane ke liye preparations karte hain."

An aide dims the room's far panel, and the blue deepens across every face.

"Changistaan chup nahin baithega," another says. "Kuch na kuch plan zaroor karega. Mujhe lagta hai Changistaani Naagmani dhoondh rahe hain."

"What nonsense?" the senior officer snaps. "Main in baaton ko nahin maanta."

"Magar Changistaani maante hain, sir," the first says. "Humaare desh ki kai aisi lok-kathaaen hain jin pe Changistaani yakeen karte hain. Aur aisi kai lok-kathaaen hain jinko hum daqoosi ya mangadhant nahin keh sakte hain — kyunki hamaare desh ke itihaas mein kai aise incidents hue hain jinse ye saabit hota hai ki jab bhi — jab bhi hamaare desh ke upar koi bada sankat aaya hai, toh kisi andekhi, adrishya shakti ne hum sabko bachaaya hai. Koi toh power hai jinka zikr in lok-kathaaon mein hai. Isliye woh hamesha galat nahin hoti."

The senior officer's pen stops moving halfway down the margin, and stays stopped.

"Main sirf science ko sahi maanta hoon," the senior says. "In lok-kathaaon mein mera koi yakeen nahin hai."

"Lekin hamaare scientist Vishal Puri sir toh science ke saath-saath in baaton mein bhi yakeen rakhte the," the first says. "Don't mind, sir. Lekin aaj se 24 saal pehle unhone jo jo baatein bataayi thi, woh saari baatein sach saabit hui hain. Unhone kaha tha ki 2025 ke Mahamahakumbh mein tabaahi laane ke liye dushman hamla karega. Aur hamla hua. Lekin hum tabaahi se bach gaye — kyunki kisi anjaan shakti ne aake dushman ke hamle ko nakaam kar diya."

"Magar ye kisne kiya, ye kisi ko nahin pata," a third officer says. "Sabse badi baat toh ye hai ki us khoonkhaar aur khatarnaak jeev ko kisne maara — ye bhi koi nahin jaanta. Mujhe toh in saari cheezon ke peeche supernatural power ka haath lagta hai. Puri sir ke kuch papers bhi the. Kisi ko bhi nahin pata un papers mein kya tha. Lekin sir un papers ko hamesha apne saath rakhte the — bahut sambhaal ke. Lekin ab woh papers kahaan hain, kiske paas hain — ye koi nahin jaanta. Ab hum in papers ko dhoondh paayenge ya nahin — we don't know. Officer, aap is baare mein kya kehna chaahenge?"

Nobody looks at the pendrives. Every eye stays on the old man's empty chair at the table's head.

"Aap sahi keh rahe hain," the senior says slowly. "Isliye hum desh ki suraksha kisi ichchadhari naag-naagin aur Naagmani ke bharose nahin chhod sakte. 20 din mein hamaari Ramos missile bankar taiyaar ho jaayegi. Aur wahi banegi hamaari asli dhaal, jo dushman desh ke har vaar se humein bachaayegi. Ye paper — aur ek pendrive — aap sabke paas hai, jismein code hai jo hamaare missile banaane ka kaam poora karega."

The pendrives lie in a row on the steel table, five identical black sticks, each tagged with the same red thread, beside a tray of untouched glass cups.

Above them the big screen shows the border lake, blue and still, and a wall clock ticks the seconds away unheard.

## The Crossing

The lake between the countries lies black and flat under a storm sky, and the boat's lamp throws a small ring on the water as the landing stage's posts go by.

"Bahut kam log jaante hain," Parmeet says, standing in the prow, "ki Hindustan aur Changistaan ke beech mein bas ek badi jheel ka faasla hai. Aur har saal kisi na kisi toofaani raat ko hamaare baadshahon ka humein bulaawa aata hai — aur humein jaana padta hai. Aur is saal hum ek bahut hi wahiyaat khabar lekar ja rahe hain."

"Erul ko lagta hai ki hum Naagmani lekar aa rahe hain," Rishabh says. "Lekin hum ab khaali haath ja rahe hain. Arre, tum logon ko kuch samajh mein aa raha hai ki woh hamaare saath kya karne waala hai? Pata nahin, pata nahin ab kaun hamaara swaagat karega — Erul ke magarmachch, ya uske woh bhookhe sher."

The far shore rises out of the dark: Changistaan, coal-black, without lights.

"Ye hai hamaara desh — Changistaan," Parmeet says. "Baap aur patang ki zameen. Yahaan andhera hi andhera hai — kisi kaid-khaane jaisa. Ye desh Hindustan jaisa nahin hai. Koyle sa kaala, chhal-kapat se bhara hua hai hamaara desh. Yahaan log gareebi aur muflisi mein jeete hain. Yahaan logon ke paas paisa nahin hai. Aur jinke paas betahaasha paisa hai, unke paas insaaniyat nahin hai. Log yahaan dilon mein nafrat paale jeete hain — aur bas ek hi sapna dekhte hain. Padosi desh Hindustan ko mita dene ka sapna — jo hum poora kar sakte hain. Money — Naagmani se. Hamaare desh ki niyat jitni kaali hai, usse bhi kaali hai hamaari Pashaon ki roohein — jo maaf karna jaanti hi nahin. Kya karein? Is muflisi mein logon ko khush karne ke liye sirf do hi cheezein kaam aati hain — nafrat aur tabaahi. Magsad saaf hai. Agar hum Hindustan se jeet nahin sakte — toh kam se kam use barbaad kar dein. Mitaa kar rakh dein."

The boat's lamp gutters in the wind off the black water, and steadies.

On the far shore a single dog barks, once, and stops, and the dark takes the shore back, all but one lit door.

## Brought Home

The house by midnight stands dark except for the porch lamp, and the car comes up the drive with its headlights sweeping the gate.

Vihaan comes down the stairs in his nightclothes with one phone in his hand. The car woke him — no house car goes out on a wedding night without his knowing, and this one went out with the new bride driving.

In the porch light the driver and the chowkidar unload Aryaman from the passenger seat: drunk past standing, one hand cut and wrapped in a handkerchief, reeking of the street. The bride is nowhere — upstairs already, by the stairs' sound; a door latches above as Vihaan reaches the last step.

"Chhote sahab," the driver says, straightening. "Madam gaadi chala kar laayin. Sahab — sahab sadak par the. Gaadi — bach gaye."

Vihaan looks at his brother's cut hand, at the torn collar, at the seatbelt crease run crooked across the sherwani.

"Doctor ko mat bulao," he says. "Upar le jaao. Uske kamre mein, uski biwi ke kamre mein nahin. Paani rakh do sirhane. Aur ye haath — subah dekhte hain."

"Ji, chhote sahab," the driver says.

"Madam ne gaadi kahaan se uthaayi thi?" he asks.

"Ghar se, sahab," the driver says. "Khud nikaali. Chabiwale ko kuch nahin kaha. Wapas bhi khud laayin."

Vihaan files it with the day, shut: the bride drove at midnight, alone, and brought the groom home torn, and the house slept through all of it.

"So jaao," he says. "Subah kisi se kuch mat kehna. Ye ghar ki baat hai."

"Ji," the driver says, and they carry Aryaman up, and the porch goes quiet.

Upstairs, behind her latched door, the bride stands at the window with the moon on her face.

"Teri maut mere haathon likhi hai, Aryaman," she breathes. "Aur main tumhein itni aasaani se nahin marne dungi."

The porch lamp burns on over the empty drive, haloed with the night's first insects. The wedding's last marigold string hangs torn across the gate, swaying a finger's width in no wind at all.
