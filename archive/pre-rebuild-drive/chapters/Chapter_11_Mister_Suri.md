## Two Sisters on the Grass

The lawn behind the side veranda at half past one in the morning, with the house's lights standing in its windows and the grass wet under both of them. Sheshnaag's hood is not shown, but the lawn feels like a temple that has forgotten its god.

Purvi gets her elbow under her and sits up, and her sister's hands stay on her shoulders.

"Ek baar aur bata." Ananta's voice is very quiet. "Dhyan se. Jo tune dekha."

"Ravish sir aur ek aurat." Purvi's mouth is dry. "Side veranda pe. Usne mujhe dekha aur bola — kaam hai, meri life hai, meri marzi."

Ananta sits back on her heels and looks at the lit windows of her own engagement.

"Tu yahin baith." She gets up and pulls her dupatta straight. "Main abhi aati hoon."

"Deep—"

"Baith, Purvi."

Purvi sits, and the grass is wet through her salwar, and her sister's heels go across the lawn toward the side of the house, and stop once, and go on.

Ananta does not look back. That is the thing Purvi will remember about the walk: her sister does not look back at her, and does not slow down, and does not put her dupatta over her head, and goes round the corner of the house the way a woman goes into a room to settle something small.

Purvi gets up to follow, and her heel goes out from under her on the wet stone edging of the lawn.

A hand is at her elbow before her knee reaches the ground, and it takes her weight and puts her back upright, and it is on her elbow a beat after that, and then it is not.

Vihaan is standing there with his phone in his other hand and a call going on in it.

"Grass geeli hai." He says it to the lawn. "Andar se aaiye. Side door se."

"Sir, main theek hoon—"

Vihaan takes the phone away from his ear and says one word into it, and puts it in his pocket. He smiles small and private at the ended call, thumb steady on the phone's edge, then looks up at her.

"Maine poochha nahin ki aap theek hain." He takes her by the elbow, thumb steady on her arm, hand warm through the cotton of her kurta, and he walks her round the wet stone toward the side door at his own pace. "Maine kaha andar se aana hai. To andar se aayengi."

"London—"

"London chhod dijiye." He opens the side door with his free hand. "London ko pata hai ki wo kab intezaar kare."

At the door he lets go of her elbow and stands back and lets her go in first, and he looks at her back for a moment longer than he needs to, and only then takes the phone out again.

"Haan." He is already talking into it. "Jahan chhoda tha."

Purvi stands on the edge of the lawn in the wet grass with her dupatta in her hand. She smiles small and wondering at the door he held for her, at the man who held it. Then she goes back and sits down where her sister told her to sit, and stays there.

## He Goes to Say It for Her

The veranda at half past one, with one bulb on over the door and the last of the guests' cars going out of the gate.

A tray of glasses stands on the parapet with one glass left on it, and the ice in it has gone to water.

Vihaan comes out of the door behind them and stays in it with his hand on the frame and does not come down the steps.

Ravish is at the far end of the veranda with his back to the wall and his glass in his hand.

Ananta comes round the corner and stands in front of him, and she is smiling, and it takes a moment to see that the smile is a thing she has put on for the walk over.

"Ravish." She keeps her voice down. "I am really sorry. Main tumse aisi baat poochhne ja rahi hoon."

"Poochho."

"Purvi ko lagta hai ki usne tumhein kisi ladki ke saath dekha." She looks at his face the whole time. "Mujhe pata hai ki tum mujhe dhokha nahi de sakte. Tum dhokha de hi nahi sakte mujhe. Haina?"

Ravish drinks.

"Maine kaha tha na, Purvi." He says the name the way a man says the name of a servant who has broken something. "Tujhe galatfahmi hui hai." He looks past her, at the corner where Purvi is not standing. "Ye maanti hi nahi hai. To tum hi maan jao na."

"Ravish."

"Tumhari behen galat nahi keh rahi hai."

Ananta's hand comes up to her own throat.

"Main sach mein ek ladki ke saath tha." He turns the glass. "Iska matlab ye nahi ki maine kabhi tumhein chaha nahi."

## It Is Fun With You

The far end of the veranda at the wall, in the light that comes out of the French windows and lies on the tiles behind them.

A dish of ashtrays stands on the parapet, and every ashtray in it is full, and the dish has not been emptied all evening.

Vihaan comes down the veranda for the dish of ashtrays, and he takes one look at the two of them at the wall, and he leaves the dish where it is and goes back in without a word.

Ravish finishes his glass and puts it on the wall beside him, and the ice shifts in it, and he does not answer for long enough that Ananta's smile starts to go.

"Ravish." She says his name a second time, and this time it is a question.

"Haan." He wipes his mouth with the back of his hand. "Main sun raha hoon."

The music from the hall comes round the corner of the house in pieces.

"I like you." He says it to the lit window and not to her. "Kaafi tez tarar thi wo. It was fun with you."

Ananta does not move.

"Lekin tab tak — jab tak tum ye TV-shivi banne ke serious zone mein nahi aayi thi." He drinks the last of it. "I loved your company. Magar ab — you cheat."

"Main cheat—"

"Mera pyaar tumhare liye khel tha." He sets the glass down on the arm of a chair. "Tumne time pass kiya."

"Maine tumse pyaar kiya." Her voice goes up and she brings it back down. "Maine tumse pyaar kiya, aur tumne — time pass kiya? Time pass kiya?"

"Shaadi wo tumhari behen ke saath." He says it without lifting the glass. "Aur tumhare saath — I am done with."

"Ananta—"

"Ek number ke gire hue aur ghatiya insaan ho tum." Her chin is up. "Pehle meri behen ko pyaar mein phansaya, aur uske baad shaadi ke baad se muh kar rahe ho."

Ravish smiles at that, and it is the smile of a man who has heard the sentence before and knows how it ends.

## The Hand

The veranda at half past one, in the light out of the French windows, with the hall's noise coming through them.

Her hand comes up and it comes fast.

Aryaman's hand is there before it lands, and it closes round her wrist, and holds.

"Ananta."

"Chhodo—"

"Ananta." He does not raise his voice. "Andar. Sab log andar hain."

Ananta goes, because he is holding her wrist and because the door is the only way out of that corner. The room inside has the family in it. Parmeet by the sideboard, Dimple on the sofa with her hands in her lap, Tina's father in the good chair, Arjun by the curtains, Karan standing, Vihaan at the edge of the carpet with a glass he has not raised, and Tina at the edge of it all with her plate still in her hand.

"Kya hua?" Parmeet says it without moving. "Aryaman, kya hai?"

Aryaman lets go of the wrist.

Ananta pulls her dupatta straight and stands in the middle of the room and says it to the whole of them at once.

"Ravish ne mere saath cheat kiya." Her voice carries. "Main aur Ravish relationship mein the. Aur ab ye mujhse peecha chhudana chahta hai — kyunki main iske bacche ki maa banne waali hoon."

The room does nothing. The music from the hall goes on through the wall, one room away, with a dholak in it.

Dimple's hands come together in her lap and stay there. Tina's mother says *hai ram* under her breath and looks at her husband, and her husband looks at the carpet. One of the men by the sideboard puts his glass down on the tray and takes his hand away from it and puts both hands behind his back, and the tray is a boy's, and the boy stands still with the tray because nobody has told him what a boy with a tray does in a room this quiet.

Karan has not moved since the door opened. He is looking at his brother, and his face has nothing in it at all.

"Kya kaha tune?" Parmeet's glass comes down on the sideboard.

"Kya ye sach hai, chhote?" Aryaman turns round. "Jhooth mat bolna mujhse."

"All the damage." Ravish spreads his hands. "Ye sab drama hai."

"Aryaman." Ravish's voice changes. "Aap iski baat ka kyun vishwas kar rahe hain? Maine kuch nahi kiya." He steps closer. "Aap kiski side le rahe ho?"

"Main kisi ki side nahi le raha." Aryaman does not step back. "Main decide kar raha hoon ki saza kise deni hai."

## Chaar Saal

The veranda at a quarter to two, with the light of the hall lying in a long bar on the tiles and the party thinning behind it.

A chair has been knocked back against the wall and nobody has set it up again.

On the parapet somebody's dupatta lies where it fell, and the wind has worked one end of it over the edge.

"Ye ladki chaar saal se humare office mein kaam kar rahi hai." Aryaman says it to his brother. "Main jaanta hoon ise. Aur main ye bhi jaanta hoon ki ye aisa jhooth to nahi bol sakti."

"Aryaman—"

"To agar mujhe pata chala ki tune ise phansaya hai, ya iska galat faayda uthaya hai—" He lets it sit. "I swear to God. Main bhool jaunga ki tu mera bhai hai."

Ravish looks at his father, and his father is looking at the sideboard.

"Tina." Vihaan is at the door with the handle in his hand and his shoulders squared, his jaw tight and his thumb white on the door handle, his breath steady and slow, and he has to say her name twice before his voice comes out the way he wants it. "Aap log bahar aa jaiye. Photographer wait kar raha hai."

Tina's mother is up before her husband is. "Haan, haan, chalo." She takes her husband's sleeve.

Tina does not move.

"Tina." Vihaan says it again, quietly, from the door. "Aap bhi."

"Main yahin hoon." She has not put her plate down. "Meri engagement hai."

Vihaan looks at her plate, still in her hand, and then looks away. The two of them look at each other, and then the guests go out past him into the hall, all of them except one, and he closes the door on the party and comes into the room and stands at the wall by the sideboard, and stays there.

The room has gone into the shape rooms go into. The women are together on the sofa side and the men are together by the sideboard, and nobody has arranged it, and it takes about four seconds, and the girl who has just said the thing is standing alone in the middle of the carpet with the light on her.

"Aryaman—" she starts.

"Ruk." He holds one hand up without looking at her. "Main bol raha hoon."

"Don't forget." Aryaman's jaw is tight. "Dhokha bardasht nahi hai mujhe. I hate cheaters."

"Tu Ananta se shaadi kar raha hai." Parmeet says it from the sideboard, flatly, into the silence. "That's final."

"No." Ravish turns on him. "Dad—"

"That's final."

"Dad, ye big kya keh rahe hain?" He points at Aryaman without looking at him. "Jabardasti meri is ladki se shaadi karva rahe hain? Wo ladki jo mere saath two-timing kar rahi thi?"

## The Letter

The veranda at a quarter to two, and the light of the hall comes out through the French windows and stops on the tiles halfway to the parapet.

"Mere saath-saath wo kisi aur ke saath bhi thi." Ravish's hands are open now, and reasonable. "Mujhe pata chala. Main peeche hat gaya. Isko takleef hone lag gayi — bardasht nahi kar pa rahi ye."

"Ramesh bhai sach keh rahe hain." Arjun steps out from the curtains. "Ye ladki mujh pe bhi interested hai. Aur maine ise teen-chaar baar mana bhi kiya." He shakes his head, patient, wounded. "Phir bhi ye mujhe message karti rehti hai."

Parmeet puts his hand out.

Arjun gives him the phone, and Parmeet holds it up in the middle of the room, and reads what is on the screen in a flat voice, and the flatness is the worst part of it.

"Hello meri jaan. I love you. Main tumse bahut pyaar karti hoon. Agar tum kuch dinon ke liye mere saath raho, to tum mujhe jaan jaaoge aur pehchan jaaoge. Tab decide karna ki tum mujhe pyaar karte ho ki nahi."

The boy with the tray has gone out. Somebody in the hall has turned the music down, or the song has ended; either way the wall between the party and this room is thin now.

Tina's father clears his throat once and does not say anything.

Dimple's eyes are on her hands.

"Ye message mujhe isne likhvaya tha." Ananta's voice is steady. "Kisi ladki ko bhejne ke liye."

"Kya?"

"Dekha? Suna aap logon ne?" She turns round the room. "Koi sense banta hai iski baaton ka? Pehle isne khud mujhe message bheja, aur ab keh rahi hai ki maine tumse likhvaya kisi aur ko bhejne ke liye."

"Meri didi sahi keh rahi hain." Purvi is in the doorway now, beside the young man with the bag, and she does not care that she is in it. "Ye dono mil kar meri didi ko badnaam karna chahte hain. Sir, ye galat hai. Galat aapka bhai hai."

By the sideboard, Vihaan is not looking at Purvi and not at his brother. He is looking at his cousin, and his hands have gone still at his sides in a way that costs him something.

## Fark Nahi Padta

The far corner of the hall at two in the morning, with the chandelier still on and half the floor already taken up.

A plate of sweets stands on the sideboard with the foil still on it, and nobody has opened it.

A chair lies on its side by the door where somebody went over it, and nobody has picked it up.

"Chup kar, ladki." Parmeet does not turn his head for her. "Dekho, tum humare office ki stylish—"

"Parmeet." Dimple says the name once, and it is not the name she uses for him in this house.

"Fark nahi padta." He puts the phone down on the sideboard beside his glass. "Main apne bete ki shaadi tumhare saath kara deta, agar tumhara character sahi hota. Jo ki nahi hai." He puts his hands together behind his back. "Isliye main apne bete ki shaadi tumhare saath kara ke uski life barbaad nahi karunga. Samjhi tum?"

Vihaan says something from the door of the room, and Parmeet does not say chup — he uses him. "Vihaan. Baithe. Tumhe jo pata hai, batao." The room turns. Vihaan does not sit, but he speaks, and this time the room catches it.

Purvi's chin comes up.

"Aisi ladkiyon ko main achhi tarah se pehchanta hoon." He looks at the room, and the room is his. "Main Ravish ke saath kuch galat hone nahi dunga."

"Par galat to mere saath ho raha hai."

"Tum kyun phadphada rahi ho?" He almost smiles. "Main achhi tarah se jaanta hoon. Paise nahi mile na — isliye girebaan tak pahunch gayi."

"Parmeet ji." Dimple again.

"Papa."

It is one word, out of the wall by the sideboard, and it is not loud, and every person in that room hears it. He is the only person in that room whose voice has not moved all evening, and the calm in it is not calm; it is the thing in him that has never once in twenty-four years let a room see him hurry.

Parmeet does not turn round.

"Ae ladki." He comes across the carpet. "Dhyan se meri baat sun. Tum mere office ki staff ho." He stops in front of her. "Harassment ka case kar dunga tum par. Isliye behtar yahi hoga — yahan se chupchaap nikal jao." He lifts his hand at the door. "Get out from here."

Nobody in the room moves. Tina's father studies the ceiling of a room he is a guest in.

## Mister Suri

The hall at two in the morning, with the chandelier still on and the guests standing where the shouting began.

Purvi does not go to the door. She stands where she is and puts her hands together in front of her, and her voice comes out of her chest and not her throat.

"Agar aap logon ko lagta hai ki aap log yoon cheekhenge, chillayenge mujh par, aur main dar jaaungi —" She looks at Parmeet. "You are wrong, Mister Suri."

The room is very still, and she hears her own voice in it and does not stop.

"Aapke bete ki asliyat hai. Main saari media ko bataungi." She takes one step into the room. "Is ghar ke bete ne mere saath kya kiya hai — ye main sabko bataungi."

Her hands are shaking and she holds them together in front of her so that they will not be seen shaking, and it is a small thing and it is the bravest thing in the room.

The skin of her forearms is hot. There is a taste in her mouth that is not any food she has eaten that day, and the corner of her jaw itches, and her hand cannot reach the place it itches.

She scratches the corner of her jaw once, and a flake of the khaal comes away under her nail, thin and dry and the size of a fingernail, and she closes her hand on it and puts her hand in her pocket and leaves it there.

Nobody in the room is looking at her hands except the boy by the wall, who is looking at nothing else.

She turns and goes, and Ananta goes with her, and they take their dupattas off the chair by the door, and the handkerchiefs they had in their hands come out with them and fall on the carpet by the sideboard, and neither of them knows it.

"Bahar nikal." Parmeet says it without getting up. "Aur ye yaad rakh." He puts his glass down. "Jo aadmi is ghar ke saamne khada ho kar is ghar ka naam bolta hai, wo apna naam bhool jaata hai."

Parmeet watches them go. Then he bends down and picks both handkerchiefs up off the floor and puts them in his trouser pocket, one after the other, unhurried, and looks at the cloth of the second one for a moment in the light of the sideboard.

In the doorway, Vihaan sees him do it.

He sees it the way a man sees a car come home at two in the morning: the fact, plainly, and nothing to be done with it now.

"Tina." Vihaan says it from the wall. "Chaliye."

This time she goes. She sets the plate down on the sideboard first, and she sets it down carefully, the way a person sets down a thing they have held for a long time, and she goes out through the door he holds open for her, and he goes with her, and the door shuts.

In the hall, at the foot of the stairs, out of the hearing of the room, she stops and puts her hand flat against the wall.

"Aapko pata tha?" she says.

"Nahi."

"Aapke bhai ke baare mein." She is not crying. "Aapko pata tha, ya nahi?"

"Nahi."

She looks at him a moment longer than is comfortable, and then she goes up the stairs to the room they have given her, and her heels go all the way to the top and stop.

He lets out a breath he has been holding since the sideboard, and it comes out shaking, and he puts his hand over his mouth for a second, and then takes it away.

Vihaan stands at the foot of the stairs with his hand on the newel post, and then he goes into the study and closes the door.

Parmeet is at the sideboard with the decanter and does not turn round.

"Aapne unke rumaal uthaye." Vihaan says it from the door. "Dono. Aur jeb mein daale."

"Tum bhi dekhte rahe."

"Main dekh raha tha ki mera baap kya kar raha hai."

Parmeet pours, and sets the decanter down, and turns round with the glass in his hand. "Ek ladki is ghar mein aayi aur mere bete ko chor, badmaash, aur kya-kya bola. Tum uske liye khade ho."

"Main uske liye nahin khada tha." Vihaan comes to the middle of the room and stops. "Main uske liye khada tha jis din woh kaanch pe ghutnon pe thi. Aur aaj main is liye khada hoon ki aap ek aisi cheez jeb mein daal rahe hain jo aapke bete ne ki hai."

"Nikal jao."

"Nahin."

The glass comes down on the sideboard hard enough to ring.

"Yeh ghar mera hai."

"Haan." Vihaan does not raise his voice and does not move. "Aur is ghar ka kharcha mera hai. Chaubees lakh mahina, Papa. Bijli, guards, gaadiyan, woh teen jo aapne pichhle mahine rakhe, aur us shaadi ka tent jiske liye aapne mujhe bataya bhi nahin tha." He puts one hand flat on the back of the chair. "Main hisaab rakhta hoon. Aap khabar rakhte ho."

Parmeet looks at him for a long moment, and then he laughs, and the laugh has no kindness in it.

"Tum apne baap se paise ki baat kar rahe ho."

"Main aapse kaam ki baat kar raha hoon." Vihaan pulls the chair out and sits down in it, which nobody in that house does in front of Parmeet. "Kal subah do cheezein hongi. Woh ladki media mein jaayegi, ya nahin jaayegi. Aur woh jo bhi karegi, use Aryaman ke naam se hoga, is ghar ke naam se hoga, aur channel ke naam se hoga. Us channel mein London ka paisa hai. Mera."

"Toh apna paisa nikaal lo."

"Main nikaal lunga." He says it quietly. "Aur phir aapke paas yeh ghar bachega, yeh lawn bachegi, aur woh teen gaadiyan. Bas."

The clock in the passage goes on ticking. Parmeet drinks, and puts the glass down, and sits down at last in the chair opposite.

"Tum kya chahte ho?"

"Do cheezein." Vihaan's hands are on the chair's arms. "Pehli, is ghar mein jo bhi kaagaz hai — channel ka, mera, Kumbh ka — woh kal se is ghar mein nahin rahega. Main apne aadmi bhejunga. Aap kis ko kuch nahin bologe."

"Aur doosri?"

"Doosri." He leans back. "Aap mujhe agli baar batayenge jab aap is ghar mein kisi ko rakhte hain. Koi bhi. Naukar, mehmaan, sadhu, sipahi."

"Yeh ghar—"

"Yeh ghar aapka hai, Papa." Vihaan gets up. "Isliye main aapse poochh raha hoon. Agli baar main poochhunga nahin."

Vihaan goes to the door and stops with his hand on it.

"Aur woh rumaal." He does not turn round. "Aap unhe jeb mein rakhiye. Main unhe kabhi nahin maangunga."

The door shuts. Parmeet sits with his glass and the clock, and on the other side of the door his son stands in the dark passage for a while with his hand still on the handle.

That night Vihaan does not go up. He sits in the dining room with the light on and the laptop open, and at two in the morning Verma gets four lines from him: *Kal nau baje teen aadmi bhejo. Saara kaagaz. Channel, Kumbh, contract. Ghar se nikalna hai. Parmeet sahab ko kuch nahin batana.* And then a fifth line, which Verma reads twice: *Aur ek register banwao. Har aadmi jo is ghar mein aata hai, naam, kaam, time. Chaubees ghante.*

## Johro

The lawn at the back of the house has its lights off, and the moon is up over the wall, one night off full and going down towards the ridge, and it is the only light on the grass.

The generator is running somewhere behind the wall, and the men from the hall stand at the edge of the lawn with their glasses, keeping their voices down, and the glasses are in a row on the wall where they have set them.

A bucket stands by the wall in the dark with a cloth over the top of it.

The marigold in it is going brown at the edges, and nobody has moved it since the morning.

Parmeet comes out and stands at the dark edge of it and says a name.

A shape comes out of the black beyond the wall and stands at the edge of the light, and it is big, and it stands very still, and it does not move the way a dog moves.

"Mere bacche." Parmeet's hand goes out and rests on the animal's neck. "Aaj tere dad ko teri zaroorat hai. In dono ladkiyon ko tujhe sambhalna hai."

The men at the edge of the lawn have stopped talking among themselves, and one of them is looking at his own shoes, and one of them cannot stop looking at the animal.

One of them takes his glass away from his mouth and comes closer.

"Bhai sahab, agar us ladki ne Ravish ke baare mein sabko bata diya — ya sach mein wo media ke paas chali gayi — to hamari izzat ki dhajjiyan ud jayengi." He looks at the house. "Aur log Suri family ke baare mein sab kuch jaanna chahenge. Yahan tak ki hamara har chhupa raaz bhi."

"Uski naubat nahi aayegi." Parmeet's hand stays on the animal. "Wo ek kutta nahi hai." He says the name of it again, quietly, and the animal's head comes round. "Wo un dono ladkiyon ki khushbu soongh kar unhein dhoondh nikaalega. Aur dono ko thikaane laga dega."

"Aur shak?"

"Ek jaanwar jab attack karega," Parmeet says, "to hum par koi shak nahi karega."

## Ananta's Vow

The gate at the front of the house is shut, and its light is on over the pillar, and the man at it is sitting on his stool with his radio off.

Ananta stands on the wrong side of it with her sister and looks back at the house, at the marigolds, at the lit windows of the room where they put a ring on her hand an hour and a half ago.

Ananta puts her hand flat on her own stomach, on the cloth, where nobody in that house has been allowed to put a hand all evening.

"Suno, Parmeet Suri." She says it at full voice, across the road, at the lit windows. The guard's head comes up off his radio. "Tum sun rahe ho? Tumhara beta sun raha hai."

"Ananta—"

"Main kasam khaati hoon." Her voice cracks in the middle of it and she goes on anyway, and her sister's hand closes round her wrist. "Is bacche ki kasam khaati hoon. Is ghar ki chhat ke neeche jo bhi baithkar mere baare mein bola hai — ek ek ka naam, ek ek ka chehra, main sabke saamne rakhoongi."

Purvi's thumb moves once on her wrist.

"Tumne mere bacche ko mera nahi kaha. Theek hai. Tumne mere bacche ko tumhara bhi nahi kaha." Her chin is up and her eyes are dry. "Main tumhare ghar ka naam mitaungi. Nahi chhodungi. Bilkul nahi."

The guard has his radio in his hand and does not turn it on. Somewhere on the first floor a door shuts, and the marigold hanging off the gate moves, and does not come back the way it went.

"Un sabka pardafaash karungi," Ananta says, quieter now, to Purvi and not to the house. "Nahi chhodungi. Bilkul bhi nahi."

Behind them the study window on the second floor is the only light in the house that is not a party light, and it stays lit while they stand there, and nothing in it moves.

## The Account

The lane at twenty past eleven, with the lamp at the corner on and a dog lying under it with its chin on its paws.

A stool stands by the gate with a glass on it and a spoon beside it, and neither of them has been washed since the morning.

A boy on a cycle comes up the lane with a packet from the chemist's and stops at the Aroras' gate at twenty past eleven, and Ananta signs for it because their mother is asleep and it is her inhaler and the old one has two puffs left in it.

"Kitne ke hain?" Ananta is already getting her purse out.

The boy looks at the slip in his hand and turns it over.

"Account se hai, madam." He shrugs. "Har mahine. Paisa nahin lena hai."

"Kis account se?"
