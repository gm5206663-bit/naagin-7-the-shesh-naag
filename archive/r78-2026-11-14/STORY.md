# STORY — the laws, the names, the powers

**Owns: what this story is made of: its laws, how people and things are named on the page, and what the powers of this world are and are not.** People are in `foundation/CHARACTERS.md`. Dates are in `foundation/TIMELINE.md`.

---

## 1 · The laws

1. **Canon is the ground, not the cage** (`O020`). The style is canon's completely. The story is this serial's. Where his existence in this world makes something go differently, it goes differently, and a new event may be added when it is needed and when it follows from what the episodes have already shown. A canon scene that is played is played fully and never summarised; nothing contradicts what the episodes show.
2. **He is one more person, not a new centre.** The show's lead is its lead. In a canon scene he is present only if he would be, and then as what he is in that room: a brother, a colleague, a guest.
3. **Change needs a cause on the page.** If something goes differently because he is there, the reader sees why. Nothing moves by luck, and nothing moves because the story wants it: a change is the consequence of something the reader has already seen. The reverse also holds: a scene is not bent back to its canon result when the cause for change is already on the page.
4. **No one borrows knowledge.** `foundation/WORLD_STATE.md` §1.
5. **The show's world is the whole world.** No creature, power, rule or organisation the episodes do not have (`O013`). Where the show is silent, the prose stays as small as it can and the footer says what was authored.
6. **The show's moral weight stays.** Her revenge kills people, some of them cruelly, and the show means it. The villains are funny and frightening at once. Faith is real in this world: Mahadev answers.
7. **The reader may be ahead.** The show tells its viewer things its people do not know and makes drama of the gap. So does this.
8. **His power is last.** For the whole first arc he is a man among naagins and killers with nothing but his wits and his decency.

**The pull, and the two pairs of eyes (`O033`).** The attraction is in the room from the first look, in both of them, before any awakening and before any truth (`canon/STYLE_CANON.md` §1g). And the world and the pair do not see the same thing, and both are right: to that house **Purvi is not a beautiful girl** — the plain one in spectacles, next to a sister everybody turns for — and to **Vihaan** she is beautiful at once, and more than beautiful, *something special* he has no word for. Her eyes on him are the other half: the world already finds him beautiful, so what she sees that nobody else does is not his face but the quiet in him that frightens her. **Nothing in the book corrects the gap** — no makeover, no compliment from the room, no narration taking a side — and it is shown one device to a chapter, never announced.

**The pair, in titles (`O023`).** Canon gives the girl four in one breath: *Shiv vardani. Queen of Naaglok. Naagrani of the Anant kul. Ichchhadhari naagin. Shesh Naagin* (C, Ep 1 22:04, as Tushar Sinha speaks it). Season 7 gives the boy their counterpart, and he is never called by any of it on the page: **Nagaraj of the Anant kul, Sheshnaag, King of Naaglok, Anantveer** — the name itself carrying the kul's name, and almost nobody in the world knowing it (`O032`). **Nagaraj of the Anant kul · Sheshnaag · King of Naaglok**. Two titles face each other, and the kul they belong to is **new** — the old Anant kul vanished and nobody knew where it went (C, Ep 6 02:56). Whatever the *original* Sheshveer ended as stays unknown: these titles are the new one's, and an echo says nothing about the voice that made it (`foundation/SHESHVEER.md` §3).

**What stands between them, and why it is not a rival (`O029`).** The destiny that binds Naagrani to Nagaraj names **Veer, King of Naaglok** — and the name is Vihaan's, which nobody in this story knows, **including him** (`O021`, `O022`: the memory never returns). Two consequences, and they are one:

- **The rival is the mask.** Purvi is meant for a king she has never met and never will meet, because the king is the man standing in front of her. Every scene in which somebody speaks of *the Nagaraj* — Uttara, the temple, the kul's law — is a scene in which the obstacle is described to her and she cannot see it. Nobody may join the two names: not in a character's mouth, not in the narration. The reader holds the prophecy from Chapter 1 and watches.
- **The law forbids what destiny requires.** Canon's law is that a naagin may not love a human, and punishment for it is heavy; and the Anant kul's curse lifts only when the Naagrani marries a **naag**. To almost everyone, Vihaan is human — so the kul reads her love as the transgression, and destiny as the leash that will pull her away from him toward a faceless king. **Both of them are wrong, and neither can be corrected by an argument.**

**And the four things that follow from it (`O030`), which are the drama:**

1. **Nobody tries to marry her to the king, because there is no king to marry.** He is unawakened and unknown; the law exists without a bridegroom. So the law lands on the human instead — **the kul opposes her love for Vihaan** — and the opposition plays as family, custom and fear, never as a scheme explained to the reader.
2. **The pair together cannot be beaten.** That is a fact of this world, not a boast. So every power that intends to win has to keep them apart — and the cheapest way to keep them apart is to let the world go on believing he is human. The enemy's strongest weapon is a truth that is already standing in the room.
3. **Those who know will know when he awakens.** The awakening cannot be hidden from an insider and cannot be faked to one.
4. **A disguise is possible.** Somebody wearing the king's name or face is exactly this show's kind of turn, and it is dangerous because the true king cannot answer a false one: he does not know what he is.
5. **Almost nobody knows (`O031`, `O032`).** His name is **Anantveer**, and the short form belongs to almost no one. **Parmeet does not know. Nobody in that house knows.** So the house is full of unwitting help — the chairs, the cartons, the file, the wedding — and the man building the country's ruin is right by accident, which is worse than being right on purpose. The law of `O031` still stands: an enemy who does not know the full truth cannot be relied on to work against the pair. **So the one who knows, if there is one, comes from outside that house — and that is `OPEN-18`, open.**

What this forbids on the page: a reveal thrown to clear the air; a villain who knows and says it; a scene where Vihaan announces himself to win her; and any narration that reassures the reader that the destiny and the love are the same man. The relief, when it comes, must cost something that has been built for chapters.

**What Vihaan was (`O021`).** In his last life he was **Sheshveer**, a Sheshnaag of the old line: **Maha Naag Raj in his own right** (`O022`), keeper of the **Naagmani**, golden of hood, five-hooded. He is that being reborn, with no memory of it. Canon's own words hold the door open: the assembly of Episode 1 says "the line of Sheshnaag has ended", and the professor answers that a line with Vishnu's hand on it can never end (C, Ep 1 09:43); and the highest of the hundred-year naag-naagins are called **Mahashesh Naagraj** and **Mahanagrani** (C, Ep 5 24:14). Nothing of this is on the page before his awakening, and none of it is known to him.

**Other things the show has**

A Yeti; a wolf that survived Pragati; a Garud guarding the secret temple; a tantrik's barrier kept by dogs; lions, tigers, crocodiles and a shark at the villains' call; spirits of the dead who speak to the living; a sleeping dragon named Bahamut. All from recaps; each is read in the captions before it is written.

**What the show does not have**

Training that makes a human a naag. Levels, ranks or numbers. Spells. Anyone learning a power by watching. Technology that explains the naagins.

## 4 · Scale

There is no ladder to climb in this world and none is to be built. Strength is shown by what happens: a train stopped, a dragon brought down, a man who cannot lift a bag. He begins at the bottom of it, as a man, and stays there until the story gives him a cause to be anything else (`foundation/ADAPTATION_MODULE.md` §6).

## 5 · His Talent

`foundation/ADAPTATION_MODULE.md` owns it. In the story it has no name and no voice, and nobody is shown a cause. Before the awakening it has no power to show. What it shows is the man: he learns the control room in an afternoon, he is easy in his body, people look twice when he comes in. The people in the room notice and let it go.
