# HOUSE HISTORY — the twenty-four years in that house

**Owns: what happened between Vihaan and every person under that roof, from the night in 2001 to the morning he lands — the incidents, the nicknames, the debts and the jokes — and how any of it is allowed to reach the page.** `RELATIONSHIPS.md` owns the ties as they stand today; this page owns the *history* under them, because a relationship with no past reads as stage business. Everything here is authored (**A**) except where a canon tag appears, and it is all the author's to overrule.

**Why this file exists.** The author, 2026-11-11: *"there is more things needed for natural about things, like for example how Arjun and Karan like others history and relationship with him and others All many things, you should know, you can't just fix things only do them there is could you needed to degine everything when needed."* A son who has been in that house for twenty-four years has a childhood in it, and the reader should feel it in the way people talk to him — not read it in a paragraph.

## 1 · What the house says he is

The world's version, in the house's own words if a guest asks: **Nimrat's younger boy. The one who was in London. He runs their work there.**

The cover family, as everyone in Delhi understands it:

| Person | The house's story | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| **"Parmeet Suri"** | Of Attari; a news family; a widower | An impostor from the enemy state; killed the real Parmeet |
| **Nimrat Suri** | His wife; died when the children were small | The real Parmeet's wife; killed that night |
| **Aryaman** | The adopted elder son | The scientist's son, taken for a locker (R7) |
| **Pinky** | Their daughter | The impostor's own (C, Ep 1) |
| **Ravish** | Their elder son | The impostor's own (R13) |
| **Vihaan** | **Nimrat's younger boy** — the son the mother did not live to raise | The real Suris' son, kept (`O001`) |
| **Karan, Arjun, Nisha** | Baljeet's children | Baljeet's (R13) |

**The one thing this buys the story.** The house needs a boy who looks like Parmeet Suri standing beside the name Parmeet Suri. His face is not a risk to the cover; **it is the cover's proof** — which is why the resemblance (`OPEN-18`) can never be spoken aloud by anyone in that house, and why he was still sent away: not to hide the face, but to keep the boy from meeting the men who remember the man.

**`OPEN-19` — for the author, because it is plot-grade:** canon gives "Parmeet" one own son, Ravish (R13). This serial gives him a second in Vihaan, presented as Nimrat's. Two sons by one dead woman is the reading this file uses, and it costs nothing in canon — but it is his to allow or to change (an adopted cousin, a nephew, another mother).

## 4 · Each of them, and him

**"Parmeet" — the father.** Distant, correct, generous with money and never with words. He has never raised a hand to him and has never once said he is proud of him. He gives him work when work is needed, and a wall when the family is in the room. **What Vihaan believes:** my father is hard because he lost my mother; I have to earn him. **What is true:** he is a thing kept in good condition, and useful lately. **What he does with it later:** `OPEN` — the author's.

**Aryaman — the brother.** The only one who has never made him small on purpose. He carried him at five, drove him to the airport at thirteen, and puts him down in public now — *"then go and hold it"* — because if he does not, Karan will do it worse. **What Vihaan believes:** he is the best of us and does not know it. **What he does not know:** that Aryaman is not of the blood either, and that both of them have been standing in the same lie from different sides.

**Ravish — the brother who should have been the son.** Warm, loud, first to say "my brother". He took a beating for Vihaan at seven, stopped writing at fifteen, and will kill for money at thirty. **What Vihaan believes:** he is the best-loved and the least liked, and it is not his fault. **What he does not know:** what Ravish is, and what relief Ravish will feel on the wedding night when the truth is told.

**Karan — the cousin who cannot forgive a photograph.** His mother is cold to Vihaan and his needle is always the same: *London-returned*, *the pretty one*, *sahib*. He borrows and does not return. **What Vihaan believes:** it is affection with a bad temper on it. **What is true:** it is envy that has had fourteen years to set.

**Arjun — the one who listens.** He tells Vihaan everything and then tells Karan the same. He owes him a school exam at sixteen and pays it in small coin. **What Vihaan believes:** the kindest of that side of the family, and weak.

**Pinky — the sister who raised him more than anyone.** She slapped him at seventeen and cried for an hour; she brought him home at twenty-four. She orders him about because ordering is how she loves. **What Vihaan believes:** nobody in that house is on his side like her. **What is true:** she does not yet know that she will have to choose.

**Nisha — the teasing one.** She named him Viju at four years old and has never used anything else. She records things, which nobody in that family has noticed yet.

**Baljeet — the uncle who never says his name.** *Beta*, always. He taught him chess in one afternoon and never played again. He arranged the London post to move his own man out of it — no, to move his man *in*, and Vihaan was the instrument that made it respectable. **What Vihaan believes:** a quiet, fair man.

**Dimple — the one who said the sentence.** She has not used his name in twenty years. She killed her way to money and will run Tina off a cliff for it. **What Vihaan believes:** she does not like him, and he has decided not to mind.

**Pammi — the aunt who is kind for money.** She has been warm to him since he was a child, and the warmth has a price, and he has known the price since he was eleven and paid it without minding.

**The staff.** The driver who taught him the motorcycle and was dismissed. The cook who kept a plate aside for him when he was late, and still does. The guard at the gate who has known him since he was a child and is the only person in that house who calls him by his own name without a title.

## 5 · What he does not know, in this history's terms

- He believes **Nimrat** was his mother and that she died of an illness when he was small. He has never seen a photograph of her that he knows is her.
- He believes the **letter** from Ravish stopped because Ravish was busy.
- He believes **Dimple's sentence** at the age of nine was spite.
- He believes **Baljeet** is a fair man.
- He believes his being sent away at thirteen was **for his education**.
- He believes the **London end** was given to him because he earned it. It was given to him because it was useful to let him earn it.
- He does not know that the man he calls father **killed the man whose face he has**.

## 6 · How any of this reaches the page

1. **One thing at a time, and never as backstory.** A chapter may spend one incident from §3 — in a line of dialogue, a joke at the table, an object, a habit — and no more. Canon never explains a family with a paragraph; it lets the family behave.
2. **The nicknames are free.** *Viju* from the sisters, *Chhote* from Ravish, *Vihaan sahib* from Karan, *beta* from Baljeet, and the full name from his father. Use them without comment; the reader will do the work.
3. **The debts are load-bearing.** The window at seven, the exam at sixteen, the money at twenty-one. They can be called in later, and when they are, the reader must already feel them as old.
4. **Never let him explain his own childhood.** He does not narrate it and he does not complain about it (`O019`, `WRITING.md` §5b).
5. **The evidence is behaviour.** He puts his own plate in the kitchen because he always has. He knows which stair creaks. He sits in the corner seat without being told. He calls the guard by name and Karan does not.
6. **Nothing here may change a canon beat.** The history explains what people do in canon's scenes; it never alters what they do.
7. **The author's veto is absolute.** Any incident may be struck in one word, and `OPEN-19` must be ruled before the Ravish line is used on the page.

## 7 · The channel, and how it knows him

The channel is the family's face — "SN", the ticker, the newsroom on the floor above the car park. Vihaan is known there as a fact rather than a person: **theboss's son who was in London and is coming back to a desk on Monday.** The building's own history of him:

- **Once, years ago**, the whole newsroom saw him: the Sunday his father brought him in, ten years old, and put him in the anchor's chair and photographed him there. The photograph is still in a drawer somewhere. Half the old staff remember it; none of them mention it.
- **The night of the award** (Chapter 3) he was the one holding the ladder and the cable, and the women at the desk looked. By Monday the newsroom will have decided he is the pretty one of the family, which is the cheapest thing to decide about a man.
- **The gear is the way in.** He is not put on air and does not ask to be. The camera bay is where he becomes useful within a week: he knows the kit, he does not drop it, and he does not make a speech about it. The crew's word for him is *bhai*; the anchors' word for him is nothing at all.
