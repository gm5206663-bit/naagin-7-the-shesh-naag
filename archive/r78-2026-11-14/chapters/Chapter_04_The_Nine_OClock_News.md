The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## Wait Here

Ten past midnight, and the corridor outside the styling room, and a trolley of clothes against the wall, and the two of them standing at the end of it where the light is.

The trolley has a plastic cover over the clothes, and the cover has been pulled back at one end and left that way.

At the far end of the corridor a cleaner's bucket stands with a mop resting in it, and the water in the bucket has stopped moving.

The night roster is pinned beside the styling-room door, and its last line is in Vihaan's hand, and the time he wrote against his own name is four in the morning.

"Purvi, main Arya se baat karke aati hoon tere liye." Ananta has her bag on her shoulder and her phone in her hand. "Theek hai? Jab main boloon, toh aake usse mil lena. Tab tak tu yahan wait kar."

"Yahin?"

"Yahin." She is already going. "All right?"

"All right."

*Mubarak ho, संगीत.* The music goes under the corridor and stays there while the door closes behind her sister. *Aaj office mein ek bahut बढ़िया हो रहा है और मै ही उसे बता रही हूँगी कि तुम्हें उसके बारे में बताना हूँ — अगर हमें लगता है कि हम उससे बढ़िया काम कर रहे हैं, तो हमें उस लड़की के लिए कामियां बढ़ाने की जरूरत है. तुम्हें भी पता होगा कि मैने कितना बढ़िया काम कर लिया है.*

The corridor is empty. He has gone in without shutting the door, so the room has its light spilling into the corridor and the corridor has its own; she waits in the join of the two with her bag on both arms, because she has been told to wait, and she waits.

## The Nine O'Clock News

One in the morning, and the channel's floor is still lit.

The newsroom has been a mandap for one night and comes out of it a wreck. Marigold on the floor, trodden flat. The garland hanging off one end of the weather desk. Balloons going soft in a corner. On the big screen, the tail of the nine o'clock bulletin runs again on a loop, and the words on it are in the wrong place.

"Spelling mistakes hain. Quotation marks are missing." Aryaman Suri stands in front of the screen with his hands in his pockets. His shirt is out at the back and the tie is lost on a chair. "This is embarrassing stuff. Have you not noted all this down?"

"Sir—" The young man with the tablet is holding it in both hands. "Dekhiye, kal main ye sab note karunga."

"Kal." Aryaman turns round. "This cannot happen again. That being said." He picks his jacket up off a chair. "Have a good night."

"Sir." The young man has the tablet up. "Graphics ka sign-off Vihaan sir ke desk se gaya tha. Aaj raat, chaar baje."

The floor has gone quiet in the way a floor does when a name it does not want has been said.

"Toh uske naam pe daal do." Aryaman is at the glass with his jacket over his arm. "Mera naam us bulletin ke neeche nahin aana chahiye. Samjhe?"

"Thank you, sir."

The word goes round the floor and the floor eases. Three people stand up at once. Somebody switches a monitor off. The ticker under the ceiling still goes, red letters into the dark of the glass.

One woman has not stood up. She is at the makeup mirror with a brush in one hand and an earring in the other, and she has been watching him since he came out of his cabin.

"Dekhiye, woh outfit main baad mein dekhunga." He says it without stopping. "Lekin yeh jo material aapne mujhe diya hai, yeh nau baje ki news pe screen pe bleed kar raha tha. Tum hamari head stylist ho. Give me something decent."

"Ya, you don't worry about it. I will take care of it."

He is already at the glass doors.

"Sir." She puts the earring in. "Aapko ek assistant ki zaroorat thi na?"

"Haan haan." His phone is ringing. "I have to take this. Hello?"

He goes through the doors. She watches them swing.

Out on the floor, Vihaan comes out from between the desks with his laptop shut under his arm and his earbud in. The party is still all over that room: marigold going brown on the screens, a garland down across a chair, a crate of glasses by the lift that nobody in this building is going to carry down tonight. Two men in housekeeping are doing it, and he steps round them, and one of them says *chhote sahab* and he says *ji* and keeps walking.

The young man with the tablet catches up with him at the lift with the crate between them. "Sir, housekeeping ka overtime?"

"Crate ke saath likhwa dena." He steps into the lift and holds the doors. "Naam ke saath."

The doors close. The young man writes it on the pad with the crate still balanced on his arm.

## The Jacket

The lobby at one in the morning, and the one lamp over the security desk doing the whole room, and marble, and one guard, and the doors breathing the wet air in.

Purvi stands by the pillar with her bag on her arm and her spectacles in her hand. She has been standing there for a long time and she has stopped hoping, which is a different posture, and she holds the glasses up to the one lamp and looks through them at nothing, and puts them back on, and takes them out again.

The guard brings a register up from under the counter and opens it flat and turns it round, and puts the pen in her hand.

Something falls out of the top of her bag when she sets it down to sign, and she picks it up: one petal off the garlands at the door of the hall where she has spent the evening. She puts it into the diary she keeps her notes in and presses the cover down on it, because it scratches the page if she leaves it loose.

"Naam, madam. Aur aane ka time."

"Kis liye?"

"Chhote sahab ka hukm hai." The guard's thumb is on the column. "Is building mein raat ko aane wale ka naam likha jaata hai. Roz."

She writes *Purvi Arora* and the time under it, and he turns the register back and reads it upside down and nods, and goes back to his radio. The lift comes down twice and nobody gets out of it either time.

Then the doors at the end of the lobby open, and a woman comes through them with a garment bag over her shoulder in its plastic and her phone in her other hand, and she is talking to somebody on it and stops talking.

"Purvi."

"Ananta?"

"Main tujhe andar bula rahi thi." She puts the phone away. "Kya kar rahi hai tu yahan? Kab se khadi hai?"

"Woh bole the na, wait karo."

Ananta looks at the shut lift, and at Shyam, and at her sister's face, and something in her own face goes hard and then smooth again.

"Excuse me." She says it to the marble, and then to Purvi, and her voice is kind and hurried and pitched to carry across a lobby. "I just thought, in case agar tumhein meri koi help chahiye toh." She takes her by the elbow. "Chal. Ek minute. Mere saath chal."

## The Cabin

The corridor behind the styling room, and a trolley of glasses for the morning's shoot standing against the wall under a tube light with one end of it flickering, and Purvi coming round the corner of it too fast, the bag strap already halfway down her arm.

Whoever set the trolley out has left the slip of paper with the count on it under one of the trays, and six is written on it twice.

The trolley goes over.

Six glasses of orange juice go down the front of Ananta's saree, and the tray rings on the floor, and the two sisters stand in the middle of it with their hands out.

"Sorry. Sorry—"

"It's fine." Ananta is already peeling the wet pallu away from herself. "It's fine. Chal, andar chal."

The cabin at the end of the corridor has the light off and a chair pushed back from the desk, and a box of tissues on it, and a mirror. She gets the garment bag up onto the hook on the back of the door and starts on her saree with the tissues and her back to the room.

The door opens again.

"Rajkumari." Arjun stands in it with his hands in his pockets, and he comes in, and he does not shut it the whole way. "Yahan chhup ke kya kar rahi ho?"

"Kapde." Ananta does not turn round. "Tum yahan kya kar rahe ho?"

"Tumhein dhoondh raha tha." He is behind her now, and he has taken her wrist, and turned it over, and the juice is running down it. "Poora channel tumhein dhoondh raha hai. Aur tum yahan—"

"Nahin chhoda toh?" She pulls. "Haath chhodo."

"Nahin chhoda toh?"

"Toh main chilla-chilla ke poora news channel yahan bula lungi." Her voice has gone up and it is not shaking, which is worse. "Haath chhodo!"

"Chilla do."

"Main maar daalungi tumhein."

"Maar daalo." He lets the wrist go and steps back with his palm out and laughs, and the laugh is the worst part of it. "Arre, main mazaak kar raha tha." He goes to the door. "Tum chilla deti toh mera hi nuksan hota."

He is out in the corridor. The door does not shut.

Ananta stands with her wet wrist held in her other hand and looks at the door.

Outside, in the corridor, Purvi's voice, small: "Didi?"

"Ek minute." Ananta's voice comes back level. "Ek minute, Purvi."

She comes out with the garment bag over her arm and her pallu wet, and shuts the cabin door behind her with her heel.

"Didi." Purvi is holding her own elbows. "Tu Arya se mili kya?"

"Haan."

"Par unhone toh meri taraf dekha tak nahin."

"Koi baat nahin." Ananta puts her hand flat on her sister's head, the same flat palm from their childhood. "Woh teri taraf dekhenge bhi, aur tujhe job bhi denge."

"Aapko lagta hai aisa?"

"Arre, mujhe dikhta hai aisa." She takes her arm. "Chal. Gaadi mein baith."

## The Broken Lens

Half past one in the channel's lobby, and the marble still holds the wet off the doors, and the only light left on in the whole of it is the one over the security desk.

Purvi stands by the pillar with her bag on her arm and her spectacles in her hand. She has been standing there since half past ten.

A camera body lies on the security desk with its lens off and the cap beside it, and the guard has put his own cap on top of the cap to keep it from going anywhere.

The lift's floor carries a wet footprint that comes halfway out of it and stops.

The boardroom on the fourth floor has had its light on all night. Three men came out of it at midnight. One of them still stands at the glass door arguing into a phone in English. The lift brings him down at half past one, jacket hooked on a shoulder, one earbud in, a laptop shut under his hand, and a second phone that keeps lighting up.

"Shyam." Vihaan does not look at him. "Koi aaya?"

"Ji sahab, woh madam." The guard's head comes up. "Das baje se."

The man stops with the laptop under his arm and looks down the lobby at her, and then at the clock over the doors, and then at her again.

"London se line pe hoon." He says it to her across the marble, and it is not an apology, it is a fact being handed over. "Ek minute."

He walks to the far end of the lobby with the phone at his ear. His voice comes back off the glass in short English sentences: the number is the number; the Kumbh window is not negotiable; if the other side wants the broadcast rights it pays for the broadcast rights, not for a favour. He does not raise his voice once. He listens for a long time. Then he says, quietly, "Then we are agreed," and takes the earbud out.

"Aap kab se khadi hain?" He is in front of her now.

"Do ghante se zyada."

"Kisne kaha tha ki rukiye?"

"Ananta ne. Meri behen ne. Woh Aryaman sir ke saath kaam karti hain." Purvi's chin is up. "Aur main aapse shikayat nahin kar rahi. Aap unke chhote bhai hain. Aap kuch kar bhi nahin sakte."

"Kisi ne paani diya?"

Shyam's radio goes quiet, which is Shyam's way of answering.

Vihaan takes the bottle out of his own bag and holds it out.

"Main kisi ajnabi se—"

"Paanch rupaye ka hai." He puts it in her hand and takes nothing for it. "Shaam ko hisaab karunga."

She holds it and does not drink, and he lets her not drink it.

"Aap is building mein kya karte hain?" she says.

"Main is channel ka hisaab dekhta hoon. Aur London ka office." He puts his hands in his pockets. "Aur aap?"

"Main journalist hoon." Her voice does not shake. "Do saal se."

"Kahan?"

"Kahin nahin." The word comes out flat and it is the bravest thing in that lobby. "Do saal se kahin nahin."

He looks at her for a moment longer than he needs to.

"Woh mera bhai hai. Jo aapko bulaane wale the." He says it without any weight at all. "Woh aaj raat kisi se nahin milte. Kal subah bhi nahin, kyunki kal subah unke paas file aayegi, aur file ke baad do meeting hain."

"Toh?"

"Toh aap aath baje aana." He shifts the laptop under his arm. "Das baje woh apne cabin mein honge aur aap bahar khadi hongi. Aath baje Manoj desk pe aata hai. Uske paas roz subah ek list hoti hai. Aap apna naam us list mein likhwa lena. Uske baad aapko bulana unki majboori hoga, unki marzi nahin."

"Aap unke khilaaf kaam kar rahe hain."

"Main yeh nahin maanta ki ek laayak aadmi ko do ghante khada rakhna unka haq hai." His voice does not go up. "Bas."

"Main kisi ki sifarish se andar nahin aaungi."

"Aap apna naam likhwa rahi hain. Usme kisi ka ehsaan nahin."

The second phone goes off in his hand, and he looks at it, and does not take it.

"Naam?"

"Purvi Arora."

"Vihaan." He does not put the family name after it, and she notices that he does not. "Aur aapki gaadi nahin hai. Shyam, gaadi aage laao. Driver aapko chhod ke paanch baje laut aayega."

"Main aapki gaadi nahin lungi."

"Meri gaadi nahin hai." He is already at the doors. "Mere paas apni gaadi lene ka waqt nahin hai. Charter diya hua hai. Aap uski behen hain, woh isi building mein kaam karti hai, aur raat ke do baje main kisi ladki ko auto mein nahin bithata."

"Chaar baje." Purvi stands in the doorway. "Gaadi, aur naam list mein."

"Aath baje naam." He goes out into the rain. "Aur kisi ko yeh mat batana ki maine kaha. Main is ghar mein hisaab rakhta hoon. Faisle nahin karta."

The doors swing. The guard turns his radio up.

Purvi stands in the empty lobby and then she drinks the water standing up, all of it, and puts the empty bottle in her bag.

Purvi looks at the doors he went out of, and looks at them for longer than there is anything to look at, and if anybody asks her why, she has no answer.

At the top of the steps the car comes round the drive with its wipers going, and a boy in a black uniform holds the door open, and the woman who has waited three hours in a lobby gets in.

## The Brothers

The corridor at two in the morning, and its bins emptied and standing out from the wall with new bags in them, and the bags the cleanest thing on that floor, and the last of the party's light coming down the stairwell behind it.

A trolley of plates stands by the lift where the caterers left it, with a stack of them still under a cloth.

Aryaman comes out of the lift into the corridor with the jacket by the collar in one hand, and there is his cousin Karan at the far end of it, in a fresh shirt at two in the morning, with somebody on the phone.

Karan is twenty-eight and works for the family in a way nobody has ever been able to describe. The fresh shirt is lilac and open one button further than it needs to be, the hair is wet from a shower he has taken since the party, and there is a thin gold chain at his throat that he touches when he is lying. He is good-looking in the way that gets a boy out of trouble at nineteen and does nothing at all for him at twenty-nine.

The lift doors stand open on that floor for a long time before anybody comes out of them.

"Bhai. Bhai!" Karan puts the phone down on his chest. "Arre, tum apni dost ki party mein nahin gaya?"

"Come on, bhai." Aryaman does not stop walking. "Aapko chhod ke kaise ja sakta hoon main?"

"Ha." Karan falls in beside him. "Two rounds."

The lift doors open. Aryaman steps in and holds them.

"Yeh jacket tujh pe achhi lagegi." Karan holds it up. "Le na."

They go down together, and Karan talks all the way.

"Bhai, ek baat bata." He turns in the corridor. "Aapki jitni bhi assistant hain na— sab stupid hain. Sab. You should hire some hard-working assistant." He puts his hand up. "Waise mere paas ek ladki hai. Usko job ki zaroorat hai. Job lag gayi toh meri bhi izzat badhegi."

"Kaun si ladki?"

"Woh kya hai na—" He waves it away. "Woh main dekh loonga. Bas tu haan bol de."

"Iss floor pe?" Aryaman stops at the lift. "Nahin."

"Bhai—"

"Usse bol dena main usey le liya."

"Aur letter?"

"Letter mere desk se nahin jaayega." Aryaman gets into the lift. "Vihaan ke daftar se jaayega. Mera naam us kagaz pe nahin hoga."

## The Sattu ke Laddu

The back steps of the channel at a quarter past two, under the one bulb over the door, where the caterers load. Aryaman sits on the bottom step with the citation folder beside him on the stone, eating out of a steel box with his fingers, waiting for a car that is not coming.

"Aur ek?" Karan comes out of the door with his phone against his ear and a cigarette in the other hand and a second box under his arm, still tied with string. "Bhai, main ek ladki hai—" He stops, and starts again. "Woh kya hai na, usko I mean usko job ki zaroorat hai, maine haan kah di. Toh dekhna."

Vihaan comes down the back steps with the camera bag on one shoulder, takes a laddu out of the open box on the step without asking, eats it standing there, and goes back in through the door with the bag still on his shoulder. Neither of the two men stops him and neither of them speaks to him.

Karan sets the tied box down beside his brother's. The steel box has its lid propped against it and a spoon standing up in the sugar at the bottom of it.

Karan's phone lies face up on the step between them, a list of names open on the screen, and the top one crossed out.

"Tu ne itna weight loss kyun kar liya?" He points at the box with the cigarette. "Kitni baar kaha hai, sattu ke laddu khaya kar. Yeh le, specially tere liye mangwaye hain."

"Khuda whisky ke swimming pool mein dubki laga de, aur mujhe sattu ke laddu?" Aryaman holds one up.

"Bhai, this is not fair."

"Main mar bhi gaya toh kya farak padega?"

"Kyun nahin farak padega?" Karan's voice changes. "Aap meri lifeline ho. Woh meri jaan hai tu." He takes the cigarette out of his mouth. "Us ladki ko bol dena ki haan kar diya hai."

"Say it yourself." Aryaman gets up and dusts his hands on the seat of his trousers. "The letter goes from his daftar, not my cabin — I have had enough of carrying messages tonight."

He walks round the building to where his car is not. Karan sits down on the step with both boxes and eats, in the dark, out of the tied one.

## The Job

The steps at three in the morning. The rain has stopped and the road is black and the gate light hums, and a moth works at the bulb of it and the light goes off and on with the moth.

A tin of sweets stands open on the step with its lid off, and nobody has touched it.

"Purvi!" A scooter comes in under the gate and skids a little on the wet and stops. Ananta gets off it with her helmet still on. "Purvi! Tujhe job mil gayi hai!"

"Kya?"

Two glasses of tea stand on the parapet where the watchman left them, and the rain has got into both of them.

"Haan!" Ananta gets the helmet off and her hair comes down. "Kal appointment letter bhi aa jaayega!"

Her phone goes off in her hand and she looks at it and does not answer it.

Beyond the gate the road is black and the water on it holds the gate light in pieces.

Purvi's hands are over her mouth and then her face is wet, and Ananta has her by the shoulders.

"Kya hua? Khushi ke aansu?" She wipes them with her thumbs. "Meri jaan, tu aise hi kyun roti hai jaise chheenkne wali ho?"

"Thank you." Purvi is holding her. "Thank you, thank you."

"Ek baat poochun?" She steps back. "Aapne mujhe job kaise dilwayi? Aapne apne special powers ka use kiya."

"Of course." Ananta puts her hand on her hip, and the score comes up under her, and the gate light goes bright and then dim. "Yahi toh meri special power hai. Ki koi na nahin keh sakta."

Purvi laughs, and stops laughing.

"Mere paas bhi kuch special powers hote."

"Ek baat poochhni thi." Purvi wipes her face with the back of her wrist. "Aath baje main us building mein jaane waali thi. Kisi ne mujhe kaha tha ki Manoj ke paas roz subah ek list hoti hai, aur mujhe apna naam us list mein likhwana chahiye."

"Kisne kaha?"

"Unke chhote bhai ne." Purvi looks at her sister. "Raat do baje lobby mein. Maine teen ghante khadi thi, didi. Ek bhi aadmi nahin aaya. Woh aaya, aur woh ek minute ke liye ruka."

Ananta stands with the helmet against her hip and does not say anything at all.

"Aapke paas poora brahmand hai, Naagrani." Ananta says it lightly, and then she is not light at all. "Aapko toh ehsaas hi nahin hai ki aap hain kitni shaktishali."

"Ananta—"

"Aap hain Anant kul ki Naag Rani." She takes her sister's face in both hands. "Aapki chaubeesvi saalgirah pe aapko aapke Naaglok se milaya jaayega. Aapko aapki duniya mein laaya jaayega."

The wind comes along the road and takes the bottom of Purvi's dupatta and lets it go. Somewhere a dog barks twice and stops.

"Kabhi kabhi lagta hai"—Purvi's eyes are on the wet road—"ki main is duniya ki hi nahin hoon."

Ananta's hands come down.

"Chal." Her voice is ordinary again. "You bloody just shut up." She takes her by the arm. "Deep, jaldi chalo."

"Mujhe dar lag raha hai." Purvi is not moving. "Please."

## The Road

The road at the edge of the city at twenty past three in the morning, and the scooter's headlight on the tarmac, and nothing else lit for a mile either way.

The scooter will not start.

Ananta kicks it twice, and the third time it coughs and dies, and she stands with both hands on the handlebars looking at it.

"Oye totamal." She looks up at her sister. "Oye. Aaja, meri gaadi mein baith ja."

An old white car stands in the channel's parking with its lights on and a driver asleep in it who is not asleep any more.

"Let's have fun."

The car comes out of the gate into the empty road with the wet shining on it, and the score comes up, a dhol and a woman's voice, and the two of them are singing before the gate is behind them.

A hundred metres down, the engine dies. The lights stay on, and the wheel goes heavy, and the car rolls to a stop in the middle of the road with its bonnet ticking.

"Yeh toh toot gaya hai." Ananta is out with the bonnet up. "Ruko, ruko—"

A knot of men stands in the road ahead with a bottle going round and the headlights of somebody's jeep lighting them from behind.

"Kahan bhaag rahi ho?" One of them steps into the middle of the road with both arms out. "Arre, aaj toh maza aane waala hai."

"Arre, mat niklo bahar!" Ananta hits the horn.

"Aaram se!" The man puts his palm flat on the bonnet. "Tumhari himmat kaise hui?"

"Gaadi nikaalo!" Ananta is out of the car before Purvi can catch her arm. "Niklo tum yahan se!"

The man is laughing. He has not moved his hand off the bonnet. "Hamare saath chalo. Aish karogi?"

"Please." Purvi gets out on her side. "Please, humein jaane do."

"You are Indian. Get lost." Another one, from the jeep, with a bottle in his hand. "Hamare saath chalo."

"Mujhe zabardasti karna nahin pasand." Purvi's voice is steady and her hands are not.

"Tabhi toh—" The man's hand comes out and takes her wrist.

"Kya hua? Haath chhod mera!" She pulls. "Chhodo! Haath chhodo mera!"

The scuffle turns once, and the bottle goes down on the road and does not break, and somebody's elbow catches her face, and her spectacles come off and go under the car.

"Mera chashma!" She is on her knees in the wet with her hand out. "Mera chashma!"

Ananta's hand comes down on the man's shoulder and turns him round.

"Chhodo use." Her voice has gone quiet. "Chhodo. Maine kaha chhodo."

Then they take Ananta.

Two of them have her by the arms before she can get her hand back. They go backwards with her across the road. She is kicking. One of them turns and puts his palm flat on her face and pushes her away from him, and the sound of it goes across the road and stops the whole night.

"Didi!"

"Chhod do use!" Purvi is up off the road. "Chhod do!"

A hand takes her shoulder and turns her, and it is the flat of a palm again, and she goes down on her side with the taste of iron in her mouth and her glasses somewhere under the car.

"Mera chashma." She is on her hands and knees. "Mera chashma!"

They have Ananta at the jeep. She has stopped kicking. Somebody is laughing.

And the wind comes.

It comes up the road out of nothing and takes the dust off the verge and the bottles off the ground and the men's shirts out of their trousers, and the sky above the road turns over once. The headlights of the jeep go out.

Then they come on again, and there is a woman standing in the middle of the road in front of the jeep.

Ananta is barefoot. Her hair is down her back. There is no wind in her clothes at all.

"Kahan ja rahi hai?" She says it to the men, and the sound of it does not come out of a human throat.

"Arre—" One of them steps forward, laughing still. "Ek aur nagina."

"Nageena nahin." She lifts her head. "Naagin."

What happens next is not shown. The lights go out again and the sounds do not stop. Something heavy goes over on the road and rolls. One man's voice goes up and stops in the middle of itself. The jeep rocks on its springs, once, twice, and something hits the bonnet and slides off.

Then the wind goes away, and the road is empty.

Purvi comes to with her cheek on the tarmac and no memory of putting it there. Her head rings. The road is very quiet and the sky over it has gone the wrong colour and her glasses are gone.

"Didi?"

Ananta lies on the verge with her eyes shut and her sari over her face, and Purvi crawls to her across the tarmac on her hands and knees.

"Didi." She has her sister's head in her lap. "Didi, utho. Utho na." Her hand is flat on Ananta's chest, and it moves, and she starts to breathe again. "Didi, utho. Paani nahin hai. Paani nahin hai, didi."

Far up the road, over the fields, there is a light.

## The Glasses

Ananta lies on the ground beside the road in the dark, with her head in her sister's lap, and the scooter's headlight still on and lying across them both.

"Utho." Purvi's hands are shaking. "Utho, didi. Utho. Didi, utho!"

"Mujhe—" Ananta's mouth moves. "Paani."

A cloth bundle has come out of the scooter's basket and lies open on the road, and the rain has got into it.

"Paani nahin hai!" Purvi looks round at the fields, at the dark. "Paani nahin hai, didi!"

Her sister's eyes close.

"Utho!" She is shaking her. "Utho, please, utho!"

Far up the road, in the dark behind the fields, there is a small light.

"Bhai." Purvi says it to nobody, and then to her sister, and then to the light. "Woh mandir. Naag Mandir. Wahan paani mil sakta hai."

Purvi lays her sister's head down on her own dupatta.

"Main wahan se aapke liye paani lekar aati hoon." Her hand is flat on Ananta's cheek. "Suno. Main aati hoon. Sun rahi ho? Main aati hoon."

She gets up and runs, and her slippers come off on the road behind her, and she does not stop for them.

## Not a Nagina

The road bends in the dark, and there is a jeep at the side of it, and the men of the jeep are sitting on the ground now with their backs against the wheels and their eyes open.

The jeep's headlights are still on and pointing down the road, and the dust they have raised is not settling in them.

A thermos stands on the bonnet with its cup upside down on the top of it.

One of the men has a rifle across his knees, and a cloth folded under the stock so the metal does not sit on the cloth of his trousers.

The radio in the jeep is on and nobody in it is listening.

One of them is looking at nothing at all.

"Amazing yaar." He says it to the night, and his voice is not right. "Aaj toh kismat meherbaan hai hum par."

The man beside him does not answer, and his eyes are open, and he is looking at nothing.

"Ek aur nageena aa gayi thi." The first one is talking to keep himself company. "Bas."

## The Naag Mandir

The temple stands behind its low wall with its lamp burning, and the light of it is the only light for a mile.

Purvi comes up the steps with her sister's name still in her mouth and stops in the doorway.

Inside, the hall is not small.

Pillars, and lamps between them, and a serpent carved over each pillar with its hood up. The floor of stone is washed, and the washing still shows in the joints. A drum of water stands at the wall with a brass pot beside it and a rope in it.

And the air in there is warm, and somewhere behind the sanctum a hymn is playing, thin and old, the same two lines going round and round: *Om namah Shivaya. Om Maheshwaraya.* It has been playing since before she came in, and nobody is in there to hear it.

Purvi goes down on her knees at the drum and fills the pot, and water goes over the stone, and she is already turning.

"Woh aa rahi hai."

The voice comes from behind the lamps. Purvi's hands stop on the pot.

"Mandir mein aa rahi hai."

"Kaun?" Purvi's voice does not carry.

An old woman comes out from the far end of the hall with a lamp in her hand and her white hair loose, and she is smiling, and her eyes are wet.

"Bas ek baar unke kadam is mandir mein aa jaayein." She lifts the lamp. "Phir woh apne asli roop mein aane lagegi."

The bell rope in the doorway moves, and there is no wind in that hall.

"Bhay hai tu. Tu hi samay bhi hai." Uttara's voice goes up the pillars and comes back. "Srishti bhi tu, tu hi pralay bhi hai. Swayam hi tu agni hai. Maheshwaraya."

The lamps all lean towards the doorway at once and stand straight again.

"Naaglok ki Rani." The old woman turns. "Anant kul ki Naag Rani. Ananta."

The name goes through the hall.

"Aaiye." She holds the lamp up. "Aaiye, Maharani. Aaiye, apna singhasan sambhaliye, jise barson se aapka intezaar hai. Aaiye."

At the far end of the hall, a cloth lies over a shape and the cloth is lifting.

## She Is Coming

The hall of the Naag Mandir, and every lamp in it burning straight, and the stone floor wet where her feet have been.

The drum of water stands in the doorway with its mouth full to the top, and the diya beside it is out.

Purvi stands up with the brass pot in both hands.

"M— main—" She looks behind her at the doorway. "Meri didi bahar hai. Unhein paani chahiye. Main—"

"Ji." Uttara's voice is gentle, and she does not move out of the way. "Mandir toh jaagrit ho gaya. Lekin singhasan jaagrit kyun nahin hua, yeh aap poochhenge?"

"Kya?" Purvi looks at the shape at the end of the hall.

"Woh tab hoga." The old woman's eyes do not leave her. "Jab Maharani ki kesari wahan pe chadhegi."

A small brass bowl stands on the step of the throne with something red in it.

"Purvi."

The voice is behind her, at the door, and it is her sister's.

## The Throne

The hall at that hour with every lamp in it burning straight, and the dark standing in the roof above them where the light does not go.

Ananta is in the doorway with her sari dirty and her hair down, holding on to the frame, and she is white.

"Purvi! Tu yahan mandir mein kaise aayi?" She comes in three steps and stops, because the hall is not what a hall should be. "Tu theek hai na?"

"Didi, aap—" Purvi's hand goes out. "Aapko paani—"

"Mujhe laga"—Purvi looks round the pillars, at the lamps, at the throne—"jaise yeh mandir mujhe apni aur kheench raha hai."

The temple's bell moves on its rope again.

"Purvi." Ananta's voice is quiet and frightened. "Bahut raat ho gayi hai. Yahan se chalte hain. Theek hai. Mummy Papa bhi intezaar kar rahe honge, pareshaan ho rahe honge." She holds her hand out. "Chalte hain mere saath."

Purvi looks at the throne.

"Ji." Uttara says it from beside the lamps, and she is not speaking to either of them now. "Mahanagrani toh mandir tak aate hi wapas chali gayi."

The lamp in her hand goes out.

"Nahin maanenge." The old woman's voice goes on in the dark, and it is answering somebody who is not in that hall. "Mandir tak nahin. Mandir mein aakar wapas chali gayi."

The lamps come back.

"Woh apna ek pair mandir mein rakh chuki hai." Uttara looks at Purvi's bare feet on the stone, and at the wet marks of them. "Unki naagin banne ki shuruaat ho chuki hai."

## The Kesari

The lamps of the hall stand in their line, and their light lies on the wet stone and does not reach the throne.

Purvi keeps her eyes on the floor of the hall after that.

"Chalo." She takes her sister's hand. "Chalo, didi."

"Purvi—"

"Chalo."

The bell's rope hangs still against the pillar, and the bell has not been rung.

They go out of the doorway together, and the drum of water stands there, and the pot is still full in Purvi's other hand, and neither of them remembers it.

Behind them the hall burns straight up, every lamp at once, and holds.

And a small brass bowl of red stands on the step of a throne that has waited a long time.

## Purvi, How Did You Come Here

The temple steps at dawn, and at the bottom of them the car with its bonnet up and a tow truck's driver winding a chain.

"Purvi." Ananta is sitting on the wall with her sister's dupatta round her shoulders.

The driver's spanner lies on the wall with an oily cloth under it, and the chain is in a heap on the road with the hook turned up. "Tu yahan mandir mein kaise aayi?"

"Aapko paani chahiye tha." Purvi is looking at her hands. "Aur woh mandir—" She stops.

"Woh mandir kya?"

"Kuch nahin."

Her spectacles are in her hand with the one lens out of the frame, and she has not put them on.

Ananta looks at her for a long moment.

Then she takes her face in one hand and turns it to the light of the tow truck.

"Raja." Her voice is very low. "Sun. Bahar jo kuch bhi hua, ghar mein kisi ko mat batana. Theek hai? Sab tension lenge." Her thumb moves once across her sister's cheek. "Aur tera yeh chashma main kal banwa doongi."

"Woh aurat kaun thi?" Purvi says it to her own knees.

"Koi nahin thi." Ananta does not look at her. "Tumne kuch nahin dekha."

The car goes up onto the truck with a clank of chain.

## One Foot

The channel at four in the morning. The corridor lights are on every third panel. A woman in a saree comes out of the lift with a steel tiffin in her hand.

"Yeh kya uttar hai, ji?" She is talking to somebody on the phone, and she is not happy. "Mandir toh jaagrit ho gaya—"

A sweeper's cart stands at the end of the corridor with a bottle and a folded cloth on it.

The late bulletin runs again on the screen at the end of the corridor with the sound off, and the credits come up over a still of a shut gate, and Vihaan's name is in them, in the camera column, and the woman with the tiffin does not turn round for it.

The lift doors shut behind her.

"—lekin singhasan jaagrit kyun nahin hua?"

She walks down the corridor. In the dark glass of the newsroom window her reflection walks with her, and behind her reflection there is another shape for one frame, and it is not there when the lights change.

"Ji." She stops at the end of the corridor. "Mahanagrani toh mandir mein aakar wapas chali gayi." A pause. "Mandir tak nahin. Mandir mein aakar wapas chali gayi. Woh apna ek pair mandir mein rakh chuki hai."

The panel over her head buzzes.

"Unki naagin banne ki shuruaat ho chuki hai."

She turns the corner. The corridor is empty. The tiffin sits on the floor where she put it down to talk, and then it is not there either.

## The Suri House, Later

Five in the morning at the Suri house, and the sky over the roof going grey.

The car comes in through the gate with its wipers off and its lamps low, and stops where it always stops, and the driver gets out and stands beside it with his cap in his hand.

The bonnet has a cracked lamp on the left and a new dent in the wing, and there is storm dust all along it, and one long streak where somebody has wiped it with a finger.

Parmeet Suri stands in the doorway of the house with his phone in one hand.

"Kahan gaye the?"

"Chhote sahab ne kaha tha, sir." The driver does not look up from his cap. "Madam ko unke ghar chhodne."

"Kaun madam?"

"Woh—" The driver looks at the gate. "Woh madam jo building mein khadi thi. Chhote sahab ne kaha tha ki aapki gaadi hai."

Parmeet's eyes come up over the driver's shoulder, and Vihaan comes down the steps into the gravel with his sleeves still rolled.

"Woh car meri hai." Parmeet says it quietly. "Tera haq nahin banta ki tu meri car kisi ko de."

"Ji."

"Woh ladki kaun hai?"

"Ek ladki jo poori raat lobby mein khadi thi." Vihaan takes the keys out of the driver's hand and gives them to his father, and the driver steps back. "Naam pata nahin."

"Jhoot."

"Jhoot." Vihaan does not look away. "Naam pata hai. Aapko nahin bataunga."

The sky over the roof goes a lighter grey. Somewhere beyond the hedge a sweeper's brush goes along the road, and Shyam at the barrier stands up because the sahib is in the drive.

"Kal Khurana log aa rahe hain." Parmeet puts the keys in his pocket. "Ghar mein naya naam nahin chalega, Vihaan. Karan ki shaadi ka ghar hai. Tu Monday se apne desk pe baithega aur woh ladki channel ke bahar rahegi."

"Woh channel ke andar hai." Vihaan's voice stays level. "Appointment letter aaj ja raha hai. Aryaman ne khud kaha hai."

Parmeet looks at him for a long moment, and Shyam at the barrier keeps standing.

"Toh theek hai." He turns back into the house. "Andar aa. Khurana log subah aayenge, aur tere paas do ghante hain yeh samajhne ke ki is ghar mein kaun faisla karta hai."

The door shuts.

Vihaan stands in the drive beside the car with the cracked lamp and the streak on the dust where a finger has been.

"Chhote sahab." The guard has come across the gravel with his cap in his hands. "Gaadi dhone bhejun?"

"Abhi nahin." He puts his hand flat on the bonnet. "Abhi jaisi hai waisi rehne do."
