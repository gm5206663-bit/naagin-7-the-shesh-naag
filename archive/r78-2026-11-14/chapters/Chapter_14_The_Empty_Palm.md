A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Guest Who Was Not Asked

Morning at the Suri house, and the lawn is a working site. A man on a ladder with a staple gun. Two men carrying a length of marigold between them. A boy on his knees with a roll of white cloth and a pot of paste, marking out where the chairs will stand.

Inside, the front room smells of new paint and coffee, and the elder of the house is standing by the sideboard with his hands behind his back, and the oldest of his cousins is talking at him.

A tray of glasses stands on the side table with a jug beside it, and nobody has poured anything all morning.

Vihaan comes down the stairs with the day's list in his hand and goes along the veranda reading it, and the men on the lawn keep their heads down until he has passed.

"Brother sahab, aapne aisa kyun kiya?" The cousin has his arms out. "Ab aise log hamari barabari mein baithenge kya? Kal se woh hamare ghar mein aayenge, jaayenge, aur phir hum unke ghar jaayenge. Mammy ko bhi baithana padega."

Parmeet does not turn round.

"Ravish minister ki biwi ke saath haath pakda gaya hai." He says it to the window. "Use bachane ke liye mere paas aur koi option nahi tha. Isliye jo chal raha hai, chalne do."

"Lekin bhai sahab—"

"Mera bahut kuch daav par laga hai." He turns now, and his face is pleasant, and his voice is not. "Tumhein itna hi pata rakhna hai."

The cousin's hands come down. Nobody in that room says anything else about it.

The ladder's feet have gone into the lawn, and the man on it has a staple gun in one hand and a box of nails in the other.

On the lawn the staple gun goes off, twice, and the marigold comes down the poles and hangs.

## Attari

By ten the morning sun is on the drive and the gate is open and the cars are coming in one behind another.

Gurinder Arora comes up the drive in a cream kurta with his spectacles in his hand and his wife's shawl over his arm, and the man of the house comes down the steps to meet him with both hands out.

"Parmeet ji." Gurinder takes the hand in both of his, and then the other man's shoulder. "Bahut badhiya ghar hai. Bahut sundar."

"Aaiye, aaiye. Andar aaiye."

They sit. Water comes. A tray of something fried comes and is not touched by anybody.

"Actually"—Gurinder says it with his hands open—"yeh shaadi itni jaldi-jaldi mein ho rahi hai ki ghar-parivar ke baare mein hamari zyada kuch baat nahi ho paayi. Aap log hain kahan se?"

"Attari."

"Attari?" Gurinder's face opens. "Aap bhi to wahin se hain."

"Ji."

"Arre, yeh to bahut achhi baat hai." He looks round the room, pleased with the world. "Aap sabke naam mere puraane dost ke poore parivar se milte hain. Parmeet Suri. Baljeet Suri. Dimple bhabhi."

The room does not move.

"Woh padosi the hamare." Gurinder puts his glass down to say it properly. "Uttari pahaad ke peeche. Attari se aage, pahaad ke khatam hone ke baad, pehli gali. Ek chhota Shivling bhi tha hamare ghar ke bahar."

Nobody answers him. Somewhere behind the house a staple gun goes off.

"Bada hi pyaara parivar tha." Gurinder smiles at the memory of it. "Bachche the na unke? Ek chhota sa."

"Ji." Parmeet's glass comes down on the table. "Bahut purani baat hai."

"Bahut purani." Gurinder nods, and drinks his water, and the subject closes the way such subjects close in that kind of room.

And on the far side of the room, in the passage where the trays come through, the man who is nobody's cousin and nobody's driver leans his shoulder against the doorframe and looks at the wall.

## A Strange Coincidence

The passage at the back of the house, by the store room, with one bulb over it and the sweet boxes stacked against the wall.

"Yeh to usi jagah ki baat kar raha hai." The driver's voice is low and quick. "Jahan se humne us scientist ko pakda tha."

The man with the grey beard has his hand flat on the wall.

A stack of sweet boxes stands shoulder high against the wall, and the tape on the top one has been cut and not taken off.

A stool stands by the store room door with a glass on it that has gone warm.

"Yeh ittefaq bada ajeeb laga mujhe."

"Ismein ittefaq kya hai?" The driver is not frightened; he is annoyed the way a man is annoyed at a bill. "Punjab mein gine-chune to surname hote hain. Singh. Kapoor. Suri. Aur naam bhi gine-chune hi hote hain — Preet, Jeet, Baljeet, Parmeet. Ab iske aage peeche jo laga lo, kya fark padta hai?"

"Attari." The grey-bearded one says the word to the wall. "Uttari pahaad ke peeche. Chhota Shivling ghar ke bahar."

"Toh?"

"Toh kuch nahi." He takes his hand off the wall. "Jaakar Sapera ji se poochh lo, aur mujhe batao."

At the door of the front room the master of the house is standing with his hands together, and he is smiling at the bride's father, and the smile does not move while he says, over his shoulder, without turning his head: "Aapko kisi cheez ki zaroorat ho na, aap mujhe bataiye."

## The Report

Maa Sapela sits in the small room at the back with her case open on the floor and the been across her knees, and the light of the one bulb on the wall above her, and she does not get up when the two of them come in. She came in through the gate an hour after the house had gone quiet, and the man who let her in is not in this room.

"Yeh log Gurinder aur uska parivar hai." The elder says it standing. "Attari gaon se. Tum logon ko yaad hai? Humne ek ghar ke aage se ek scientist ko agwa kiya tha."

"I remember." Her hand is flat on the been.

"Ab saari kadiyan apne aap mil rahi hain." He puts one finger down on the table between them. "Yeh wahi family hai jiske darwaze ke aage woh scientist humein mila tha. Jo ki naagin ke baare mein poochh raha tha."

Sapelae looks up.

"Ab mujhe yeh dekhna hai"—the elder leans in—"ki is ladki, is family, aur is ichchhadhari naagin ke beech mein koi connection hai ya nahi."

"Kahin yeh parivar woh to nahi jiske yahan naagin—" The driver stops himself.

Her eyes come round to him and stay.

"Iska matlab hai"—the elder's voice has gone cold—"ki jo plan aapne banaya hai, woh foolproof nahi hai?"

"Bahut bada risk hai." The driver's hands come up. "Bahut bada."

"Koi risk nahi hai." Maa Sapela says it without heat. "Maine us ladki ko maarne ke liye jo bheja — mera bhediya mar gaya, lekin woh ladki abhi bhi zinda hai. Yeh dono behenein, Purvi aur Ananta. Inmein se ek to zaroor koi hai naagin."

She picks the been up and turns it over in her hands.

The case on the floor has its lid open and a cloth folded on top of what is inside it.

"Aapne zeher ki jaanch ki?" She looks at the elder. "Kya mere bhediye ko maarne wali ichchhadhari naagin thi?"

## Mahanaagin

The small room at the back of the Suri house at noon, with the door shut and one bulb on in it.

"Agar woh koi mamooli naagin hoti"—Maa Sapela sets the been down—"to uske baare mein batane ke liye main yahan nahi aati."

The room is small and the fan does not reach the corner of it.

The case stands open on the floor with its cloth folded back, and the driver keeps his eyes on the case and not on the women.

A glass of water stands on the floor by the case and nobody has given it to anybody.

"Woh jis naagin ka zeher tha, woh koi aam ichchhadhari naagin nahi hai." She says it slowly. "Mahanaagin hai woh."

"Mahanaagin?"

"Mahanaagin ek hi kul hua karta tha." Her finger goes round in the dust on the table. "Anant kul. Us kul ka poore Naaglok mein bahut naam tha. Usi kul ki rani hai woh — Mahanaagrani."

The driver has stopped moving.

"Lekin achanak woh kul lupt ho gaya." She looks at the dust. "Aur kisi ko nahi pata chala ki woh kahan gayab ho gaya. Yeh usi kul ki ichchhadhari naagin hai."

"Saare devtaon ki kripa hai us par," the elder says, and it is not quite a question.

"Saare devtaon ki kripa hai us par." Maa Sapela nods. "Aur sabne usey vardaan bhi diya hai. Aur woh naagin yahin kahin hai."

The driver's voice comes out of the corner at a size too big for the room.

"Matlab jonron ki tarah woh humein bhi kutte ki maut maregi—"

"Oh, shut up." The elder does not look at him. "Agar woh naagin yahin kahin hai to fauran khatam kijiye usey." He turns back to Maa Sapela. "Kumbh mein jaane se pehle mujhe us ichchhadhari naagin ki laash dekhni hai. Do din hai aapke paas apne kaam ko anjaam dene ke liye."

"Do din." Maa Sapela says it to the been.

"Lekin usse pehle mujhe jaanna hai ki woh ichchhadhari naagin hai kaun."

## A Hundred Snake-Charmers

The small room at the back of the Suri house at a quarter to one, with the bulb on and the door shut.

"Uski gaan toh mujhe tabhi aa gayi thi." She lifts the been. "Jab main is ghar mein aayi thi."

"Gaan?"

The been's case has a rope round it where the catch has gone.

She puts the mouthpiece to her lips and does not blow.

The case lies open at her feet with the cloth turned back and a tin standing in it.

"Bas ek baar aakhri tasalli karni hai mujhe." She takes it away again. "Iski madad se sau saperon ka aashirvaad mila hai is shaktishali been ko. Iski dhun sunkar Anant kul ki rani bhi apne aap ko rok nahi sakegi. Aur woh apne asli roop mein aa jayegi."

"That is a large claim," the elder says.

"It is a large instrument." She lays it down. "Aaj raat ko pata chal jayega ki aakhir woh ichchhadhari naagin hai kaun."

Outside the window the boy with the staple gun is singing to himself, badly, and the marigold is going up pole by pole.

## The Eyeliner

By eight the chandelier is on and the sound is up and the floor is full.

The bride's side has come in at seven and the groom's side has been in since four. The cousins are in three colours. The aunts are in the good saris. There is a bar at the end of the hall that four of the older men are standing at, and a table of sweets nobody has touched because the mehendi is not done.

Aryaman Suri comes through the middle of it with his jacket open and a glass in his hand and stops, and the room adjusts itself around him the way rooms do.

The chandelier has one bulb out at the side of it and nobody has been up a ladder to change it.

"Arjun." He puts his arm round the nearest cousin's shoulders. "Shaadi meri ho rahi hai, iski ho rahi hai — lekin tumhara kya scene hai? Yeh chehre pe jo plaster laga rakha hai, kisi ko gun-point pe le jaake shaadi karne ka plan hai kya?"

"Kal se pehle kar denge bhai." The cousin does not move out of the arm. "Aap apni fikar karo."

"Haan." Aryaman lets him go. "Waise agar tumhein dulha chahiye na, to paas ke zoo mein jaake try kar lena. Kyunki I am sure tumhein tumhara jodidaar wahin milega."

Two of the aunts laugh. The cousin takes it, and takes it well, and does not stop smiling.

"Big B." He says it to the room after Aryaman has gone three steps. "Big B kaise kar lete the aaj yeh?"

"Haan." Aryaman does not turn round. "Aur tum log kaise kar lete ho?" 

Purvi is by the pillar with a file against her chest. Black kurta, silver at the ear, the dark-blue spectacles folded into her waistband because she has learned not to wear them on this floor.

"Assistant." He looks at her a moment. "Tum — wao."

Purvi looks behind herself.

"Tum." He waves the glass at her. "You look great. No, seriously. Yeh eyeliner tum pe bahut suit karta hai. Theek smile ki tarah."

"Sir—"

"Generally main ladkiyon ko compliment nahi deta." He says it to the room, cheerfully. "Unhein pari kaho to unke par nikal jaate hain. Hawa mein udna shuru kar deti hain. Lekin aaj tumhein dekh ke I couldn't help it. Kyunki aaj tum sach mein bahut beautiful lag rahi ho."

The room has heard it. Two of the cousins have heard it. The woman with the clipboard has heard it.

The sound table stands at the far end under a sheet of plastic, and the leads come off it in a bundle.

"Sir." Purvi holds the file up. "Yeh meri file hai."

"Yeh teri file hai." He takes it. "Yes sir."

"Soft copy main aapko mail kar deti hoon."

"Mail kar dena." He hands it back. "Par ab jao, aur party enjoy karo."

Purvi goes. He watches her go, and then he turns round and finds three cousins looking at him.

The pillar she has been standing against has a garland half down it and the rest of it on the floor.

"Kya?"

"Kya ho gaya aapko?" The nearest one has his hand over his mouth. "Yeh ladki beautiful kahan se hai? Kisi bhi angle se nahi aai."

"Tu stupid hai." Aryaman says it pleasantly. "Main tujhe stupid kehta hoon kyunki tujhe bura lagega. Aur yeh"—he puts a hand on the next cousin's shoulder—"hamara premium quality kameena cousin. Yeh sabse bada leechad insaan hai."

"Sure, bro." That cousin spreads his hands. "Hum cousins hain. Kitna humour to chalta hai na. Big B kaise kar lete aaj ye?"

Aryaman walks away with his glass.

"Zyada khush mat ho tum log." The third one says it behind him, and does not say it quietly. "Game abhi baaki hai. Aur Karan ko game palatna bahut achhe se aata hai."

"Vihaan!" A woman in a red lehenga comes through the middle of the floor with a glass in each hand, and she is one of the bride's cousins, and she has been looking at him since seven. "Tum yahan chhup ke khade ho? Sangeet mein chhupna mana hai."

She puts one glass into his hand and stands very close, and she says the next thing with her chin down and her eyes up, and it is a thing she has done before and had work before.

Vihaan looks at her, and takes the glass out of his own hand and puts it on a tray going past, and takes a step back that is exactly one step.

"Aapka glass garm hai." He says it politely. "Aur main kaam kar raha hoon."

"Kaam?" She laughs. "Meri shaadi mein bhi aise hi kaam karoge?"

"Main aapki shaadi mein nahin hoon." He looks past her at his two guests. "Aapki behen ki shaadi mein hoon, aur main is ghar ka hisaab rakhta hoon."

She stands there with her mouth a little open, and her charm is all still on her face and none of it is landing anywhere, and she does not understand yet that it is not landing.

Behind her the bride's side of the hall is still dancing, and the drum has not slowed down for any of it.

At the back of the hall, near the service door, the older of the two women with their heads covered says one line to the younger, under the music.

"Us par kuch nahin chalta."

"Kya?"

"Woh ladka." The older one does not look at him. "Us par jaadu nahin chalta. Na kisi ka, na hamara."

The younger one looks across the hall at the man by the side table, and looks away, and looks back.

At the side table by the window, where the noise is less, Vihaan stands with two men in suits and his own glass still full in his hand.

"Aapke ghar mein shaadi hai." The older of the two has to lean in. "Main samajhta hoon. Aap andar jaaiye, hum kal baat karenge."

"Main andar se aaya hoon." Vihaan puts his glass down on the tray of a boy going past. "Aur aap yeh mat samajhiye ki main aapse isliye baat kar raha hoon ki mujhe ghar mein jagah nahin mili. Main isliye kar raha hoon ki aapke senior kal subah nau baje mere office mein honge, aur unke paas do hafte ka waqt hai."

"Mr Kessler ka sochna hai—"

"Mr Kessler ka sochna mujhe pata hai." He says it evenly. "Mr Kessler ko Mahamahakumbh ka feed chahiye, aur us desh mein koi doosra channel woh feed nahin de sakta. Unke paas option hai, Mr Menon. Mujhe option nahin chahiye. Mujhe date chahiye."

"Chhote sahab." A boy is at his elbow with his hands folded. "Tauji bula rahe hain. Photograph ke liye sab wait kar rahe hain."

"Abhi."

"Woh keh rahe hain abhi."

"Abhi, Shyam." He does not turn his head. "Menon saab, do hafte. Kumbh ke dinon mein aapki platform pe sirf mera feed. Do hafte ka likha hua, aur kal nau baje main sign karta hoon."

"Vihaan." Parmeet is there himself, six feet away, with his hand up for the photographer. "Photo."

Vihaan looks at his father for a moment. Then he picks his glass up off the tray it went to and puts it back down again untouched.

"Ho gaya, Papa." He goes, and at his father's shoulder he says, quietly, for the two of them: "Maine aapko bulaya nahin tha. Agar aap bulana chahte hain to mera naam lena padega."

"Neeche tera naam bahut hai aaj." Parmeet smiles for the camera. "Stand there. Wahan. Edge pe."

## Mehendi

The back of the Suri house at nine in the evening, with a bulb strung up over the courtyard and cushions down on the ground, and the mehendi artist on one of them with her box open beside her and her own hands brown to the wrist. Three women are round her. Roma is lying half across a bolster with her hand out.

"There you are." Roma does not lift her head. "Purvi, aa na."

"Arre, achha achha." Ananta is behind her with her hands on her shoulders. "Aap pehle isko laga dijiye. Yahan baith. Haath de."

Purvi sits. The woman takes her left hand and turns it over and begins at the wrist.

The box of mehendi stands open beside the cushion with the cones laid out in a row and a cloth under them.

Vihaan comes into the courtyard and sits down on the edge of the next cushion and puts his right hand out flat, and the artist laughs at him and takes it, and he sits there with his hand out while she works and the women talk over his head.

"Wah." Roma sits up on her elbow. "Tu to fauran maan gayi."

"Kyun nahi maanegi?" Ananta crouches down beside her and looks at the palm, and her voice changes. "Bhabhi, I think isko koi pasand hai."

"Kya—"

"Likh do." Ananta is grinning now, and the women are grinning with her. "Likh do. Aap iske uska naam bhi likh do."

"Ab kya likhoon naam?" Purvi pulls her hand back, and the artist catches it and pulls it down again. "Nahin, aisa koi nahi hai."

"Aisa koi hai nahi?" Ananta's eyes are bright. "Mujhse chhupa rahi hai."

"Nahin Deep." Purvi's voice is flat and quiet and it does not wobble. "Aisa kuch bhi nahi hai. Aur jab koi hai hi nahi, to kiska naam likhwaungi?"

"Ek akshar." Roma is up on her elbow now and the whole cushion has turned to listen. "Ek akshar bata de. Baaki hum khud jod lenge."

The artist's brush stops in the air over the palm, and waits, because the women are waiting.

Somewhere at the back of the house a pressure cooker goes and nobody gets up for it.

"V."

The room goes off all at once. Vikrant. Varun. Vineet. Vishal. The cousins shout them across each other, and somebody says Vijay and somebody says Vivek, and the artist, who has been doing this for eleven years and knows what a room wants, puts a V into the middle of the palm and begins to fill it in with vine.

Ananta crouches there with her hands on her sister's shoulders and does not shout a name with the rest of them.

"Deep?" Purvi does not turn her head. "Kya hua?"

"Kuch nahi." Ananta's hands come off her shoulders. "Kuch nahi. Achha lag raha hai."

She gets up and goes to the far side of the courtyard where the tea is, and stands with her back to the cushion, and the only V she knows in that world is a boy who came out of a lift at two in the morning with a bottle of water and a car.

A bowl of water stands by the artist's knee with a cloth over the edge of it.

The vine goes up the wrist and round the base of the thumb, and by the time the artist is done the letter is under it, and a stranger looking at that hand sees a flower.

## Forty-Eight Hours

In the hall at nine, the chandelier turned down and the sound with it, and Roma takes the microphone.

"Guys, guys, attention." She waits for the room. "Aaj ka din to bach special hai. Full double celebration hai aaj. Yeh mera bhai Karan"—she puts her hand out—"aur yeh mera cousin Ravish, jo ki basically mere sage bhai jaisa hi hai."

The room applauds both of them, and Ravish puts his glass up. Vihaan, at the back of the hall with his phone face down in his hand, claps three times and stops.

"Next forty-eight hours mein inki aazadi chheen ne wali hai." The room laughs. "Toh pehla performance hamare couple ki — maximum noise for Tina and Karan."

The room makes the noise. Karan and Tina stand up, and Tina's mother puts both hands over her mouth, and the two of them go onto the floor with the practised reluctance of people who have been rehearsing for a week.

"Aur yeh rahe Ravish bhai and Ananta."

Ravish finishes his drink first. Ananta is already standing.

The chair the two of them have got up from is pushed back and stays pushed back.

They dance the way people dance at their own sangeet, badly and happily, and the room claps in time, and Gurinder Arora stands at the edge of it with his hands folded and his son-in-law's shoulder under his hand.

Then the floor is open, and the cousins go out on it, and the music changes.

The tray of glasses goes past along the wall and the boy carrying it has his shirt out of his trousers.

"Arjun." Karan comes back to the edge with his collar out. "Kya achha dance kiya aapne."

The two of them look at each other and start laughing.

"Tune aaj apni dawaai nahi li kya?"

"Ismein dawaai ki kya baat bhai?" Arjun spreads his hands. "Dulhe ke bhai ho. Dulhan ki behen ke saath ho. Ek performance to banta hai na."

"Roko." Karan's hands come up. "Chalo na bhai."

"Nahin chal. No chance."

"Come on, bro." One of the cousins is at Purvi's elbow, and Purvi is looking at the floor and then at the ceiling. "Request aai hai dulhan ki behen ki. Aapke saath dance karna chahti hai."

"Kiski request?"

"Purvi." The cousin grins. "Dulhan ki behen to tu hai na? Teri baat kar rahe hain. Ja."

"Dulhe ka bhai aur dulhan ki behen." Purvi says it to nobody, and then, quietly, to herself: "Yaani ki main. Yeh sach mein mujhe bula rahe hain?"

"Haan, sach mein tujhe hi bula rahe hain." The cousin pushes at her back. "Dekh kya rahi hai? Ja. Ja na."

She goes onto the floor, and Arjun takes her hand, and the room claps, and the two of them make one turn and one more and then she is turned out of it and into the crowd, and her shoulder goes into somebody's chest.

"Oh no." Purvi's hands come up. "Sorry — sorry sir. Sorry maam. Mujhe laga ki main chali jaati hoon."

"Let me guess." Aryaman steadies her by the elbow and lets go of it. "Yeh das sisters ka kamaal hai."

"Sir—"

"Ja." He is looking past her, at his cousins. "Ja, enjoy karo."

## The Incident

At the side of the hall, in the chandelier's light, where the sound is less, Tina has her hand on Aryaman's sleeve.

A tray stands on the windowsill beside them with nothing on it but one ring of water.

The curtain is half drawn and the drive beyond it is full of cars with their lights off.

"Bhaiya, I am glad aapne sangeet mein dance kiya." She says it quickly, and then keeps going before he can answer. "I know aapko yeh shaadi aur sangeet bilkul pasand nahi hai. After that incident, par Arjun dobara mujhse—"

"Us incident ki kabhi baat mat karna." Aryaman's voice does not go up. "Mujhe uske baare mein na kuch bolna hai, na kuch sunna hai. Is that clear?"

"Sorry."

"Aur maine un ladkon ke saath dance isliye kiya"—he picks a glass off a passing tray—"kyunki otherwise woh mera peecha nahi chhodte. That's it."

He walks back into the noise.

A glass stands on the floor by the wall with a straw in it.

## The Music Stops

The hall at ten at night, with the chandelier on and the floor full, and the song stops in the middle of a bar.

"Arre, music kyun off hua?" A cousin turns round with his arms out. "Arre DJ wale babu, kahan reh gaye? Yeh gaana kyun band kar diya? DJ, kisne mana kiya yeh bhai?"

The hall goes quiet in a way the hall has not been quiet all evening. Two men are at the sound table with their hands on it and their faces doing nothing.

And under the quiet, from the side of the room where the bride's family is not standing:

"Jaise hi Maa Sapera ji been bajayenge"—the voice is low, and sure of itself—"to pata chal jayega ki aakhir un dono behenon mein se kaun si behen hai jo hamare liye khatra bani hui hai."

"Aaj us naagin ka aakhri din hoga."

At the sound table, Vihaan stands with the channel's shirt on and the vendor's sheet in his hand, and the man beside him is talking into his ear.

Vihaan does not look up from the sheet.

"Yeh kisi ka mann nahi hai. Yeh order hai." He puts his pen down on the table. "Kisne diya?"

"Parmeet sir ne."

Vihaan looks at the hall, and at the four people standing at the side of it, and at the case on the woman's shoulder, and he does not ask anything else.

"Aaj raat is table se jo bhi jaayega"—he turns the sheet over and writes on the back of it—"uski recording mere paas aayegi. Har ghante. Mere laptop pe." He tears the sheet off and gives it to the man. "Aur yeh mera hukm hai, Parmeet sir ka nahin. Samjha?"

"Ji, sahab."

"Kisi ko mat batana." He picks his pen up. "Aur agar is table se kuch aisa jaaye jo aapne pehle kabhi nahin bheja, to mujhe us waqt phone karna, baad mein nahin."

The man folds the sheet into his shirt pocket.

## The Folk Music

The hall at a quarter past ten, with the chandelier on and the floor opened up.

"Dad." Karan comes across the floor. "Yeh kaisa music hai?"

"Arre, folk music beta." Parmeet puts his arm round his son's shoulders. "Folk music."

Vihaan comes across the floor behind his brother and stops when his brother stops, and he looks up at the speakers and not at his father.

## The Hymn, Again
