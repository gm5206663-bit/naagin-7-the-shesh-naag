The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Gate

The lane at one in the morning, and a tube light burning over the gate with the moths going round it, and a paper shade over the tube that has been rained on and gone stiff.

A plastic sheet is tied down over a stack of bricks at the corner of the lane, for the work that has not started.

A tube light burns over the gate and the moths go round it. The car stands outside on the road with its engine off and its lamps still on, and the two of them come up the lane in the wrong light.

"Papa."

"Time." He is in his kurta and slippers with his phone in his hand. "Ho gayi tumhari party khatm. Koi time hai ghar aane ka?" He holds the phone up. "Ek phone karke tum dono bata nahin sakte the ki late ho rahe ho? Kab se call kar rahe hain? Phone kyun nahin utha rahe the?"

"Amma." Ananta has her arm through her sister's and her sari over the side of her face that the tube light is on. "Woh party mein pata hi nahin chala time ka. Aur bahar mausam itna kharab hai ki network bhi nahin tha." Her voice goes soft at the end of it. "Hum bahut thak gaye hain. Subah baat karte hain."

"Subah baat kar sakte." He stands back from the gate. Then he stops, and puts his hand under Purvi's chin. "Ek minute. Ek minute, yeh kya hai? Yeh kya hua? Haan? Yeh chot kaise aayi? Yeh kahan lagi tumhein?"

"Kuch nahin hua, Papa." Purvi turns her face away and then back. "Main gir gayi."

"Haan, woh—" Ananta steps in front of her. "Woh ekdum bhulakkad hai. Kuch ladke party mein mile the, isko congratulations bol rahe the. Iski job lag gayi hai na?"

"Job lag gayi?" His hand comes down.

"Job lag gayi, Papa."

"Arre bhaiya." He looks round at his own gate. "Beta, job lag gayi. Toh bahut khushi ki baat hai."

"Excitement mein jump ki"—Ananta's hands come together—"aur gir gayi, toh chot lag gayi."

Their mother's voice comes from inside the house, coming down the steps, already up before she is out of the door. "Pata hi tha. Meri bachchi badi kaabil hai."

"Sahi hai." The father puts both hands out. "Iski bhi job lag gayi. Tab toh apnon ko late aana banta hi hai. Arre, mast."

"Mummy, bahut neend aa rahi hai." Purvi leans her head on her mother's shoulder. "Aur subah jaldi bhi uthna hai. Hum dono ko."

"Haan, good night, Maa." Ananta touches her feet and then her father's. "Good night, Papa."

"Achha achha." The mother puts her shawl round Purvi and turns her towards the door. "Woh kuch laga dena beta. Main aati hoon. Kuch laga dena. Good night, good night."

The watchman's keys hang in the gate's lock where he left them, and they knock once against the iron and go still.

The lane smells of wet earth and of the drain at the far end of it.

The gate comes shut. The tube light keeps the moths.

Inside the house one window is lit and the rest are dark. On the stairs Purvi's slippers are laid side by side, and one of them is missing its toe strap, and Ananta turns them round the right way with her foot as she goes up.

## The Man in the Room

A room on the ground floor of the channel's building at four in the morning, and the light of the corridor bulb coming in under the door, and a ceiling fan that is not turning. A bed with no sheet. A jug of water on the floor beside a pair of shoes, and a newspaper taped across the window from the inside.

A bulb in the corridor hums, and goes orange, and comes back.

"Woh maar degi mujhe." The man on the bed throws off the hands on him and comes up on his knees. "Maar degi mujhe. Maar degi mujhe."

"Utho—" One of them takes him by the shoulder. "Arre, usey uthao na. Uthao."

"Maar degi mujhe! Chhodo! Chhodo mujhe!" He is holding the pillow against his chest with both arms. "Woh maar degi mujhe. Woh—"

"Kaun?" The oldest of them goes down on one knee beside the bed. "Kaun maar degi? Dekho meri taraf. Kaun?"

"Woh." He lifts his head. "Woh naagin."

The word goes through the room and the three of them do not look at each other.

"Woh naagin hai." He says it to all of them now, and his voice is loud in the small room. "Woh naagin hai! Woh naagin hai!"

Two of them hold him down. The third stands at the foot of the bed and then turns and looks at the door.

The bulb goes out.

"Rajat."

The voice comes from the doorway, low, and the man under the hands on the bed stops fighting.

"Rajat. Yahan kuch nahin hai."

His mouth stays open. The bulb comes back on. There is nobody in the doorway.

Outside in the corridor a boy says *"Sar, sar, sar, woh aapki coffee"* and a voice inside answers him *"Abhi nahin"*, and the corridor goes back to the hum of the bulb.

## Pinky

Morning. The back door of the Suri house stands open on a garden that smells of wet earth and diesel. A gardener kneels on the lawn with a rose branch in his hand, looking at his own thumb.

Inside, the dining table is bare except for a bowl of flowers that has been moved twice. A newspaper lies folded on the sideboard with the crossword half done in pencil. A tray of coffee comes round the corner and stops.

The boy's tray has a cloth over one arm and four cups on it, and he counts them twice on his way across the floor.

"Sir, sir, sir—" The boy holding it has to side-step to keep it level. "Woh aapki coffee."

Pinky takes the tray off him herself and sets it on the table before her brother can lift anything from it.

Pinky is the eldest and has decided that this is a job, not a fact. She is short and wide-hipped and wears her hair in a bun with a pen through it, and her sari is always pinned in three places because she is always in a hurry. She has her mother's face and her mother's voice and has never once in her life been able to let a brother leave the house without eating. "No, no, no, no. Bhai, I am so sorry, but aaj aap nahin drink kar sakte."

"You can ask me why." Aryaman sits back. "Pinky, why?"

"Woh aap poochh sakte hain. Why Pinky why?" She lifts the cup off the tray herself and puts it down beside her. "Kyunki aaj Karan bhai ke rishte ke liye ladki waale aane waale hain. Unki shaadi ki date bhi toh fix karni hai na, bhai."

"Toh?"

"Toh." She picks up the tray again. "Ab woh cousin—" She stops, and starts again. "Uski bhi barbaadi shuru ho hi gayi."

"And the Bombay people?"

"Arre, Bombay waale toh sirf baatein hi karte rehte hain." She is already going. "Kya hai Bombay mein? Jisko dekho ramp walk karta rehta hai. Vihaan!"

Vihaan comes down the back stairs with his sleeves rolled; he has been in the building since one and home since five, and the house has decided that he will therefore attend to the guests.

"Jaa ke apni shirt badal le. Ladki waale aa rahe hain, dhobi nahin." She does not break stride, and a girl from the floor takes the tray off the boy's hands and carries it away.

"Kaun si shirt?"

"Neeli waali." She is walking. "Aur baal mein kanghi kar lena. Aur front room mein pair phaila ke mat baithna, kuch bhi ho jaaye, kyunki Tauji dekhenge."

"Woh Tina ko dekhenge."

"Woh sab kuch dekhenge." Her voice goes round the corner ahead of her. "Hamesha sab kuch dekhte hain."

He comes back down in the blue shirt two minutes before he is wanted, and the girl from the floor carries the tray of cups in after him, because in this house the cups are not his.

On the step the two crates of last night's bottles stand empty and wet and waiting for the kabadi man, and the house has had him back since five, asleep through the hour the rain actually came.

Then he goes up the back stairs two at a time, and the camera stays on the step, and on the bottles.

## The Khuranas

By eleven the front room is full and the curtains stand open on a grey morning. Sofa covers changed. Good glasses out. A caterer's boy goes through with a tray of kebab on sticks and is pointed from one side of the room to the other by four people at once.

"Nanand ji." The woman has one foot still in the doorway. "Mujhe bataiye, ghar kaisa lag raha hai?"

"Ghar kaisa lag raha hai?" The aunt puts her hands on her hips and looks round her own front room. "Tumhare class ki tarah hi ghar dikh raha hai. Yeh lo, maine bata diya."

"Sirf isliye ki maine baais saal padhaya hai. Yeh ek beemari hai."

"Ladki waale marble waale ghar se aa rahe hain, aur mere paas main hoon."

Vihaan comes through between them. The blue shirt. Collar open, sleeves turned back once, and his hair still wet where he has pushed it down with his hand.

The woman who taught for twenty-two years stops in the middle of her sentence, and the aunt reaches past him into the box on the side table and takes one and holds it up to the old woman.

"Nimrat's boy?"

"Nimrat ka chhota." Pinky says it from the doorway, and there is a beat in it. "Vihaan. Woh London mein tha."

The woman looks at him once, frankly, the way women of her age look at a boy who has turned out well.

"Bada ho gaya." She says it to Pinky and she does not look away from him. "Bada ho gaya, haan. Bahut acchha lag raha hai. Nazar na lage."

The aunt, passing with the box on her arm, puts one into the old woman's hand and gets one back on her own wrist for the trouble.

The room laughs, and the old woman laughs loudest of all, and the sweet in the aunt's hand is forgotten.

"Nimrat bhi aisi hi thi." She says it comfortably, to the room, the way women of her age say true things at a party. "Mithai parhosi mein baant kar khud ek bhi nahin khaati thi. Aadhi raat tak rasoi mein khadi rehti thi, aur subah poochhti thi ki meetha theek tha ya nahin."

The room does not laugh at that.

"Kab gayi woh?" The old woman turns the sweet over. "Chaubees saal, hai na?"

"Chaubees." Pinky says it from the doorway, and her voice has gone up a little. "Aunty, aapki chai—"

"Ek din bhi nahin bataya kisi ne." The old woman bites the sweet. "Woh log chale gaye, aur poora mohalla poochhta raha. Aur aap log aise baithe ho jaise kuch hua hi nahin."

Parmeet's hand comes down flat on the sofa cover and stays there.

"Aunty ji." He says it pleasantly, without turning round. "Mithai khatam ho rahi hai. Vihaan, ek box aur kholo."

"Ji, Papa."

"Sharam nahin aati?" Pinky says it from the doorway.

"Aati hai," he says, and goes.

Then she turns back to the sofa covers, because the covers are the thing that matters.

He sets a carton on the side table for the room to fall on.

The caterer's man comes in through the far door with a folder against his chest, looking round for whoever is in charge.

"Sahab—"

"Vihaan se poochh lo. Woh hisaab dekhta hai."

The man comes across the room with his folder open. "Sahab, mithai do darjan kam hai. Mandap ke saamne rakhenge to—"

"Mandap se pehle pandrah minute mein lao." Vihaan does not lift his head from the folder.

The man's mouth closes on the rest of his sentence and he goes.

Pinky, at the doorway, has her eyebrows up.

The man finds him at the side table with the folder open in his hands and reads the third page.

"Chhote waale chalees aur. House account pe daal do. Subah main sign kar doonga."

"Ji sahab." The man goes out through the passage at once, and the room goes on with what the room is doing, because the room has never had a reason to stop.

"Aa gaye." Their mother has both arms open before the two of them are through the door. "Yeh lo, mere Karan-Arjun aa gaye."

Both of them come in together, Karan first in a dark bandhgala with the seam showing along the shoulder, and Arjun half a step behind in a shirt the colour of new milk.

"Oh God." She takes her elder son's face in both hands. "Kitna handsome lag raha hai mera bachcha. Thank you, Maa. Nazar ka kaala teeka laga do, koi laga do. Kahan hai?"

"Maa, main aaj bahut khush hoon." Karan bends and touches her feet and then the aunt's. "Mere aur Sameer ki dosti aaj rishtedaari mein badal rahi hai. Aur yeh hua hai iski wajah se."

"Tauji." Pinky's father comes out of the passage with his reading glasses in his hand. "Yeh toh—" He opens both arms. "Aashirwaad le. Le, le."

"Arre, ladki waale yahan hain." The aunt is laughing. "Celebration pehle hi chalu ho gaya."

Parmeet puts both hands on Karan's head. "May you be as capable as your brother."

"Aur kya hua hai, bhai?" Arjun speaks low, with his shoulder to his cousin's, and a sweet in his hand. "Tumhari shaadi hone waali hai Tina ke saath, yaar. Tauji ke best friend ki beti hai woh. So why that super-fixed smile?"

"Ek choice, voice—" Karan's voice stays low and flat. "Kuch nahin hai. Main jo bhi kar raha hoon apne matlab ke liye kar raha hoon. Matlab dekh." He turns his head. "Meri shaadi toh honi thi. Tina Tauji ke best friend ki beti hai, aur Tina ke dad hain minister. Toh I am definitely sure ki mujhe bahut bada gift milne waala hai. Jaise ki unka channel."

Vihaan stands at the end of the table with nothing in his hands.

The sister takes one sweet and then a second. The aunt comes past him and takes one too, and does not look at him, and the room moves on.

## The Kundali

The front room at half past twelve, with the trays cleared to the sideboard and the sun off the drive coming in at the window and lying in a bright square on the floor, and a man outside washing a car and the water going down the slope of it in two streams.

The minister's men have left their phones face down in a row on the arm of the sofa.

The minister comes at half past twelve with two men and a driver, and he has his hand out from the doorway before his foot is over the step.

"Minister sahab." Parmeet takes it in both of his. "Kaise ho? I am so happy to see you."

"The traffic. Forgive me."

"Aaiye, aaiye." He draws him in by the arm. "Aaiye, idhar aaiye. Baithiye. Baithiye yahan. Paani? Koi paani laao."

"Pyaas lagi hai. Paani milega?"

"Ji, baithiye, baithiye."

A boy of the caterer's goes out to the dining room and comes back with the jug and four glasses on a tray and sets them down at the priest's elbow.

The priest looks up. He looks at the boy's face for a second and no longer, and then down at his pages, and his thumb goes on turning them.

Two horoscopes lie open in front of him and a red cloth and a small brass box wait at his hand.

The priest's pages stand in a cloth bag at his feet with the strap under the leg of his chair.

The jug and four glasses stand between them on the table, and the jug has sweated a ring onto the wood.

"Main ek bada gift dene wala hoon. Mera channel." Parmeet says it with his arm still round the minister's shoulders, and the minister answers him *"Oh yes, yes"* for the pair of them, and they both laugh.

"Yeh mere Karan ki kundali hai." Parmeet sits down opposite him. "Zara Tina ki kundali mila kar bataiye na."

"Ek second." The priest turns one page and then the other and reads, and turns the first one round to face the room. "Kundaliyan toh dono ki bahut acchhi mil rahi hain. Bahut acchhi."

"Toh Pandit ji, agar kundaliyan mil gayi hain toh rishte ki baat shuru karein."

"Lekin ek samasya hai." He puts a finger on a line. "Yeh bitiya ki kundali mein Mangal ka dosh hai. Toh gun acchhe se mil nahin rahe hain."

"Pandit ji." The minister leans forward. "Koi upaay hoga?"

"Upaay toh hai hi."

"Kya upaay hai? Zara vistar se bataayenge." Parmeet has both hands out. "Matlab kya karenge aap?"

"Bitiya ko le jaayenge." The priest says it plainly, the way a man says a thing he has said a hundred times. "Pandrah minute mein ped ke saath shaadi karaayenge. Aur uske baad uska jo Mangal ka dosh hai, woh ped ke oopar chala jaayega. Toh dono ki shaadi ho sakti hai phir."

"Wow." The aunt has her hand at her mouth. "Lekin phir ped ka kya hoga?"

"Usey kaat denge."

"Dekhiye." Parmeet has his chin up. "Is duniya mein insaan kam ho na ho, ped zaroor kam hote ja rahe hain. Aur pedon ki humein zyada zaroorat hai. You know, for oxygen?"

"Woh toh hai. Lekin kya hai ki ped nahin katega."

"Ped nahin katega, theek hai." Parmeet puts his palm flat on the table. "Insaan kar sakta hai. Aisa karte hain, iski shaadi insaan se kara dete hain. Phir insaan ko kaat denge."

The book lies open on the table with the priest's thumb in it, and the corner of the page is soft from being turned.

The room laughs. The minister laughs with his head back. Karan laughs with his eyes shut.

## The Colour of Blood

The front room at one in the afternoon with the curtains half drawn against the sun and the priest's book open on the table in front of him.

The priest puts his pen down.

"Yeh mrityughaat ki baat kyun karte hain?" He looks round the room, and the room goes quiet. "Lekin yeh ghaat jaat nahin jaanta. Kundali mein jo likha hota hai, woh toh hoke hi rahta hai."

"Jyotish ji, yeh upaay ki baat toh aapne ki thi na?" Parmeet's hands are up. "Yeh aapko kya mazaak lag raha hai?"

"Toh yeh bataiye." The priest's eyes come round. "Aapki mrityu kis maheene mein hogi? Assi saal par aapko kuch nahin hoga. Aazma ke dekhein."

The minister's men look at the floor.

"Nahin, nahin, main kya bolta hoon." Parmeet sits back, and the smile comes back onto his face, and does not go anywhere near his eyes. "Koi upaay karne ki zaroorat nahin hai. Ab aap shaadi kar sakte hain. Upaay ke bina bhi kar sakte hain."

"Thank God." Karan lets his breath out. "Thank God aapne mujhe bacha liya. Varna yeh log toh meri shaadi ped se karwa dete."

"Yeh shaadi na jaane kitni maut ka karan banegi." The priest says it to his own book, and his voice is not loud. "Is shaadi mein rang toh dikhega. Lekin sindoor ka nahin." He closes the book. "Khoon ka rang."

Nobody in the room has heard him. Trays come in. The minister's men stand up. Parmeet carries the priest's bag out to the passage himself, and the priest takes it from him at the door and goes down the steps.

At the bottom step he stops and turns and looks up at the door.

"Khoon ka rang," he says again, to the door, and the driver swings the car out into the road.

Upstairs the empty glasses are collected by the girl from the floor, one tray, and she takes it down the back way.

"Yeh dekho." Parmeet stands in the middle of his front room with one arm round his son's shoulders. "Hua kya hai, sab dekho. Aryaman, tumne meri Tina ko khatron se bacha liya. I am really proud of you." He takes him by both shoulders and turns him. "Isiliye aaj se yeh mera channel tumhara."

"Tauji—"

"Arre, sun. Aaj se mera channel tumhara." He holds a finger up. "Jis tarah tumne apne channel ko itni oonchaiyon pe le aaye ho, usi tarah I am confident ki tum hamare channel ko bhi wahin tak le jaoge. Aur aaj se hum dono channels ko collaborate karenge. Aur usko head karega—" He turns to the room. "Yeh."

The clapping starts at the back and comes forward. The minister takes Aryaman's hand and holds it and says, to Parmeet, *"Apni beti ko mere gale mein daal de"*; Pinky has her hands together at her mouth. Somebody says *congratulations, sir* and somebody says it again.

"Aaj mere channel ka gift aa gaya." The aunt is wiping her eye with her thumb. "Rishte ki baat ke beech mein, channel ka gift aa gaya."

Karan claps twice and then puts his hands in his pockets and looks at the carpet.

Aryaman crosses the room to him while the room is still clapping.

"Karan. Sun." He keeps his voice low and his eyes on him. "Yeh okey, na?"

"Of course."

"Dekh." Aryaman puts a hand on his cousin's shoulder. "Jo kuch mera hai, woh tera bhi hai. Yeh kabhi bhoolna mat."

"Okey."

## Breaking News

The front room with the afternoon coming in flat through the curtains, and a tray of cups on the floor where the boy set it down and went out, and the mother's spectacles on the table with one arm folded back.

The front door bangs. The youngest of them comes in with a phone in one hand and her shoes in the other, talking before she is through the passage.

"What is this? Mera phone kyun kaata?" She has one shoe on and the other in her hand. "Maine usko guest house mein rakha hai. Kahin nahin jaayega. Saaf kahunga usse, kahin nahin jaayega."

"Kyunki tum meeting ke waqt call karti ho."

"Mujhe tumhein ek bahut badi breaking news deni hai."

"Kya?"

"Mere ek dost ko saanp ne attack kiya." She holds the phone up. "Lekin woh koi chhota-mota saanp nahin tha. Pata hai, ek bahut badi naagin thi. Aisi naagin jise aaj se pehle kisi ne nahin dekha."

The clapping stops. The room turns.

"Woh keh raha tha ki woh koi ladki thi jo naagin ban gayi. Something like that." She puts the phone against her chest and says the rest from memory, with the cadence of something learned off a channel. "Na naari, na nar. Na jaanwar, na insaan. Na farishta, na shaitaan. Bhagwan par bhagwan ki sabse badi bhakt. Ichchhadhari naagin. Shesh Naagin."

A folded paper stands under somebody's arm at the door, and it does not come out.

A cup goes over on the tray on the floor, and nobody in the room looks at it.

Nobody moves in the room.

Her phone goes on lighting up in her hand, and she turns it over on her palm.

"Kya breaking news di hai tumhein?" Aryaman's voice comes from the middle of it, flat, and the score goes out from under the room. "Hum ek news channel chalate hain. Yeh mangadhanth kahani ka platform nahin hai. Hum yeh news nahin run karenge." He looks at her. "You know why? Because this is all rubbish."

"Bhai, we are getting late for the meeting." One of the cousins has his phone out. "Come na."

There is a silence. Into it, from the side of the room, comes an older man's voice, and it is not steady.

"Kahin woh naagin toh nahin jiski baat us scientist ne ki thi? Tumhein yaad hai? Usne kaha tha—" He is not looking at anybody. "Hum sab ko maarne— na nar, na naari, na insaan, na jaanwar, na farishta, na bhagwan. Humein maregi ichchhadhari naagin."

"Aur kahin aisa toh nahin hai"—the cousin again, and his laugh comes out wrong at the end of it—"ki woh aa gayi hai. Aur woh bhi tab, jab hum apne sabse bade mission ko anjaam dene waale hain."

"Nahin nahin." The older man shakes his head. "Itne saal ho gaye. Ab tak toh usne kuch kiya nahin."

"Yeh itne saal humne kya kiya hai?" Parmeet has not moved from the middle of the room. "Sirf paise hi toh kamaye."

## Find Out Who She Is

The sun has gone round to the west of the house and the marble of the front room holds the light of it, and the sofa covers have been straightened twice.

"Agar us scientist ki bhavishyavani sahi hai—" Parmeet says it into the middle of the room, and stops, and then he says the rest of it. "Toh matlab naagin hai."

Nobody answers him.

"Tauji." Karan steps out of the line of the room and stands in front of the old man. "Woh naagin ho sakti hai. Mujhe lagta hai ki yeh news humein cover karni chahiye."

"Tauji." Arjun comes up beside him. "Bahut saare log TRP ke liye kuch bhi jhoothi khabrein dikha rahe hain. Lekin agar yeh khabar sach nikli—" He looks round the room. "Toh humara channel woh sabse pehla channel hoga jisne yeh news cover ki ho."

"It will be great," Karan says, and his uncle's face does not change, and Parmeet says over it: "Bhai sahib, iski baat."

"Us naagin ka pata lagao."

"Māin pata karta hťn." Karan has his chin up. "Aur main yeh sabko dikha doonga ki Karan Suri mein bhi dam hai."

Vihaan comes in through the passage door with his tie already off and his collar open, and stands at the end of the table where the chairs are pushed back, and the room looks at him because the room is his house and he is the one man in it who had to be called.

"Baithe rahiye." Parmeet does not turn round. "Tu bhi sun le. Yeh tera bhi channel hai."

"Main khada hoon."

"Toh khada reh." Parmeet puts both hands on the back of his chair. "Karan ko list chahiye, file chahiye, hisaab chahiye. Channel ki, police ki, apne logon ki. Number wale kaam tere liye hain — tu unhein dhoondh, unka hisaab report kar. Do din."

"Ji."

"Aur yeh file ghar ke bahar nahin jaayegi." Parmeet's voice does not change at all. "Jo bhi tu dhoondhe, woh is ghar ke saamne aayega. Chahe woh tera bhai ho, chahe teri maa ho, chahe—" He stops. "Chahe koi bhi ho."

"Ji."

"Aryaman." Parmeet turns his back on both of them. "Ab bata, tu kya jaanna chahta hai?"

"Achha hai." Aryaman has his arms folded and he says it to the middle of the room and not to Vihaan. "Hum bhi jaanna chahte hain ki aakhir yeh naagin hai kaun."

The file is put in Vihaan's hand in the front room, in front of both his brothers, by a nephew of Parmeet's who has been told to fetch it; it is a blue office file with a white label on it and the label is blank.

He takes it out through the passage and up the stairs and does not open it in the passage, and the room behind him begins to talk about a naagin again and this time the talking has his name in it.

In his own room he sits on the edge of the bed and opens the file.

The first page is the channel's staff list. Every person who has joined in the last two years, name and designation and date, printed off the personnel system and stapled in at the front by somebody with a long stapler.

Vihaan turns it over, and there, four names down, in the block for this month, printed and not written:

**Purvi Arora. Trainee. Appointment letter issued.**

Vihaan sits with the file open on his knees and looks at the line for a while.

Then he closes the file and puts it under his hand and looks at the window, and the window has the lane in it and the lane has a car going down it, and he does not move until the car has gone.

## Purnima

Out in the road the caterer's boy folds his table and ties it to the back of a scooter. The lane goes quiet. The light over the Suri gate comes on and the moths come to it.

A cart stands at the corner of the lane with a lamp on it and the man asleep under the lamp.

The moon comes up over the city, and there is no cloud in front of it.

"Purnima ki raat kuch der aur." The voice is not in that lane. It is in a hall of stone with lamps in it. "Phir unki asli roop mein aane ki shuruat."

The lamps stand straight.

"Aaj ki raat unka shareer apni kechuli chhodega. Aaj Purnima ki raat hai. Chaand nikalne se chaubees ghanton ke andar har naag-naagin apni kechuli badalte hain."

"Sharnagatam."

The word comes out of the dark behind the lamps, and the bell at the door moves.

"Kshan bhi hai tu. Tu hi srishti bhi hai." Her voice goes up the pillars and comes down again. "Tu hi samay bhi hai."

"Hum chaah ke bhi unki madad nahin kar sakte." The other voice, older, and it is grieved. "Unhein is dard se guzarna hi hoga. Yeh dard unhein bahut takleef dega."

"Anant kul ki naagin ka janm hone waala hai."

"Woh naagin..." The first voice stops.

"Aaj usey uski jadein yaad aayi." Uttara's lamp comes up. "Yeh mandir. Yeh gaddi. Usey bula rahi hai."

In the Arora house the television is off and the dishes are done and the lights go out one after another. On the first floor a girl sleeps with her arm over her face, and her calendar hangs from the bedpost by its string, and the moon comes in and lies across the bed and does not wake her.
