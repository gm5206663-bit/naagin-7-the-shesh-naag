The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Constable

The police van stands at the mouth of the lane with one door open and its light still going round, and it is not going round for anything.

The constable has his notebook on the bonnet and his cap pushed back, and he has been writing for ten minutes.

"Kal raat ko us bheediye ne mujh pe hamla kiya." Nandini says it for the third time, and her voice is steady now the way a voice goes steady after a night of not sleeping. "Lekin phir uske baad kya hua, ye mujhe nahin pata."

"Aapke bhai?"

"Doctors injection dekar gaye hain. Rohan ko abhi tak hosh nahin aaya." Her hand goes to her own throat. "Aur meri behen Purvi kahan mil nahin rahi thi. Aur mummy papa ghar pe nahin hain. To please meri help kar dijiye."

"Mil gayi?" The constable looks up.

"Main yahin hoon."

Purvi stands at the edge of the road with a shawl over her salwar and her hair still wet from the tap, and her spectacles are in her hand and the frame of them is broken at one corner.

The constable looks at her for a while.

"Aap toh keh rahi thi kisi bheediye ne hamla kiya hai." He puts the pen down. "Lekin dekh ke lagta nahin ki aapko gambhir chot hain."

"Nahi."

"Kahan thi aap?"

"Idhar hi." She points down the lane, at the broken brick and the scrub and the plot where the buildings stop. "Giri thi."

The constable writes it.

Behind him, in the lane, a dog comes out of a doorway, and looks at the four of them, and goes back into the doorway.

"Aapka number mere paas hai." He tears a page out and gives it to Nandini. "Aas-paas ke area mein chhanbeen karke jaldi inform karte hain."

The van goes. The light stops going round and the lane is only morning again.

## Five or Six Things

The side of the Arora house at eight in the morning, and the tap is still running where somebody left it, and Purvi turns it off, and stands with her hand on the tap.

A bucket stands under the tap with a mug in it, and the mug has a chip out of the rim.

A tray of tea things stands by the door of the house, and the tea in the cups has gone cold.

The washing on the line behind the house is still up from yesterday, and the night's damp is still in it.

Purvi remembers the running. She remembers the dark going past her on both sides, and the shouting behind her, and her own voice going out ahead of her down the lane.

After that she remembers falling.

"Mujhe nahi pata ki main bachi kaise." She says it to the tap. "Mujhe itna yaad hai ki main giri. Aur phir kuch ajeeb ajeeb cheezein chadhne lag gayi. Paanch chhe cheezein. Ek saath."

Purvi turns her own wrist over and looks at it. There is nothing on it.

The ground where she woke is three feet from the tap and it is dry. The rest of that yard is wet from the night and dark in a patch round the drain, and the patch she woke on is dry, and the dry patch is the size of a girl lying down.

She stands on it and does not see it.

"Purvi!" Her mother, from the kitchen door. "Andar aa. Naashta thanda ho raha hai. Aur wo chashma kahan hai?"

"Toot gaya."

## What Ananta Said

The Arora house at nine in the morning, with the sun lying in one bar across the floor and the fan going over it.

Ananta sits on the edge of the cot with her feet not touching the floor, and her dupatta over her head, and her hand flat on her own stomach.

The glass of water on the stool beside the cot has not been touched, and the dust has settled in it.

"Faltu us Ravish ke liye itna royi." Her eyes are dry. "Itna pareshaan hui. Aur uske liye kya mila?"

"Didi—"

"Mere liye tum log ahmiyat rakhte ho." She says it plainly, to her sister, in the small room with the fan going. "Bas. Ab main sirf tum logon ke baare mein sochungi. Aur apne hone wale bacche ke baare mein."

The fan turns. The newspaper on the floor lifts at one corner and lies down again.

"Aur us aadmi ka naam is ghar mein nahin." She gets up. "Chai banegi?"

## The Sample

The animal's body is on a tarpaulin in the yard behind the Suri house in the morning sun, and the vet has come and gone, and there is a collar on the study desk with a brass plate on it and no name.

"Bhai sahab, doctor ka kehna hai ki iski body mein zeher tha." The man keeps his eyes on the desk. "Bahut zyada."

A steel tray stands on the ground with a knife on it, and the knife has been wiped and put down and left.

A bucket of water stands by the wall with a cloth over the top of it.

Parmeet turns the collar over with one finger.

"Johro toh un dono ladkiyon ko khatam karne gaya tha." The second man says it to the room. "Aur wapas nahi aaya. Matlab jisne johro ki jaan li hai wo ichchhadhari naagin se bhi takatwar hai."

"Ya phir wo dono ladkiyan ichchhadhari naagin hain." Parmeet says it without looking up. "Aur hamare paas ek hi bheji hui jaanwar thi."

Nobody in that room says anything.

"Zeher ka sample le kar Mahasapere ke paas jao." He puts the collar down. "Humein jaanna hai kaun hai hamara naya zehreela dushman. Ichchhadhari naagin, ya koi aur."

"Aur agar wo sach mein ichchhadhari naagin hui—"

"To phir duniya dekhegi." Parmeet stands. "Ki Parmeet Suri maut ka khel kaise khelta hai."

The collar sits on the desk with its brass plate turned up, and the plate catches the tube light once as the door closes.

## Organic Mehendi

The front room of the Suri house at ten in the morning, with the light of the windows on the cushions, and two women on the sofa with their hands out and a girl on a stool between them with a cone.

A tray of mehendi cones stands on the table with one of them split and leaking onto the tray.

"Mujhe hamesha se organic mehendi chahiye thi." Tina turns her hand over. "Waise ye mehendi lag to badi kamaal ki rahi hai. Dekhiye, kaise lagate hi rang chhod gaya."

The dish of bangles on the floor by the girl's stool has half of them still in their wrappers.

The tin of powder is open on the arm of the sofa with its lid lying on the cushion.

"Haath dhokar aaoon?"

The smell of the mehendi is in the whole room and it is stronger than the powder.

"Haan." Tina waits until the girl has gone, and then says it to Roma, in a voice pitched for the room. "You know, tumhare dad ko tumhari stepmother se bada pyaar hai."

"Haan."

"Isliye to wo mujhe batati rehti hai ki meri shaadi pe wo kab, kahan, aur kitne paise kharch kar rahi hai." Her chin comes up. "Jabki saare paise mere dad ke hi hain. And you won't believe it — meri shaadi pe har chhote bade kharch karne se pehle mujhe uski permission leni padti hai."

"I hate her."

"I hate her." Tina puts her chin back down. "Dekho, meri mehendi pe bhi comment karengi. Ye design purana hai, ye design mehnga hai."

Roma laughs, and then her phone goes, and she looks at it, and her face changes.

"Kya?"

"Kuch nahin. Office." Roma puts the phone face down on the cushion. "Purvi ka message hai. Wo poochh rahi hai ki aaj chhutti milegi ya nahin."

"Hai na?" Tina is looking at her own palm. "Uska boss to tum ho."

"Uska boss Aryaman hai." Roma's hand closes. "Main sirf uske saath kaam karti hoon."

The girl comes back with the water and the room goes back to being a room with two brides in it and a wedding in two days.

## Kumbh

The lawn is being dressed for a sangeet that is now two days early, and the men on the ladders are working in the dark with a torch propped on a chair.

A drum of cable stands under the ladder with its rope round it, and the rope goes up over the rung.

A sheet with the mandap plan on it lies on a chair with a brick on top of it.

Parmeet walks the length of it with his hands behind his back and his son beside him.

"Pasha se baat hui Kumbh ke plan ke baare mein?"

"Teen baar phone aa chuke hain." Vihaan has his hands in his pockets. "Poochh rahe hain ki hum taiyar hain ki nahin."

"Tum poori tarah se taiyar ho?"

"Sau percent." He stops at the end of the lawn where the marigold goes up the veranda post in a spiral. "Unke paas land ka paper nahin hai. Unke paas sarkar ka muh hai. Aur hamare paas dono."

Parmeet's face does the thing it does when a figure comes out right.

"Tumhe pata hai ye Kumbh India ka number one tourist destination banne wala hai." He turns and walks back. "Duniya bhar ke rais, ministers, businessmen, global icons — sab yahan aane wale hain. Aur jab wo aayenge na, unke desh ke naam ki goonj duniya bhar mein goonjegi."

"Aur unke saath unke camera aayenge." Vihaan says it to the marigold. "Jo humare ghar ke andar bhi jhaankenge."

Parmeet stops walking.

"Chinta mat karo." He puts his hand on his son's shoulder and takes it off again. "Ghar ke andar jo hai, wo andar hi rahega."

Above them the man on the ladder ties the last knot and the whole spiral of marigold comes alive in the wind and settles.

## The Door in the Passage

The passage on the first floor of the Suri house at noon, with the sun coming in at the far end of it and the doors on both sides shut.

The shouting starts in the first-floor passage with the band still tuning on the lawn.

"Kholo darwaza! Samir, teri himmat kaise hui meri biwi ke saath?"

The guests in the hall put their glasses down and turn their heads the way guests turn their heads — not towards the noise, but away from it, so that they are not seen to have heard.

Vihaan is up the stairs four at a time.

The passage is four feet wide and there are six people in it. A man in a black waistcoat with his fist on a door and his face gone the colour of old brick. His wife's sari is on the floor inside the room. A man called Samir stands in the middle of the room with his shirt half buttoned and says "Kuch bhi to nahin" to nobody in particular.

"Samir." Vihaan says it from the top of the stairs, and he says it at a volume that carries no further than the passage. "Neeche jao. Abhi."

"Vihaan, main—"

"Neeche. Bar mein baitho. Main aata hoon."

Samir goes. The husband turns round with his fist still up, and Vihaan puts his own body between that fist and the stairs, and takes the man's elbow, and walks him, talking, to the small room at the end where the family keeps the loudspeaker equipment.

"Uncle. Andar. Baithiye. Paani laata hoon."

"Wo meri biwi—"

"Main jaanta hoon, uncle. Andar."

By the time Parmeet comes up the stairs, the passage is empty and the guests are looking at the band.

Parmeet shuts the door of the small room behind himself and stands with his back to it.

"Parmeet." Rishabh has his hands on his knees. "Pachaas saal ki dosti hai hamari. Aur ye baat bahar tak jayegi."

"Rishabh—"

"Main jo kehta hoon wo karta hoon. Aur main ye baat bahar le jaunga. Sabko bataunga. Tere plan ki saari pol khol dunga." He is breathing hard. "Teri nayi biwi ke peeche tu pagal hai, theek hai. Lekin agar isne gusse mein kisi ko bata diya ki hum kahan se hain, ki hum yahan pachees saal se reh rahe hain aur hum doosre desh se hain — to hamara poora plan flop. Aur hum khatre mein aa jayenge."

The room is quiet. Outside, the band begins.

"Kya hai yaar." Parmeet's voice comes out soft and easy and it is the softness that is frightening. "Itni purani dosti hai hamari."

"Jhooth bolega to pachhtayega."

Parmeet looks at Rishabh for a long moment. Then he opens the door and calls down the stairs.

"Vihaan. Neeche se Samir ko bhejo. Aur Suvarna ji ko bhi."

## The Girl Who Was Sent For

The passage on the first floor of the Suri house at noon, with the sun coming in at the end of it and the chairs along the wall.

"Uncle, uncle." Parmeet has his arm round the man's shoulders and is walking him back into the passage. "Aap jaise soch rahe ho, bilkul waisa nahin hai."

"To kya hai?"

"Girlfriend hai uski." Parmeet says it with his hand open. "Wo kisi aur se pyaar karta hai. Deewanon ki tarah. Isne meri naak mein dam kar diya hai."

"Girlfriend?"

The passage has two chairs against the wall where the guests have been sitting out the noise, and one of them is still warm.

"Haan. Office ki ek ladki hai." He turns to his son, who is standing at the head of the stairs. "Bulao use yahan. Main use milunga tabhi maanunga."

A fan stands at the end of the passage with its cable taped down along the skirting.

Vihaan goes down the stairs two at a time.

At the bottom, in the hall, the woman who came to the door with her father is standing with her dupatta on her shoulder and her hand on a chair back, and she is looking up at the passage with everybody else.

"Ananta madam?"

"Haan?"

"Parmeet sir aapse milna chahte hain. Abhi."

Her father is a big man in a white shirt with his spectacles in his hand and his sandals still dusty from the road. He puts his spectacles on.

"Main bhi chalta hoon."

They go up. Ananta's father is standing in the passage with his spectacles in his hand, and he looks at the girl in the doorway, and he looks at Parmeet.

"Ye hai wo ladki?"

"Ye hai wo ladki." Parmeet's hand is out. "Ananta. Hamare Ravish ki dost."

Ananta's father stops in the middle of the passage.

"Parmeet sahab." His voice is polite and it is not quiet. "Kal aapke bete ne jo kiya meri beti ke saath—"

"Galtfahmi." Parmeet has both hands up. "Chhoti si galatfahmi ho gayi thi. Mujhe laga hamare bacche hamari zid ke saamne jhuk jaate hain. Lekin nahi. Aaj ki generation jhukti nahin hai."

"Aap mera mazaak uda rahe hain."

"Main aapko apna samdhi bana raha hoon." Parmeet turns and calls down the stairs, and his voice goes over the band. "Ravish! Idhar aa. Idhar."

Ravish comes up. He has a glass in his hand and he puts it on the windowsill before he comes.

"Inke saamne sach-sach bolna." Parmeet's hand is on his son's back. "Tune kal mujhe kya bola tha?"

Ravish looks at Ananta, and Ananta looks at the floor.

"Main Anu se pyaar karta hoon." He says it to the passage. "Sachcha pyaar karta hoon." Then, because his father's hand is still on his back, he says the rest of it. "Aur wo pregnant hai. Aur wo baccha mera hi hai."

Gurinder Arora takes a step back into the wall.

Ananta's father takes his spectacles off.

"Ananta." His voice is very low. "Ye sach hai?"

"Papa." She has her hand on her own stomach now, in front of all of them. "I am really sorry. Mujhe laga nahin tha ki aap allow karenge."

"Tumne mujhe subah bataya bhi nahin tha."

"Ravish." Parmeet is already talking over the top of it, into the passage, into the house. "Ravish ne subah mujhe bataya. Aur main maan gaya." He turns to Gurinder with his hands out. "Dekhiye, Ravish aapki beti se sachcha pyaar karta hai. Isi liye to do din mein shaadi karne ko taiyar ho gaya."

"Do din?"

"Kal sangeet aur mehendi." Parmeet has his palm flat. "Aur parson shaadi. Usi mandap mein, Karan ke saath. Ek ki jagah do-do shaadiyan ho jayengi."

Ananta's father looks at his daughter. Then he looks at Parmeet's hand, which is still out.

"Bacchon ke pyaar ke beech mein nahin aana chahiye," Parmeet says. "Aaj Suri parivaar mein double khushi hai."

He takes his own phone out and calls down to the hall for mithai.

Ananta stands in the passage with her hand on her stomach and her eyes wet and her mouth open on the beginning of something, and her father puts his hand on the back of her head, once, the way a father does.

"Chal." He is not looking at her. "Ghar chalein."

## The Thali

The car is at the gate in the morning light, and Gurinder is on the drive with his keys in his hand and his spectacles in the other, and he is in no hurry to go, and the house knows it.

Gurinder is looking at the side table by the door where the gifts have been stacked, and there is a plate on the top of the stack, and it is silver, and there is a name cut into the rim of it.

"Ye naam ise maine pehle kahin dekha hai." He turns the plate over. "Kora hai yaar?"

"Pushtaini hain, sir." The boy at the door has his hands behind his back. "Bhai sahab ke khaandaan ke. Sab ke naam khudwe hue hain."

Gurinder looks at the plate for a long moment longer than a plate is worth. Then he puts it back on the stack, square, so that nobody can tell it has been moved.

"Chalo." He gets into his car. "Arre, gaadi. Chalo."

The boy watches the car go down the drive and then he goes back to his work, and the plate sits on the stack in the sun.

## The Names on the Thali

The gifts go out of the Suri house at eleven in the morning and come into the Arora house at noon.

Three men carry them. There are cards in a stack, and sweets in boxes, and cloth in paper, and under it all, wrapped in a bedsheet and tied with tape, twelve kilos of silver.

"Gurinder ji." The driver has the last of it in his arms. "Bhai sahab ne ye khaas bheja hai. Ye khaandani aur pushtaini bartan hain."

Gurinder unwraps the sheet on the floor of the front room, and sits back on his heels, and looks at what is inside.

Twelve pieces of silver, plates and bowls and a covered pot, hammered, and on the rim of every one of them the same four names cut into the metal.

"Ye naam ise maine pehle kahin dekha hai." He says it to himself.

"Oye, Kora hai yaar." His wife is already putting her hand in the box of sweets. "Kya naam?"

"Parveen Suri." He turns the plate. "Baljeet Suri. Dimple Suri. Pammi Suri."

The room goes quiet. The fan turns.

"Shalini." He gets up with the plate in both hands. "Attari mein humare padosi the. Wahi Suri parivaar. Main is plate ko saalon pehle Parveen bhai sahab ke ghar dekha tha. Aur unse uske kisse bhi sune the."

"Aap kya kehna chahte hain?"

"Main kuch nahin keh raha." He puts the plate down very carefully and does not let go of it. "Pachees saal pehle har channel pe khabar aa rahi thi. Hamare padosi desh ke jaasoos humare desh mein ghuspeth kar gaye. Aur hamare us Attari ilake mein unke chhupe hone ke chance bahut zyada the. Phir ek khabar aayi ki poora Suri parivaar achanak gayab ho gaya." He looks at his wife. "Kisi ne kaha, maargaye. Kyunki uske baad unhein kisi ne dekha hi nahin."

"Gurinder."

"Aur ab ye ek parivaar. Praveen Suri. Aur family. Wahi jagah, wahi naam." His hands are flat on the table. "Ye ittefaq nahin ho sakta, Shalini."

"Aap apne crime wale serials dekhna band kar dijiye, please." His wife takes the plate out of his hands and puts it with the others, and then, because she is a kind woman, she says the rest of it kindly. "Aap aise acchhe logon pe shak rakhna band kijiye. Main ja rahi hoon. Subah se list bhi milani hai."

Shalini goes out with the fan turning behind her.

Gurinder stands in the middle of his own front room with twelve kilos of silver at his feet and the names on it.

"Kya ye wahi ghuspethiya jaasoos hain," he says, to the empty room, "jinhone asli Suri family ko khatam karke khud unki jagah le li?"

The tube light in the front room flickers once, and holds.

## The Eyelash

The road out past the bypass at ten in the morning, with the sun through the glass of the car lying across the seat between the two of them.

The car smells of the polish the driver uses and of the city coming up the bypass, and the radio is on the weather.

"Aur ab ek weather update. Dilli ka mausam aaj achanak badla hai. Hawaaon mein kuch ajeeb sa hai." The voice on the radio pauses a half second. "Kuch to hone wala hai. Achha ya bura, ye to waqt hi batayega."

A garland of plastic marigold hangs off the rear-view mirror and it has been there long enough to have faded.

A file lies on the seat between them with the corner of a photograph showing out of it.

His spectacles are folded into the door pocket beside him.

Aryaman turns it off with his thumb without taking his eyes off the road.

"Aaj nau baje ki special edit hai." He changes gear. "Tumhe wahan hona hai. Infact tum mere saath chalogi."

"Abhi, sir?"

"Abhi."

The lights of the flyover come down on the car one at a time.

"Aap kaam pe kyun nahin ja rahi ho?" He glances at her. "Aaj tumhare ghar pe bhi shaadi ho rahi hai."

"Sir, aapke ghar pe bhi ho rahi hai."

He makes a sound at that which is almost a laugh and is not one.

"Sir." She is looking at him and then not looking. "Aapke chehre pe vish hai."

"Vish?"

"Jab hamara eyelash toot jata hai to usse wish maangte hain." She points. "Yahan. Nikaal dun?"

"Nikaalo." He turns his cheek a little towards her and keeps his eyes on the road. "Jaldi nikaalo."

Her hand goes out and stops two inches short of his face, and she says, "Wo gir gaya," and puts her hand back in her lap.

"Kya maanga?"

"Main ne aapke liye maanga." She is looking out of the window now. "Love of your life. Ki aapko aapki zindagi mein sachcha pyaar mil jaaye."

Aryaman drives for a while.

"You are funny, unbelievable." He shakes his head. "Tum jaanti ho kitni stupid cheez hai ye pyaar?"

"Sir—"

"Pehle mujhe bhi aisa hi lagta tha." He puts his hand out of the window and pulls it back in. "Logon ko sach mein lagta hai ki koi kisi ke liye ek pal mein apni jaan de sakta hai? Free fund mein milti hai ye life? Aur mujhe koi farishta certificate mil jayega jo main kisi ke liye mar mitoon?" He looks at her. "Do you hear? I am stupid. That sounds?"

"Nahi, sir."

"Main pyaar jaisi bakwaas cheezon pe apna time waste nahin karta." The car comes off the flyover. "Tum bhi mat karo. Jyaada mat socho. Hamara nau baje ka bulletin hai. Uske baare mein socho. Understood?"

"Yes, sir."

## Forty-eight to Seventy-two

The Naag Mandir at eleven in the morning with the floor washed and the lamps not yet lit, and two women kneeling at the low step with a stone between them.

The stone is a shivling the size of a fist, and it is clear as glass, and it has an eye of light in the middle of it that comes and goes.

"Chandrama aadra nakshatra se guzar raha hai." Uttara's hands are flat on the step. "Jo hamari Naagrani pe vippati laayega."

"Aapne toh kaha tha na ki agle 48 se 72 ghante ke andar hamari Rani ichchhadhari Mahanaagrani ka roop le lengi." Bharani's voice is careful. "To phir mausam kyun bigad raha hai?"

"Kisi bhi naagin ko naagin se ichchhadhari naagin banne ke liye kam se kam sau saal tak Shivji ki kathin tapasya karni padti hai." Uttara lifts the stone. "Hamari Naagrani us tap se bach gayi hai. Lekin us tapasya mein milne wali ghor kashton se nahi bachegi."

She puts the stone down on the step between them and lets go of it and does not take her hand away.

"Agle 48 se 72 ghante unke liye sau saal ke tap ke barabar kasht le aayenge. Jitni badi shakti, utni gehri peeda." Her old hand closes round the crystal. "Aur is waqt un par jaanleva hamla bhi ho sakta hai. Isliye hamara unke saath rehna bahut zaroori hai. Ye sphatik shivling unhein wo asahniya dard sehne ki shakti dega."

"Par hum use un tak pahunchaenge kaise?" Bharani's eyes are on the door. "Wo us ghar mein hai, Maa. Us ghar mein hamare liye koi jagah nahin hai."

Uttara does not answer that. She is looking at the eye of light in the stone.

"Ek baat bataiye." Bharani sits back on her heels. "Aapne mujhe us ladki ko jaakar ye batane kyun bheja ki uski shaadi Aryaman se ho rahi hai? Kis baat ka dar hai aapko?"

"Chaahe wo Anant kul ki naagin ho. Chaahe janmajat divya shakti wali naagin." Uttara's voice is flat. "Insaan se pyaar karne ka paap kisi naagin ko baksha nahin gaya. Kabhi nahin."

"Matlab pehle bhi aisi ichchhadhari naagin hui hain jo insaani pyaar mein padi hain?"

"Haan." The old woman's chin comes up. "Aur un sab ne bahut badi keemat chukai."

The lamps in the hall are unlit and the hall is full of the daylight that comes in at the door.

"Ye baat barson purani hai." Uttara's hand is still on the stone. "Mere Ratiq ko mujhse chhinne ke liye. Aur us maut ki keemat un ladkon ko apni jaan deke chukani padi."

Bharani's hand comes up over her own mouth.

"To hamari Mahanaagrani ko aisa sochna bhi mat." Uttara's eyes are dry and her voice does not shake at all. "Ichchhadhari naagin ko kisi insaan ke pyaar mein padna sabse bada shraap hai. Ek mahapaap hai."

The eye of light inside the crystal goes out, and comes back.

## The Specialist

The newsroom floor at nine in the evening, with the lights down over the desks, and the nine o'clock special being built on four screens at once and one of them showing a plate of food.

At the far end of the floor a man with a clipboard says "Get the team ready. Excuse me," into a phone, and the woman coming past him with two cables over her shoulder stops.

A boy goes past with a trolley of tapes and one of the trolley's wheels is flat, so the trolley goes round in a slow circle and he keeps saying sorry to it.

"Mere lineup ka printout kahan hai?" Aryaman comes down the newsroom floor with his sleeves turned back. "Aur koi coffee dega?"

"Roma, ek baar padh le." The producer is holding two pages up. "Do minute."

The producer's two pages are clipped together, and the clip has been bent and bent again until it does not hold.

"Guys, come on. Be ready."

"Sir, ye aapka aaj ka lineup." Purvi puts it in his hand. "Das minute mein roll karna hai. Aaj ke saare segments —"

## The List

The Arora house at eleven in the morning, with the light of the front door lying down the passage, and the front room full of cardboard and the floor full of names.

"Tum dono itni late kyun ho gayi?" Their mother has a list in one hand and a pen in the other and a dupatta over her shoulder. "Purvi, tu office se. Ananta, tu—"

The cardboard boxes are stacked three high against the wall, and one of them has a wedding invitation stuck to its side with tape.

"Mummy, list."

"Haan." She sits down on the arm of the sofa. "Janakpuri wali bua ka naam likha hua hai. Aur Preet aur Simran bhi aa rahe hain Dwarka se. Unhone kaha hai ki wo time pe aa jayenge. Mujhe yaad nahin aa raha. Aur koi baki hai?"

"Ghaziabad wali bua."

The list in her hand is a page torn out of a register, written down one side and turned and written down the other.

"Haan!" She writes it down. "Hai meri Anita ki shaadi. Sabko bulaenge hum." She looks up. "Mithai wala nahin aaya abhi tak. Ek ghanta ho gaya usse order diye."

"Main dekhti hoon, mummy." Purvi has her phone out. "Mere paas number hai."

"Aur wo Bulbula aayega." Her mother is up again. "Use ye de dena. Aur beta—" She stops in the doorway. "Wo chashma kahan gaya tera?"

A tin of sweets stands open on the sideboard with its lid leaning against it, and nobody has taken one.

"Toot gaya, mummy."

"Naya lena padega."

The front room empties.

The room settles. The fan turns. A chair stands where somebody got up from it with a dupatta over the back.

Ananta sits down on the floor with her back against the sofa and her legs out in front of her, and her churidar is ridden up at one ankle, and she is smiling at nothing at all.

"Purvi."

"Haan?"

"Tu ye feeling nahin samjhegi." She says it to the ceiling. "Ek din mein sab kuch kitna badal gaya na. Stress to hai ki kaafi kam time hai. Kaafi kuch karna hai. Bahut saari taiyari karni hai. Lekin sach bataun, kuch matter nahin karta."

"Didi—"

"Wedding dress jaisi sochi thi wo ho na ho. Koi aaye na aaye. Kuch farak nahin padta." She turns her head. "Sirf matter ye karta hai ki meri shaadi usse ho rahi hai jisse main pyaar karti hoon. Aur un sabke saamne ho rahi hai jo mujhse pyaar karte hain."

Purvi sits down on the floor beside her.

The cardboard against the wall has a name written on each box in marker, so that nothing goes to the wrong house.

"Kash tu bhi kisi se pyaar karti." Ananta's head comes round. "Tera chehra kyun utra hua hai? Kuch pareshani hai?"

"Kuch nahin hai, Deep." She picks at the edge of her own sleeve. "Main bas ye soch rahi thi ki jab aap shaadi karke yahan se chali jaogi, to main bilkul akeli ho jaungi."

"Oho." Ananta sits up. "Tu bhi na. Main tujhe mar ke bhi akela nahin chhodungi."

"Aisi baatein kyun karti hain aap?"

"Main sirf ek normal baat bol rahi hoon." She takes her sister's face in one hand and turns it. "Main duniya mein kahin bhi hoongi, tere liye mera pyaar kabhi kam nahin hoga. Bas mujhse kuch chhupana mat. Okay? Kabhi bhi koi pasand aaye to sabse pehle mujhe batayegi."

"Haan."

"Aur jis ko tu pasand karegi na, wo ladka bahut lucky hoga." She lets go. "Itni pyaari behen jise milegi."

In the kitchen a steel plate goes down on the counter hard enough to be heard from here.

"Ye kuch zyada nahin ho raha? Matlab mujhe pa ke koi bhi ladka lucky to nahin ho sakta."

"Idhar aa." Ananta takes her by the shoulder. "Mujhe dekh. Aur mere chehre ko dekh. Kitne defects hain ismein. Aise chehron ko unka manchaha milna bahut mushkil hai."

"Didi—"

"Aur kisne bola tujhse ye bakwaas baat?" Ananta's voice goes up. "Tujhe dekhne wala koi milega. Zaroor milega. Main bol rahi hoon na."

## Two Days' Notice

A roll of marigold lies half used against the veranda step.

The generator at the side of the lawn is running for the lights, and its noise sits under everything anybody says.

"Aaiye, samdhi ji." Parmeet has his hands out from the veranda. "Aaiye. Aane mein koi dikkat to nahin hui?"

"Nahin, bilkul nahin." Gurinder looks at the marigold going up the post. "Bas ye sab itna jaldi mein ho gaya ki hamare bahut saare rishtedaar aa nahin paaye."

## The Back Stairs

The store room off the back stairs at half past eight, and one bulb, and the shelves to the roof, and a girl sitting on the third step with her head down and her dupatta in her fists.

The pain comes in waves and it is behind her eyes, and each wave is worse than the one before it, and there is no name for it in her head and no doctor she can go to.

She has her spectacles in her hand. She has been holding them so hard that the frame has made a mark in her palm.

A bucket stands at the bottom of the shelves with a cloth over it, and the cloth has not been moved.

Vihaan comes down the back stairs with two bottles of cold water in one hand and his shirt cuff undone on the other, and stops on the step above her.

"Aap yahan kya kar rahi hain?"

"Sir." She sits up. "Kuch nahin. Bas do minute."

Vihaan comes down and sits on the step below hers, which puts his shoulder level with her knee, and puts one of the bottles against her wrist.

"Garam hai." He says it plainly. "Aapka haath garam hai. Aaj poora din garam tha."

"Mausam—"

"Mausam theek hai." He looks at the bottles. "Ghar ke andar sab log kambal le rahe hain."

She does not say anything. The bulb hums.

"Sar dard?"

"Thoda."

"Aur?"

"Aur kuch nahin." She takes the bottle from him and her hand closes on it and she cannot get the cap off. She tries, and it slips, and she tries again, and he does not take it out of her hand and does it for her, and he does not say anything about it either.

He waits.

"Sir, aap andar jaaiye." Her voice is not steady. "Aapki maa aapko dhoondh rahi hongi."

"Meri maa mar chuki hai." He says it without any weight in it at all. "Chachi dhoondh rahi hongi. Chachi ka intezaar ho sakta hai."

She looks at him.

"Aapki maa—"

"Main do saal ka tha." He is looking at the shelves. "Kuch yaad nahin hai. Sirf ek kamra yaad hai jismein bade the, aur ek khushbu." He turns the bottle round in his hands. "Log kehte hain wo sugandh aati hai kabhi kabhi. Mujhe nahin aati."

The bulb hums. Somewhere in the front of the house the makeup woman says something and the room laughs.

"Aap ye tray nahin uthayengi." He says it to the shelves. "Aaj raat. Kal bhi nahin."

"Sir, mera kaam hai—"

"Main jaanta hoon aapka kaam kya hai." He stands up. "Aur main jaanta hoon ki aap kal se kya kar rahi hain. Aap teen baar seedhiyan chadh chuki hain aaj. Har baar kamra badal raha hai. Har baar aap wapas aa rahi hain." He puts his bottle down on the step beside her. "Main ye nahin keh raha ki aap kuch chhupa rahi hain. Main ye keh raha hoon ki aap thak gayi hain, aur is ghar mein thakna kisi ke liye koi baat nahin hai. To main bata raha hoon. Aaj raat aap sirf baithiye."

"Aur agar main nahin baithi?"

"To main bhi nahin baithunga." He is already going up. "Aur main mehmaan hoon. Achha nahin lagega."

She laughs, once, and it hurts her head, and she puts her hand up to it.

At the top of the stairs he stops.

"Purvi."

"Ji?"

"Wo bulb kharab ho raha hai." He does not turn round. "Agar andhera ho jaye to chillana. Main sun lunga."

He goes. The bulb hums, and holds, and hums.

## The Door at the End

The Suri house at nine at night has the mandap half up and the women of the family in the front room with a makeup case open on the table.

"Sabse pehli baat." The makeup woman has her brush up. "Hum lashes ko curl karenge. Theek hai? Ab kya karna hai? Blusher."

Vihaan comes in from the hall with a laddoo in his hand and sits down on the arm of the sofa beside Pinky, and Pinky puts her hand out without looking up and he gives her half of it.

"Tera mehendi ka cone idhar de." He takes it out of her hand and turns her palm over. "Haath khol. Khol."

"Tu kharab kar dega."

"Main kharab hi karunga. Ye to kabhi theek nahin hota." He draws a line down her palm that is not a design and has no intention of being one, and Pinky laughs and pulls her hand back and hits him on the shoulder with it.

"Bhai sahab ka beta. Sangeet se pehle hi haath kharab."

"Aur tu?" Vihaan leans across to Roma. "Ye TV pe kya chhod aayi hai? News readers ki jodi?"

"Oye." Roma throws a cushion at him. "Kuch to hai. Sources bata rahe hain."

"Sources." He catches the cushion. "Sources ne ye bhi bataya ki tuney teen ghante se isi channel pe apna hi naam dhoondha hai?"

The room laughs at that, and Dimple says "Vihaan" in the voice of a woman who is not really objecting, and he is off the sofa arm and standing with his hands in his pockets, easy, in the middle of his own family, at nine at night, two days before two weddings.

"Purvi!" Roma's voice from the sofa. "Do minute!"

"Aa rahi hoon."

"Clear look dene ke liye sabse aakhri mein kya karte hain? Lipstick." The brush goes up. "Arre Purvi, teri shaadi nahin hai. Chal na."

On the television in the corner the nine o'clock show is ending and the entertainment bulletin is beginning.

"Fans alert!" The anchor has her hands together. "Sources bata rahe hain ki dono news readers ki jodi jald hi rab ne bana di jodi mein badalne wali hai. Sath hone wale hain dono!"

Roma puts her hand over her mouth and looks at the ceiling and says "Oh my God" twice.

"Roma." Purvi is at the table with the plates. "Wo mera naam nahin liya."

"Usne mera liya." Roma smiles at the mirror. "Tune suna nahin?"

The doorbell goes at the gate, and the man at the gate says something, and then the door of the front room opens and the housekeeper comes in with her hands folded.

"Memsahab. Bahar koi aaye hain."

"Kaun?"

"Maa Sapela ji hain." The housekeeper's voice goes down. "Keh rahi hain ki unhe bhai sahab ne hi bulaya hai."

The makeup brush stops in the air.

Dimple puts her glass down on the table and looks at the door, and the door is a doorway with the veranda light behind it and nobody in it yet.

The easiness goes out of Vihaan's face in one movement, and it is not a change of expression, it is a man taking off a coat.

Vihaan goes to the door himself, and he does not open it wide, and he does not stand aside.

"Kaun hain?"

"Parmeet Suri ne bulaya hai." Her voice is an old woman's voice and it goes past him into the house. "Zeher ka sample laaye hain. Woh dikhana hai."

"Aapka naam."

The woman looks at him for a moment with her chin up.

"Maa Sapela."

"Aur ye?" Vihaan's eyes come down to the cloth case and stay on it. "Isme kya hai?"

"Jo main laayi hoon."

"Yahan kholiye." He says it flatly. "Verande pe. Andar nahin."

Behind him, in the front room, Dimple's voice comes out into the hall: "Vihaan. Andar bulao. Bhai sahab ne bulaya hai."

"Andar nahin." He does not turn his head to say it, and his voice does not go up. "Jab tak main dekh nahin leta."

The woman's mouth moves at one corner of it.

"Aapko bulaya gaya hai." Vihaan says it to her, and he says it quietly. "Iska matlab ye nahin ki aap ghar ke andar aa sakti hain."

Then he puts his hand out for the case, and does not take it off her, and waits with his hand out until she puts it in it, and he has decided that too.

Vihaan is the one who carries it in.

Behind the woman, at the gate, the street lamp goes out.

### Continuity and source note

This chapter is **Episode 5**, in the episode's own order: the constable at the mouth of the lane and the girl who says she fell; the animal's body with poison in it and the order that sends a sample to the Mahasapera; the Kumbh and the two men who want it; the locked door in the first-floor passage and the secret that nearly comes out of it; the girl who is sent for and the two weddings fixed in one breath; the twelve kilos of silver with the old names on it; the car and the eyelash; the temple, the crystal and the law that a naagin who loves a man costs that man his life; the nine o'clock floor and its specialist; the sisters on the floor of the front room; and the hunter who comes to the door at nine at night. Beats **B01 to B38** of `canon/E05_BEATS.md`.

**Why the ledger came before the prose, and what it cost.** Episode 5 is the first episode in this workshop cut from the caption track **without a recap beside it** — every line above is the track's, and the track names almost nobody. So the ledger was written first, with the six rulings in §2 of it made and written down before a word of prose, because an attribution error cannot be caught after the scene reads well.

**The readings this chapter declares**

1. **The Kumbh answer is Vihaan's.** The track gives Parmeet the question about Pasha, the three calls and the readiness, and gives the answering voice no name. In this serial the family's London end is his, so the voice is his, and it is his one scene with his father in this episode — and the last thing he says in it is the only thing in the scene that his father does not want to hear: *"Unke saath unke camera aayenge. Jo humare ghar ke andar bhi jhaankenge."*
