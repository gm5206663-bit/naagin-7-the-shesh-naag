## Which Tonic Do You Take

The Arora house at seven in the evening, and the song on the radio going under everything, and the one bulb over the hall door on with the moths round it.

The dining table has four plates set at it and a jug of water in the middle, and nobody has sat down.

The scooter's key hangs in the lock of the front door with a red thread tied to it.

The gate bangs. Purvi comes up the path at a run with her bag flying off her shoulder, and bursts in through the door, and the door hits the wall.

"Ye kaun hai?" She looks round the hall at nobody. "Koi nahin. Koi nahin, baby. Koi ajeeb baat nahin hai." She holds her own arms out and looks at them. "Itna daud ke bhi mujhe koi thakaan hi nahin ho rahi hai."

Upstairs a door closes and a fan is switched on, and the sound of it comes down the stairwell.

"Ab samjhiye." Ananta comes down the last three steps with a glass in her hand. "Ye sab Papa ke tonic ka kamaal hai. Woh tonic jo piya tha na—" She puts the glass down on the table. "Woh tonic nahin tha. Woh energy warm tha. Mazaa aa gaya."

"Baby, mujhe ye nahin jaanna."

"I wish, I wish filmi scene hota." Ananta sits down on the arm of the sofa. "Us tonic ka taste ajeeb tha, lekin result poochho mat."

"Are, maine poochha hi kab tha?"

"Tum itni der se bolti chali ja rahi ho." Ananta is up again. "Par ab stop ho jaogi."

"Poochho kyun?"

"Kyunki mera stop aa gaya." She picks her bag up. "Bye. Thank you."

The gate bangs. The song goes on under the empty hall.

"Ye kaun sa tonic leti hai yaar?" Purvi says it to the ceiling. "Bhagwan, ye kaun sa tonic leti hai?"

## The Test

Ananta at the corner of the lane in the last of the light, under the neem, with the phone hot against her ear and her hand over the other one.

A scooter stands at the corner with a cloth over the seat and a helmet hanging off the handlebar.

Down the lane a shop puts its shutter down, and the noise of it goes on longer than the noise of a shutter should.

"Ravish." Her voice is low and fast. "Maine teen baar already check kar liya. Sab ek hi result hai." She shuts her eyes. "Positive."

"Relax." The voice on the phone is easy and warm. "Relax hai na. Sab theek ho jayega. Tum tension kyun le rahi ho?"

"Main tension kaise na loon?" She turns her face away from the road. "Mere maa-baap modern hain. Lekin itne modern nahin ki beti ki pregnancy accept kar lein shaadi se pehle."

A scooter goes past and she waits for it.

"Tum mujh pe trust karti ho na?" he says.

"Karti hoon."

"Bas ek baar Karan ki shaadi ho jaye na—" He says it slowly, the way a man says a thing he has prepared. "Phir pakka main apne ghar waalon ko bata dunga."

"Kab tak?"

"Bas ek baar. Ek baar Karan ki shaadi ho jaye."

The lane's lamp comes on above her while she stands there, and the moths come to it at once.

"Achha theek hai." She looks at her watch. "Nikal rahi hoon."

"Sorry, baby." He says it cheerfully. "Mujhe office jaana padega. Woh gyarah baje ki urgent meeting hai. Review meeting hai." A pause. "Bhai ka order. I can't say no."

The phone goes dead. She stands under the lamp with it in her hand.

## A Line in Love

The Arora house at nine, and the one bulb on in the room and the fan going under it, and the two of them on the bed with a plate of fruit between them that neither of them has touched.

The plate has a knife lying across it, and the knife has not been used.

On the dressing table the tin of powder is open and the mirror has a thumbprint on it.

A steel glass of water stands on the floor by the bed, and both of them have forgotten it.

"Tu baar-baar kya dekh rahi hai?" Purvi puts her head on her sister's shoulder. "Positive hai. Usi baat ki tension hai— ki kaise, aur kyun?"

"Please." Ananta puts her hand over her sister's eyes. "Itna sochna band kar."

"Kaise band karoon?"

"Maine Ravish se baat ki hai." Ananta takes her hand away. "Woh mere saath hai. Bade log hain woh. Ameer log hain. Aur humein dekho—" She lifts her hand at the room, at the fan, at the wall with the calendar on it. "Door-door tak kahin koi match nahin hai."

The fan turns, and the light of the bulb goes round the room with it.

Somewhere below in the house a tap is running, and the sound of it comes up through the floor.

Beyond the window a dog barks twice and stops, and a scooter goes past with its lights off.

"Didi—"

"Aur uske bade bhai ko dekha hai tumne?" Ananta sits up. "Khadoos Hitler hai. Number one ka." She holds one finger up. "Aur soch ke dekho. Agar Ravish ne tumhare saath aisa kiya hai, toh uske ghar waale kaise honge? Uska parivaar kaisa hoga?"

"Didi, sab theek ho jayega." Purvi takes her hand. "Ek baar mere Ravish ki shaadi ho jayegi na—"

"Shaadi?" Ananta stares at her. "Uske ghar waale nahin maane toh? Mummy-Papa kitna bharosa karte hain tum pe. Tumne aisi harkat apni hadd paar kyun ki?"

"Pyaar mein koi hadd nahin hoti."

"Woh baat sirf sunne mein acchhi lagti hai, didi." Ananta's voice goes soft and it does not stop being hard. "Praktical nahin hai. Aur sahi bhi nahin hai."

"Pyaar mein sahi-galat ki sudh nahin hoti." Purvi sits up too. "Sach mein. Jab tumhein pyaar hoga na, tab samajh aayega ki tumhare bas mein kuch hai hi nahin. Tumhara jo yeh dil hai—" She puts her hand flat on her own chest. "Yeh tumhara rehkar bhi tumhara nahin hoga. Uska ho jayega. Aur woh saari cheezein hoti hain jo humne kabhi sochi hi nahin hoti."

Ananta looks at her.

"Main sach mein, sach mein wish karti hoon—" Purvi's eyes are bright. "Ki tumhari life mein bhi koi ek aisa insaan aa jaye jisse tumhein bhi deewanon ki tarah pyaar ho jaye. Sote, jaagte, uthte, baithte— tumhein sirf wohi ek insaan nazar aaye." She smiles. "Phir main bhi dekhoon, didi. Tum kitna apne usoolon pe chalti ho."

"Usool." Ananta says the word to the fan.

"Pyaar karne waalon ke beech mein bhi ek line draw hoti hai. Ek hadd hoti hai. Jise kabhi cross nahin karna chahiye." Purvi puts her head back against the wall. "Aur rahi baat meri, toh main kabhi aisa nahin karungi. Ravish aur main ek doosre se bahut pyaar karte hain. Karan ki sagai hote hi woh apne ghar waalon se baat kar lega. Mujhe Ravish pe apni jaan se bhi zyada bharosa hai."

"Dhokebaaz bana ke hi chhodogi tum mujhe." Ananta says it lightly, and then she is not light. "Tu bhi. Woh bhi."

The fan goes round. The fruit sits between them, untouched.

## The Mall

The mall at nine, and the escalators going up and down in their own light with nobody on them, and a fountain running in the middle of the floor.

Vihaan comes down the escalator. The woman by the pillar watches him and does not speak again until he is past her and out through the doors.

Ravish comes across the marble with his phone in his hand and his collar open.

"Kahan thi itni der?" The woman by the pillar turns round. She is in green, and her hair is done, and her wrist is heavy with gold. "Ravish, main yahan tumhara intezaar karte-karte thak gayi."

"Sorry, baby." He puts the phone away. "Kaho na. Ek boring se kaam mein phans gaya tha. Ab aa gaya hoon main." He opens his arms. "Mall yours."

"Yeh safe place nahin hai." She looks past him, once, at the floor. "Pura office jaanta hai ki main tumhare dad ki dost ki wife hoon."

"Toh?"

"Main us tharki buddhe ki teesri biwi hoon." She says it flatly. "Jiske saath life sirf aur sirf compromise hai." Then she smiles, and it is a different face. "Fun toh life tum jaise logon ke saath hai. I will see you at the hotel."

"Okay."

She goes, and her heels go across the marble, and she does not look back.

"Wah." Arjun comes out from behind the pillar with a coffee in his hand. "Kya player ho, bhai."

"Arjun—"

"Par aapka cousin hone ke naate ek baat boloon?" He sits on the edge of the fountain. "Bolo na."

"Bhai." Arjun puts his coffee down. "Aap na aag ke saath khel rahe ho."

"Relax, Arjun." Ravish sits down beside him. "Main jaanta hoon. Dad aur us buddhe minister ki saali ki dosti hai. Lekin buddhe ne apni beti ki umar ki ladki se shaadi ki hai." He spreads his hands. "Uska kya?"

"Par bhai—" Arjun turns to him. "Aapki feelings toh us ladki Ananta ke liye thi na? Ab woh shaadi ki baat kar rahi hai, toh?"

"Arre yaar, dekha." Ravish laughs. "Main aisa hi hoon. Yeh pyaar-wyaar main jhel sakta hoon. Lekin shaadi—" He shakes his head slowly. "Shaadi bilkul bhi nahin jhel sakta. Haan, feelings mein zaroor believe karta hoon. Lekin forever mein bilkul bhi nahin."

"Kal sagai hai."

"Kal sagai hai." He stands up and puts his collar straight. "Kal saara drama khatm ho jayega."

A trolley of glasses stands at the juice counter with a cloth over it.

The fountain's pool has coins in the bottom of it and a plastic bottle floating among them.

The lift at the far end of the floor opens and shuts and nobody comes out of it.

A security guard walks the length of the floor and does not look at either of them.

Above the escalators the lights change colour once, and then again.

Behind them the fountain goes on falling into its own pool, and the escalators go up and down with nobody on them at all.

## The Edit Bay

The edit bay at half past nine, and four screens going at once and their blue light standing on the face of everybody in the room.

A stack of printouts stands on the desk with a pen on top of it, and the pen is out of ink.

A plastic tray of tea glasses stands by the door, and every glass in it has been drunk and none of them taken away.

The day's running order is taped to the wall in three columns, and the third column has been rewritten twice.

"Everyone." Aryaman comes in with the day's script in his hand. "Yeh bakwaas kisine likhi hai?"

Nobody answers.

"You know what?" He holds the page up. "I'll have them fired immediately." The page goes down. "This works. Good." He turns. "The rest of prime time is looking decent. Okay, for the night show we will start with the minister's secret diary. Uske baad update package, B-2, and roll it. Everyone, get to work."

"Sir, aapke paanv zameen pe aa gaye hain." The girl at the far desk has her hands up. "Sorry, sir."

Aryaman stops. "Kya kaha?"

"Matlab—" She is already standing. "Sir, sorry."

"Toh tumhara circus khatm ho gaya?" He turns to Ananta, who is at the second screen with her headphones round her neck. "Toh main apna show shuru karoon?"

"Nahin sir, mat kijiye." Ananta pulls the headphones down. "Aaj aapka koi associate kar lega na."

"Kyun?"

"Actually Chachi ji mujhe baar-baar call kar rahi hain." She holds her phone up. "Keh rahi hain ki ghar pe function hai, aur sagai nau baje hi hai, sir."

"This channel's TRP comes from my show." Aryaman says it into the room without raising his voice. "Kisi sagai se nahin. Now—" He turns round. "Koi news pointers laayi hai, ya ghoomne aa gayi hai?"

"Ji sir." The girl comes forward with her tablet. "U.P.S.C. student affairs, Mahendragiri Hills latest attack, cold weather safety tips, A.I.-based weather forecasting, electricity bill 2025 to light India's energy future—"

"Sab chhod ke."

"—minister sting operation." She looks up. "The most important, sir. Iska agla part humein mil gaya hai. Jo aapne meeting mein expose kiya tha— wohi chhupaya gaya audio humare haath lag gaya. Jahan pe minister saaf-saaf bol raha hai ki consultancy ke naam pe contract ke paise liye gaye the, aur jo transfer approval hua tha woh uske sage bhai ke firm se sign hua hai, sir." She holds the tablet out. "Yeh link pehle kisi ko nahin mila. Yeh humara exclusive connect hai. Yeh lijiye."

Aryaman takes it. Behind him, at the third screen, Vihaan has a drive in one hand and a headphone in the other, and he is running the audio back over itself.

"Sir." He stops it on a frame. "Yeh edit nahin hai. Do jagah pe haath lagaya gaya hai. Baaki file asli hai. Do jagah—" He plays it. "Yahan aur yahan. Aur yeh line uske pehle bhi hai, yaani usne apne bhai ka naam doosri baar bhi liya hai."

"Kaun si line?" Aryaman looks at him, and then at the screen.

Four headphones hang on their hooks with the pads worn through.

The air conditioning ticks, and the room's door stands open on the corridor.

At the far end of the room somebody laughs at something on a screen and stops when Aryaman turns round.

Vihaan turns the screen round so that the room can see the waveform and the strip under it.

"Consultancy ka paisa. Aur firm ka naam."

Vihaan does not move for a moment, and the room does not notice, because the room is looking at Aryaman.

"Ruk." Aryaman's hand is up. "Vihaan. Kya hai?"

"Kuch nahin."

"Tera chehra kuch keh raha hai."

"Nahin." He takes the drive out and puts it on the desk and pushes it away with one finger. "Main yeh bol raha hoon ki is tape se pehle jaanch karo ki consultancy kis naam pe bani thi, aur kis ne kisi ko us consultancy ka address diya. Yeh cheez aise nahin banti. Yeh cheez kisi ke saamne banti hai."

"Kisko pata hoga?"

"Jo is tarah ka hisaab rakhta hai." He picks his jacket up off the chair. "Aur Aryaman—" He says it at the door and not to the room. "Jab tum ise chalao, to yeh batao ki yeh chhota khel hai. Desh ki consultancy, videsh ka address, aur paisa jo har saal ek hi mahine mein jaata hai. Yeh Dilli ke do aadmiyon ka jugaad nahin hai. Yeh ek office ka kaam hai."

"Tujhe kaise pata?"

Vihaan looks at his brother for a moment, and the answer is on his face and it does not come out of his mouth.

"Main hisaab rakhta hoon." He goes. "Tum khabar rakhte ho."

"Everyone." Aryaman straightens. "Sir, and then we roll."

## So Good-Looking, and So Rude

The corridor outside the bay, and the floor emptying into the night.

Purvi stands at the door with the day's files in her arms and watches Aryaman go down the corridor, jacket over a shoulder, and the tablet still in his hand.

"Seedha toh is ulti khopdi mein kuch jaata hi nahin." She says it quietly, to the files. "Itna good-looking ho ke zaroori hai itna khadoos banna?" She turns. "I really hope ki iska bhai iske jaisa na ho."

A man comes out of the bay with a camera in its case, and she steps out of his way, and he steps out of hers, and neither of them gets through the door for a moment.

Then he stands back with his hand out towards the corridor and a small bow that is entirely a joke and is not mocking her at all.

"After you. Aap pehle. Main aapke peeche." He looks at the files in her arms. "Ya phir aadhi files main utha loon, aur phir hum dono ek saath ghusenge, aur koi humein kuch nahin kahega."

"Sorry." She goes round him. "Sorry, sir."

"Sir theek hai." He shifts the camera case to his other shoulder. "Par aapne abhi abhi andar kisi ko gaali di, aur wo gaali mujhe sunni padi. To main jaanna chahta hoon ki main kis ki gaali kha raha hoon."

"Main—" She stops. "Main Aryaman sir ke baare mein keh rahi thi."

"Achha." He takes that in. "Aur kya kaha?"

"Ki itna good-looking ho ke zaroori hai itna khadoos banna?" She says it again because there is no way out of it now. "Aur main ye umeed karti hoon ki inka bhai inke jaisa na ho."

Vihaan looks at her for a moment.

"Main hi inka bhai hoon."

## Mayuri

The tenth floor after nine, and half the lights off and the corridor in the dark past Parmeet's cabin, with its door not quite shut.

A drawer stands open behind that desk with its papers standing up out of it, and a file lies open on the floor where it fell.

The desk lamp is off and the screen is still on, and its light lies out across the floor.

Purvi stops.

The corridor's tube light flickers once and steadies.

"Mayuri?" She pushes the door open. "Tum Parmeet sir ke cabin mein kya kar rahi ho? Wahan jaana mana hai, maloom hai na tumhein?"

"Haan haan, main jaanti hoon." Mayuri is on her knees behind the desk with a drawer open. "Par main yahan apne boyfriend ke baare mein pata karne aayi thi. Woh yahin isi office mein kaam karta tha." She looks up. "Par tum Parmeet sir ke cabin mein kya dhoondhne aa rahi ho?"

"Mayuri—"

"Matlab—" Purvi looks at her own hands, which have nothing in them. "Dekho, yeh thoda ajeeb hai."

"Haan." Mayuri gets up. "Ajeeb mere boyfriend ke saath hua tha. Isi office mein." She takes her phone out of her pocket. "Yeh dekho."

The screen comes on and the light of it comes up on both their faces.

"Ye kya hai?"

"Yeh hai woh." Mayuri's thumb is steady. "Isiliye main basement ka secret password jaanne ke liye boss ke cabin mein aa rahi thi."

Purvi looks at the screen for a long time, and her hand goes out and then comes back.

"Aaj Karan ki sagai ke mauke pe main tum logon ko ek bahut hi badhiya surprise dikhaungi." Mayuri says it fast, in the voice of somebody who has rehearsed it in a mirror. "Mera yakeen karo, Purvi. Mere boyfriend ke saath zaroor kuch aisa hua tha jiske baad uska yeh hashr hua. Basement mein zaroor kuch hai." She puts the phone into Purvi's hand. "Tum ise sambhal ke rakhna. Shayad tumhare kisi kaam aa jaye."

"Mayuri—"

"Tum sabke liye ek khaas tohfa hai." She lifts both hands, and the corridor light comes into the cabin with the door going wider, and half the floor is at the door now. "Diamonds. Jewellery."

"Arre, woh sab toh wives ke liye hota hai." A boy at the door grins. "Humein toh kuch wild chahiye."

"Wild?" Mayuri turns round. "Wild." Then she smiles, and it is not a nice smile at all, and she holds up the phone. "Aur yeh hai tumhara surprise."

## The Mark

The landing outside the forbidden cabin at ten, and the two of them in the corner of it with the phone between them and the light of it on their faces.

"Gaur se dekho ise." Mayuri's voice has gone quiet. "Mere boyfriend ko bas jalaya nahin gaya. Balki uski body par yeh ajeeb sa nishaan bhi hai. Jaise kisi jaanwar ne jaanleva hamla kiya us pe."

A floor-polishing machine stands at the end of the landing with its flex wound in a coil round the handle.

Purvi's thumb moves the picture.

"Aur yeh koi mamooli jaanwar nahin lag raha." Mayuri is not looking at the screen. "Koi anjaan sa, bhayanak sa jaanwar hai. Aur hairani ki baat toh yeh hai ki yeh sab kuch isi office mein ho raha hai."

"Kaun hai yeh?" Purvi looks up. "Kya hai jo insaan ko itni precision se maar sakta hai?"

The lift at the end of the corridor comes up and stops and does not open, and the light of it goes out again, and the two of them look at it until it does.

"Basement mein zaroor kuch hai." Mayuri takes her phone back and holds it against her chest. "Isiliye main password dhoondhne gayi thi."

Purvi looks at the phone, and then at the corridor, and then at the phone again.

"Ye photo mujhe de de." Her voice is not steady but it is not asking. "Kisi ko dikhana hai."

## The Basement

Under the building, at the bottom of a passage that turns twice under one bulb, the wall of it warm on one side.

Parmeet comes down last with a key in his hand and puts the key away in his pocket. Four men are with him and they have stopped talking, because the passage is the kind of passage that stops talking.

"Yahan se aage." He goes to the head of them. "Chalo. Sab."

At the bottom is the room with the iron shutter, and the shape worn into it, and two men standing with their backs to it. They do not turn round.

"Hey Bhagwan." One of the four takes a step back and then comes on again. "Ye kya hai, Parmeet?"

"Gaur se dekho ise." Parmeet walks to the shutter and puts his hand flat on it. "Ise hum kai saalon se paal rahe hain. Saanpon ko sungha ke humne ise bada kiya hai."

Behind the shutter something moves. It is a sound of weight going from one side to the other, and then a scrape along iron.

"Yeh koi mamooli dragon nahin hai. Iska DNA humne modify karke iski nason mein humne itna zeher ghol diya hai. Tabhi yeh itna khatarnak aur itna vishaal bana hai." The iron hums under his palm. "Yeh itna bhayanak hai ki jab yeh shaitan apni aankhein kholega, toh saare dushman ko sula dega."

One of the four says something under his breath. The others do not answer him.

"Naag aur naagin ka yeh chaandi dushman hai. Mujhse nafrat karta hai." Parmeet says it pleasantly. "Agar naag aur naagin iske aas-paas bhi phir gaye na, toh unhein pakad ke cheekh maar dega. Isliye hum logon ko chinta karne ki koi zaroorat nahin hai. Humare padosi desh ne ise humari suraksha ke liye bheja hai."

"Yeh tumara sabse khoobsurat experiment hai." The oldest of the four says it slowly and means it.

"Logon ka kehna hai ki kai saal pehle, kai zamane pehle, dragon hua karti thi, aur apne munh se aag ugalti thi." Parmeet steps away from the shutter. "Hai toh yeh naagon ki family ki, lekin unki jaani dushman hai. Isliye agar humein ichchhadhari naagin ko maarna hai, toh humein iski zaroorat padegi. Kyunki ek yahi hai jo naag aur naagin ka khoon door se hi sungh kar usey daboch kar maar dega."

"Wah, Parmeet." Ajeet laughs, and the laugh goes up the passage and comes back. "Wah. Ab humein kisi bhi ichchhadhari naagin se darne ki zaroorat nahin hai."

The two men at the shutter do not move. The floor under their feet is warm and it stays warm.

## Another Way

The canteen floor at half past ten, and the chairs upside down on the tables, and one tube light on over the counter.

The counter has a steel tray of spoons on it and a cloth that somebody has wrung out and left.

The clock over the counter is four minutes ahead, and nobody has ever set it.

Vihaan sits at the corner table with a glass of tea and the day's paper folded down to the crossword, and he fills a word in and looks up when somebody says his name, and it is somebody passing with a tray.

"Yeh tareeka sahi nahin hai." Mayuri has her hands round a cup she is not drinking. "Agar yeh sab kuch sach hai, toh humein sach tak pahunchne ke liye koi aur tareeka dhoondhna hoga."

A fan stands in the corner on a stand and it is not switched on.

Outside the window the car park is empty except for the watchman's chair.

"Toh ek hi raasta hai." Purvi puts her own phone down on the table between them. "Ek hi insaan hai jo humari madad kar sakta hai."

"Kaun?"

"Aryaman sir."

Mayuri looks at her for a long moment.

"Kal subah." Purvi is already standing. "Main unse baat karungi. Pehle unse. Phir kahin aur."

"Purvi—"

"Mayuri, woh jo photo hai na—" Purvi's hand is flat on the table. "Us aadmi ki body pe jo nishaan hai, woh kisi cheez ka nishaan hai. Aur woh cheez isi building mein hai. Kal tak koi aur nahin jaanna chahiye. Samjhi?"

"Samjhi."

## The Bulletin

The studio floor at eleven, and the red light on over the door, and the autocue going.

A sheet of the running order is taped to the camera's stand, and the last three lines are crossed out.

Vihaan is on the far camera with his eye at the finder and his shirt sleeves turned back, and the floor manager holds up four fingers at him, and he nods without taking his eye off it.

A cable lies across the floor with a strip of tape over it, and everybody steps over it without looking.

"Aryaman sir, and that's all for two-day Super News at Super Night." He looks into the lens and does not blink. "For tomorrow's news, join us on the same channel at the same time. Good night."

The autocue runs back to the top of the script by itself, and the words go down the screen and disappear.

The red light goes off. The floor moves at once.

"Sir—" Purvi is through the door before the floor manager can stop her. "Sir, ek minute. Please. Suniye. Bahut important hai."

"Not right now." Aryaman is up out of the chair with his mic still clipped. "Nahin, suno—" He takes the clip off and holds it out to somebody. "Please, sir, listen. I cannot handle your clumsiness right now, miss assistant."

"Please, mujhe maaf kijiye sir." Her hands are together. "Mujhe aapse bahut zaroori baat karni hai."

"Kya hai?"

"Sir—"

"Listen." He turns. "Next bulletin ke points ready hain, sir."

"Bulletin se zyada important hai yeh." She is walking beside him. "Mayuri. Uske boyfriend ke baare mein, sir. Woh bhi yahin kaam karta tha—"

"Oh, kaun, kya baatein kar rahi ho tum?" He stops dead in the middle of the corridor. "Ye—"

His phone rings. He looks at it and holds a finger up.

"Give me a second. Hello?" His face changes. "Haan, Chachi." A pause. "Kab se? Chachi, main bus nikal raha hoon." Another pause. "Haan, haan, aa raha hoon."

He puts the phone down.

"Sir, woh photo—"

"I'll see it later." He is walking. Then he turns round and looks at her properly for the first time that day. "Suno. Poore staff ko bata do— aaj mere bhai Karan ki sagai hai. Aur sabko aana hai." He starts walking again. "Tumhein bhi. Files leke."

"Ji, sir."

Karan is gone round the corner. The corridor is empty and the studio behind her is already being struck.

"Files leke." Purvi says it to the empty corridor. "Kaun se files?"

## The Invitation Cards

The corridor at half past eleven in the dark of the floor shutting down behind her, and the lift doors opening on a young man and a carton. Tall, sleeves turned back twice, and the carton stands on the lift floor behind him at the exact place where a heavy thing stops being heavy, and he is standing in front of it without thinking about it at all.

Purvi stops with the files in her arms.

"Ye kya hai?"

"Invitation cards." Vihaan kneels at the carton where it stands in the lift and opens the flap. "Poore staff ke liye. Kal shaam ki sagai." He takes one out and holds it up. "Naam likhe hain. Sabke."

Purvi comes over and looks into the carton, and her hand stops above the cards.

"Mera bhi?"

"Sabke." He goes through the first ten and stops, and takes one out, and gives it to her.

She reads it twice. On the front there is a gold border and the two names in a script that has been chosen by somebody with a great deal of money, and inside, on the second line, in the same script, there is her name.

"Purvi Arora." She says it aloud, quietly, to the card. "Yeh mera naam hai."

"Haan."

"Sir ne kaha ki files leke aana." She looks up at him. "Main kya pehn ke jaaungi?"

Vihaan looks at her for a moment over the open carton. The carton came up from the printer's on his order and on his money, and he went through the stack in the corridor until he found the one with her name on the second line, and he put it back in the middle of the others, and it is not in his face at all.

"Jo pehna hai, woh theek hai." He puts the lid back on. "Aap court nahin jaa rahi hain."

She laughs before she can stop herself, and then the lift opens and two men come out with a trolley, and the doors close on him and the carton, and he is gone.

Purvi stands in the corridor with her name in her hand.

In the lift, going down, Vihaan takes the printer's list out of his shirt pocket and looks at it again, at the eleventh name on it, and folds it, and puts it back.

The lift goes down. The numbers change over the door.

Vihaan takes the list out a third time and looks at the eleventh name on it. A girl who has been given nothing will keep that card, and he wants to be there when she opens it, and neither of those is a thought he has a name for yet.

The lift stops at four and the doors open on nobody smoking in the corridor, and the man at the fire door moves his bucket, and the doors close.

Vihaan folds the paper and puts it in his shirt pocket, and puts his hand flat on the carton, and does not take it out again.

## Ajeet and the Four

Down in the basement of the channel again, an hour later, in the room with the iron shutter, under the one bulb, with the bulletin over and the building emptying above their heads.

Four chairs have been brought down and none of the four men is sitting in one. The iron shutter stands at the end of the room with its long shape on it, and the floor in front of it is bare and swept.

A tin stands on the floor with its lid off and its contents gone.

A bucket stands by the wall with a mop in it, and the water in the bucket is not clean.

"Arre yaar Parmeet." Ajeet turns round in the middle of that room with his hands out. "Ek baat toh bata. Plan kya hai? Hum chaaron mein se sirf tujhe pata hai ki asli plan kya hai."

"Sahi baat hai, Parmeet." The second one, and he is not laughing now. "Hum yahan kyun bheje gaye? Chaubees saal ho gaye. Tune kabhi kuch nahin bataya. Aaj bata de yaar."

"Haan bhai sahab." The third. "Mujhe bhi aadhi-adhoori baat hi pata hai. Aaj poori baat bata dijiye aap."

Parmeet looks at all four of them, and then he sits down on the crate at the foot of the shutter, and he is the only one who sits.

"Haan, Ajeet, tum sahi keh rahe ho." He puts his hands on his knees. "Ab sach batane ka waqt aa gaya hai. Poori kahani sunane ka waqt aa gaya."

Nobody moves in the room.

"Hum jis desh se aate hain—" He says it without hurrying. "Wahan gareebi, bhukhmari aur berozgari ke siva aur kuch nahin tha. Aur doosri or Bharat chadhte sooraj jaise taraqqi kar raha tha. Hum log apne padosi desh se waise hi jalte the jaise ek dragon naaginon se jalta tha."

The iron hums behind him.

"Isiliye chaubees saal pehle humare paason ne ek plan banaya. Bharat ki barbaadi ka plan. Tabhi toh hum chaaron ko border paar bheja gaya, taaki hum is system mein ghusse aur logon ko aur system ko andar se khokhla kar dein."

"Tab bhi Kumbh chal raha tha." One of them says it to himself. "Jab hum yahan aaye the."

"Tab bhi Kumbh chal raha tha." Parmeet nods. "Kumbh ja rahe hain? Pyaas lagi hai. Paani milega? Plan toh yahi tha ki chaubees saal baad, jab Maha Mahakumbh aayega, hum mahavinash karenge." His voice does not go up at all. "Tabaahi ki shuruaat ho chuki hai. Lekin asli tabaahi laayega 2025."

"In paintalis, athtalis dinon mein duniya bhar se log yahan aate hain. Ganga mein dubki lagane, apni bhakti mein doobne." He looks at the four of them one after another. "Aur humein bheja gaya tha usi Ganga, usi dharti ka naam-o-nishaan mitane. Toh waqt aa gaya hai humare paason ki soch ko sach karne ka. Is baar ke Maha Mahakumbh mein is desh mein maha tabaahi laani hai."

"Waise bhai sahab." Ajeet scratches his jaw. "Woh professor humare plan ke baare mein jaan hi gaya tha. Tabhi toh woh jaan se gaya."

"Woh alag baat hai."

"Lekin Parmeet." The second one comes closer. "Woh naagin toh humare haath nahin aayi."

"Woh bhi aayegi."

"Parmeet, ek baat bata." The third, and his voice has gone quiet. "Maha Kumbh ko tabaah karna— bas yahi ek plan tha? Aur koi plan nahin?"

Parmeet stands up off the crate.

"Nahin." He puts the key back in his pocket. "Woh main waqt aane par bataunga. Ab taiyari Maha Kumbh ki."

They go back up the passage in a line, Parmeet last, and the shutter stands behind them with the two men still at it with their backs turned.

## The Suri House

The Suri house at midnight with the gate light on and the drive full of hired chairs, and the engagement is tomorrow.

The hall is a building site with flowers in it: a mandap half up against the far wall and its poles leaning, marigold strung from the chandelier in four long loops, and a man on a ladder with a staple gun. Two hundred chairs stand in stacks of ten along the passage, and the caterer's boys are going through with crates, and Pinky is in the middle of it with a list and a pen in her teeth.

"Arjun!" She takes the pen out. "Yeh chairs abhi mandap ke paas le jao. Aur phir upar jaake Tauji ko bol dena ki Pandit ji saat baje aa rahe hain."

"Ji, Pinky." Arjun takes the first stack of ten, and a boy takes the second, and the staple gun goes off above both of them and a shower of marigold comes down on their shoulders.

"Arjun." She is looking at her list. "Chai. Chaar cup. Aur Tauji ke kamre ke bahar rakh dena, andar mat jaana."

"Chaar cup?"

"Kuch log aa rahe hain." She writes something and underlines it. "Tauji ke mehmaan."

At the side of the hall, Vihaan stands with his phone at his ear and the caterer's final bill open on the table beside him, and he is not in that conversation at all.

"Chaar sau plate." He says it into the phone. "Aapne pandrah din pehle chaar sau plate ka quote diya tha. Aaj aap paanch sau bol rahe hain. Main paanch sau nahin doonga. Aap chaar sau pe deliver kariye, ya main aaj raat kisi aur se karva loonga."

Above the mandap on the wall there is a framed photograph of Karan and Tina with a garland hung over the top corner of the frame, and the glass of it catches the light from the hall.

Then the gate outside opens and a car comes in and stops, and four men get out of it and go round the side of the house towards the back steps, talking in low voices.

Vihaan's head comes up and he stands there with the phone at his ear.

"Pinky." He puts the phone in his pocket. "Yeh chai kis ke liye?"

"Tauji ke mehmaan."

"Tauji ke mehmaan saamne ke darwaze se aate hain." He is already walking. "Pehle kabhi peeche se nahin aaye."

Vihaan goes down the passage and out to the back steps, and the steps are empty by then, and the tray comes out of the kitchen behind him with four cups on it and goes past him to a door with nobody at it, and the car is standing on the drive with its lights off.
