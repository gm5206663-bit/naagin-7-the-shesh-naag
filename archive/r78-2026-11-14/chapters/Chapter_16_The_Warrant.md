The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Wish

Half past nine in the morning, and the house has been up since five, and the sun is in the passage to the kitchen.

The women's room at the back of the Suri house has two beds and a mirror and a window that looks at the tent wall, and there is a sari laid out on one bed with its gold side up and a girl sitting on the other bed with her knees drawn in.

The passage to the kitchen has a bucket against the wall and the floor round it is wet.

"Purvi." Ananta is in the doorway with her hair half done and the pin still in her hand. "Mujhe pata tha yahi pasand hai teri."

Purvi does not look up from the phone in her lap.

"Mat kar ye galti." Ananta comes in and sits down on the edge of the bed, and the mattress goes down under her. "Woh andar tak toota hua insaan hai. Woh tera bhi dil tod dega."

"Di—"

"Aryaman kisi bhi ladki ke liye sahi nahi hai." She says it plainly, the way she says everything at half past nine. "Use nafrat hai pyaar se. Aur auraton se bhi. Main nahi chahti ki tu bhi us dard se guzre jis dard ne us ladke ko aisa bana diya."

The pin goes into her hair. The mirror has both of them in it, one sitting and one standing.

"Aaj agar meri shaadi wale din mujhe ek wish milti na"—Ananta's hand comes down on her sister's knee—"to main yahi maangti ki mere hisse ki saari khushiyan tere hisse aa jaayein."

"Di, aisa mat bol."

"Waise toh mujhe koi doubt nahi hai ki us Aryaman ke dil mein sirf nafrat hi hai." Ananta looks at the mirror and not at her. "Lekin agar uske dil mein itne se bhi pyaar ki gunjaish hai na, to woh sirf tere liye ho."

"Aaj meri shaadi wale din meri bas yahi wish hai." She gets up. "Ab chal. Mera mangalsutra dhoondh. Mummy ne kisi ko diya hai aur koi jaanta nahi kisko."

## "Ab Aapki Baari"

Downstairs, the hall has been swept and the chairs are in two rows and the fire is lit in its square of brass.

"Chalo guys." Roma has the microphone and the plates are going round. "Tina toh ab officially meri bhabhi ban chuki hai."

The room claps. Tina has her head down and her dupatta over her wrist and Karan has his hand on the back of her chair.

"Ab aapki baari." Roma points the microphone at the far end of the row. "Ravish bhai and Ananta — the bride and the groom, everybody."

Ananta is in the doorway with her sari held up in one hand, and she stops there, and Ravish gets up to come and get her, and the room laughs at him for being quick about it.

At the back of the hall, Vihaan is on his knees at the sound table with a cable in his hand, and he pulls it out of the loop and puts the plug into the second socket, and Roma's voice comes out of the speakers a little louder than it went in.

"Wah." Roma turns round. "Yeh sahi hai."

"Volume chhota tha." He winds the cable round his elbow. "Ab theek hai."

"Bhai, tu kab se yahan hai?"

"Subah se." He tucks the coil under the table with his foot. "Paanch baje se."

"Paanch baje?" Roma looks at him properly. "Shaadi mein koi paanch baje aata hai?"

"Koi kuch karta hai." He stands up and hands her the spare microphone. "Yeh doosra hai. Pehla phenk mat dena, woh kiraye ka hai."

Tina laughs. Vihaan does not stay for it. Vihaan goes out through the passage with the cable over his shoulder and the gate's book under one arm, and on the step he stops to let a woman carrying a tray of glasses go past him, and she says *thank you beta* and he says *dhyan se, chai garam hai*.

## The Gate

At the gate at ten in the morning sun, Shyam has the book open on the flat of the wall and the lane behind the flower man's tempo is full.

The guard waits with his cap in his hand and does not speak until he is looked at.

"Chhote sahab." The guard's cap is in his hand. "Yeh log poochh rahe hain andar kaun hai."

Vihaan comes out with the cable still over his shoulder and looks down the lane.

Four cars in since seven and two out. The flower man's tempo has blocked the mouth of the lane since eight, and the lane behind it has three cars in it now. The third one is white and has a light on the roof that nobody has bothered to take off. There is a fourth behind it with a grille across the back window.

"Kitni der se khadi hain?"

"Pandrah minute." The guard shifts his weight. "Woh doosri gaadi mein constable hain, sahab. Uniform mein."

"Naam poochha?"

"Poochhne ki himmat nahi hui."

Vihaan gives him the cable to hold and takes the pen out of the book's spine.

The book is a school register with a cloth spine, and it is there because of one night in the study, and because of five lines he wrote to Verma at two in the morning: every man who comes into this house, name, work, time, twenty-four hours. Shyam keeps it. Shyam has kept it every night since, and in it there are three names from four days ago at half past one in the morning that nobody in this house has explained to him.

"Naam poochh." He writes the number of the white car on the day's page, and the time, and the number of men he can count in it. "Aur gate band mat karna."

"Chhote sahab—"

"Band mat karna, Shyam." He closes the book with the pen inside it. "Jo darwaza band milta hai, woh todne layak ho jaata hai. Khula chhod do."

Vihaan walks back up the drive, and does not run, and goes straight through the hall where the fire is burning with the two of them at it.

"Papa." He says it at his father's shoulder, quietly. "Gate pe police hai. Char gaadiyan. Do mein constable hain. Aur ek mein light hai."

Parmeet does not turn round.

"Char?"

"Do lane mein khadi hain. Ek andar aana chahti hai." He keeps his voice where it is. "Naam Inspector Dayal likha hai."

The fire goes on burning. Ravish says something to the pandit and the pandit nods.

"Le aao." Parmeet says it to the fire.

"Papa." Vihaan does not move. "Woh warrant le kar aaye hain ya aapko bulane?"

Parmeet turns his head then, and looks at his son for a moment, and the look is the look he gives a bill he has not asked for.

"Le aao," he says again, and turns back to the fire.

## The Warrant

Inspector Dayal comes in through the hall in the light of the chandelier with his cap under his arm and two men behind him, and the music stops in the middle of a phrase because Vihaan is standing at the sound table with his hand on the fader.

"Parmeet Suri?"

The room turns round.

The white car at the gate has a driver in it with the engine running and the window down.

"Inspector sahab." Parmeet's hands come up. "Aapka phone lagatar baj raha hai subah se. Ab aap khud hi aa gaye. Bataiye."

"Aapne phone kyun nahi uthaya?" Vihaan says it from the side, and it is not loud, and the whole hall hears it. "Aath baje se lagatar. Aath. Nau. Sawa nau."

The inspector does not look at him.

A tray of tea stands against the wall with every cup on it cold.

"**Parmeet Suri, aapke khilaf arrest warrant hai. Aapko humare saath chalna hoga.**"

The hall makes one sound and then no sound at all.

"Kya?" Parmeet's hands come down. "Tumhara dimaag theekane pe hai na? Kya bol rahe ho? Aapko pata bhi hai aap kisse baat kar rahe hain?"

"Humne aapke office par raid ki." The inspector's voice is flat and unhurried. "Kal raat, do baje. **Wahan se 200 kilo RDX mila. 12 AK-47. 20 hand grenade.** Yeh hathiyaar wahan kya kar rahe the?"

The hall is very quiet.

"Dad." Aryaman is on his feet. "Yeh office kya keh rahe hain?"

"Aap chup rahiye." The inspector raises one hand without looking at him.

"Dad, aap kuch boliye." Aryaman is at his father's shoulder now. "Tauji, aap commissioner sahab ko phone kijiye—"

"**Uska koi zaroorat nahi hai.**" Parmeet says it without turning his head. "Tum gaadi bulao, aur bachchiyon ko ghar le jao."

"Dad—"

"Vihaan." Parmeet finds him without looking for him. "Main sab sambhal lunga. In ladkiyon ko apne ghar le jao."

Vihaan is at the sound table with both hands flat on it.

"Ji."

"Teen minute." Parmeet has already turned back to the inspector. "Teen minute mein gaadiyan gate pe."

Vihaan takes his hands off the table and picks up the coil of cable, and he does not look at the bride, and the bride does not look at him.

## The Two Who Stay

The cars come up to the gate in the morning light one after another, and the children go out through the hall with their shoes in their hands.

Upstairs the bags are coming down, and Pinky has two of them against her hip and one on her shoulder and is telling a boy to be careful with the third.

A card slides out of a green handbag and lands face up on the landing.

Purvi puts the bags down and picks it up. It is a sagai card with a gold border, and the two names on the front are her brother's and his bride's, and inside, on the second line, there is a third name in the same gold script.

**Purvi Arora.**

Pinky stands on the landing and looks at it for a while. Then she puts it inside her blouse, and not back into the bag, and takes the bags up again.

"Ananta. Purvi." Dimple has a shawl round her shoulders and her hand out. "Chalo. Gaadi mein baith jao."

The inspector's hand comes out and stops them at the step.

"**Aap dono behenein kahin nahi ja sakti.**"

"Kya?" Ananta's sari comes down out of her hand. "Kyun?"

"**Kyunki aapke papa ne hi complaint ki hai.**"

Purvi's hand is on her sister's arm, and she is not looking at the inspector.

She is looking down the hall, past the fire, at Vihaan standing at the sound table with his hand still on the fader, and her hand is on her sister's arm.

Ananta's fingers close on her wrist. "Purvi."

"Di."

"Aaj subah maine tumse kaha tha." Ananta's voice is low and it does not shake. "Ab dekh. Yahi woh ghar hai. Aur tum usko dekh rahi ho."

Purvi's eyes come down to the floor and stay there.

The hall turns again, and this time it turns to Gurinder Arora in the cream kurta standing by the fire with his hands folded in front of him.

"Papa?" Ananta's voice goes up. "Papa, meri shaadi hai aaj. Ravish se. Aapne usi family ke against complaint kar di?"

Gurinder does not answer her.

"Hum baith ke bhi to baat kar sakte the na." Her sari is on the floor and she does not pick it up. "Ek baar baith ke?"

"Sahi toh keh rahi hai bachcha." Their mother is at the girl's shoulder with her hand up. "Humari bachche ki shaadi hai. Aur aapne inhi ke against complaint kar di. Kyun? Kyun kar rahe hain aap aisa? Boliye na."

The man by the fire says nothing.

"Papa, aap kuch bol kyun nahi rahe hain?" Ananta is crying now and she does not wipe it. "Batayiye na. Kyun kiya aapne aisa? Bolo na, Papa. Boliye na."

## "Kyunki Ye Log"

The hall at half past ten in the morning, with the chandelier on and the chairs pushed back against the walls.

"**Kyunki ye log woh nahi hain jo woh dikhte hain.**"

Gurinder says it to his daughter, and then he turns round and says it to the room, and his voice does not shake.

The register in the inspector's hand is open to a page with a carbon under it, and the carbon has gone through to the page beneath.

"Yeh log humare jaise nahi hain. Dushman jaise log hain ye. Ye Parmeet. Ye Baljeet. Ye mummy." He does not point at anybody. "Ye sab jhooth aur nakli hain. Inki asliyat main jaanta hoon."

The sweet boxes at the edge of the hall are still tied, and the tray they came on has one corner bent down.

"Gurinder ji." Dimple's hand is at her throat. "Aap kya keh rahe hain?"

"Aur meri beti ki khushi ke liye main desh se gaddari nahi kar sakta."

"Aap saboot dikhaiye." The inspector has his register out now. "Aapke paas kya hai?"

"Pakke saboot hain." Gurinder opens his folder on the low table and does not take anything out of it. "Lekin main aapke munh se sunna chahta hoon. Ye jo keh rahe hain, woh sach hai ya nahi."

Gurinder turns to the family.

"Aap bas haan aur na mein boliye." He waits. "**Aap ghus baithe hain ya nahi?**"

Nobody in that hall answers him, and every one of them who does not answer is an answer.

## Kaun Ho Aap

The hall at eleven in the morning, with the chandelier on and forty people standing where they got up.

"Aapke saare paper jhoothe hain." The inspector is reading off his own register now, and his finger is on the page. "Aur ye Parmeet Suri — ye hain." He lifts his head. "**Toh aap kaun ho?**"

Parmeet stands by the fire with his hands folded.

The tray of tea that came in an hour ago stands against the wall and nobody has taken a cup from it.

The fire in the grate is lit and nobody has put anything on it.

The inspector's cap is under his arm and the badge on it has lost its pin.

"Bolo." The inspector's voice is still flat. "Kaun ho aap?"

"I'll tell you." Parmeet takes his spectacles off and folds them. "Aap toh humare haan hamesha aate hain. Aaj thoda sahi sawaal poochh liya."

Parmeet looks at his family, one at a time, and he smiles at them, and the smile is the one he uses on the lawn.

"Hum is Bharat desh ke nivaasi nahi hain." He puts the spectacles in his pocket. "Main, mera bhai, meri behen, aur mere do dost. Hum ghus baithe hain."

The hall has stopped breathing.

The tray of tea that came in an hour ago stands against the wall with every cup on it cold.

"**Chauvees saal pehle, pichhle Kumbh, aaye the hum. Ek mission par.**" He says it slowly, and the slowness is the performance. "**Hamara mission — Kumbh mein tabahi.**"

"Dad—" Aryaman's voice comes out of the front row.

"Hamara asli desh Changistan." Parmeet's hands are behind his back now. "India se do sau kilometre door. Wahin se humein bheja gaya tha."

"Papa, please—"

"Main—" He stops, and he thinks about it, and it is the only time in the whole morning that he stops. "Mera asli naam—"

"That is not necessary." The inspector's pen is up.

"Ye minister ka naam hai Sameer." Parmeet does not hear him. "Mera bhai Daljeet — iska naam hai Kareem. Aur ye—" He stops again, and this time he does not go on. "Yahi humare asli naam hain. Indian naam toh humne sirf apni pehchaan chhupane ke liye rakhe the."

"News channel se lekar ministry tak, khabrein humne saari ki saari apne desh Changistan bheji." His chin comes up. "Hum system mein ghus kar system ko andar se khola karte rahe."

"Aur tum"—he looks at the inspector—"ab yahi humara sabse important target hai. Kumbh. Kyunki us din duniya bhar ke saare log wahan hote hain. Aur tum mein ek baar poore desh mein haahakaar macha dega."

He turns back to his family.

"Humare desh ke logon ke naseeb mein tum jaise logon ki zindagi nahi hoti." He says it pleasantly. "Humare yahan faslon se zyada terrorist paida hote hain."

## The Papers

The hall in the chandelier's light is a room with forty people in it and nobody is looking at anybody except the boy who is walking out of it.

Vihaan is gone for four minutes. When he comes back he has a carton under his arm and a folder in his hand, and he puts the carton down on the low table beside Gurinder Arora's, and he opens it where everybody can see.

"Yeh London ke books hain." He takes them out one at a time and lays them flat. "Chaar saal. Har mahine ki entry. Mere haath se, mere naam se."

The inspector's hand comes out of his pocket with a card in it, and he turns it over once.

"**Yeh card kisne chhapa tha?**"

"Main." Vihaan does not look at Pinky, who is standing by the door with the bags. "Poore staff ke liye. Chaalis aadmi. Naam ke saath."

The inspector looks at the second line on the inside of it, and then at the boy, and he puts the card in the file and the file under his arm.

The inspector looks at them and does not touch them.

"Aur ye." The folder comes down on top. "B-46. File number. Iska koi naam nahi hai, sirf number hai. Yeh maine banaya tha."

"Beta." Parmeet's voice from the fire. "Yeh sab—"

"In papers pe mera naam hai." Vihaan turns round and looks at his father for the first time that morning, and his voice is flat and it does not go anywhere. "Maine inhein likha hai. Main in jinse paisa aage bhejta hoon. **Toh yeh jhoothe hain.**"

The room is very silent.

"Chaar saal." He says it to the inspector now. "Aap jo keh rahe hain, woh chaar saal se mere desk se guzar raha hai. Aur mujhe pata nahi tha."

Ananta is standing at the back of the hall with her sari in her hand, and she is looking at the books.

Her purse is under her arm. In the purse there is a chemist's slip from a night three weeks ago, with a date on it and the name of a medicine and nothing else, and a boy on a cycle who takes no money and says nothing about whose account it is. *Kisi ko batana nahin.*

The writing on the top book is small and it leans left and the numbers sit flat on the line.

Purvi looks at it for a long time.

Then she looks at her sister, and then at Parmeet, who is standing in front of the inspector with his hands at his sides, and she puts her hand flat on her purse and keeps it there.

The inspector looks at the books, and then at Gurinder Arora by the fire, and then at Parmeet.

"Tumne inhein la kar theek nahi kiya." He says it without kindness. "Tumne apne aap ko ismein daal diya."

"Pata hai."

"Yeh sab evidence mein jayega."

"Pata hai."

Parmeet Suri has not moved from the fire, and he is looking at his son with a face that has nothing on it at all.

"Tum woh likhte ho jo tumhein diya jaata hai." He says it quietly. "Bas."

"Bas." Vihaan nods once. "Aaj ke baad main kuch nahi likhunga."

## The Handcuffs

The hall at noon, with the chandelier on and the bride's side of it gone quiet.

"Achha hua aapne sab kuch khud hi bata diya." The inspector lifts the handcuffs off his belt and holds them up. "Ab main aapko hathkadi pehna sakta hoon. Parmeet Suri, haath badhaiye."

Parmeet holds out his wrists.

The handcuffs on the inspector's belt have been hanging there long enough to have worn the leather.

The inspector looks at the handcuffs, and then at the room, and then he puts them back on his belt.

"**Par pehnaunga nahi.**"

"Kya?" Aryaman is on his feet again. "Inspector sahab, yeh kya—"

"Baithe rahiye." The inspector turns round, and his voice is the voice of a man who has made up his mind and has enjoyed making it up. "Sochne ke liye main bhi desh ke baare mein soch sakta hoon. Lekin main sochne laga, to mere baare mein kaun sochega?" He puts a hand on his own chest. "**Parmeet sahab kai saalon se mera ghar chala rahe hain.** Ab inse wafadari nahi karunga, to kisse karunga?"

The hall does not understand him yet.

At the side of the hall a chair has gone over and nobody has picked it up.

"Yaar, ye deshbhakti do-teen din ke liye theek lagti hai. Dhoti-kurta pehna, photo liya, ho gayi deshbhakti." He laughs and the laugh is a real one. "Ab iske liye ek imaandar aadmi ko kyun badnam karna?"

"Sir." Gurinder's hand is still on his folder. "Sir, aap—"

"Waise tumse pehle bhi ek aadmi aaya tha inke khilaaf complaint karne." The inspector says it to the whole hall. "**Maara gaya bechara. Uski girlfriend bhi aayi thi complaint karne.**" He lifts his shoulders. "Lekin ye paisa hota hai na. **Paisa complaint se bada hota hai.**"

"Sir, please."

"Chal." The inspector turns away from him. "Arre, simple hai yaar. **Jis haath se note milte hain, uske khot nahi ginte.**" He waves a hand at the room. "Parmeet sahab ke toh sau khoon maaf dikh gaye hain. Aap bindas raho, sir." He puts his cap on. "Main inko dekh lunga."

Vihaan stops at the door with his hand on the frame and looks back at the low table and the ledgers and the file with no name on it.

"Beta." He says it to Vihaan. "Woh carton utha kar andar rakh do. Bahar rakhna acha nahi lagta."

"Ji."

The inspector goes. The door shuts. The white car stays where it is.

## The Book and the White Car

At the gate in the morning sun the children are in the second car with their shoes in their hands, and the first car has already gone, and Shyam is holding the book flat against the wall.

"Chhote sahab." Shyam turns the book round. "Char gaadiyan andar aayi thi aaj. Do abhi tak khadi hain."

Vihaan takes the pen out of the book's spine and looks down the lane. The white car is third in the line with the light still on its roof, and the fourth has a grille across the back window.

The guard's table has a tin on it with the lid off and the tea in it gone cold.

"Number likh liya?"

"Likh liya." Shyam puts his thumb under the line. "Aur teen naam bhi, raat ke do baje wale. Woh teen aadmi jo aapke papa se milne aaye the."

Vihaan writes the white car's number on the day's page himself, and the time under it, and the number of men he can count inside it, and then he gives the book back.

The lane beyond the gate is full of the flower man's tempo and the marigold in it is going over.

"Yeh apne paas rakho. Kisi ko mat dena."

"Ji."

"Gate band mat karna, Shyam." He opens the door of the second car. "Jo darwaza band milta hai, woh todne layak ho jaata hai. Khula chhod do."

"Ji, chhote sahab."

Vihaan puts the folder on the seat and gets in, and in the back the smallest child has a shoe on the wrong foot and has begun to cry about it.

"Arre." He turns round with his elbow on the seat. "Woh left wala hai. Dekh."

"Pata nahi hai."

"Main pehna doon?" He reaches back over the seat, and the child puts the foot up on the armrest, and he gets the shoe on and pulls the strap and lets the child push his fingers away. "Ho gaya."

"Chhote bhaiya."

"Haan?"

"Woh gaadi hamare peeche kyun aa rahi hai?"

Vihaan looks in the mirror. The white car has pulled out of the line behind him and is one car back, and it has been there since the gate.

"Garmi hai." He rolls the window down for the child. "Woh kanpatti pe khol deti hai."

The car goes out into the lane and turns left at the flower man's tempo, and the gate of the house stands open behind it the way he left it.


## Eleven O'Clock

The hotel's fourth floor at ten past twelve, with one bulb every ten feet down the ceiling, and the lift opening on a corridor with a carpet that takes the sound of his shoes.

Verma is standing outside the boardroom with his tie straight and his phone in both hands, and he has been standing there a while.

"Sir."

"Kitne log?"

"Chaar the. Barah baje se pehle nikal gaye." Verma hands him the folder he is carrying, and it is the folder Vihaan gave him at nine. "Sir, unhone kaha—"

"Woh kya baith kar gaye the?" Vihaan opens the door.

The boardroom is empty. Four cups on the table, two of them with the tea still in them. A screen on the wall showing a map of the sangam with the pontoon bridges marked in blue, and the cursor left in the middle of a bridge. A chair turned out from the table, and a pen lying beside it with the cap off.

"Mr Menon andar hain. Woh do minute ruke." Verma keeps his voice down. "Baaki teen nahin."

Menon is at the window with the jacket folded on the sill beside him. He turns round and he does not put out his hand.

"Mr Suri. Aap ke office pe kal raat raid hui hai."

"Kal raat do baje. Aur aaj subah gyarah baje ek warrant." Vihaan puts the folder on the table. "Aapko yeh subah aath baje batana chahiye tha. Verma ka phone aapke paas bees baar gaya."

"Hum compliance pe hain, Mr Suri." Menon looks at the folder and does not touch it. "Do sau kilo. Ek company jiske office se do sau kilo nikalta hai, woh humare group ke saath sign nahin karti. Yeh mera faisla nahin hai."

"Kis ka faisla hai?"

"Mr Kessler ka." Menon picks his jacket up. "Woh aaj shaam London mein baith rahe hain."

Vihaan opens the folder and turns it round on the table, and it is not the contract in it, it is a single printed page.

"Screens tumhare. Feed mera. Poore Mahamahakumbh ka, pandrah din, aur kisi ke paas yeh licence nahin hai." His hand is flat on the page. "Jo group Bombay mein aapse baat kar raha hai, uska licence nahin hai. Woh aapko camera aur cable bech sakta hai. Sangam pe khada nahin kar sakta."

Menon reads the page. He reads it the way a man reads a thing he has already been told.

"Yeh Mr Kessler ko dikhana padega."

"Toh dikhaiye."

"Do hafte," Menon says. "Aur daam us waqt woh hoga jo Mr Kessler kahega."

"Do hafte." Vihaan closes the folder. "Aur aaj raat tak mera office aur mera naam akhbaar mein hoga. Woh aapke compliance ko batayiye, Menon saab. Yeh bhi."

Menon goes. The door shuts on the map and the four cups and the chair turned out from the table.

Verma stands by the door with his hands together. "Sir, woh contract—"

"Gaya." Vihaan picks the folder up. "Aur aaj shaam main Mr Kessler se khud baat karunga. Abhi mujhe gaadi chahiye."

"Sir, aapne kuch nahin khaya subah se."

"Gaadi, Verma."

The lift goes down. In the lobby four men in lanyards are putting up a standee for a property conference, and the standee has a photograph of the sangam on it, and the man in the photograph is standing at the rail of a pontoon bridge with his hands behind his back.

Vihaan goes past it and out through the glass into the heat, and the folder is under his arm and it is the only thing he is carrying.

## Kacche Khiladi

Parmeet Suri comes down the two steps of the fire into the hall and puts his hand on his samdhi's shoulder, and he is still smiling.

"**Samdhi ji, aap toh bade kacche khiladi nikle.** Humari chaal ko samajh hi nahi paaye."

Gurinder does not move under the hand.

A tray stands at the edge of the hall with the sweet boxes on it still tied.
