**What the author asked for:** *"Please check everything please check."* Not a chapter, not a rewrite. Run every gate on the real
files, then read the tree against itself, and repair what is the workshop's own fault. Written twice in one message, so it was
taken as: no corner unlooked at.

**The short answer.** The shape holds. Eighteen chapters, 105,904 words, every gate that measures the writing green, and **not one
line of canon dialogue out of place, not one naag thing explained, not one secret announced before its episode.** Nine faults were
found. **Seven are repaired here.** The two that are not are documentation, not prose: the chapter checkpoints in
`foundation/WORLD_STATE.md` stop at Chapter 9, and that is nine chapters of knowledge state owed — the largest hole in the
workshop and the one that can do the most damage to a chapter written from it. Both files now carry a warning at the head so that
nobody trusts them by accident.

## 2 · What was wrong, and what was done

**1 · The two screens a reader opens first were a month stale.** `README.md` said *"**Chapters:** seventeen written — Episodes 1 to 5
in Chapters 1–13, Episode 6 in Chapters 14–15, Episode 7 in Chapters 16–17"* and *"**State on 2026-11-11:** … Episodes 1 to 7 are
written whole — seventeen chapters … to `Chapter_17`"*. `STATUS.md` — the file that says *read this first* — opened on *"**Nine
chapters are written — 41,724 words** — and … Episode 4 has not been begun. The next chapter is 10."* Nine chapters and 26,000
words out of date, on the two files whose whole job is to be true. All three lines now read eighteen chapters, Episodes 1 to 8,
105,904 words, and name the real next move: Chapter 19, with the returning brother as its first decision.

**2 · A cousin was named Vikram.** Chapter 14 has the mehendi courtyard shout the V-names across each other — *"Vikram. Varun.
Vineet. Vishal."* — and canon's **Vikram** is not a Suri cousin: he is Rishabh's son and Aryaman's childhood friend, and he walks
into the Suri hall in **Episode 9** as Pinky's intended and Ahana's beloved. A reader who met him as a cousin in Chapter 14 would
have to unlearn it in Chapter 19. The shout now reads **Vikrant**, the footer names Vikrant, and the rule is written into
`canon/E09_BEATS.md` ruling 12 so no later chapter hands his name to a cousin to save itself a name.

**3 · The place was spelled two ways.** Chapter 14 spelled it **Atari** — in its scene heading and in six lines of speech — while
Chapter 13, `foundation/`, `canon/SEASON.md` and every source spell **Attari**, which is canon's own spelling of the border village
in the E06 and E09 captions. Corrected in Chapter 14 and in `canon/E06_BEATS.md` (5 more). A place name is not a canon line: it is
written the way the tree writes it, everywhere.

**4 · Three name typos.** *Shalinie* twice (Chapters 2 and 13) and *Aaryaman* once, inside Vihaan's own speech in Chapter 11 — *"use
Aaryaman ke naam se hoga"*. All three repaired. Nothing else survived the near-miss sweep against all twenty-four canon names
(*Daljeet* and *Karant* are canon's own badly-heard names and stay).

**5 · Two action tics.** *"With his jacket over his arm"* stood **eleven times in eight chapters**, and *"with her bag on her
shoulder"* **fourteen times in four** — the same stage direction doing duty for a picture in a book that is supposed to see. Both
are varied now, not deleted: the jacket goes over two fingers at the shoulder, by the collar, hooked on a finger, folded on a sill;
the bag rides on an elbow, comes up on one strap, hangs off a hand, is held in both hands. Five and six instances are left where
the object is doing a beat's work. `check.py` and `camera_check` green after.

**6 · `O034` had him engaged to a Suri.** The row the author's own arc is written in said *"engaged to Vikram Suri"*. He is not a
Suri and the page cannot afford the confusion (§2.2). Corrected in `OPEN.md`.

**7 · The London reading did not survive Chapter 18 — and now it does.** `O034` and `foundation/TIMELINE.md` §4a said he *"takes the
London end of the business"*, which reads as a man going back to run his desk. But Chapter 18, `## List`, is already written and
already better: Parmeet takes his spectacles off and says *"London office. Isko sabse pehle"* — **the house is selling the London
end, first**, and Vihaan is the one who can read its papers, because *"London office mere naam pe hai."* So the six months are not
a business handed back to him; they are the closing of it, with his own signature on the last page, and nobody in that house stops
him. Both rows now say that, in the author's arc's own terms, because the Episode 9 chapter stands on it.

**8 · The Episode 9 ledger had not ruled the contradiction in canon itself.** Ep 6, 27:25, has Dimple say *"mere Vikram aur Pinky ki
shaadi"* in the front room — and Chapter 15 writes it, faithfully. Ep 9 then runs the same engagement as a surprise *"use
engagement ke baare mein toh pata hi nahin"* (07:28) that nobody has told the groom about (07:55). Both are canon. Nothing ruled
it, so a later chapter could have "fixed" one of them and called it a repair. **Ruling 11** now holds it: the page keeps both,
smooths neither, and has nobody in the hall explain it.

**9 · Nine chapters of knowledge are unrecorded.** `foundation/WORLD_STATE.md`'s newest checkpoint is **Chapter 9**;
`foundation/OC_STATUS.md`'s is **Chapter 7**. Everything the tree learned after that — Gurinder Arora's identification of the
impostors (13–14), the warrant (16), the wedding night, the stabbing and the burial (17), the pit, the naming, the sell-off list and
the missing-person report (18) — is in the chapter footers and nowhere in the file that exists to hold it. **Not repaired**, because
it cannot be honestly repaired by guessing: nine checkpoints have to be written out of the nine chapters, one reading pass each.
Both files now carry a warning at the head saying exactly that, so the next writer reads the footers instead of trusting the table.
This is the largest item on the debt list below, and it goes before Chapter 19.

## 3 · What passed, and the evidence

- **Not one naag thing is explained in eighteen chapters.** A script that strips every quotation mark and looks for the vocabulary
  finds the show's title card, the temple's name, the wall paintings, and the promos. Nothing else. The snake is a body and a
  sound: *"a hood moves, and nothing in the picture says what it is joined to"*, *"the cloth is warm in a cold room, and it stays
  warm"*, *"her voice is a girl's voice and the words are not"* — and each of those three sentences is in the file that quotes it.
- **Not one secret is announced early.** The stabbing on the wedding night is not Aryaman's here and the page does not say whose
  face it is borrowing: one note lower than the man she knows, a button whose thread *"is not a shirt's thread"*, and *"there is
  nobody on the stairs"* — while the real Vihaan is on a kerb in Ghaziabad with three children asleep in the car. Canon's own
  Ep 15 flashback explains it; the page does not, and must not until that episode.
- **The camera is clean across all eighteen**: no comparison in narration, no past tense, no sentence over sixty words, and speech
  between 32% and 69% of paragraphs.
- **Every chapter's dialogue is one hand**: plain double quotes, transliterated Hindi, attribution after the line, no italics, no
  curly quotes anywhere in the tree.
- **No repeated scene closer, no repeated eight-word sentence between chapters, no duplicated prose.** The faults of the last sweep
  have not come back.
- **Episode 9's ledger is evidence, not memory**: 24 beats, 8 citations, every citation on a caption block that exists.
