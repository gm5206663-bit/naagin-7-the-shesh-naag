## §2 · The rulings, made before the prose

1. **B01 is not written.** The cold open has no face, no room and no addressee, the episode never returns to it, and any assignment of it would be an invention at the highest possible price. It is declared absent in the footer instead of guessed at.
2. **The man told to take the girls out is Vihaan** (canon's *Rishabh*, `OPEN-04`, `O003`). He goes out through canon's own door, with the children of the house, and is not in the building when the killing begins. What keeps him out is not invented removal but the errand itself and the storm that canon has already put over the city.
3. **The two sisters stay**, because canon says so twice: Parmeet sends the girls out and the inspector stops them — *"aap dono behenein kahin nahi ja sakti, kyunki aapke papa ne hi complaint ki hai."* One order empties the room of the daughters it can empty; the two it cannot stay.
4. **Ananta's claim is a lie she chooses.** Canon has her father say the truth (B18) and has her say the opposite one breath later (B20). The page plays it as her decision, in her own voice, and never lets the narration call it noble.
5. **The body at 20:39 is Ananta's.** The track is ambiguous for about ninety seconds — a second naagin comes in (B26) and the killing order (B27) names *"is naagin"* — but B30, B31 and B41 all stand over one woman, the bride, and the season that follows is Purvi's revenge for her family. Uttara comes in, is recognised, and is driven back; Ananta dies of the reed and the brass knife. **Declared reading, reversible.**
6. **The been is Maa Sapela's, and she is not on this page.** Canon orders it played (B22) and never shows who plays it. The page keeps her off the scene and leaves the instrument audible from another room — the one thing in Episode 6 the family did not know it was bringing into the house.
7. **The names of the men are not invented.** The track gives **Kareem** for Daljeet and mangles the rest; the page uses what the track gives clearly, and leaves the speaker's own Changistani name unvoiced, in his mouth, unfinished — he is interrupted, and canon never finishes it either.
8. **Pradeep** is canon's one-use name and is used once here.
9. **The naagmani's hiding places are spoken as canon speaks them** — southern temples, the Nilgiris, the caves of Kailash — because the men are reciting a legend they do not know the end of.
10. **The two faces, whose face, and whose hand (corrected by the author twice, 2026-11-12).** Canon's `B34`–`B35` are spoken by a man wearing **Aryaman's** face; `CANON.md` R10 settles who is inside it — **Bharani, one of the kul's naagins, Uttara's companion**, who strikes away from the spine on purpose so that Purvi will seem dead and live. Nothing of the enemy is in that passage: it is the kul's own hand, on the kul's own law, in the face of the human the girl loves. **In this adaptation the face is Vihaan's** (`OPEN.md` line 82, `O016`), so the promise, the laugh, the mockery and the knife are his shape and a woman's voice, and Purvi is left believing that the man who told her *koi list nahin hai* put a blade in her back. Aryaman is nowhere near it, which is the one change that flatters him. Canon's cruelty about her small eyes and big dreams is not spoken at all: a kul's naagin does not insult a girl's face, she tells her what she has done — *tumne insaan chun liya.*
