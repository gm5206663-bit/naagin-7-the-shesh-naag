# TWO PAIRS OF EYES — the working file for `O033`

**Owns: how the two of them see each other and how the world sees each of them, in working detail — the canon evidence, the map of what is already on the page in Chapters 1–9, the plan for Chapters 10 onward, the ban list in one place, and one specimen passage so the law can be seen working before it is used. The law itself is `canon/STYLE_CANON.md` §1g. The ruling is `OPEN.md` `O033`.**

Read this before writing any scene in which both of them are in the frame, and before writing any scene in which somebody in that house has an opinion about Purvi's face.

---

## 1 · The law in five lines

1. **The pull is there before the awakening**, in both of them, from the first look. It is weather, not a decision, and it is never named.
2. **The world's eyes and his eyes disagree, and both are true.** To that house she is plain. To him she is beautiful — and more than beautiful, *something special* he has no word for.
3. **Her eyes on him are not about his face.** The world already finds him beautiful (`O025`). What she sees is the quiet in him, and it frightens her.
4. **Nothing corrects it.** No makeover, no compliment from the room, no narration taking a side.
5. **It is shown in one device per chapter**, and the devices are in §1g.4.

## 4 · The plan, Chapter 10 onward

One device a chapter. Never two. Never announced. Never in a chapter where a canon beat has already spent the room.

| Order | Device (§1g.4) | Shape it can take |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One frame, two seeings** | Somebody in the hall decides out loud what she looks like; the frame stays on his face while they say it. |
| 2 | **The object** | The thing in her hands when he comes in — a plate, a file, the broken frame, a stack of invitation cards he takes off her before she drops it. |
| 3 | **The held look after she turns away** | The show's own grammar, and the chapter's last shot. She goes; the camera stays; he is still standing where she left him. |
| 4 | **What he remembers** | A small thing she said that nobody recorded, returned to her later by him, in passing, without weight. |
| 5 | **Her looking back** | She looks one beat too long and then finds work for her face. He does not see it. The reader does. |

**Never, in any of them:** the word coming out of his mouth, the narration grading her, a third party's compliment, or a change in her clothes meant to explain his attention.

## 5 · A specimen, so the law can be seen working

**Declared: this is a study, not a scene.** It belongs to no chapter, it is not counted in the ledger, and it is not canon. It is here so the author can say yes or no to the *method* before Chapter 10 spends four devices on it. The occasion is the engagement hall of Chapter 9, seen from the side of the room Chapter 9 did not stand in.

---

Three chairs stand on their heads by the side door and Vihaan carries them two at a time, and the fourth under his arm.

"Yeh kameez?" An aunt has a girl by the elbow at the buffet. "Ismein nahin. Change karo. Nimrat ji ko dekho, kaisi lagti hain."

The girl goes. The flies stand on the rasgulla tray and nobody moves them.

"Woh teen kursi zyada hain." Pinky's pen comes up off her list. "Woh teen. Hatao. Photographer ko jagah chahiye."

"Ji." Vihaan puts them where the photographer wants them, and comes back along the wall with the last one, and stops.

Purvi comes in past the side door with her dupatta sliding off one shoulder and a steel glass in each hand, walking carefully, watching the glasses.

"Purvi!" The aunt's voice goes over the hall. "Woh glass yahan rakh do. Aur yeh dupatta—" She waves at it without looking up. "—theek karo. Aaj photo khichwa rahe hain."

"Ji, Masi." Purvi sets the glasses down and puts her chin down and pulls the dupatta up, and it slides off again, and she puts a hand on it and leaves the hand there.

She turns to go back for the rest of them.

Vihaan does not move out of her way.

"Sorry—" She stops. "Aap—"

"Main hoon." He steps back against the wall for her, and she goes past, and one lens of her spectacles has a light in it from the chandelier, and then she is through the door.

He stands where he is with the chair still in his hand.

Then he looks at the chair, and puts it down with the others, and goes to help the man at the shutter.

---

*What the study is doing, device by device, so it can be judged and not just read:*

1. **One frame, two seeings.** The aunt's mouth and his eyes are in the same room and the page never connects them. Nothing says the aunt is wrong and nothing says he is right.
2. **The world's mouth does the dismissing.** *Ismein nahin. Change karo.* Not the narration.
3. **The object.** The steel glasses, carried carefully, watched while she walks. Then the chair, still in his hand after she is gone.
4. **The held look after she turns away, inverted.** She does not see him not moving out of the way, and the room does not see him not moving, and the reader sees both. No sentence tells the reader what it means.
5. **The pull before the awakening.** He is in her way — the friction canon demands (`§1e.3`) — and neither of them knows why the room has gone quiet for one second.
6. **The omen, unattended.** The flies on the rasgulla tray, and nobody in the hall moves them.

*What it deliberately does not do:* it does not say she is beautiful, it does not use the word, it does not have him think a thought, and it does not let anybody in the hall agree with him. **The difference between what the aunt sees and what he sees is in the timing of one step back, and that is all it is allowed to be.**
