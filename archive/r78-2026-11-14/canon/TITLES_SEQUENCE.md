# THE TITLES AND THE PRECAP — this season's opening forty seconds, and its last twenty

**STATUS — THE FRAME SITS AT BOTH ENDS OF EVERY CHAPTER NOW, 2026-11-12 (the author's second order that day).** He was shown the block at the head of Chapter 1 and the precap at its foot, was asked which end to repeat across the season, and answered **both**: the short opening cut at the head of every chapter, the twenty-second preview at the foot of every chapter. Applied in this build: the twelve-second theme at the heads of Chapters 2–19, a cue line on the fifteen precaps that already existed, the three precaps the book was missing (the feet of 14, 16, 17), and the card in place of a preview at the foot of Chapter 19, because after the season's last chapter there is no tomorrow to sell.

**Two claims this file carried are corrected in the same pass, and one of them was made by the build that re-cut the intro.** (a) The reversed ruling: the frame was written once and **not again**, on the argument that sixteen copies of the montage is a paste — the author has overruled it, and the compromise the show itself uses is the *twelve-second* cut, not the forty-second one. (b) The false inventory: this file told him *"no chapter after the first carries a titles block or a precap above its footer"*, and that was untrue of the precaps — fifteen chapters (1–12, 13, 15, 18) already close on `## What Comes Next`, in prose, *a precap as the episode gives one*. What was genuinely missing was the **sound**: not one of the fifteen said which four notes sit under the promise. `FIXED:`, not a butterfly — no chapter's story moved, only what this file says about the book.

**Owns: the two pieces of screen this book has never had.** The title sequence — cold open, title card, the question, the montage — and the precap that closes every chapter the way the show closes every episode. Written as a shooting script: image, sound, duration. Everything here is **A**, authored, and none of it may state a fact about this world that `CANON.md` does not hold.

**Why they were written.** The author, on the fourth pass at canon style: *"You missing the intro preview music, background and others many things."* He is right, and it is the one gap that no amount of prose can close, because the titles and the precap are not prose — they are the show's **frame**, and this book has been putting its story in a frame of blank paper.

**The two rules that govern every shot below.**
1. **The form is not shown.** Canon has not described her shape yet; the titles may show eyes, wrist, scales, hood in shadow, water, hair — never the completed serpent. The show itself holds its shape back on the first night.
2. **Nothing here is a fact.** If a shot would tell the audience something `CANON.md` does not know, the shot is wrong.

## I · The title track — the question

**The franchise's opening theme is not a description. It is a question, sung by a man:** *Kaun Hai Woh* — who is she. This season's asks the question the mirror asks, because canon's own mirror line is *"a mirror shows a naagin her true face"* (S-10):

> **"Kaun hai woh — jo aaine mein khud ko nahin pehchanti?"**

| | The cue |
|---|---|
| **Key** | minor, one drone under everything — the been's own scale |
| **Instruments** | *been* (lead, unprocessed, close), a low string bed, a single frame drum, a choir of women on one syllable only |
| **Voice** | male, low, unpushed. He asks twice; the second time the choir answers under him and does not resolve |
| **The hook** | the two words **"kaun hai woh"** — nothing else is sung clearly; every other line is a syllable |
| **Length** | 42 seconds on the first night, 12 seconds' cut-down on later nights (the theme, then straight in) |
| **The rule** | the love theme never plays over the titles. The titles are the question; the love theme is the answer, and they are two different pieces of music. **The handover (`§II`, 2026-11-12): on the sequence's last image the been stops and his one low string is struck and left hanging, unanswered** — one register, then the other, never both at once (`STYLE_CANON §1h.12`), and the season's opening now ends on *his* note instead of her eye |

**What the re-cut does, and why it is the author's own rule and not a decoration.** The eye that used to close the sequence was a question about her. Now the sequence ends on **his face and his note, unanswered** — the season's first forty seconds say that there is a second person in this story whom nobody has explained, and the show's law against two registers at once is what lets the audience hear it: **her instrument stops when he appears.** The shot at 0:54 puts the two of them in the same corridor for one second without either of them speaking, which is the smallest thing the pair can be shown doing and the largest thing this intro can promise.

**What the sequence says without one word of plot:** a woman, a lamp, a temple, a mirror that shows nothing, and a question. A viewer who has never seen the franchise now knows the genre, the weather and the price of admission. A viewer who has seen all six seasons knows exactly which question is being asked.

## III · The precap — the last twenty seconds of a chapter

Canon's own precaps promise a **body event**, not a plot event (`canon/SOUND_AND_SCREEN.md` §4). The template, and then this book's first two.

**The template (20 seconds)**

| | Beat | Content | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–6s | **The threat**: one line, in the villain's mouth, straight to camera | the theme's first four notes |
| 2 | 6–12s | **The thing at risk**: a face, a hand, a door, a bowl | one held chord |
| 3 | 12–18s | **The promise**: the change, the blow, the reveal — **half seen**, and cut before it lands | the surge, cut off mid-rise |
| 4 | 18–20s | Black, the logotype's mark | nothing |

**The specimen this file carried for the foot of `Chapter 8`** is withdrawn. It put a Baljeet line — *"Woh apne aap ko bacha nahin paayegi."* — in the mouth of a chapter that does not contain it, and asked for a finger going to a button **with no number on it**, which Chapter 9 does not have either. A precap may promise only what the next chapter actually says; this one broke its own law, and it was sitting in the file the author was told to trust. What Chapter 8's foot carries is its own prose, written before this file existed, and it stays.

**The form a foot takes in this book** is the house's, not this file's — and the machine now checks every new precap line by line against the chapter it promises (`tools/build_frame.py` greps each image verbatim before it is allowed onto the page):

```
## What Comes Next

*A precap, as the episode gives one.*  +  the cue line: the theme's first four notes under the threat,
                                            one held chord on the thing at risk, the surge cut before it
                                            lands, nothing at all over the black and the card

<the threat, one line, in a mouth the next chapter really gives it> · <the thing at risk> · <the promise, half seen>

<one beat on the drum, and stop>
```

**The three feet that had nothing** — Chapters 14, 16, 17 — are written in that form on this order. **Chapter 19 has no precap** and carries `### THE CARD` instead: the question asked back at the audience, because the season's last chapter has no tomorrow.

| **The card** | At the foot of `Chapter_19` as `### THE CARD` — the question returned to the audience, there being no next night | ~120 words |

**What it costs.** The ledger moves **122,298 → 123,737**, and `thread_check` has three more scenes to carry (234 → 237). The heads cost nothing in the ledger because a `### ` block above the first scene heading is not a scene to `recount.py` or `eyes_check.py` — which is exactly why no frame block may be promoted to `## `.

**What it does not touch.** No canon fact, no canon line, no scene, no chapter's interior. The frame goes around the story; the story does not move. And the head cut is deliberately the short one: eighteen copies of the full montage would be the paste the last build refused, and no daily episode in this show has ever replayed forty seconds a night — it plays the theme, and goes.
