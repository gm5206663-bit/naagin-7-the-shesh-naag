# SOUND AND SCREEN — the show's furniture: the four musics, the titles, the precap, the palette, the weather

**Owns: everything you hear and everything behind the actors.** The franchise's music in its four registers, the anatomy of a Naagin title sequence, the anatomy of its precap, the colour and set grammar of the world, and the weather. `canon/FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` owns the devices, `canon/NAAG_LEXICON.md` the words, `canon/SITUATIONS.md` the scenes between the leads. This file owns the **furniture**, which is what the author named: *"You missing the intro preview music, background and others many things."*

Tag **F** = franchise ground, **C** = this season's canon, **A** = authored here.

---

## 1 · The four musics — the franchise runs on four scores, not one

| Register | What it is | Evidence | Ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The QUESTION** | The opening theme. The franchise's title track asks *who is she* — *"Kaun Hai Woh"* (Kailash Kher), and behind it the older snake-chant title, the *na naaga naaga naagin* rhythm. It is not a description of the heroine; it is a **question about her**, sung in a male voice, over a montage | **F**, all seasons; Season 7 has its own theme and title track | **Absent.** This book has no title sequence at all (`canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` writes one) |
| **The LOVE THEME** | One melodic identity for the couple, reused every season — *"Tere Sang Pyaar Mein Nahi Todna"* (Pamela Jain), which is literally a vow lyric: *with you, in love, I will not break it*. Seasons add their own licensed romantic song on top: *O Re Piya*, *Bol Do Na Zara*, *Kaise Hua*, *Baarish*, *Dil Ibadat*, *Tose Naina*, and Season 7 its own love theme and sad-love theme | **F**; S7's own pair of themes | **Named now** (`§9.3`, 2026-11-12): *Woh Do Log* — the pair's own identity. The chapters that use it are 9 and 10; the rest of the book is scored with the household and the sting |
| **The POWER / DEVOTION** | The *shakti* register: chants, aarti, the conch, drums, the *tandav* — and a title of its own (*"Naagin Ki Shakti Shiv"*). This is the register that plays when the god is in the scene or the naagin is about to be | **F**; C (the temple, the trial, the dance) | **Partly**: bells, aarti, the lamps. Never the **drums** |
| **The STING** | The show's punctuation: the **been** motif, the hiss, the transformation surge, the kill hit, the precap cut. Short, loud, and always attached to the same three events | **F**; C (the Mahasapera) | **Partly**: the been is named in eight chapters as score; no hiss in six chapters; no surge, no hit |

**The law that comes out of it (`§1h.12`): every chapter is scored, and the score is named.** Not a mood word — a **cue**: which register, which instrument, at which beat, and what it does to the scene. The show is never unscored, and it never scores two registers at once.

## 2 · The cue sheet — the show's own beats and what plays under them

| Beat | The cue | Length | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| A naagin enters a room | the **been** phrase, then a low drone | 6–10s | tells the audience before the room knows |
| She is compelled | the been, **played straight and close**, no reverb | as long as the flute | the horror is that it is *music* doing this |
| The change begins | a rising string surge, under the skin | 4–8s | the audience hears the body before it sees it |
| The change completes | drums, one hit, then **silence** | 2s hit + 3s of nothing | the silence is the effect |
| A hiss | the hiss, unattended, no music under it | 1s | the show's oldest sound |
| A kill | the sting, then the love theme reversed | 3s | the franchise's cruellest joke: the love melody turns |
| A darshan (the sight of the true thing) | the devotion register, full | 10s+ | the god is in the room |
| The couple alone | the **love theme**, instrumental first, vocal only when they touch | 20s–3min | this is where the song sequence lives |
| The couple separated | the sad-love theme, one instrument | 15s | *Season 7 has a named one; it is the couple's own* |
| The household | folk percussion, a radio, a pressure cooker, a bhajan from next door | continuous | the show's comedy and its realism both ride on this |
| A ritual | aarti bell, conch, the crowd's murmur dropping out entirely | 10s | the world stops politely |
| **Vihaan decides, lifts, or says a flat line** | **one low string, struck and left ringing**, with the flat of a hand on a drumhead under it | 3–6s | *his* cue (`§9.2`). No melody, ever |
| **The two of them alone, or touching** | *Woh Do Log* — his low string, and the been's second phrase over it; the vocal only on contact | 20s–3min | their theme (`§9.3`). The one place the show's question is answered |
| **Aryaman and Purvi in one room** | **nothing of the score's own**: the band, the radio, the dhol — the room's music, and it stops when they are alone | as long as the room | `§9.4` — the cut. The score does not want this pairing |
| The precap | the theme's **first four notes**, then a hard stop | 20s | see §4 |
| The title | the question, sung | 30–45s | see §3 |

## 3 · The title sequence — what is on the screen for the first forty seconds of every episode

The franchise's titles share an anatomy, and it is worth writing down because this book has never had one:

1. **The cold open first** — the episode's first scene, usually a killing or a birth, with no music at all.
2. **A hard cut on a face or an object** — the eyes, a hand, a lamp.
3. **The title card**: the season's logotype over the weather of the show — moon, cloud, a temple's silhouette, a slow-motion fall of something red.
4. **The montage**: the naagin's body in fragments — the eyes, the wrist, scales catching light, a hood, water, a sari's edge, a flame — never the full form on the first night. Every season holds its shape back for at least one episode.
5. **The question**, sung, over the last image, and a cut to black.
6. **The story begins**, and the score does not return until the first sting.

**Ours exists, and it is at the head of every chapter.** `canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` writes the season's own — cold open, title card, the question, the montage, and the precap that closes the chapter — as a shooting script, because the author was right that the missing thing was not a note *about* titles: it was the titles. Chapter 1 carries the full 42 seconds; Chapters 2–19 carry the **12-second cut-down** (the card, the question, the string left hanging, then straight in), which is what a daily episode actually plays after its first night; the shot list and the ledger effect are in that file's §IV.

## 4 · The precap — the franchise's last twenty seconds, and the little machine that sells the next night

Canon's own recaps carry them verbatim (*"Precap: Parmeet menaces Ananta, disclosing his knowledge of her being an Ichchadhari Naagin possessing a Naagmani…"*). The anatomy never changes:

| | Beat | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The threat**, one line, in the villain's mouth, to camera | the theme's first four notes |
| 2 | **A shot of the thing at risk** — a face, a hand, a door | one held chord |
| 3 | **The promise**: the change, the blow, the reveal, half-seen | the surge, cut off mid-rise |
| 4 | **Black**, and the title card's mark | **nothing** |

**The rule that makes it work:** the precap promises a **body event**, not a plot event. Not *"will she find out?"* but *"her skin will come off on Sunday."* This book closes on one at the foot of **every chapter but the last**: fifteen `## What Comes Next` scenes were already there and now carry this cue line, three were written on the author's order of 2026-11-12 (the feet of Chapters 14, 16, 17), and Chapter 19 takes the card instead of a preview. `tools/build_frame.py` will not write a promise whose images it cannot find in the chapter it points at; `tools/score_check.py` SC4 fails a precap that tries to sell a couple with soft music, which is the one thing a precap here is never allowed to be.

## 5 · The background — palette, sets, weather, objects

| Element | The franchise's grammar | Ours, measured |
|---|---|---|
| **Palette** | three colours carry the whole show: **gold** (the divine, the throne, the lamps), **green** (the snake, the poison, the garden), **crimson** (sindoor, blood, the cloth on a throne). Everything else is neutral, and the neutral is what makes the three work | gold 33 uses, red 100, **green 2**. The dark side of the palette — the snake's own colour — is not in the book |
| **Sets** | the **haveli/mansion** (marble, corridors, a lift, a chandelier, a kitchen); the **temple** (stone, lamps, a throne, a bell); the **workplace** (newsroom glass, monitors, the studio); the **street** (the market, the bus, the rain); and **Naaglok**, which is a country with a jail and a court | all present and good — the Arora flat, the channel floor, the Suri house, the mandir, the corridor, the lift |
| **Weather** | **rain is the franchise's romantic instrument.** Couple after couple has its defining scene in the rain; the love theme is written for it. Second is **moonlight** (the change), third is **heat** (the temple, the summer, the wedding season) | **rain, as weather, once in nine chapters — and wetness carries the book's best scene** (the water, the parapet, the wet sleeve in `Chapter 4`). The franchise's first instrument is played one note in 41,724 words |
| **Festival** | the Indian wedding season is the show's stage furniture: **mehendi, haldi, sangeet, the marigold, the dhol, the colour, the crowd**. Canon's own Mahasapera scene happens *at a sangeet* | **half there.** Marigold and dhol are in six chapters (24 uses) and the mandap in `Chapter 9` is real. But **mehendi 0 · haldi 0 · sangeet 0 · sindoor 1**: the book has the *decor* of a wedding and none of its **rituals**, which is where the crowd is |
| **Objects** | the show gives objects close-ups and lets them carry feeling: the *diya*, the *kalash*, the anklet with nine pearls, a ring with a past life in it, a broken lens, a *kesari* bowl, a mop and bucket | **good** — this is the book's strongest furniture: the broken lens, the sattvic box, the coins, the invitation cards, the eleven buttons with letters and no numbers |

## 6 · The picture between the leads — where the romance actually happens

The franchise does not build romance in dialogue. It builds it in **three-to-four-minute song sequences** — rain, garden, sari, a licensed romantic song — and in a short list of repeated physical situations. That catalogue is `canon/SITUATIONS.md`, with the four laws that constrain this book attached to each entry (`O033` no world-verdict on her face; `O017` the romance must be natural; `L-23` he is never there to do a job; `O029` the obstacle is *Veer* and *he is human for most people*).

**The measured fact.** Nine chapters, and the two of them have: a broken lens handed back, a corridor, a lift, a passage behind a hall. All of it is *right* and all of it is *dry* — no rain, no song, no festival, no food, no dance, no wet sleeve except one. The romance is currently carried by restraint alone, and restraint is a flavour, not a meal.

4. **The festival**: the engagement and the wedding are canon's, and canon's own Mahasapera plays at a *sangeet*. Mehendi, haldi, marigold and dhol are not decoration — they are the crowd that watches the couple, and the crowd is the point (`O033`).
5. **The drums.** The devotion register has no percussion in this book. It should have.

## 9 · Three identities, and the one pairing that gets no music — the author's ruling, 2026-11-12

**A**, authored on the author's own words of 2026-11-12, given when the episode's furniture was put in front of him: *"In start that' intro came, well there is it but change it's of vihaan and Purvi you design accordingly to everything. Then music, like female lead own that canon stay, but vihaan also had background music, you design. And there Purvi and vihaan romantic one also you design. Completely cut off Aryaman and Purvi canon romantic background music."*

Four orders, four answers below. Nothing in this section touches a canon line, a canon beat, or the law that the supernatural is never explained.

### 9.1 · Hers — the canon, and it does not move

| | |
|---|---|
| **The identity** | the **been** and the drone the titles sit on, and the question *Kaun hai woh* |
| **Status** | **canon, untouched.** The female lead's own theme is the franchise's and it stays exactly as §1 has it: a question about her, in a man's voice, never resolved |
| **What may not happen** | nobody may take it away from her, thin it, or hand a phrase of it to another character as a comfort |

### 9.2 · His — **`Ek Taar`** (*one string*)

| | |
|---|---|
| **The identity** | **one low string, struck and left ringing**, with the flat of a hand on a drumhead once underneath it. Three to six seconds |
| **What it is not** | **it is not a tune.** He has no melody in this season. A tune is something an audience can hum, and hum is what a crowd does for a woman it is watching; the man the crowd turns to gets a **note that is left hanging** and a room that stops talking. That is the whole design |
| **When it plays** | when he **decides** (a line said flat and meant), when he **arrives** somewhere he was not expected, and when he **holds still while a room moves around him** — three positions, all of them about where a man is standing. The fourth trigger this row used to carry, *when he **lifts** (a crate, a tray, a man's arm out of the way)*, was struck on 2026-11-14: `foundation/OC_VIHAAN.md` §3b runs *He does not carry things. He does not mend things.*, and a score note may not licence on the soundtrack an errand the OC file forbids on the page. Two chapters had already paid for the licence in prose that had to come out — the lobby crate in Chapter 4 and the brass crate and the garland tray in Chapter 9 — and `RULES.md` `L-23` says a struck errand is not re-caused, so the licence goes rather than the scenes staying quiet. If he is lifting something for the cue to play on, the cue is not what is wrong |
| **When it does not** | never when he is merely looked at — the looks are in the room, with the people in it, and the score may not tell the audience to want him; never in a temple, at a ritual, or in a room where the naag world is spoken or played, because the been owns those and the two instruments never share a scene (`§1h.12`: never two registers at once); never over a joke of his, which is played dry |
| **The tell** | his cue **stops the moment somebody thanks him, explains him or apologises to him.** The note is left hanging; the room fills it; that is the joke and the wound at once |
| **Why that is fair to canon** | the franchise's male lead is scored with ambience and a sting. He gets the **sting's own family** — the hit and the held low note — and no theme, because in this book no one has earned the right to sing for him yet |

### 9.3 · Theirs — **`Woh Do Log`** (*those two*)

| | |
|---|---|
| **The name** | taken out of Parmeet's own canon line in Episode 9 — *"Do log. Ek woh jo bolta hai, ek woh jo darta hai."* The season names their theme with the words the villain's father used to describe the two of them watching the same thing |
| **How it is built** | **his** low string, and **her** been's second phrase laid over it. It is the only piece of music in this book where the two registers touch, and that is why it can be used so seldom: it is not a mood, it is an event |
| **The voice** | a woman's voice, one line, in Hindi, and **only on contact** — a hand taken, a shoulder held, a wrist caught in the air. Instrumental otherwise (`§2`) |
| **Where the book already is** | Chapter 9's `The Song` has it on one string and will not let it resolve; Chapter 10's dance takes it up with the voice for one line and the band takes it back. Both are on the page already and neither moves |
| **What it never does** | it does not resolve. Not this season, not on his side of the story: it goes out on the string it came in on. It also never plays under **any** other pairing (`§9.4`) |

### 9.4 · The cut — **Aryaman and Purvi get no romantic music, and none, ever**

**The order.** *"Completely cut off Aryaman and Purvi canon romantic background music."*

**What that means as a law, not a mood:**
1. No romantic cue of any register — the love theme, a string, a flute, a vocal, a music-box figure, anything soft — may attach to a beat between Aryaman and Purvi. Not at the engagement, not at the dance he pulls her into, not at the ring, not at the mandap, not when she says the canon line about wanting the man the whole world wants.
2. The only music in their scenes is **the room's own**: the band, the radio on a caterer's table, the dhol at the sangeet, the bhajan from next door. It is diegetic, it is loud, and **it stops when they are left alone.** The silence is the cue.
3. What he is allowed, score-wise, is **the tasha knock** — the franchise's own villain's decision-sting, which Episode 6 already puts under his lines. He is given **a decision, not a feeling.**
4. Chapters 9 and 10 were checked and are clean: the love theme in both is on **Vihaan and Purvi** and nowhere else, so the cut binds the future, not a repair.

**What it costs, and it is registered (`foundation/BUTTERFLY.md` BF-76).** Canon softens that pairing and lets the audience want it for two or three episodes before the show takes the floor out from under it. This book refuses to do the softening, so Aryaman reads colder and the trap reads as a trap on the first night — which is what the author has been asking for since the second pass at him, and it means every later scene that canon plays as *wasted warmth* now has to be carried by blocking, by faces, and by the room, with no help from the music.

### 9.5 · What a chapter's footer must now say

The footer's `STAGING:` line (or its `Cue:` note) names **which of the three identities plays**, at which beat, for how long — the same discipline `§1h.12` already demands of the cue, now with names to use. A chapter in which Aryaman and Purvi share a scene says **`no score`** or names the room's own music. `tools/score_check.py` enforces the cut and counts all three: **0 faults in 19 chapters**, selftest green.
