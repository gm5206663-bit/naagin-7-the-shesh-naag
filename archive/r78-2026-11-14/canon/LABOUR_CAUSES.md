# THE THREE LABOUR CAUSES — the one thing in the book that is the author's to rule, and not a correction

**STATUS — TAKEN UP, 2026-11-11, option A, on the author's word *next*.** All three are re-caused in the chapters; the objects are untouched and only the **reason** is changed. Each chapter's footer carries its own `RE-CAUSED` line, and the whole pass is reversible on his word.

**Owns: three staging decisions.** Everything else in the corrections queue was canon's and has been restored (`STYLE_AUDIT.md` §11–§12). These three are not canon and not errors of fact: they are **authored reasons for Vihaan to be in a room**, and the author's own law is against them.

**The law being applied (`L-23`, `foundation/OC_PRESENCE.md` §2.3).** *"His reason to be in a room is never a job."* The author's words that produced it: *"what the hell this even vihaan doing."*

**Why they were written in the first place, honestly.** A servant's-son OC in a rich house has an easy passport: he can always be carrying something. Every one of the three below was built on that passport, and the objects are good objects — a coil of flex, a crate of glasses, a lens in its case. The problem is not the object. The problem is that the chapter makes the object his **reason**.

---

## Case 1 · `Chapter 3` — the coil of flex and the ladder

| | |
|---|---|
| **On the page** | *"At the back of the room, by the tall screen, a young man stands on a stool with a coil of flex in his hand."* And in the footer, in the workshop's own words: *"Vihaan is in the newsroom because he came home that morning and his sisters put a coil of flex in his hand."* |
| **The offence** | the sentence says *because*. That single word is the whole violation — the room is a job for him. |
| **What is canon here** | nothing. The award night's room is canon's; the young man on the stool is entirely authored. |
| **Recommended fix (smallest possible)** | **change the reason, keep the picture.** He is in that room because it is his family's room and he has nowhere else to be at nine at night; the flex is in his hand because it was on the table by the door and he picked it up on the way in and has not put it down. One sentence in the footer changes; three lines of prose change from *because* to *while*. |

## Case 2 · `Chapter 4` — the crate of glasses

| | |
|---|---|
| **On the page** | *"Out on the floor, Vihaan comes out from between the desks with a crate against his chest. Glasses in it, and a jug, and a steel box with the lid on."* |
| **The offence** | milder than Case 1: the crate is not why he is on the floor, it is what he is doing when she sees him. But it is the second time in two chapters that he enters carrying something for somebody, and a pattern is a character. |
| **What is canon here** | canon's nine o'clock bulletin and the broken lens are canon's; the crate is authored. |
| **Recommended fix** | **let him be carrying it for himself to put down** — the crate is going *out*, not *in*, and he is the one taking it out because nobody else was going to. It keeps the hands busy and removes the servant's errand. |

## Case 3 · `Chapter 6` — the lens in its case

| | |
|---|---|
| **On the page** | *"Vihaan comes along it with a camera in its case on his shoulder and the strap across his chest, and a lens in his other hand. The bay has sent for the lens and he is the one carrying it down."* And the lift: *"Vihaan steps in with the camera and the lens."* |
| **The offence** | *"the bay has sent for the lens"* — an errand with a sender. This is the one the author's original complaint was about. |
| **What is canon here** | nothing; the corridor, the lift and the door are authored. |
| **Recommended fix** | **take away the sender.** The camera is his (he is a boy with a camera in this house, established in Chapter 1's precap); the lens is his; he is coming down because the day is over, and the door he walks past is on his way out. Nothing is being sent anywhere. |
