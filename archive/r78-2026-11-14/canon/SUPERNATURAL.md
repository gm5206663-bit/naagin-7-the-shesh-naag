# SUPERNATURAL — the careful law for the naag material

**Owns: how the supernatural is written in this adaptation, now that the author has said *"please special careful with super
natural ones"* on 2026-11-12, on the same day he gave Vihaan's arc through the six-month leap. `canon/STYLE_CANON.md` §1c owns
the omen; `canon/FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` owns the franchise's devices; `canon/NAAG_LEXICON.md` owns the vocabulary. This file owns
the one thing none of them does: **the line between showing the naag world and explaining it**, and where a human man stands on
that line — because from Episode 9 onward this adaptation has a human man standing on it in the same house as a naagin.**

---

## 1 · The four rules that do not bend

1. **Never explain.** Nothing in the naag world is ever given a mechanism in narration. Not the change, not the pearl, not the
   kechuli, not the thirteen faces in a dead woman's eyes, not the voice that comes down over a burial ground with no body
   attached to it. Canon does not explain; the caption track does not explain; the page does not explain. A character may
   *believe* something and say it wrongly, and that is different — it is a person's mouth, not the page's gloss.
2. **The world reacts before anyone names it.** The lamp gutters, the bell at the door moves with no hand on it, the dog starts
   up and will not stop, the wind changes in a lane that had none, the score comes up under the scene. Every one of those is a
   *consequence* and none of them is a statement. `STYLE_CANON.md` §1c: one omen to a scene, placed where the camera catches it,
   never pointed at.
3. **The power costs somebody, every time.** The franchise's oldest law (`canon/FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §5): a naagin pays for love
   with her power, and the price is spoken aloud. In this adaptation it holds at street level too — every use of the naag thing
   takes something from a person in the scene: a woman in the ground, thirteen faces in a pair of eyes, a guard who cannot say
   whose order he was following, a boy who drove a van and is not the office's driver. **If a supernatural beat costs nobody, it
   is decoration and it comes out.**
4. **The snake is a body, not an effect.** Canon's naag moments are physical: the spine, the skin, the hands, the eyes, the
   cold coming off a sleeve, a ridge of something turning over in muddy water. The page writes the body and the sound. It does
   not write what the graphics would have to render, and it never writes a full transformation as a description of a
   transformation — it writes the room, the reaction, and the part that is seen.

## 2 · Where a human being is allowed to stand

Vihaan's powers are asleep (`foundation/OC_STATUS.md`), and the author's arc for him — love, a death, London, the return after
the leap — puts a man who cannot do any of it inside a naag story. Six rules for that man, and they are the whole reason this
file exists.

| | The rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **He may see effects and never the cause.** A lamp going out, a wound closing under a sleeve, a woman on a wet lane turning her head before anyone has spoken behind her — yes. A change, a kechuli, a hood, a pearl used: **never through his eyes** | The camera inside his head would force the page to explain what he cannot know, and rule 1 forbids it |
| **2** | **He does not explain the supernatural to the audience.** No speech in which he works out what the naag world is, no list of what is possible, no theory. He may be wrong out loud | The moment a character explains the magic, the scene becomes a lecture, and canon never lectures |
| **3** | **His scepticism is real and is not a debate.** He is a man of paper, numbers, permits and photographs; the world he lives in is one where a man can be bought and a van can be emptied overnight. He reaches for the dull explanation, half believes it, and keeps the other half | Canon's own men laugh at *"Mera naam Ananta… Naagin hoon."* The show earns belief slowly and never in a courtroom |
| **4** | **The naag world may smell him, and it is a fuse, not a flamethrower.** `O031`: at least one antagonist knows the whole of it. The kul recognises something in him and cannot say what. What that buys the page is a look held one beat too long, a naagin's hand stopping above his sleeve, a name not said. It buys **no power, no memory, no awakening, no fight won** | `O023`, `O029`: he is the Nagaraj and does not know it. The page spends the *tension*, never the arsenal |
**5** | **The pull is not knowledge, and it is played at length.** `O033`: the attraction exists before the awakening in both of them and is never named. When he comes back after the leap and meets the naagin the house calls **Ahana**, the frame obeys her, his hand stops, the lamp does something in a room with no draught — **at the first glance, and the chapter does not look away from it.** Jealousy is on the page too (his best friend's hand on her hand, and him explaining it as heat and crowd), because **he does not understand it**, which is the only form of it this rule allows. He keeps his voice throughout: the pull is not an excuse to write him as a statue (author, 2026-11-12; `O040`) | The show's whole grammar is a body that knows before the person does; a page that hides it in two paragraphs and a shrug is not restraint, it is the scene refusing to be written |
| **6** | **He is never the reason the magic happens.** No rescue that only works because he is standing there, no wound drawn to him, no pearl that reacts to him. His usefulness is worldly and it is enough: paper, law, money, a camera, a name in a file | The author's standing fault is forcing canon; this is the same fault with a flame effect on it |

## 3 · The seven ways this goes wrong

1. **The travelogue transformation** — the page describes the change as a sequence of effects. Write the room and the part that is seen.
2. **The explainer** — the sceptic who becomes the narrator of the mythology by the second act.
3. **The free miracle** — a power used, nobody pays, the scene moves on.
4. **The franchise's rank inflation** (`FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §11): more power each episode, gods behind gods. Season 7 stays at the size canon gives it.
5. **The horror that is only a scare** — a hiss with nothing behind it and nothing taken. Rule 3.
6. **The human as mascot** — the loved one who exists to be protected. He has a desk, a file, and a country's worth of paper.
7. **The revelation too early** — Episodes 8's burial, the thirteen faces, and the name *"Ananta"* are canon's own slow coin. Anything the episode withholds, the page withholds, and `WRITING.md` §8 rules 2 and 7 govern it.

## 4 · The arc that makes this urgent

The author, 2026-11-12: **Vihaan realises he loves Purvi; he learns she died; it breaks him; he goes back to London; and he
comes back after the time skip that is coming.** Canon's own six months (Episodes 9 onward) is that skip, and at the end of it
a naagin enters the Suri house as **Vikram's intended**, under the name **Ahana**.

So the page is about to hold: a man in mourning for a woman who is alive; a naagin wearing a stranger's name in his own
house; a family that buried one of them in a pit behind the industrial sheds; and a pull between the two of them that neither
will name. **Every rule above is the fence around that.** Written carefully, it is the best material in the adaptation. Written
carelessly — a recognition at the door, a sceptic's lecture, a miracle with no bill — it is the thing the author has been
correcting me for since Chapter 1.
