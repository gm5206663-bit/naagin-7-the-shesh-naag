# CANON STYLE — the show's own grammar, as a working spec

**Owns: what "canon style" means on the page, now stated at the strength the author asked for on 2026-11-11: the sound, the score, the omen, the scale of feeling, the leads' dynamics, the episode machinery, and Hindi dialogue. `WRITING.md` remains the prose law and cites this file; where the two touch, this file is the newer ruling and wins.**

**Why this file exists.** After seven chapters and 31,504 words the author said the thing that matters most: *"canon naagin style completely missing… i don't feeling like i reading episode… specially the original things you creating."* He was right, and it was measurable. Across seven chapters: **zero hisses, three passages of wind, four of thunder, seven ritual words, no slaps, four mentions of music, and two voiced thoughts** — in a show built on all of them. The workshop had written a restrained literary novel about a naagin; the show is a maximalist Hindi daily soap with the occult in every frame.

**The cause, named so it is not repeated.** The camera law (no comparison in narration; and, until 2026-11-12, no reported interiority) is a *lens*, not a ceiling. **The interiority half of it is deleted — see `O035` and §2 below; the author removed the ban on feelings from every file.** The workshop treated its lens as taste, and taste drifted to what reads well in English literary fiction. **Correct facts, clean prose, wrong show.** Canon style governs the invented material hardest, because nothing forces an invented scene to be big — only the law does.

---

## 1 · The five things that were missing, and the laws that replace them

### 1a · Hindi is the voice of the show (`O027`)

Dialogue is **romanised Hindi**, and wherever the caption track carries the line, the line is **the show's own words transliterated**, not a translation of them: *"O Nimrat, teri packing-shacking hui ki nahin?"* · *"Museebat aayi nahin hai. Abhi tak aane waali hai."*

- **Scheme.** Plain ASCII romanisation, no diacritics: `aa`, `ee`, `oo` spelled out as they are heard; `hai` / `hain`; `nahin`; `kya`; `kyun`; `tum` / `aap`; `ji`. One word, one spelling, for the whole book. A glossary of the kept words lives in `STORY.md` §2 and grows.
- **Untranslatable things stay Hindi and are never italicised:** *naagin, naag, kechuli, tapasya, pralay, srijan, vardani, ichchhadhari, Naagmani, kul, akhara, aarti, sindoor*. English narration may gloss a word once, in a clause, and never again.
- **Narration stays English.** The camera says what is in the room. When a character thinks aloud, the aside is italic and in Hindi, because that is the voice in their head.
- **A canon line is not improved.** If the caption garbles, the page renders what is heard and the footer says so.
- **What this buys.** Every scene now sounds like the episode instead of describing it: the Punjabi of the Suri house, the Delhi Hindi of the Aroras, the pandit's shloka-flat Sanskritised speech, the villains' clipped orders.

### 1b · Sound and score are on the page

The caption track itself marks music: `[संगीत]` stands in the cues where the show's score comes up. **So the score is evidence, not decoration.**

- A scene may open on its sound: a bell, a been, a hiss, a horn, a scream, a door, a phone, rain.
- The score may be named as it behaves: *the score comes up*, *the theme under it*, *the strings go out and leave one flute*, *the music stops dead*.
- **A song may be named where the episode names it. No lyrics are quoted** (`WRITING.md` §1).
- A hiss with no snake in the frame is this show's oldest device: it is allowed, and it is declared in the footer as authored unless canon has it.
- Silence is a device too, and it must be earned: the show goes quiet only for a face.

### 1c · The omen in every scene, invented ones included

Canon never plays a scene in a clean room. Written into the frame, unexplained, unattended by any character:

| Device | How it is used |
|---|---|
| **The lamp** | A diya that gutters when the wrong person enters, or stands straight up when a right one does |
| **Wind and curtains** | They move when something not human is near; the room notices the curtains, never the cause |
| **Thunder, storm, a sky going dark in an instant** | The show's punctuation before a reveal; the weather of Episode 1 is canon's own |
| **The mirror** | Naag things show true in mirrors and water |
| **The dog, the crow, the flies, the ants** | The street's reaction, never explained |
| **The cold patch** | A stair, a lift floor, a doorway that is warm or cold for no reason |
| **The moon** | The clock of this world; on Poornima it is not background |

**The rule:** one omen to a scene, placed where the camera would catch it, never pointed at, never explained, never mentioned by anyone in the room.

### 1d · The scale of feeling is the soap's, not the short story's

- **Big delivered big.** A mother's blessing, a curse, a threat, a vow: said at full voice, in Hindi, with the room as witness. Canon gives these four to eight lines and the actor earns them. So does the page.
- **The family is a chorus.** Every household scene has more than two people in it, and the ones not speaking react: a gasp, a hand to the mouth, a name called out, a child pulled back.
- **Touch is physical.** A slap, a wrist caught, a forehead under a palm, a hand over a mouth, a blessing hand on a head, feet touched, a pallu pulled over the face.
- **Repetition is music.** A line said twice, or said again with one word changed. *"Chhod mera haath." "Chhod mera haath."*
- **Crying is allowed, and named once.** The show's women weep openly and the show means it (`O019` permits the plain feeling where the face shows it).
- **No irony about faith, fate or the kul.** Ever. The show believes every word of it, and so does the page.

### 1e · How the leads are played — the thing the author named first (`O028`)

The male lead and the female lead are never played as two nice people meeting. This show has a grammar for them, and the invented scenes must use it:

1. **The frame obeys her.** When Purvi is in a scene the camera is hers: she enters and the shot slows; wind moves her hair; her pallu, her bangles, her spectacles, her broken lens are given a close-up; the room's eyes go to her before anyone speaks. **And the room never says why, and the room never says she is beautiful** — the eyes come to her for something nobody in that room can put a name to, and the moment a relative or a colleague supplies the word *beautiful* the scene is wrong (§1g).
2. **The world reacts before the people do.** When the two of them are near each other and neither knows why: the lamp gutters, the wind changes, a dog barks, the score comes up under the scene for the first time. It is never explained and never mentioned.
3. **Friction before warmth.** A first meeting carries a dare, a correction, a small humiliation one of them refuses to accept, an object taken out of a hand — not two courtesies.
4. **An object changes hands.** The lens, the screwdriver, the garland, the spectacles, the glass of water, the file. The object is the scene.
5. **He is looked at, and he does not perform.** Canon's way with a beautiful man: other people's eyes, held too long, and his own complete uninterest in being looked at (`O025`).
6. **A held look after the other has turned away.** The camera stays on the one still looking. This is the show's whole grammar of falling in love.
7. **The interruption arrives at the worst moment.** A horn, a phone, a guard, a mother, a shutter coming down.
8. **Their scenes are stolen back by canon.** The two of them get their scenes in the gaps the episode leaves, and never at the cost of a canon beat (`RULES.md` gate 5).
9. **The romance is never announced.** The narration does not know they are in love. The lamps, the wind and the score know, and that is all.

### 1f · The episode machinery

- **Cold open** on a voice and a place, with place and year given the way a title card gives them.
- **Title card** once, at the head of the book's first chapter: the serial's name, as the show gives it.
- **Ad-break hooks.** The show cuts to its break on a face, a line or a door opening. The chapter cuts there too, and the next scene starts cold.
- **The precap.** Canon ends an episode with seconds of what is coming. A chapter may close on a short authored precap, declared in the footer, showing only what the next chapter actually contains.
- **The hold.** Every scene has minutes with no words: a face, music, a slow walk, a snake in the grass. A hold is written as a hold.

### 1g · Two pairs of eyes — how beauty is seen in this story (`O033`)

Canon's leads are not two average people who grow on each other. They are the pair the whole cosmology is arranged around, and **the pull is in the room from the first look** — before the awakening, before the truth, before either of them has a reason. And the world does not see what they see. Both of those are now law.

**1 · The pull comes first, and it is nobody's decision.** It is there in the first scene they share and it never had a beginning the page is allowed to show. This is the naagin grammar: made for each other, and the bodies find out before the people do. What it looks like on the page: he does not move out of her way; she says a line she has rehearsed and it comes out wrong; a hand is offered and both of them look at it; someone says an ordinary sentence and neither of them hears it. **It is never spoken, never named, never explained by the narration, and never used as a plot device to hurry them.** It is weather, not a decision.

**2 · Two pairs of eyes, and neither pair is wrong.**
- **The world's eyes.** To that house, that street, that newsroom, **Purvi is not a beautiful girl.** She is the one in spectacles with a broken hinge, plain clothes, a shopping list. Her sister is the one the room turns for. This is not the world being cruel — it is the world being accurate about the surface, and it must be given without venom and without pity: an aunt pairing people off and skipping her, a cousin's friend looking past her to Ananta, a beautician who assumes the sister is the client.
- **His eyes.** To Vihaan, in the same frame, she is **beautiful** — at once, before anything is true, before he knows her name — and more than beautiful: **something special**, and he has no word for it. He does not compare her with anyone. He does not think a verdict. **It is in his body and not in his mouth:** he stops in the middle of a sentence; he is still standing where she left him; he remembers a thing she said three weeks ago and nobody else did; he looks at the door after it has closed behind her; and when she laughs at something stupid, his face does the thing that the room would have to be told about, because the room is not looking at him either.
- **And her eyes on him.** The whole world already finds him beautiful (`O025`) — that is not what is different. What is different is that in her eyes he is **quiet**, and something behind his face at rest frightens her, and she cannot look at him for long without having to look away and start a sentence about nothing.

**3 · The canon evidence, so this is not a taste.** In the material the workshop holds, canon has a man call Purvi beautiful **once** — Aryaman, drunk, to Ahana (`canon/SEASON.md`, second arc) — and by then she has awakened and lives in that house under another name. Before the awakening, canon never has anybody praise that face. The show itself keeps the world's eyes off her until she is no longer the girl in spectacles. **Vihaan is the exception, and that is the point.** He is the only person on earth who thinks the plain one is the event, and he does not know why, and it will turn out to be the one true thing anybody in this story ever felt.

**4 · How it is written — the mechanics.**
| Device | Use |
|---|---|
| **One frame, two seeings** | Never a paragraph about his taste. The same shot: the aunt's sigh over her *kameez*, and his eyes on her hands. Nothing says the two are related. |
| **The world's mouth does the dismissing** | The dismissal is always *spoken by someone*, never narrated. The camera does not agree with it and does not argue with it. |
| **The object** | The thing in her hand at that moment — the broken lens, the shopping list, a plate of something, a chair. He sees her holding it. Give it to him in a close-up. |
| **The held look after she turns away** | The show's whole grammar of falling (`§1e.6`). This is where the difference is shown and never stated. |
| **What he remembers** | The cheapest proof of a man in love in this show: he remembers a small thing about her that she did not think was being recorded. |
| **Her looking back** | One beat, then she finds something else to do with her face. She does not hold it. She is the one with everything to lose in that house. |

**5 · The ban list, and it is hard.**
- **No makeover.** She does not get a *sari-and-lens-off* scene that makes the world see what he saw. If a canon transformation later changes her (and canon's own record says it does), the seeing has already happened, and the moment the world starts looking is **a loss, not a triumph** — the one thing that was hers, and the one man who did not need it, are not the same thing any more.
- **Nobody in the Suri house ever calls her beautiful**, and no servant, guard or colleague supplies the compliment on the show's behalf.
- **The narration never calls her beautiful.** Not once, not in her own scenes. The narration is the camera; the camera loves her and it does not grade her. `tools/eyes_check.py` reds a narration line that tries.
- **No line of his says it yet.** One day he will say it and it will be a scene. It is not said as a compliment across a table, and it is never said to make her feel better about an insult.
- **No self-pity in her.** Purvi already knows the arithmetic and says it herself, plainly, once, to her sister (*"Ek tu hai jise sab dekhte reh jaate hain. Aur ek main hoon jise koi nahin dekhta"*, Chapter 2). She is not fishing and not wounded. She has decided the world is right, which is what makes his seeing her cost him nothing and mean everything.

**6 · Where it lands next.** Chapter 10 onward, one device per chapter, never two, and never announced. The map is in `canon/TWO_PAIRS_OF_EYES.md`.

### 1h · The franchise's own body — what the other seasons do that this one was not doing

*Added 2026-11-11, out of the franchise study in `canon/FRANCHISE_STYLE.md`. The author asked for canon style across **all** the Naagin seasons, not only Season 7, and measuring the nine chapters against the franchise found the sickness exactly: the omens are in good order (18 of 27) and the **body is missing** (13 of 45) — no fang, no venom, no scale on a living girl, no vow sworn aloud, no stolen face. Season 7 has been kept faithfully; the *genre* has not. These are laws now.*

**1h.1 · The body speaks before the girl does.** A change is never announced by a line, never explained by the narration, and never clean. It goes in the franchise's order — the body's warning, the eyes, the sound, the skin, the form, the cost — with at least three of the six steps present and in that order (`FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §2). Canon's restraint holds: **the form is not described until canon describes it;** what the page shows instead is the room's face and the body's bill. The bruises, the cold, the fever, the lost hours are the device, not the scenery.

**1h.2 · A hiss needs no snake in the frame.** At least one unattended body-sound in every chapter where the danger is nearest — a hiss, a dry rustle, the floor taking a weight that has no feet. Nobody in the room hears it but the reader. **Never underlined.**

**1h.3 · The been compels; it does not accompany.** Where the flute is on the page it is allowed to be **music** only when no naag is in the scene. If she is in it, the been is the franchise's oldest switch and her body answers it against her will — and canon has already put the Mahasapera in this season's hands (E07+). Until then the compulsion is prepared, not played.

**1h.4 · Rank is said aloud, in a chain.** The franchise's own ritual: a naagin listing her titles before a kill. It may be spoken only by a mouth with the right to say it — a sapera, a keeper, a villain naming what he is hunting. **Never by Purvi:** she does not know what she is, and the day she says it will be a scene the whole season has been saving.

**1h.5 · The dead record the killer.** The franchise's founding rule — the killer's face traced in a dead naag's eye. Used **once**, on the right death, and it changes what the revenge means. Which death it hangs on is the author's (`S-14`).

**1h.6 · A face is not proof of a person.** The enemy in this world wears somebody. Canon already has the worst version of it (`R17`, `O016`), so the reader must be taught, in a small way and early, that a face can be stolen — before the night it is.

**1h.7 · The vow is sworn aloud, and the room hears it.** Revenge in this world is a contract spoken in Hindi at full voice over a body, a photograph or a shrine. Purvi has not sworn hers yet. When she does, it costs her something to say, and from that line the season is honour-bound.

**1h.8 · The hold before the strike.** Slow motion, wind, hair and *pallu*, backlight, the room going quiet, the score rising — and then the strike. Reserved for her first kill; never spent on anything smaller.

**1h.9 · She is inside a count, not an event.** The franchise runs the change in four phases and gives the last one an hour — the final hour of her twenty-fourth birthday (`FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §9). A chapter that stages the body must know **which phase she is in**, show that phase and no other, and let somebody in the room name it (*pehla charan*, *chautha charan*). **The word "transformed" is not a scene.** Without the count, the pain is only suffering; with it, the pain is the meter running.

**1h.10 · Somebody says the price.** The franchise's oldest law is that a naagin **pays for love with her power** — four seasons, the same bill: the pearl for her *sindoor*, the office for a husband, a name for safety, a memory for a life (`§5`). The kul's objection (`O030`) is an attitude until a mouth says the number. **Which of the three she pays — the pearl, the body, the name — is the author's (`S-16`, `S-17`); that the price is spoken in Episode 4 and not in the last chapter is not.**

**1h.11 · The god is an agent, and nothing decisive is luck.** Shiva curses a clan and fixes its redemption, chooses a vessel, summons the pearl out of a villain's hands mid-ritual, judges a dance (`§11`). Any large turn must be traceable to a **decision** — the god's, the kul's, the pearl's — never to coincidence, which the workshop has already ruled is not a cause. And the divine is **never explained and never mocked**: a lamp that leans, a bell with nobody near it.

**1h.12 · Every chapter is scored, and the cue is named.** The show is never unscored and never plays two registers at once (`SOUND_AND_SCREEN.md` §1–§2). A chapter names **one cue**: which register — the question, the love theme, the power, the sting, and **this season's own two identities** (his `Ek Taar`, theirs `Woh Do Log`: `SOUND_AND_SCREEN.md` §9) — which instrument, at which beat, and what it does to the scene. **§9.4 also binds the score's loyalty: no soft cue of any register may attach to Aryaman and Purvi, and the male lead is never given a melody.** *"Music plays"* is not a cue. And from Chapter 10 the chapter's **frame** belongs to it: the four-image theme at the head and the **precap** at the foot, promising a body event and not a plot event (`TITLES_SEQUENCE.md`).

**1h.13 · The picture between them is a situation, not a conversation.** The franchise builds its couples out of twelve repeated physical situations — rain, the catch, the caught *pallu*, the wrist in a crowd, the song, the festival, the one lamp, the shared thing, the photograph, the secret rescue, the wrong face, the doorway (`SITUATIONS.md`). **One a chapter, named in the footer, never two in a scene**, and always inside the four laws: no verdict on her face from the world (`O033`), the romance never imposed (`O017`), he is never there to do a job (`L-23`), and he is human for almost everyone (`O029`).

**The ban list that comes with it.** No rank inflation, no resurrection without a price, no power granted to solve a scene, no borrowed face the reader can see through, nothing ironic about faith or the kul, and no hand on the pearl that canon has not named (`FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §5).

**The gate.** `tools/franchise_check.py` prints the matrix and the body/omen totals. It is descriptive, not a gate — but from Chapter 10 on, **a chapter with no body device at all is a chapter in the wrong genre**, and the footer must say which device of `FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §7 it carries.

## 2 · What does not change

- **The camera law.** Present tense. **Comparison in narration is allowed (`O037`, 2026-11-12 — the ban was cut entirely on the author's word: an Indian soap runs on similes).** Short sentences. Narration in English. **A named feeling is no longer restricted in any way:** the author deleted that rule on 2026-11-12 (`O035`) and no file may restore it. Feeling is written the way a daily soap writes it — she is frightened, he is furious, the room gasps, the tears come in front of everybody.
- **Every canon gate.** `RULES.md` 1–25, `tools/oc_check.py` OC1–OC8, `tools/camera_check.py`. Adding Hindi, score and omens does not license a single invented canon fact, a canon line moved, or a canon scene taken.
- **Evidence.** Every line still resolves to a witness: `C` for a caption (now also the source of the Hindi wording), `TR`/`TB` for a recap, `V` for a frame, `A` for authored. The footer names which is which.
- **The OC's limits.** Nothing in this file gives Vihaan a power, a knowledge or a scene he has not earned (`foundation/OC_STATUS.md`).

## 3 · The test, in one line

**Shot as written, would this scene hold a viewer through a commercial break — and would this line be said out loud in Hindi by that actor?**

If the answer is no, the scene is not in this show's style, however correct it is.
