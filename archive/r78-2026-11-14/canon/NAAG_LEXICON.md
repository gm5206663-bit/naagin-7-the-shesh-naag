# THE NAAG LEXICON — the franchise's own working vocabulary

**Owns: the words.** Every term this show uses for its own machinery, what each one actually means in the franchise's mouth, who has the right to say it, and whether this book may use it. A companion to `canon/FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` (the devices) and `CANON.md` (Season 7's witnesses). Tag **F** = franchise ground, **C** = Season 7's own use, **A** = authored here.

**Why a lexicon and not another essay.** The chapters speak Hindi taken from the caption track, which is correct — but the caption track is a *transcript*, not the show's vocabulary. The show has a technical language of its own, and nine chapters use perhaps a fifth of it: *naagin, kechuli, naagmani, tapasya, ichchhadhari, kul, Naagrani*. The words below are the rest, and a scene that reaches for them sounds like the show without a single canon fact being invented. **The rule: a word from this list is free; a fact about this world still needs a witness.**

---

## 1 · The being, and its shape

| Term | What it means in the franchise's mouth | Who may say it | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **naag / naagin** | a male / female serpent, and specifically one that can take a human body | everybody, constantly | **free** |
| **ichchhadhari** | "form at will" — the shifter, by Shiva's boon. Not a species name and not a title: a *capacity*, and the show says it as one word, never explained | elders, saperas, the knowing enemy | **free**; Purvi would not use it of herself before she knows |
| **roop** | form, and *roop badalna* — to change form. The franchise's own infinitive for the thing the whole show is named after | everybody | **free**; the fans' own complaint is that the later seasons stopped doing it (`FRANCHISE_STYLE.md` §5) |
| **kechuli / khaal** | the shed skin. **The first kechuli** is the franchise's marker of a naagin's first change; a kechuli found in a room is proof of identity, and the show has used it as evidence more than once | naagins, saperas, hunters | **free** — and already in the book (`Chapter 6`, `Chapter 7`) |
| **ansh** | a part, a portion of the divine — *Sheshnaag ka ansh*, *Shiv ka ansh*. How the show says "descended from" without saying "related to" | elders, priests, villains | **free** |
| **vardaan / shraap** | boon / curse, and both are **legal instruments** in this world: a curse has terms and a way out, a boon has a price | gods, elders, the cursed | **free** — the Anantkul curse is **C** |
| **vish / zeher** | venom, and the show uses the same word for malice. A naagin's venom is *hers*, not a weapon she picks up | everybody | **free**; the word is absent from nine chapters and should not be |
| **naagmani** | the pearl: crystallised venom and held rain, the franchise's treasure. **Its owner is Mahadev**; a human who holds it is holding stolen property, and the show says so | everybody | **C** rules on touching apply (`canon/FRANCHISE.md`) |
| **naagpash** | the serpent noose — a binding a naagin can put on another being. Corporal, not symbolic | naagins | **available**, unused here |
| **tapasya / taap** | penance; the hundred years that earn the boon, and the *contest* the last act of a season is decided by. Two rivals sit and burn for the same god's favour | elders, gods, rivals | **free**; the trial, not the hundred years, is what this book could ever use |
| **tandav** | Shiva's dance, and in the franchise's endgame grammar the last contest between two claimants, judged by the deity in person | gods, elders | **C** (S7's finale) |
| **darshan** | the sight of the divine or the true form. The show treats a darshan as a *scene*, not a description | everybody | **free** — the single most useful word in this list for how a reveal is staged |

## 2 · The ranks, and the chain

The franchise ranks its naagins like a court, and every season outranks the last. The chain as the show actually says it:

> *Shesh Naagin → Sarvashresth Shesh Naagin → Maha Shesh Naagin → Naagrani → Mahanaagrani → Aadinaagin*

| Term | In the franchise's mouth | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| **Shesh Naagin** | the inherited office, the one the succession passes to; every season's heroine ends up here | **C** — Purvi's, per Episode 1 |
| **Naagrani / Mahanaagrani** | queen of Naaglok / the great queen; **C** gives Purvi both | **C** |
| **Sarvashresth** | "greatest of all" — a superlative used as a rank, and the show's favourite word | **free** as a word; **no new rung** may be invented (`OPEN-15`) |
| **the chain of titles** | *Main naaginon ke naagin, shreshth, mahashreshth, sarvashreshth…* — the ritual self-declaration before a kill | **free to a mouth with the right to say it; never Purvi's** (`STYLE_CANON.md` §1h.4) |
| **the roll-call** | the temple scene where an elder names the line aloud — *Shivanya, Shivangi, Bela, Brinda, Bani, Pratha, Pragati…* — and says whose turn is next. **Season 7 Episode 1 does exactly this**, and it is why `Chapter 3`'s six naagins are the most franchise-true scene in the book | **C**, and it should be *named*, not implied |
| **Shiv vardani** | "blessed of Shiva" — the epithet, used before a name in a challenge | **C** (Ep 1, of Purvi) |

## 4 · The enemies, and the counterfeits

| Term | What it is | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| **Garud** | the eagle, the serpent's ancient enemy — and the myth in `Chapter 7` (Kadru, Vinasha, the massacre) is the franchise's own origin story for the feud | **C** |
| **cheel / nevla / mahishmati** | rival shifters: an eagle clan, a mongoose, bees. The franchise's villains are usually **shifters too**, and that is where its horror comes from | **available**; nothing invented |
| **sapola** | an **artificial** shifter — Season 6's invention, and proof that "the enemy makes counterfeit serpents" is already franchise grammar | **available**, and it fits this season's science plot better than anything else in the list |
| **the countermeasures** | the show's own anti-naagin toolkit: **a potion that weakens**, **a net**, **a flute**, **a cursed blade**. Not a gun | **available** |
| **memory as an object** | the franchise takes memories: a naagin wipes a man's memory to protect herself; a woman forgets her own life and is renamed. Forgetting is a *physical event* here, not a metaphor | **C-adjacent**, and it is exactly what `O029` says about Vihaan |

## 5 · The bargain — the franchise's oldest law, and the one this book needs most

Four seasons say the same thing in different words: **a naagin pays for love with her power.**

- Pratha gives up the Naagmani, and later her powers, for her husband — twice, and it is the hinge of Season 6.
- Season 7 asks the same price in public: the pearl *for her sindoor*.
- And the franchise's bittersweet endings always settle the account: someone's power, someone's memory, someone's life.

**A · This is structural, not decorative.** In this world love is not a reward for virtue; it is a **transaction with the kul, the pearl or the body as currency**, and the show is honest that the naagin chooses it anyway. That is the engine the author's `O030` (the kul opposes her love) has been asking for, in the franchise's own currency.

**B · How it lands on this book, without deciding anything for him.** Purvi's arc already has the three sides of the bargain in place: the kul (`O030`), the pearl (the season's object), and the body (`S-16`, the compelled change). What has not been decided — and is his — is **which of the three she pays with**, and whether she pays it knowingly. Until he rules, the page can build the *pressure* of the bargain without fixing the price: a title offered and refused, a ritual that asks for her *sindoor*, an elder who says what the last four naagins gave up.

**C · The line this produces, in the franchise's grammar** — not canon, a specimen of register, for the author to accept or bin:

> *"Har naagin ne is kul ko kuch diya hai. Kisi ne apna zeher. Kisi ne apna roop. Kisi ne apna naam. Tum kya dogi, Bharani?"*

## 6 · How to use this file

1. **Words are free; facts are not.** Using *ansh*, *darshan*, *naagpash*, *sapola* invents nothing. Using one of them to state a new rule of this world needs a witness and a row in `CANON.md`.
2. **A term belongs to a mouth.** *Ichchhadhari* in Uttara's mouth is authority; in Purvi's, before she knows, it is a continuity error the author will catch.
3. **The register test.** If a line could be said by a woman in a saree in a Marathi kitchen drama with the snake words removed, it is not a naagin line — it is a nice line. The franchise's speech is **public, declarative and mythological**; it names the clan, the god, the curse and the price.
4. **Nothing here replaces the caption track.** Lines that canon speaks stay canon's words (`STYLE_CANON.md` §1a). This file is for the words canon's *world* speaks when canon is not talking.
