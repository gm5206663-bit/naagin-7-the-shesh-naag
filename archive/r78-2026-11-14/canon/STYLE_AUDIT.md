# STYLE AUDIT — every scene of every chapter, judged against the show

**Owns: the scene-by-scene verdict on whether the chapters are staged the way this show stages, and what is still owed.** It is not a canon proof and not the author's acceptance. Read with `WRITING.md` §1a (the heightened grammar) and `CANON.md` R26 (the first-meeting template).

**The question asked of each scene:** shot as written, would it hold an audience through a commercial break? Where the answer is no, the reason and the repair are named.

**Rebuilt 2026-11-11 (`O027`, `O028`).** The old verdicts in this file asked one question — would it hold through a break? — and passed scenes that were correct and small. The new question has four parts: does it sound like the show (Hindi, the score, the sound), is the omen in the frame, is the feeling at the show's scale, and are the two leads played the way this show plays them? All seven chapters were re-done against it in one day. **REBUILT** marks a scene re-done to that standard. **All seven chapters are rebuilt; there is no scene left carrying an old verdict.**

**Verdicts:** **STAGED** — the show's own kind of scene, with its beat, its object and its push-in. **THIN** — canon's material played at less than its length. **REPAIRED** — was thin, rewritten. **HELD** — deliberately short because canon gives one beat and no more.

**The correction, and why it was needed.** The first draft of this chapter staged the dragon's speech as a voice coming up a stairwell shaft to Purvi and Mayuri, and put the twenty-four-year plan behind the study door of the Suri house. Both were inventions in the wrong direction: the words are the track's, but the room is canon's, and canon's room is the basement — the recap says so plainly (*Parmeet takes his friends to the basement to reveal the dragon*), and the captions' own timecodes put the plan in the same gathering. The staging gave Purvi an hour of knowledge canon does not give her. It is withdrawn: the basement is Parmeet and four men, nobody else, and Purvi leaves that night with a photograph, a mark and the word *basement*.

**What this chapter had to get right, and did.** The talk. Episode 3's second part is fourteen minutes of conversation with one basement in it, so the chapter lives or dies on whether people sound like themselves: Ananta's *relax hai na* doing all of the consoling, Ravish's *I can't say no* about his brother's order, Aryaman's flat *good night* and then the corridor, and four men being told a plan at its own length with nothing hurried.

## What this audit says about the workshop

1. **Chapters 1 to 5 are rebuilt and the canon style is now on the page in all five.** Chapters 6 and 7 carry verdicts written under the old question and are next in the queue.

### What this pass proved, in one paragraph

Chapters 10 to 12 were not thin on devices — by the last count they carry more of them than Chapters 1 to 9 do. They were thin in **one place only, and it is the place the author has named four times**: the male lead was present, correct, and still. Three scenes were repaired against that and nothing else — the study door, the lawn, and the jacket — and in all three he does something: inspects her wrist and lies for her, catches her and keeps walking, refuses his father and keeps the cloth. **The measurement that matters is not the device count. It is whether anything in the chapter would stop if he were not in it.**
