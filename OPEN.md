**OPEN-18 · Which antagonist holds the full truth?**

Raised by `O031` (2026-11-11), and **answered the other way by the author the same day (`O032`): nobody in that house knows the full truth of what Vihaan is. Parmeet does not know. Almost no one does.** Ahana's Chapter 21 exception is only the name *Anantveer* and the duty to search; she does not know the person, face, location or Vihaan identity.

So the workshop's first answer — that Parmeet knew, and was trapped by it — is withdrawn. What stands:

- **The house is full of help it cannot see.** Baljeet, the four men, Karan and Arjun, Pinky, the whole wedding: every one of them pushes the two of them into the same room for their own reasons, and not one of them knows what the push is worth.
- **Parmeet is dangerous for a different reason, and a worse one.** He does not know what the boy is, and he is still building a plan that ends in 2025 with a dragon in a cellar that hates naag and naagin. A man who is right by accident is more frightening than a man who is right on purpose, and he cannot be talked out of it, because he has no idea what he has.
- **The knowing enemy, if there is one, is not in that house and not yet on the page.** `O031` still holds as a law of the drama — an enemy who does not know the full truth cannot be depended on to work against the pair — so whoever does know arrives from outside: the world the dragon came from, the leadership that sent four men across a border, or the naag world's own. **This is open, and it is the biggest open question in the season after the identity itself.**
- **Asked and answered here, because the author left it to the workshop (2026-11-11): *does anyone in the naag world know Anantveer walks?* — No. Not one.** This is a **Working** answer, not a ruling, and it is open to him. What the naag world has is the old litany and a promise in it: the Nagaraj of the Anant kul comes, and a queen comes with him (`R21`, `R28`). **A name with no face, a date with no year, and no kul on earth that can walk up to a man and claim him.** The wider naag world is not looking for a person; it watches for a **sign**, and the sign is the awakening (`O030`, point 3), which nobody has missed because it has not happened. **Amended narrowly by O059 (2026-11-14):** after Chapter 21, Ahana is the personal exception: Bharani names her partner as Anantveer and tells her to find him. She learns the name and her duty, not his face, location or identity as Vihaan; this authorial rule does not mean the naag world knows he walks. A naagin may still stand beside him at a wedding with her shoulder touching his and go home without knowing, and the wider kul can put his back to a wall and its law to his face without guessing what it is doing.
- **What knows him anyway, without a face.** Exactly two things in this world, and neither of them knows anything *about* him. (i) **The Naagmani** — the author's own words, it knows him "too much very well" (`O022`); it does not wait for an awakening and it will not need to be told. (ii) **A nagin's body in his presence**, which does things it does not do for a human — and that is not knowledge. It is weather noticing another weather.
- **So the enemy who knows (`O031`) is human, and that is the irony the season is built on.** The naag world is waiting on a sign and cannot read the man standing next to it; the only people on earth who know a Sheshnaag's line survived 2001 are the men who were paid to end it — the ones who kept a boy they could not find, the ones who put a snake-killer in a basement and called it an experiment and a "silver enemy" (`canon/E03_BEATS.md`, B26–B28). They do not know the name. They know **the arithmetic**: something was born that night, it was not in the wreckage, and it is twenty-four years old now. That is enough to work against a pair, and it is exactly half of the truth, which is the most dangerous amount of the truth to hold.
- **What it forbids on the page:** any scene in which Parmeet behaves like a man guarding a secret about Vihaan; any line of his that says what the boy is; and any hint that the house's neglect has a purpose. The neglect is real, and it is ordinary, and that is the point — the king of the naags carried the chairs because nobody there knows any better.

**OPEN-17 · Does Airul learn that the protector walks in 2025?**
Asked, and the author left the answer to this workshop (**"you make yourself", 2026-11-11**).

**DECIDED — Airul never learns who he is. He learns only that the pearl answers him, and he reads it wrong.**

The reasoning, so that it can be overruled on sight rather than argued with:
- **Canon gives Airul the character of a man who misreads naag things.** He is fooled by a lookalike diamond, has to test the pearl on Poornima to learn it is false, and holds a naagin's whole world at arm's length from a laboratory and a tiger (C, Ep 14 onwards). A man who cannot tell a pearl from a stone is not the man who recognises a Sheshnaag. He is the man who sees a door and calls it a doorkeeper.
- **So his conclusion is wrong and useful:** that Vihaan Suri is a *marked servant* of the pearl — a chowkidaar born to it, a human who may carry it, on the pattern the franchise itself began with (F, S1: a marked man as the pearl's protector). Not a Sheshnaag; not a rebirth; nothing of the old world.
- **What that changes is his tactics, not his goal.** He still wants India's ruin and the pearl. But a door you own is worth more than a door you burn, so he wants **Vihaan's hand, alive**. Vihaan becomes the thing the villains want to *use*, which is a humiliation and a danger rather than a promotion, and Purvi's revenge stays the spine of the season (`RULES.md` gate 17).
- **It also explains a hole canon leaves.** Canon needs Vihaan away from the Arora house on the Episode 7 night (`O003`). A villain who values him alive is a cause the reader can already have seen.
- **The mystery is kept by who may speak.** No one in 2025 says "Sheshveer" except a naag of the old world — Uttara, or Bharani — and even they only half-know, so the word, if it ever comes, arrives without an explanation attached. Only the pearl behaves as if it knows everything, which is exactly the leak the author allowed (`O022`).
- **The reader stays ahead of everyone.** The reader is shown five hoods once, in the dark, and no character counts them. Airul's wrong theory is the point: the audience can watch the most dangerous man in the season make the wrong guess about the boy he has decided to use.
