The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Book and the White Car

At the gate in the morning sun the children are in the second car with their shoes in their hands, and the first car has already gone, and Shyam is holding the book flat against the wall.

"Chhote sahab."

Shyam turns the book round.

"Char gaadiyan andar aayi thi aaj. Do abhi tak khadi hain."

Vihaan takes the pen out of the book's spine and looks down the lane.

The white car is third in the line with the light still on its roof, and the fourth has a grille across the back window.

The guard's table has a tin on it with the lid off and the tea in it gone cold.

"Number likh liya?"

"Likh liya."

Shyam puts his thumb under the line.

"Aur teen naam bhi, raat ke do baje wale. Woh teen aadmi jo aapke papa se milne aaye the."

Vihaan writes the white car's number on the day's page himself, and the time under it, and the number of men he can count inside it, and then he gives the book back.

The lane beyond the gate is full of the flower man's tempo and the marigold in it is going over.

"Yeh apne paas rakho. Kisi ko mat dena."

"Ji."

"Gate band mat karna, Shyam."

He opens the door of the second car.

"Jo darwaza band milta hai, woh todne layak ho jaata hai. Khula chhod do."

"Ji, chhote sahab."

Vihaan puts the folder on the seat and gets in, and in the back the smallest child has a shoe on the wrong foot and has begun to cry about it.

"Arre."

He turns round with his elbow on the seat.

"Woh left wala hai. Dekh."

"Pata nahi hai."

"Main pehna doon?"

He reaches back over the seat, and the child puts the foot up on the armrest, and he gets the shoe on and pulls the strap and lets the child push his fingers away.

"Ho gaya."

"Chhote bhaiya."

The smallest child looks up at him.

"Haan?" he asks.

"Woh gaadi hamare peeche kyun aa rahi hai?"

"Garmi hai."

He rolls the window down for the child.

"Woh kanpatti pe khol deti hai."

The car goes out into the lane and turns left at the flower man's tempo, and the gate of the house stands open behind it the way he left it.

## Eleven O'Clock

The hotel's fourth floor at ten past twelve, with one bulb every ten feet down the ceiling, and the lift opening on a corridor with a carpet that takes the sound of his shoes.

Verma is standing outside the boardroom with his tie straight and his phone in both hands, and he has been standing there a while.

"Sir."

"Kitne log?"

"Chaar the. Barah baje se pehle nikal gaye."

Verma hands him the folder he is carrying, and it is the folder Vihaan gave him at nine.

"Sir, unhone kaha—"

"Woh kya baith kar gaye the?" Vihaan opens the door.

The boardroom is empty.

Four cups on the table, two of them with the tea still in them.

A screen on the wall showing a map of the sangam with the pontoon bridges marked in blue, and the cursor left in the middle of a bridge.

A chair turned out from the table, and a pen lying beside it with the cap off.

"Mr Menon andar hain. Woh do minute ruke."

Verma keeps his voice down.

"Baaki teen nahin."

Menon is at the window with the jacket folded on the sill beside him. He turns round and he does not put out his hand.

"Mr Suri. Aap ke office pe kal raat raid hui hai."

"Kal raat do baje. Aur aaj subah gyarah baje ek warrant." Vihaan puts the folder on the table.

"Aapko yeh subah aath baje batana chahiye tha. Verma ka phone aapke paas bees baar gaya."

"Hum compliance pe hain, Mr Suri."

Menon looks at the folder and does not touch it.

"Do sau kilo. Ek company jiske office se do sau kilo nikalta hai, woh humare group ke saath sign nahin karti. Yeh mera faisla nahin hai."

"Kis ka faisla hai?"

"Mr Kessler ka."

Menon picks his jacket up.

"Woh aaj shaam London mein baith rahe hain."

Vihaan opens the folder and turns it round on the table, and it is not the contract in it, it is a single printed page.

"Screens tumhare. Feed mera. Poore Mahamahakumbh ka, pandrah din, aur kisi ke paas yeh licence nahin hai."

His hand is flat on the page.

"Jo group Bombay mein aapse baat kar raha hai, uska licence nahin hai. Woh aapko camera aur cable bech sakta hai. Sangam pe khada nahin kar sakta."

Menon reads the page.

He reads it the way a man reads a thing he has already been told.

"Yeh Mr Kessler ko dikhana padega."

"Toh dikhaiye."

"Do hafte,"

Menon says.

"Aur daam us waqt woh hoga jo Mr Kessler kahega."

"Do hafte." Vihaan closes the folder.

"Aur aaj raat tak mera office aur mera naam akhbaar mein hoga. Woh aapke compliance ko batayiye, Menon saab. Yeh bhi."

Menon goes. The door shuts on the map and the four cups and the chair turned out from the table.

Verma stands by the door with his hands together.

"Sir, woh contract—"

"Gaya." Vihaan picks the folder up.

"Aur aaj shaam main Mr Kessler se khud baat karunga. Abhi mujhe gaadi chahiye."

"Sir, aapne kuch nahin khaya subah se."

"Gaadi, Verma."

The lift goes down.

In the lobby four men in lanyards are putting up a standee for a property conference, and the standee has a photograph of the sangam on it, and the man in the photograph is standing at the rail of a pontoon bridge with his hands behind his back.

Vihaan goes past it and out through the glass into the heat, and the folder is under his arm and it is the only thing he is carrying.

## Kacche Khiladi

Parmeet Suri comes down the two steps of the fire into the hall and puts his hand on his samdhi's shoulder, and he is still smiling.

"Humari chaal ko samajh hi nahi paaye — **samdhi ji, aap toh bade kacche khiladi nikle.**"

Gurinder does not move under the hand.

A tray stands at the edge of the hall with the sweet boxes on it still tied.

"Jaan-boojh ke yahan se bheja hai humne bachchiyon ko."

Parmeet looks round at the empty chairs and the women at the back and the cars at the gate that have not gone.

"Aur aapko heart attack dilaayein — taaki hum aaram se aapko saparivaar kahaniyan sunayein."

"Papa."

Ananta's voice is small.

"Papa, yeh sab kya hai?"

"Aur ye pandit ji."

Parmeet turns round.

"Pandit ji, aap kahan chale gaye the?"

"Bachao."

Gurinder's voice comes out of him all at once, and it is the first loud sound he has made all morning.

"Mantra padhkar mujhe bachao! Bachao! Pandit ji, bachao!"

The man with the books under his arm stands up from behind the fire and puts them down.

The pandit's book is under his arm and the strap on it has broken and been knotted.

"**Ye pandit asli nahi hai.**"

Parmeet does not raise his voice at all.

"Ye humare hi aadmi hai."

The pandit steps back into the shadow of the passage and stays there.

The fire at the side of the hall has gone down to its ash.

"Ye shaadi-waadi. Sindoor. Mangalsutra. Bhagwan ko saakshi maankar ye aag."

Parmeet looks at the fire for a moment, and it is not a fire to him.

"Ab mujhe iski kadar bhi nahi hai — **ye humare yahan kuch nahi hota.**"

"What a relief, Dad."

Ravish is standing with his hands on his own head and he is laughing.

"Aapne ise pehle kyun nahi bataya? Finally. Nahin, main honestly thak gaya tha."

"Beta."

Parmeet sits down on the chair by the fire, and he takes his time about it.

"**Isse bada raaj abhi khulna baaki hai.**"

"Kaun sa raaj, Dad?"

"**Tumhari biwi ka.**"

The room goes still in a different way. Ravish turns round, and Karan turns round, and the women at the back of the hall are looking at the bride.

## Kaun Ho Tum

The hall at half past twelve, with the chandelier on and the fire lit at the side of it.

"Kyon bahu rani."

Parmeet does not get up.

"**Ya phir main bataun — tum bataogi ki kaun ho tum,**" the score sounding twice over the asking (m08:55 x2 as one).

Ananta's hands are folded in front of her sari and her face has gone the colour of the wall with fear.

"Ki hum aatanki hain — maana ki humne raaj chhupaya."

Parmeet lifts one finger.

"Lekin **tumne bhi toh baat chhupai na — ichchhadhari naagin ho.**"

The word goes round the hall and nobody picks it up.

"What?"

Ravish stops laughing.

"Naagin?"

"Haan."

Parmeet nods at Ravish, pleased with him.

"**Ye woh naagin hai jiske paas naagmani hai** — aur aisi-waisi koi naagin nahi hai ye," the score sounding three times over the telling (m09:17 x3 as one).

"Dad—"

"Vishnu ka vardaan. Shiv ka aashirvaad."

He is reciting now, and he is good at it.

"Koi kehta hai Kailash ke parvat ki gufaaon ke andar hai. Koi kehta hai Neelgiri ke pahaadon ke neeche hai. Koi kehta hai dakshin ke mandiron mein chhupi hai. Ek naagmani, jiski kahaniyan poore duniya mein phaili hui hain."

He looks round the room.

"Lekin ek baat sabko pata hai — kisi ko nahi pata."

His hand comes down flat on the arm of the chair.

"**Ek Shesh naagin uski raksha karti hai.**"

"Papa."

Ananta's voice is not working properly.

"Papa, yeh kya—"

"**Ananta — aur Shesh mein Shesh hai.**"

The hall makes a sound. Purvi's hand is on her sister's arm and her sister is not looking at her.

"Dad."

Aryaman is up out of his chair.

"Dad, yeh nahi ho sakta. Dad, suniye—"

Parmeet lifts two fingers without turning his head.

Two of the men come round the fire and take Aryaman by the arms, and Aryaman is not a small man and he does not go quietly, and one of them puts a cloth over his mouth and nose and holds it there, and the other one holds him up while it works.

"Papa!"

Ananta's voice.

"Papa, usko—"

"Isko andar le jao."

Parmeet does not look at his adopted son.

"Isko samajhne mein waqt lagega. Aaj raat nahin."

They carry Aryaman out through the passage with his shoes dragging on the marble, and they put him on the bed in the room at the top of the stairs, and one of them stays at the door.

## The Bargain

The hall at a quarter to one, with the chandelier on and the chairs by the wall still filled.

"Bahu rani."

Parmeet leans forward with his elbows on his knees, and he is reasonable, and that is the worst of it.

"**Tu naagmani de degi na?**"

Ananta does not answer.

The chair Parmeet sits in has been pulled round to face the wall and the floor under it has a ring on it.

"Kuch nahi karunga — main tere baap ko chhod dunga."

He holds a hand up.

"Zinda chhod dunga — haan, maa-baap ko kuch nahi karunga."

"Papa—"

"Desh ki badnami hogi. Tourist nahi aayega. Humara kaam ho jayega, beta. Bas **kal Kumbh mein dhamaka hoga**."

He sits back.

"Aur is desh ka beda garak kar jayenge — hum apne ghar chale jayenge. Apne aish-o-aaram ki zindagi mein wapas — aur naagmani le kar."

"Papa, papa, kyun?"

Ananta's hands come down at her sides.

"**Par na hi main naagin hoon, aur na hi mere paas naagmani hai** — mujhe nahi pata aap kya-kya bakwaas kar rahe hain. Maine theek kaha na?"

"Beta."

Parmeet closes his eyes for a moment, the way a man does when a clerk has got a column wrong.

"Main phir se samjhata hoon — tu samjhi nahi."

Parmeet stands up.

A glass stands on the floor by his foot with a spoon in it.

"**Mujhe naagmani nahi di tumne agar—**"

Parmeet does not finish it. He nods at the man standing behind Gurinder Arora, and that man takes the cream kurta by the collar and puts him on the floor.

"Papa!"

Purvi is through the chairs before anybody catches her and a hand takes her by the waist and holds her where she stands.

"Papa!"

The sound the floor makes is not loud, the score sounding three times over the beating (m10:53+m11:06+m11:16 as one).

The sound after it is.

## Ruk Jao, Pradeep

The hall at one in the afternoon, with the chandelier on and the floor in front of the fire empty.

"**Ruk jao, Pradeep.**"

The man on the floor has stopped moving.

A bottle stands on its side by the wall with a cloth under it.

The pandit's book lies open on the floor beside him with its pages down.

"**Is par apni energy waste mat karo.**"

Parmeet comes round the fire and stands looking down at him, and he is not angry.

"Lekin tere marne par na kisi ko afsos hoga — mujhe toh tujhe pehle hi maar dena chahiye tha," the score sounding twice over the stopping (m11:54 x2 as one).

Gurinder's spectacles are on the floor beside his hand. Nobody picks them up.

"Yahin baith isliye, aur apni saans le."

Parmeet crouches down to be on his level.

"**Tab tera number aayega — jab hum sabko maar denge na.**"

He straightens up.

"Tere marne par maza nahi aayega humein."

He turns his back and goes to the chair.

"**Maza aayega tere dard ko dekh kar.**"

## What a Father Says

They leave him on the floor. They put the bride and her sister on the two chairs by the wall, with a man standing at the side of each of them. The hall's light is on the two of them and on nobody else, and the hall fills up again with the ones who did it, and the fire keeps burning because nobody has put it out.

"Ananta."

Gurinder's voice comes off the floor.

"Meri baat dhyan se suno, beta."

Ananta's chin comes up.

The man at her chair does not stop her looking down.

The tray the men came in with stands on the floor with its glasses still full.

The two chairs by the wall have been pulled out of line by the men standing at them.

"**Purvi hai naagin. Tum nahi.**"

Purvi's head comes round.

"Ek khaas maksad se hua hai Purvi ka janm," the score sounding under the purpose (m11:54).

Gurinder's hand finds his spectacles and does not put them on.

"**Humne uska naam tujhe de diya tha use bachane ke liye,**" the score sounding under the naming (m12:43).

"Papa, aap kya keh rahe hain?"

Purvi's voice comes out too loud.

"Papa, yeh kya—"

"Mere paas waqt bahut kam hai, beta." He says it to Ananta, and only to Ananta, with grief.

"**Tu Purvi ko bacha le — kuch bhi ho jaye.**"

"Papa, please—"

"Ananta."

He holds his daughter's eyes for as long as the man at the chair allows it.

"Suno. Kuch bhi ho jaye."

## Drama Band Karo

The hall at two in the afternoon, with the chandelier on and nobody left in it who is not family or staff.

Vihaan drove the children out that morning, on Parmeet's order, and has not come back.

"Wahan kya khush-bus chal rahi hai?"

Parmeet's voice comes across the hall from the chair by the fire.

"Humein bhi batao — kya baatein chal rahi hain?"

The men at the chairs step back half a pace.

The sari on the floor has one end of it under the chair.

"**Drama band karo.**"

He waves a hand at the room.

"Aur hum hain dramebaaz — humare yahan drama bahut hote hain."

Parmeet looks at the bride.

Her sari's pallu has come off her shoulder and nobody has put it back.

"Lekin **tum ho naagin**."

Ananta stands up, the score sounding under the standing (m13:23).

Her sari is still on the floor and she steps out of it, and she comes forward on the bare floor with her hands loose at her sides and her chin up, and she stops in front of the fire.

"**Haan. Hoon main naagin,**" one low string under the claiming.

Purvi says her sister's name. The hall does not hear it.

"**Aur hai mere paas mani.**"

Ananta's voice is steady and it carries to the back of the room.

"**Meri family ko jaane do.**" Ananta lifts her chin.

"**Kisi ko bhi chhua agar,**" Her voice does not shake.

"**to mani nahi dungi main,**" the score sounding under the refusing (m13:31).

Parmeet Suri looks at her for a long moment, and then he gets up out of the chair, and the hall watches him do it, and he comes across the floor and stops in front of her with his hands behind his back.

"Ye sun liya sab ne?" he says, pleasantly, to the room.

Nobody in the hall answers him.

The fire at the side of the hall is the only thing in it making a sound.
