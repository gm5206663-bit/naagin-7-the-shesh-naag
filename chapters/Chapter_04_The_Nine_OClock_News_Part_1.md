# Chapter 4 — The Nine O'Clock News (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 2 (first part).*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## Wait Here

Half past ten at night. The corridor outside the styling room. A trolley of clothes against the wall. The two of them standing at the end of it where the light is.

The trolley has a plastic cover over the clothes. The cover has been pulled back at one end. Left that way.

At the far end of the corridor a cleaner's bucket stands with a mop resting in it. The water in the bucket has stopped moving.

The night roster is pinned beside the styling-room door. Its last line is in Vihaan's hand. The time he wrote against his own name is four in the morning.

"Purvi, main Arya se baat karke aati hoon tere liye." Ananta has her bag on her shoulder and her phone in her hand. "Theek hai? Jab main boloon, toh aake usse mil lena. Tab tak tu yahan wait kar."

"Yahin?"

"Yahin." She is already going. "All right."

The score comes up under the promise and goes before the door shuts.

The corridor is empty. She waits in the join of the two lights with her bag on both arms. She has been told to wait. She waits, with patience.

* * *

## The Nine O'Clock News

One in the morning. The channel's floor is still lit.

The newsroom has been a mandap for one night and comes out of it a wreck. Marigold on the floor, trodden flat. The garland hanging off one end of the weather desk. Balloons going soft in a corner. On the big screen, the tail of the nine o'clock bulletin runs again on a loop. The words on it are in the wrong place.

"Spelling mistakes hain. Quotation marks are missing." Aryaman Suri stands in front of the screen with his hands in his pockets. His shirt is out at the back and the tie is lost on a chair. "This is embarrassing stuff. Have you not noted all this down?"

"Sir—" The young man with the tablet is holding it in both hands. "Dekhiye, kal main ye sab note karunga."

"Kal." Aryaman turns round. "This cannot happen again. That being said, the score sounding under the closing (m00:12, second of two)." He picks his jacket up off a chair. "Have a good night."

"Thank you, sir."

"Congratulation, sir."

"Thank you."

The word goes round the floor. The floor eases. Three people stand up at once. Somebody switches a monitor off. The ticker under the ceiling still goes — red letters into the dark of the glass.

One woman has not stood up. Ananta is at the makeup mirror in her yellow saree. A brush in one hand. An earring in the other. She has been watching him since he came out of his cabin.

"Sir." She puts the earring in. "Award ceremony ke liye maine aapko ek design outfit diya tha. Woh aapko kaisa laga?"

"Dekhiye, woh outfit main baad mein dekhunga, the score sounding under the deferring (m00:38, first of four)." Aryaman says it without stopping, with the score sounding under the rebuking (m00:38, second of four). "Magar ye jo material aapne mujhe diya hai, ye nau baje ki news mein screen par bleed kar raha tha. Tum hamari head stylist ho. Give me something decent."

"Ya, you don't worry about it. I will take care of it."

He is already at the glass doors.

"Sir." She is after him. "Aapko ek assistant ki zaroorat thi na? Uske liye mere, the score sounding under the interrupting (m00:38, third of four)—"

"Haan haan." His phone is ringing. "I have to take this. Hello?"

He goes through the doors. She watches them swing, with frustration.

"God, he left." She says it to the shut glass, with the score sounding under the resolving (m00:38, fourth of four). "Par baat toh mujhe inse karni hai."

Out on the floor, Vihaan comes out from between the desks with his laptop shut under his arm and his earbud in. His eyes go to the screen — the tail of the bulletin, his name under it in the credits he does not ask for. He smiles small and private at it, the smile of a man who has done his work and does not need anyone to see it. The party is still all over that room: marigold going brown on the screens, a garland down across a chair, a crate of glasses by the lift that nobody in this building is going to carry down tonight. Two men in housekeeping are doing it. He steps round them. One of them looks up at him and says *chhote sahab*. He says *ji*. He keeps walking.

The young man with the tablet catches up with him at the lift with the crate between them. "Sir, housekeeping ka overtime?"

"Crate ke saath likhwa dena." He steps into the lift. "Naam ke saath," he says, holding the doors.

The doors close. The young man writes it on the pad, the crate still balanced on his arm.

## The Register

The lobby at midnight. The one lamp over the security desk doing the whole room. Marble. One guard. The doors breathing the wet air in.

Purvi stands by the pillar with her bag on her arm and her spectacles in her hand. She holds the glasses up to the one lamp. She looks through them at nothing. She puts them back on.

The guard brings a register up from under the counter. He opens it flat. He turns it round. He puts the pen in her hand.

Something falls out of the top of her bag when she sets it down to sign. She picks it up: one petal off the garlands at the door of the hall where she has spent the evening. She puts it into the diary she keeps her notes in. She presses the cover down on it, because it scratches the page if she leaves it loose.

"Naam, madam. Aur aane ka time."

"Kis liye?"

"Chhote sahab ka hukm hai." The guard's thumb is on the column. "Is building mein raat ko aane wale ka naam likha jaata hai. Roz."

She signs. He turns the register back. He reads it upside down. He nods. He goes back to his radio.

Then the doors at the end of the lobby open. A woman comes through them with a garment bag over her shoulder in its plastic and her phone in her other hand. She is talking to somebody on it. She stops talking.

"Purvi."

"Ananta?"

"Main tujhe andar bula rahi thi." She puts the phone away. "Kya kar rahi hai yahan tu? Kab se khadi hai?"

"Woh bole the na, wait karo."

Ananta looks at the shut lift. At Shyam. At her sister's face. Something in her own face goes hard and then smooth again, with anger.

"Chal." She takes her by the elbow. "Ek minute. Mere saath chal."

Purvi lets Ananta pull her along.

* * *

## The Cabin

The corridor behind the styling room. A trolley of glasses for the morning's shoot standing against the wall under a tube light with one end of it flickering. Purvi coming round the corner of it too fast, the bag strap already halfway down her arm.

Whoever set the trolley out has left the slip of paper with the count on it under one of the trays. Six is written on it twice.

She goes into a man coming the other way. The trolley goes over between them.

"Excuse me." He has caught her elbow. "I just thought, in case agar tumhein meri koi help chahiye toh."

Six glasses of orange juice go down the front of Ananta's saree. The tray rings on the floor. The two of them stand in the middle of it with their hands out.

"Sorry. Sorry—"

"It's fine." Ananta is already peeling the wet pallu away from herself. "It's fine. Chal, andar chal."

The cabin at the end of the corridor has the light off. A chair pushed back from the desk. A box of tissues on it. A mirror. She gets the garment bag up onto the hook on the back of the door. She starts on her saree with the tissues, her back to the room.

The door opens again.

Arjun stands in it with his hands in his pockets. He comes in. He does not shut it the whole way.

He puts his arms round her from behind. After a moment her hands come up over his. They stand like that in the dark glass of the mirror.

Then his hand closes on her wrist. He turns it over. The juice is running down it.

"Ouch. Haath chhodo." She pulls. "Mera haath chhodo."

"Nahin chhoda toh?", the score sounding under the daring (m01:16).

"Toh main chilla-chilla ke poora news channel yahan bula lungi." Her voice has gone up and it is not shaking, which is worse, with fury. "Haath chhodo!"

"Chilla do."

"Main maar daalungi tumhein."

"Maar daalo." He lets the wrist go and steps back with his palm out and laughs, and the laugh is the worst part of it. "Arre, main mazaak kar raha tha." He goes to the door. "Tum chilla deti toh mera hi nuksan hota."

"Tumne apne bhai se baat ki?" She says it to his back. "Meri behen ke kaam ke baare mein?"

He smiles. He does not answer. He is out in the corridor. The door does not shut.

Ananta stands with her wet wrist held in her other hand. She looks at the door.

Outside, in the corridor, Purvi's voice, small: "Didi?"

"Ek minute." Ananta's voice comes back level. "Ek minute, Purvi."

She comes out with the garment bag over her arm and her pallu wet. She shuts the cabin door behind her with her heel.

## The Broken Lens

The channel's lobby at half past one in the morning. The marble still holding the wet of the doors. The only light left on in the whole of it is the one over the security desk.

Purvi stands by the pillar with her bag on her arm and her spectacles in her hand. She has been standing there since half past ten.

A camera body on the security desk with its lens off and its cap beside it. The guard has put his own cap on top of the cap to keep it from going anywhere.

A lift door with a wet footprint coming halfway out of it and stopping.

The boardroom on the fourth floor has had its light on all night. Three men came out of it at midnight. One of them still stands at the glass door arguing into a phone in English. The lift brings him down at half past one — jacket hooked on a shoulder, one earbud in, a laptop shut under his hand, a second phone that keeps lighting up.

"Shyam." Vihaan does not look at him. "Koi aaya?"

"Ji sahab, woh madam." The guard's head comes up. "Das baje se."

The man stops with the laptop under his arm. He looks down the lobby at her. Then at the clock over the doors. Then at her again.

"London se line pe hoon." He says it to her across the marble, and it is not an apology, it is a fact being handed over. "Ek minute."

He walks to the far end of the lobby with the phone at his ear. His voice comes back off the glass in short English sentences: the number is the number; the Kumbh window is not negotiable; if the other side wants the broadcast rights it pays for the broadcast rights, not for a favour. He does not raise his voice once. He listens for a long time. Then he says, quietly, "Then we are agreed," and takes the earbud out.

"Aap kab se khadi hain?" He is in front of her now.

"Do ghante se zyada."

"Kisne kaha tha ki rukiye?"

"Ananta ne. Meri behen ne. Woh Aryaman sir ke saath kaam karti hain." Purvi's chin is up, with defiance. "Shikayat nahin kar rahi main aapse. Chhote bhai hain aap unke. Kar bhi nahin sakte aap kuch."

"Kisi ne paani diya?"

Shyam's radio goes quiet, which is Shyam's way of answering.

Vihaan takes the bottle out of his own bag and holds it out.

"Main kisi ajnabi se—"

"Paanch rupaye ka hai." He puts it in her hand and takes nothing for it. "Shaam ko hisaab karunga."

She holds it and does not drink. He lets her not drink it.

"Aap is building mein kya karte hain?" she says.

"Main is channel ka hisaab dekhta hoon. Aur London ka office." He puts his hands in his pockets. "Aur aap?"

"Main journalist hoon." Her voice does not shake. "Do saal se."

"Kahan?"

"Kahin nahin." The word comes out flat and it is the bravest thing in that lobby. "Do saal se kahin nahin."

He looks at her for a moment longer than he needs to, with curiosity.

"Woh mera bhai hai. Jo aapko bulaane wale the." He says it without any weight at all. "Woh aaj raat kisi se nahin milte. Kal subah bhi nahin, kyunki kal subah unke paas file aayegi, aur file ke baad do meeting hain."

"Toh?"

"Toh aap aath baje aana." He shifts the laptop under his arm. "Das baje woh apne cabin mein honge aur aap bahar khadi hongi. Aath baje Manoj desk pe aata hai. Uske paas roz subah ek list hoti hai. Aap apna naam us list mein likhwa lena. Uske baad aapko bulana unki majboori hoga, unki marzi nahin."

"Aap unke khilaaf kaam kar rahe hain."

"Main yeh nahin maanta ki ek laayak aadmi ko do ghante khada rakhna unka haq hai." His voice does not go up. "Bas."

"Main kisi ki sifarish se andar nahin aaungi."

"Apna naam likhwa rahi hain aap. Kisi ka ehsaan nahin usme."

The second phone goes off in his hand. He looks at it. He does not take it.

"Naam?"

"Purvi Arora."

"Vihaan." He does not put the family name after it, and she notices that he does not. "Aur aapki gaadi nahin hai. Shyam, gaadi aage laao. Driver aapko chhod ke paanch baje laut aayega."

"Main aapki gaadi nahin lungi."

"Meri gaadi nahin hai." He is already at the doors. "Mere paas apni gaadi lene ka waqt nahin hai. Charter diya hua hai. Aap uski behen hain, woh isi building mein kaam karti hai, aur raat ke do baje main kisi ladki ko auto mein nahin bithata."

"Chaar baje." Purvi stands in the doorway. "Gaadi, aur naam list mein."

"Aath baje naam." He goes out into the rain. "Aur kisi ko yeh mat batana ki maine kaha. Main is ghar mein hisaab rakhta hoon. Faisle nahin karta."

The doors swing. The guard turns his radio up.

Purvi stands in the empty lobby. Then she drinks the water standing up, all of it. She puts the empty bottle in her bag, with trust.

Purvi looks at the doors he went out of. She looks at them for longer than there is anything to look at. If anybody asks her why, she has no answer.

At the top of the steps the car comes round the drive with its wipers going. A boy in a black uniform holds the door open. The woman who has waited three hours in a lobby gets in.

* * *

## The Brothers

The office floor at two in the morning, thinning out. Aryaman crossing it with his jacket over his arm and his phone in his hand.

Purvi sees him from across the desks. Something in her face changes. She starts towards him with her diary in her hand, with hope.

The diary slips. It goes down between the desks. She goes down after it. When she comes up with it, he is gone through the glass doors without a word.

"Didi." She finds Ananta by the lift with the diary held flat against her chest. "Tu Arya se mili kya?"

"Haan."

"Par unhone toh meri taraf dekha tak nahin."

"Koi baat nahin." Ananta puts her hand flat on her sister's head, the same flat palm from their childhood, with tenderness. "Woh teri taraf dekhenge bhi, aur tujhe job bhi denge."

"Aapko lagta hai aisa?"

"Arre, mujhe dikhta hai aisa." The score touches the line and goes. "Chal."

The lift doors stand open on that floor for a long time before anybody comes out of them.

Aryaman comes out of the lift into the corridor with the jacket by the collar in one hand. There is his brother Arjun at the far end of it — in a fresh shirt at two in the morning, with somebody on the phone.

"Bhai. Bhai!" Arjun puts the phone down on his chest. "Arre, tum apni dost ki party mein nahin gaya?"

"Come on, bhai." Aryaman does not stop walking. "Aapko chhod ke kaise ja sakta hoon main?"

"Ha."

"Two rounds."

The lift doors open. Aryaman steps in and holds them.

"Yeh jacket tujh pe achhi lagegi, the score sounding under the offering (m01:56, second of two)." Aryaman holds it up. "Le na."

They go down together. Arjun talks all the way.

"Bhai. Bhai, aapki jitni bhi assistant hain na— sab stupid hain. Sab. You should hire some hard-working assistant." He puts his hand up. "Waise mere paas ek ladki hai, the score sounding under the suggesting (m02:39, first of two)."

"Kaun si ladki?"

"Bhai, woh kya hai na—" He waves it away, with the score sounding under the admitting (m02:39, second of two). "Usko, I mean, usko job ki zaroorat hai. Maine haan kah di."

* * *

## The Sattu ke Laddu

Aryaman's room at a quarter past two. The desk lamp the only light in it. The citation folder from the award night lying on the desk where he dropped it.

Arjun is in the chair across the desk with his phone face down. A steel box of laddus stands open on the desk between them. Its tying string still round the lid.

"Oke, oke." Aryaman pushes the box across. "Achha, ye saari baatein chhod. Tune itna weight loss kyon kar liya? Kitni baar kaha hai, sattu ke laddu khaya kar. Ye le, specially tere liye mangwaye hain."

"Khuda whisky ke swimming pool mein dubki laga de, aur mujhe sattu ke laddu?" Arjun holds one up. "Bhai, this is not fair."

"Main mar bhi gaya na, toh mujhe koi farak nahin padega." Aryaman looks at the desk. "Lekin agar tujhe kuch ho gaya, toh mujhe bahut farak padega."

"Kyon nahin farak padega?" Arjun's voice changes. "Aap meri lifeline ho. Woh meri jaan hai tu. Us ladki ko bol dena, maine use le liya hai."

The score stands under the hiring and goes.

"Letter mere desk se nahin jaayega." Aryaman gets up. "Vihaan ke daftar se jaayega. Mera naam us kagaz pe nahin hoga."

Outside in the corridor, Vihaan comes past with a camera bag on one shoulder. He goes on without stopping. Above him, on the step's edge, a snake the colour of wet earth pauses with its head raised. The tube light over the door dips once and steadies. Vihaan does not look up. He does not see it. He walks past it without knowing it is there.

* * *

## The Job

The office floor at three in the morning, emptying out. The sisters by the glass doors. The rainswept parking beyond them.

"Purvi! Purvi! Purvi!" Ananta comes across the floor with her phone in her hand. "Tujhe job mil gayi hai!"

"Kya?"

"Haan!" Her hair comes down as she shakes her head, with joy. "Kal appointment letter bhi aa jaayega!"

Purvi's hands are over her mouth. Then her face is wet. Ananta has her by the shoulders.

"Kya hua? Khushi ke aansu?" She wipes them with her thumbs. "Meri jaan, tu aise hi kyun roti hai jaise chheenkne wali ho?"

"Thank you." Purvi is holding her. "Thank you, thank you."

"Ek baat poochun?" She steps back. "Aapne mujhe job kaise dilwayi? Aapne apne special powers ka use kiya?"

"Hmm. Of course." Ananta puts her hand on her hip. "Yahi toh meri special power hai. Ki koi mujhe na nahin keh sakta."

Purvi laughs. She stops laughing.

"Kaash mere paas bhi kuch special powers hote."

"Aapke paas poora brahmand hai, Naagrani." Ananta says it lightly, and then she is not light at all. "Aapko toh ehsaas hi nahin hai ki aap hain kitni shaktishali. Aap hain Anant kul ki Naagrani. Aapki chaubeesvi saalgirah pe aapko aapke Naaglok se milaya jaayega. Aapko aapki duniya mein laaya jaayega."

The score comes up, and under it, quietly:

"Kabhi kabhi lagta hai ki main is duniya ki hi nahin hoon."

Out in the parking, Ananta's car stands with its lights on. The rain coming down round it.

"Oye Totamal. Oye." Ananta has the door open. "Aaja, meri gaadi mein baith ja. Let's have fun."

"Ek baat poochhni thi." Purvi stands by the open door with the rain in her face. "Aath baje main us building mein jaane waali thi. Kisi ne mujhe kaha tha ki Manoj ke paas roz subah ek list hoti hai, aur mujhe apna naam us list mein likhwana chahiye."

"Kisne kaha?"

"Unke chhote bhai ne." Purvi looks at her sister. "Raat do baje lobby mein. Maine teen ghante khadi thi, didi. Ek bhi aadmi nahin aaya. Woh aaya, aur woh ek minute ke liye ruka."

Ananta stands with the door against her hip. She does not say anything at all.

"You bloody just shut up." She takes her by the arm. "Deep, jaldi chalo."

"Mujhe dar lag raha hai." Purvi is not moving. "Please."
