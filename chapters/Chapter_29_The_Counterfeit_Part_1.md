# Chapter 29 — The Counterfeit (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 18, 22 February 2026.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Switch

Vikram's floor keeps the night past midnight in turning light and bass, and the masks have all learned each other's names by now.

"Mask to pehen hi liya hai."

Radhika holds her hand out to Purav.

"Hum bhi dance karte hain na?"

"Tumhein pata hai main kitna bura dance karta hoon."

Purav does not take it.

"Please."

She does not lower it.

"Party mein aap hi lekar aaye hain na. Aap chaliye. Chaliye. Lijiye."

A waiter's tray floats past with untouched glasses.

Across the floor, the letter's words go through Ahana again where she stands.

Ye kaun sa naya dushman hai jisse mera purana hisaab hai?

Aakhir hai kaun jise main jaan ke bhi pehchaan nahin pa rahi.

The question finds no mouth to answer it.

The score comes up under the letter.

"Kuch majedaar karke hi aata hoon."

Vikram rises with mischief on.

"Ruk."

Laughter follows him down.

The music turns the floor, and the partners switch mid-song — and Aryaman finds Radhika spinning across from him, and the past floods up between them, and neither of them looks away in time.

Vikram's laugh finds Ahana in the switch, and his hand pairs her into the turn.

Something turns under Vihaan's ribs at the sight — Vikram's hand where his hand belongs — jealousy, bodiless and certain, before thought can.

Thought, arriving late, calls it a party mixer.

His eyes stay on the pair all the same.

Aryaman stops mid-spin.

Frustration clouds past grief, and he storms off the floor and out of the hall, and the masks part to let him through.

An empty glass stands abandoned on the stairs.

"Aryaman." Vihaan is already moving after his brother.

The score comes up under the storm-out.

Ahana watches them go — the stormed-out wound, the husband following — and unease settles under her breastbone like a stone.

Family, breaking audibly at a party.

She turns back to the floor with it unsaid.

## The Corner

The service corner off the hall keeps its own dimmer bulb, and the music reaches it dulled.

"Arre piyo ise."

A young man's hand pushes a glass on a terrified girl.

"Please — chhod do."

"Ladkon ko ye baat samajh kyun nahin aati."

Ahana's voice cuts through the dull.

"Ki na ka matlab na hota hai."

She steps in.

"Lagta hai aaj ye baat samjhaani hi padegi."

The score comes up under the lecture.

Her bangles ring against the stone pillar.

"Mujhe jaane do."

The girl twists.

"Kaun — baby — bas please please please — mujhe chhod do."

"Jab usne bola tha tab hi chhoda tha tune use."

Ahana's eyes have gone serpent-gold at the edges, and the scales begin at her temples.

"Nahin na? To main tujhe bhala kaise chhodoongi?"

A shuttered lamp buzzes over the service door.

She strikes — swift, partial, terrible — and the predator begs it at the floor.

"Help — help — arre Shekhar kahaan hai tu?"

His friends come running — and she spares him to their hands, and is gone from the corner before their eyes find more than a girl leaving.

Fury — swift, partial, terrible.

The spiked glass spills unnoticed across the tiles.

## The Exhibition

The exhibition hall keeps its noon glare a world away from the party night, and Pammi holds her phone like a weapon.

Diamond light breaks cold across the display glass.

"Hello — bhai sahab."

Her voice shakes the line to Parmeet.

"Mujhe aapse Ahana ke baare mein bahut zaroori baat bataani hai. Bhai sahab — main yahaan jewellery exhibition mein Mr. Dawan se mili. Unhone mujhse khud kaha — unki sirf ek hi beti hai, jo US mein padhti hai."

"Kya bol rahi ho tum?"

Parmeet's voice comes back iron.

Pammi's bangle strikes the counter as she turns.

"Bhai sahab — ye ladki hum sabko — aapko — pagal bana rahi hai."

Pammi rushes on.

"Lekin mujhe yakeen hai ki wo naya heera usi ke paas hai."

"Mummy — aisa kaise ho sakta hai?"

A young voice beside Pammi, small.

"Mujhe kuch nahin maaloom."

Pammi's eyes stay hard.

"Main Dawan ke ghar ja rahi hoon. Aap log bhi wahin pahunchiye."

A price card flutters on its string.

"Tum wahin ruko."

Parmeet cuts the line to order.

"Main Ahana ko lekar hi aata hoon."

"Ji."

A beat.

"Kya hua, bhai sahab? Aapke paseene kyun chhoot gaye?"

A relative stares.

"Gaadi — Vikram — yahaan le chalo."

Fear — sweating through the order.

## The Clash

The hall edge has gone tight and listening under the side lamps by the time Vihaan catches his brother's shoulder past the doors.

"Tune kya socha tha?"

Vikram is already there, drink-heavy and old-wound raw.

"Aryaman — apni haalat dekh."

The taunt comes curdled with unconceded ground — Pinky, the mandap, the name spent.

"Itna taras to mujhe tujh pe tab nahin aaya tha jab Radhika tujhe chhod gayi thi."

Aryaman swings.

Vikram meets him.

The childhood-friend line holds for exactly one breath — then fists.

Rage — old wounds, fresh fists.

Vikram's glass shatters somewhere past the doors.

"Bhaai — kya kar rahe ho?" Vihaan steps between them and takes the next swing on his raised forearm.

"Chhodo ek doosre ko." His voice goes flat.

"What the hell? Control — control."

One low string is struck, and left hanging.

The door staff look away in practiced unison.

Ahana arrives at the run's end.

"Ye jhagda band karo."

She looks from one heaving man to the other.

"Aap paagal ho gaye ho kya? Kya kar rahe ho?"

"Aa — Ahana beta — aap mere saath chalo."

Parmeet arrives with the exhibition call burning his phone.

"Oho — beta — ye bachpan ke dost hain, aise jhagadte hain."

Smooth as oil.

"Aap mere saath chalo — zaroori baat hai."

"Main dekhta hoon." Vihaan's eyes stay on the pair.

"Rao."

A nod sends the staffer to hold the door.

Parmeet takes Ahana's elbow.

"Haan — chal. Please."

## The Denial

Dawan House keeps its late-night lamps behind high gates, and the cars arrive in a line — Parmeet's, Pammi's, and Vihaan's with his wife beside him.

"Ye hum kahaan aaye hain — Dad?"

A young voice looks up at the gates.

"Are beta ji — aapne pehchaana nahin?"

Parmeet savours it, turning to Ahana.

"Yahin to — aapke Daddy ka ghar."

"Off — of course — main kaise nahin pehchaanoongi."

Ahana lifts her chin.

"Par hum yahaan kyun aaye hain?"

"Andar chalo pata chal jaayega."

Parmeet leads them in.

"We have a surprise for you. Come — aao."

"Aao — beta ji."

The hall goes cold.

"Darasl hum yahaan aaye the ki aap apni beti Ahana se — "

The relative's smile freezes.

"Tum logon ka dimaag kharaab hai."

Dawan does not rise.

"Main kitni baar kahoon — Ahana naam ki meri beti hai par wo America mein padhti hai."

The score comes up under the denial.

"Are — kaisi baatein kar rahe hain?"

The relatives press closer.

"Are ye Ahana — ye aapki beti hai."

"Ye koi fraud hogi."

Dawan's hand cuts the air.

"Meri beti nahin hai ye."

"Ahana aur Vihaan ki shaadi mein aap hamaare ghar aaye the."

A relative finds the counter.

"Hum mile bhi the."

"Are bhai sahab — ye kahin chhadari naagin ka chakkar to nahin hai?"

A whisper goes round.

"What? Kya bakwaas kar rahe ho tum log?"

Dawan slaps the chair-arm.

"Main keh raha hoon — ye meri beti nahin hai."

"Fraud — " Vihaan steps between his wife and the old man.

He knows she is not Dawan.

Connections, years in that house, paper that talks — he has known, and never said it, and never cared.

Her telling is hers to give; her bharosa he keeps like a vow.

"Dawan ho ya na ho — " His voice goes flat round the hall.

"Ye meri biwi hai."

A beat.

"Baat khatm."

One low string is struck, and left hanging.

The hall goes quiet enough to hear the lamps hum.

Parmeet looks at his son a moment longer than the night needs.

Pammi's mouth thins to a line.

Her fraud-case dies in her brother's hall, and the whole hall watches it die.

The relatives find the floor suddenly interesting.

His eyes find hers across the sudden quiet — and wait there.

Her fingers unclench at her sides.

"Meri beti to US mein padhi hai."

Dawan's bluster has gone hollow.

He pushes up from the chair.

"Main uski photo abhi dikhaata hoon."

A step back.

"Mobile — rukiye — main abhi aaya."

The score comes up under the photo-fetch.

He steps through the inner door — and does not come back as himself.

Bharani's lamp goes out behind Dawan's doors; the old man's breath evens into drugged sleep; his face comes away like a borrowed shawl, and she wears it out past his own threshold.

Bharani's duty goes with her through the door, unspoken.

Jo kal kiya, wo bhi dharm tha; jo aaj kar rahi hai, wo bhi dharm hai.

The thought steadies her hands on another man's face, and none of it reaches the hall.

"Dekhiye — ye meri beti hai."

Bharani-Dawan holds the phone out.

"Ahana."

"Haan — ye Ahana hi to hai."

A relative peers, and the denial thins to nothing.

The score comes up under the photo-match.

Ahana cannot thank Bharani aloud — not in Dawan's face, not before this hall.

So it stands behind her eyes, whole and unsaid: Bharani — main jaanti hoon tumse naraaz hoon.

Magar aaj tumne meri bahut madad ki.

Main to soch bhi nahin sakti ki tumhaare bina main is mushkil se kaise nikalti.

Thank you.

The anger stays.

The debt joins it.

The score comes up under the thanks.

"Ye Ahana hai?"

Dawan's eyes film over.

"Haan na — tum meri beti ho."

"Dad — aapne phir se davaai nahin li na?"

Ahana catches the cue mid-air.

"Very bad. I know it — davaaiyaan."

She turns to the hall.

"Ye kaun si davaai khaata hai, Dad?"

A beat.

"Ko — short-term memory loss hai."

The score comes up under the cover.

"Golmaal hai bhai — iske Dad Ghajini hai kya?"

A relative snorts.

"Are chup raho — kya bol rahe ho."

"Suniye ji — haan — inhein kuch saalon se bhoolne ki beemaari hai."

The cover builds itself.

"Dad — aapki ek girlfriend bhi thi na — jo aapki is beemaari ka faayda utha ke aapse shaadi karna chahti thi."

The score comes up under the girlfriend.

"Haan."

Bharani-Dawan plays it true.

"Taaki wo meri saari property dhokhe se apne naam karwa le."

"Aur wo naayab heera bhi to hadap lena chahti thi — jis par sirf aapki beti ka hak hai."

Under the hak, the score comes up.

"Nahin."

Dawan's voice goes iron.

"Jo meri beti ka hai usse koi nahin chheen sakta. Maine decide kar liya hai."

He takes her hands.

"Tumhaari amaanat, beta, tumhein hi saunpoonga — abhi ke abhi."

"Really?"

Her breath catches.

"Haan — to — chalo."

The hall stirs.

"Chaliye chaliye — kahaan ja rahe hain aap?"

"Are — mera — aapke bedroom ke locker mein hoga na? Chaliye."

"Tumhein main paagal lag raha hoon?"

Dawan is already moving.

"Itna naayab heera main apne kamre ke locker mein rakhoonga?"

"To kahaan hai heera?"

## The Chamber

The pit head keeps its night in rock and rail-lamps, and Bharani-Dawan's stride leads the line down past the old search-marks.

The score comes up under the descent.

A lamp gutters in the shaft draught.

"Us raat ye jagah to humne hi dhoondhne di thi."

Ahana reads the marks on the rock.

"Maine kaha tha na, Dad — mere Daddy ke paas mines mein aisi bahut saari safe aur hidden jagah hain."

The score comes up under the reading.

"Bhai sahab — ye Dawan sahab bhi aapki tarah chatur-chalaak nikle."

Baljeet marvels down the shaft.

"Jaise aapne office ke neeche dragon paala hua tha — usi tarah inhone apne mines ke neeche ye secret room banaaya hua hai."

He waves them on.

"Chalo chalo chalo — aao."

The score comes up under the marvel.

A coil of rope hangs unstirred on the rock.

"Ise pehen lijiye."

The robing is done in the lamplight.

Under the robing, the score comes up.

"Lo — ye tumhaari amaanat hai, beta."

Bharani-Dawan sets the stone in her hands.

"Khush raho."

"Thank you, Dad."

It sits heavy and cold in her palms.

"Par Dad — itni mehengi cheez main kaise rakh sakti hoon? Ek kaam kariye — ise aap hi rakh lijiye."

"Chalo chalo — finally — hamaare haath mein aa gayi."

Parmeet takes custody for the safe.

"Ab chalte hain. Ab chalte hain."

"Beti ji — ye aapki amaanat hai."

Baljeet steadies the case.

"Ise hum safe mein rakh ke aate hain."

The score comes up under the custody.

"Haan — sambhaal ke."

Ahana does not let go till the case shuts.

"Bhai sahab — bahut bhaari hai."

Baljeet shifts the weight.

"Laao — main main main le leta hoon."

Parmeet takes it from him and strides for the shaft with the case, and Baljeet falls in behind.

"Agar inhein pata chal gaya ki ye asli nahin hai — to?"

Bharani-Dawan murmurs it under the shaft-noise, for his daughter's ears.

"Pata nahin chalega."

Her answer comes back just as low.

"Samundra ki gehraaiyon mein mila tha — chamak, khushboo, sab Naagmani jaisi. Nakli hai, par pakda nahin jaayega."

Vihaan catches the tail of it a step behind her — nakli — and files it with everything else he carries unsaid, and his face does not change.

What she does not say aloud stays behind her eyes while the lamps move out.

Ab samajh aa raha hai ki kyun wo ichchhadhaari naagin Ananta apni jaan dekar bhi ye Naagmani kisi ko dena nahin chahti thi.

Kaun si bevakoof aurat apna itna bada heera kisi ko aise hi de degi.

Main achche se jaanti hoon ki tum log khush kyun ho — tum samajh rahe ho ki ye mani asli hai.

Lekin jis din tumhein pata chalega ki ye Naagmani asli nahin, nakli hai — us din tumhaare pairon tale zameen khisak jaayegi.

Agar ye Naagmani hamein Changistaan bhejni hoti na — to main ise bhej ke arabon kama leti.

Aur phir apni raani-mode mein hoti.

Phir mehel jaisa ghar, lavish cars, bada swimming pool.

None of it reaches her face.

"Tum theek ho?" Vihaan asks it low at her shoulder, walking out past the rail-lamps.

She nods once.

The night has accused her of fraud and handed her a fortune, and his hand finds the small of her back, and that is the whole answer.

Tenderness — the whole answer.

Baljeet's shadow peels after Parmeet's car.

## The Apology

The Suri hall holds the small hours in tired lamplight, and the stone sits cased on the rosewood table like a guest nobody trusts.

"Arre — Ahana beta — tum hi is ghar ki asli bahu ho."

Pammi's turn comes lacquered.

"Asli bahu. Varna jis ladki se Ravish ki shaadi hone wali thi — wo to ek number ki luteri thi — balki poora ka poora parivaar 420 tha."

A breath.

"Us ladki ne Ravish pe ilzaam lagaaya tha ki wo Ravish ke bachche ki maa banne waali thi."

The score comes up under the turn.

"Exactly."

A relative nods along.

"Pata nahin kiska paap apne pet mein lekar ghoom rahi thi."

"Isiliye aajkal ka daur to kisi pe bharosa karne ka hai hi nahin, beta."

Under the bharosa-talk, the score comes up.

"Isiliye to aap mujh pe bhi bharosa nahin karti na."

Ahana meets her eyes.

"Sach hi to keh rahi hoon. Maine to bina soche-samjhe apni itni keemti cheez aap sabko saunp di. Lekin aap sabko mujh pe phir bhi shak tha."

A beat.

"Tabhi aap chali gayi na mere papa ke paas ye confirm karne ki main unki beti hoon ya nahin. Seriously — kya soch rahe honge wo?"

The score comes up under the receipt.

"Haan na beta — aap bilkul theek keh rahi ho."

The elders murmur.

"Mummy — tumhaari galti hai."

A young voice, small.

"Ahana bitiya bilkul sahi keh rahi hai. Tumhein is par shak nahin karna chaahiye tha."

"Arre tum Dawan sahab ke saath thi aur poore time wo shaadi mein the."

Pammi rallies.

"Lekin bhai sahab main to aapko bolna chahti hoon — "

"Chup."

Parmeet does not raise his voice.

"Bahu se maafi maango. Abhi ke abhi."

"Haan maafi maang lo."

The hall agrees.

"Sorry."

Pammi says it like a stone dropped down a well.

Humiliation — down the well.

"It's okay."

Ahana folds her hands.

"Par main ummeed karti hoon ki ye naayab heera paane ke baad aap logon ko shayad mujh par bharosa ho jaaye."

"Arre of course — 100%."

The hall rushes to agree.

"Haan haan beta — ye bhi bhala koi kehne ki baat hai. Aisi baatein karke aap hamein sharminda kar rahe ho."

"Main ise tijori mein rakh ke aata hoon."

Parmeet lifts the case.

"Itni naayab cheez — itni keemti cheez — baahar nahin rehni chaahiye. Baljeet — chal."

Baljeet falls in.

"Aaiye — bhai sahab."

Vihaan holds his wife's side through the theatre of it, and files the forced sorry with the fraud-charge that bought it, and says nothing at all.
