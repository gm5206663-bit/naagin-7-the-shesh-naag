# Chapter 33 — The Locker (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 22, 8 March 2026.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Drawer

Morning stands in their room.

Her side of the bed is empty.

The bathroom door stands open on nothing.

The night-lamp still burns beside the bed, small in the daylight.

He misses her, and it is only morning.

Vihaan sits at the desk with the drawer open.

The interview day has taken her early — the studio, the officers, the biggest story of the channel.

The room ticks round him.

Files lie in a row where his hands laid them.

One file lies squared apart from the row — shut, where sleep left it under his hand.

Six feet of him folded into the chair.

A white kurta, sleeves rolled to the elbow.

The jaw set the way it sets over paper.

His hands move sure among the files — the hands of a man who reads for a living.

"Chai."

Pinky stops in the doorway with the tray — in a rose-pink morning suit, her hair still in its night braid — and stares.

"Bhai — itni subah? Ye kya — poora daftar bistar pe?"

"Audit ka kagaz," he says, his eyes on the page.

"Train waali inquiry — jo maangegi, wo pehle se taiyaar milega."

Pinky sets the tray down.

She looks at the row of files.

She looks at him.

"Oh God. Kitne handsome lag rahe ho — kagaz mein ghuse hue bhi."

She pulls at his sleeve.

"Maa hoti na — nazar utaar deti."

"Pinky."

He looks up at last, and almost smiles.

"Chai de. Gyaan baad mein."

She laughs, and pours, and the steam stands up between them.

"Do din mein board meeting hai — pata hai na? Dus saal — channel ki anniversary. Ghar mein sab kehte hain — kuch bada hone waala hai."

"Jaanta hoon."

He takes the cup.

"anniversary apni jagah. Ye kagaz apni jagah."

Her voice drops.

"Neeche phone aaya tha — inquiry waalon ka. Kagaz maange hain. Aaj shaam tak."

"Kaun aayega lene?" he says.

"Wo bole — aadmi bhejenge. Shaam ko."

She twists the tray's edge.

"Viju — sab theek hai na?"

"Kagaz hain, Pinky," he says. "Kagaz ka jawaab kagaz hota hai."

Her eyes go over the files — the feed paper, the licence fold, the pass with his signature, the staff cards in their sleeve — and she swallows the question.

She knows the look.

When Viju reads, the house waits.

"Verma waali file?"

She nods at the thin report at the row's end.

"Uska kuch aaya?"

"Nahin."

His thumb rests on it one breath.

"Abhi bhi jawaab nahin aaya."

He slides it back — shut, uncarried, held.

"Ye inquiry ka nahin hai. Ye mera hai — yahin rahega."

His voice goes flat — no smile left in it.

"Aur Pinky — is file ko koi haath nahin lagaayega. Koi bhi nahin," he says.

She nods — too fast — and picks up the empty tray.

"Naashta neeche. Jaldi aana — thanda ho jaayega."

"Tu chal," he says, and lifts the cup.

"Main ye nipta ke aata hoon."

She goes.

The door stands open behind her.

The house comes up the stairs in sounds — the pressure cooker, the news, somebody's laugh.

He drinks the tea standing at the window.

Below, the lane wakes.

A vendor calls once, and does not call again.

She chose the maroon under this lamp, before the daylight.

She took it to the studio.

Then he sits, and sorts.

The feed paper goes into the carry-folder — Kumbh, fifteen days, his.

The licence fold goes in — the paper the trucks stood on.

The vans pass goes in — his signature on it, his exposure, carried open.

He knows what his name buys him today.

It goes in all the same.

The staff cards go in — forty names, his hand.

The tape stays.

Kumbh's night, unwatched, unplayed — it stays in the drawer, under the shut files.

One low string is struck under the ticking, and left hanging — the decision, said flat in paper.

Flat.

Paper-flat.

His plainness, packed with the folder.

He shuts the drawer.

His hand stays on it one breath longer than paper needs.

His eyes go to the night-lamp, burning its small burn in the full morning.

He lets it burn.

The folder lies shut on the desk.

## The Pitch

Morning light lies across the newsroom floor.

The day's rundown is already creased in Aryaman's fist — folded, unfolded, folded again.

His sleeves are rolled to the elbow.

His collar open.

Yesterday's tiredness still under his eyes.

"Feature waise hi?"

Ahana does not look up from her desk.

Her sari is maroon today, dark as sealing-wax — chosen under the night-lamp, before daylight.

Her hair is pulled back tight.

Her bangles are stacked to the wrist.

"Waise hi."

He turns from the glass.

"Changistaan. Adtaalis ghante. Sansani-khez news. Tum host kar rahi ho — mere saath."

"Main saath hoon."

She shuts her file.

"Board do din mein."

He holds her eyes.

"Tenth anniversary."

A breath.

"Uske baad — out, bhabhi. Tumse kya chhupaana."

The tube-light over the assignment desk flickers once.

Nobody looks up.

He taps the rundown once against his palm.

"Isiliye ye waala dhamakedaar hoga. Final telecast hai."

"Final nahin, bhai."

She stands.

"Aakhri mauka."

The deal of last night stands between them, said and settled.

"Taiyaari shuru."

He turns for the door.

"Main news prepare karne jaa raha hoon."

"Par tumhein ye bataana hoga, bhai — "

Ahana follows him out between the desks.

"Ki tum Women's Day special kyon nahin karte?"

"Wo tum karo."

He does not slow.

"Mujhe Women's Day mein koi interest nahin hai."

The floor bustles round them — a phone, a printer, a peon with files balanced to his chin.

"Aur waise bhi —"

He stops at the assignment desk and lays the rundown down.

"Inke liye alag din kyon chaahiye? Har din to unka hota hai."

The score comes up under the newsroom noise — tight, waiting.

"Pehle ladke ko date karti hai."

Something shuts in his face.

"Phir bore ho jaati hai. Phir dump. Phir kisi aur se pyaar. Phir use bhi replace."

Ahana's hand stills on her file.

She knows the name under it.

She keeps it.

Keeping is what she does now.

She keeps his everything.

She keeps this.

"Radhika ne —"

He catches himself.

"Chhodo."

A breath.

"Jo chhod ke jaata hai — wo laut ke nahin dekhta."

He is ashamed of the sound of himself — and he says it anyway.

"Aryaman."

She holds his eyes, and her bangles slide and settle as she folds her arms.

"Studio mein ye zabaan nahin chalegi. Feature mere saath karna hai — gussa desk pe rakh ke aana."

She is angry now.

Over the assignment desk, the tube-light holds steady.

"I hate women."

He turns back to the desk.

"Ye lo. Mera Women's Day special."

Ahana holds on his turned back one beat past his turning.

The floor's noise drops half a note.

Somebody's cup stops halfway to somebody's mouth.

The rundown's pages whisper — paper on paper — and lie still.

Behind them, at the floor's edge, Radhika has stopped walking.

Purav beside her.

They have heard the last of it.

## The Dates

Noon light slants across the newsroom floor.

Monitors burn in rows.

"Hi, Aryaman."

Radhika comes forward — in a mustard suit, her dupatta pinned neat — too bright.

"Tum log kuch zaroori discuss kar rahe the?"

Purav follows a step behind, his shirt half-untucked, like a man who dressed in a hurry.

The glass wall throws the four faces back at them, doubled.

"Kuch nahin."

Aryaman recovers fast.

"Hum wo — board meeting ke baare mein baat kar rahe the."

"OK."

Purav glances between them.

"Do din mein board meeting hai na?"

"Finally — enough."

Aryaman almost smiles.

"Wo board meeting is news channel ki exact tenth anniversary pe hai."

"Tenth anniversary?"

Ahana turns to them both.

"Wow. Sach mein?"

She looks from Radhika to Purav, and her voice goes light and sharp at once.

"Hey guys — anniversary se yaad aaya. Tum logon ki wedding anniversary kab hai? I mean — shaadi ki date kab?"

"Fifth."

Radhika says it at once.

"Fifth November."

Purav says it a breath later — and the two dates do not match, and the floor goes quiet round them.

Purav's ears go hot.

The score lifts — one questioning note.

Nobody on the floor breathes.

Two interns turn in their chairs.

"Kamaal hai."

Ahana smiles.

"Donon alag-alag dates bata rahe ho. Shaadi to ek doosre se ki thi na?"

She tilts her head.

"I mean — kuch time pehle to tum donon ki shaadi hui hai na? Phir itni jaldi date bhi bhool gaye?"

*Ye kya kar rahi hai, Ahana? Maine to use confidence mein sab sach bataaya tha.*

Radhika's hands tighten on her bag.

*Kahin use sab pata to nahin chal gaya?*

She does not say the true thing.

It stays shut.

Confidence is confidence.

His everything taught her that.

"Honestly — "

Ahana looks round the watching floor.

"Logon ko lagega ki tum donon mil ke hum sabko buddhu bana rahe ho."

She is afraid — and lifts her chin anyway.

"Fifth November ko hi hui thi shaadi."

Radhika keeps her eyes on Ahana.

"Fifth November ko hi hui hai. Wo — phere subah chaar-baje-hue the — to technically sixth November."

Somebody's cup cools on the assignment desk, untouched.

A monitor hums.

Somebody's phone rings once, and stops.

"Excuse me."

Aryaman picks up his rundown.

The morning's creases are still in it.

"Mujhe ek meet mein jaana hai."

"Main bhi chalti hoon."

Ahana falls in beside him.

At the cabin door she turns back, sweet as poison.

"Agli baar rehearsal achche se karke aana. I mean — dates — sab kuch achche se yaad kar lena."

Her eyes stay on Purav one beat past the taunt.

Purav looks away first — into the glass, where his doubled face looks back.

The cabin door shuts behind them both.

A paper rustles once in the draught from the shutting door, and lies still.

## The Pallu

The corridor runs empty outside the studios under the noon lights.

Their footsteps go down it together.

The studio doorway breathes cold as they near it.

"Kuch ajeeb nahin tha?"

Ahana keeps her eyes on the corridor.

"Matlab — donon ko apni wedding date hi yaad nahin hai. Aisa kaise ho sakta hai?"

Aryaman walks on.

"Bhool gaye honge."

Her bangle clicks once against the steel door-frame.

"Kabhi-kabhi to lagta hai — ye donon saath hain, par saath nahin hain."

She stops at the studio door.

"Kuch bhi to add up nahin hota. Inka ek wedding picture nahin hai. Shaadi ke baad log ghoomne jaate hain — romantic pictures click karwaate hain — par inka ek bhi picture nahin hai. Maine poora social media check kiya hai. Ek bhi picture nahin."

"Kyon check kiya hai?"

Aryaman turns.

"Kya jaanna chahti thi tum?"

The score breathes under the corridor's quiet — one held note.

"Kyonki tum mere devar ho."

Ahana holds his eyes.

"Aur is ghar ka present mera kaam hai. Agar mere devar ka dil ab bhi kahin atka hai — to us ghar ka sach jaanna — mera farz banta hai. Bhabhi hoon tumhaari."

She means it.

"Seriously — "

She shakes her head.

"Kuch ajeeb nahin lagta? Married-couple waali ek bhi vibe nahin hai in donon mein."

"Bhabhi — "

He starts, and stops, and the studio door opens, and the floor swallows the rest of it.

She steps through first — and her heel catches on the cable taped across the doorway, and she falls sideways with a gasp.

His hand shoots out and catches her elbow.

She steadies, breathing hard, and looks down — the maroon pallu hangs twisted over her shoulder, the pin winking.

A cable lies taped across the doorway, black on grey tile.

She rights it herself, one quick pull, her eyes on the floor.

Nobody's hands but her own.

She is his wife in that too.

The threshold is behind her.

She crossed it on her feet.

"Everything is complicated with you."

He lets go of her elbow at once.

"Nothing is ever simple."

He is exasperated.

He walks away into the studio's noise.

Her cards stay fanned in her hand — fresh ink, morning dealt.

She lets him go.

## The Threat

The studio floor gleams under the morning lights.

The officers come in through the glass doors in a line — four uniforms, four caps under four arms.

"Hello, sir."

Aryaman goes forward with both hands out.

"Thank you — aap sab ne hamaare saath interview ke liye haan ki. Welcome to our channel. I'm Aryaman Suri."

"Hello, Aryaman."

The eldest officer shakes his hand.

"Aapne bulaaya — isiliye hum aa gaye hain. Ye sab jo hum kar rahe hain — ye sab desh ke hit mein hai."

"Please make yourself comfortable."

He waves the floor in.

"My team will get you mic'd up — and we will start in exactly ten minutes."

"OK. Thank you."

"Everybody — "

the floor manager's voice cuts across.

"Places, everybody."

In the wings, where the lights do not reach, Vikram watches the welcome with his hands in his pockets — in a black shirt with the sleeves buttoned, his hair still damp from the shower.

Ahana stands two steps from him, checking her cards.

A work-lamp gutters once beside him.

"Karne do ise jitna drama karna hai."

Vikram keeps his eyes on the lit floor.

"Agle do din mein ye apna sab kuch gawaane waala hai. Ye position bhi."

A smile.

"Waise bhi — sirf ek hi cheez to bachi hai iski life mein. He loves his job. Aur ab main isse yehi kaam chheenunga. To mazaa aayega. Will be fun to watch."

The score comes up under the floor's bustle — pleased with itself.

"Jaise isne mujhe toda hai — "

Vikram turns to her.

"Waise hi ise toot-ta hua dekhoonga main. Aur phir main tumhein waapas paa loonga."

He steps closer.

"Tumhein koi problem to nahin hai na?"

"Nahin."

Ahana keeps her eyes on her cards.

"Mujhe kyon bata rahe ho? Tumhaara khel — tum jaano."

Vikram's hand closes round her wrist.

His cheek burns where yesterday's slap landed — and his fingers close tighter for it.

"I know — tum mujhse — mujhse pyaar karti ho."

She goes very still.

She is not afraid — not here, not of him, not any more.

Then she looks up — and her eyes are winter.

"Haath chhodo."

One line, cold to the bone.

"Abhi."

Two words.

His plainness in her mouth — paper-flat, packed this morning.

The work-lamp flares — and holds.

She takes her wrist back out of his fingers herself.

Without hurry.

Then she straightens her bangle — one finger, one slow pass.

The bangle settles over the wrist he gripped — covering the red.

He lets go — slowly — with the smile still on his face and none of it in his eyes.

"Mujhe Aryaman ke saath is segment mein jaana hai."

She picks up her cards.

"Perfect. I'll see you after."

Aryaman comes down the wing steps at that moment — and stops — and sees her cold face, and Vikram's smile, and the space between them.

"Ahana?"

He comes forward.

"Sab theek?"

"Segment paanch minute mein."

She walks past him.

"Mic lagwa lo."

He looks from her back to Vikram's face.

His jaw tightens.

The floor manager calls again, and the moment breaks.

Vikram stands alone in the wings.

His hand closes on nothing.

The work-lamp gutters over his empty hand.

## The Interview

The studio burns bright. The panel sits in a row behind the desk. The red light stands over them all.

Dumare's bulk, Prateek's clipped moustache, Surya's parade-ground back, Raghav's ink-cuffed sleeves.

"Hello — and welcome to the special news bulletin."

Aryaman faces the camera.

"Aaj hamaare saath hain — IB officer Mr Ashutosh Dumare. RAW officer Mr Prateek Singh. Army officer Mr Suryakumar Yadav. Aur jaane-maane scientist — Mr Raghav."

He turns to them.

"Ye sab ek bahut important mission pe kaam kar rahe hain. Hamaare desh ko bachaane ke mission pe. Sir — hum jaanna chaahenge is mission ke baare mein. To please — kuch bataaiye."

"Dekhiye — hamaara mission hai desh ko kisi bhi bade khatre se bachaane ka."

Dumare sits forward.

"Aur aane waale kuch dinon mein hum kuch bada kadam uthaane waale hain."

In the gallery above, the house watches through the glass — Parmeet, Baljeet, Radhika, Purav, Vikram, Karan, Arjun — seven faces in a row.

His is not among them.

He is home with paper.

What breaks here, she breaks.

"Is Aryaman ko dekh ke na — mera na khoon khaulta hai."

Karan's hands knot on the rail.

"Bas thoda waqt aur."

Arjun does not take his eyes off the floor.

"Uske baad — you are the king."

"Dekhiye — aaj hum aapko interview dene ke liye taiyaar ho gaye hain."

Raghav raises a hand on the floor below.

"Par aap ye interview saat din ke baad telecast keejiyega. Kyonki ye ek bahut serious matter hai. Pehle hi hamaari team ke do log maare jaa chuke hain. Mujhe lagta hai — ki dushman desh ko hamaari planning ka pata chal gaya hai."

The score holds its breath with the studio.

"Sir — we totally understand your concerns."

Aryaman nods.

"Waise to hum news waalon ka kaam hai sabse pehle khabar pahunchaana. Lekin hum aapke hisaab se ise kuch din baad telecast karenge."

He leans in.

"Lekin sir — main aapse poochna chahta hoon — ki aap sab mile kaise? Ye mission start kaise hua? Matlab — ye idea aapke dimaag mein aaya kaise?"

"Ye sab shuru hua — ek visionary insaan ki wajah se."

Dumare's voice drops.

"Jo hamaare professor the. Unka naam tha — Scientist Vishal Puri."

The sting lands on the name — one knock, and gone.

"Ye dekhiye."

Prateek Singh nods — and the monitor on the desk fills with the photograph: a man's face, gentle, bespectacled, gone twenty-four years.

"Ye wo hain — jinhone ye sab formulate kiya tha."

Behind the panel, the big screen fills with the same face for the gallery.

The rundown lies at the desk's edge, creased from his fist.

Ahana's breath stops.

The cupboard face — burned into her eyes in lamplight, now staring up from the desk.

Her bangle has slid.

The wrist shows red where the wings gripped it.

She is shaken — and live on air.

Aryaman's hands tighten once on the desk's edge.

His knuckles whiten.

Overhead, the red light burns on, unblinking.

"Yahi wo insaan the — jinki wajah se hum sab yahaan aaye hain."

Raghav's voice goes soft.

"Hamaara pehla secret mission inhone hi plan kiya tha. Aur inki death ke baad — hum sab ne government ko join kar liya."

The score comes up under the photograph's held face — low, mourning.

In the gallery, Parmeet's face shuts like a door.

Baljeet looks away.

The pin holds.

The maroon sits straight.

"To chaliye — "

Ahana's voice cuts in — too bright, too fast.

"Waqt ho chala hai ek chhote se break ka. We will be right back."

She looks up once, into the red eye — and calls it.

"Excuse me."

Aryaman is on his feet before the red light dies.

The desk holds his empty chair, and the panel's four faces turn to follow him.

## The Cabin

The cabin holds its noon quiet. The blinds stripe the desk. Aryaman stands at the window with his back to the door.

In the window glass, his face is already wet.

"Ye mere real dad hain."

He keeps his back to the door.

"Maine kisi ko nahin bataaya — ki ye great scientist mere father hain."

Ahana shuts the door behind her and leans on it one breath.

"Lekin jab bhi main dekhta hoon na — ki log inki kitni respect karte hain." His voice thins.

"Tumne dekha — ye high-level log bhi inhein apna guru maante hain."

A breath.

"Tab mujhe realise hota hai — ki main inke baare mein kuch nahin jaanta."

A steel glass of water stands untouched at the desk's edge.

The score comes up, low — grief, plain.

"No idea."

He turns at last, and his eyes are wet and furious both.

"Main bas itna jaanta hoon — ki jin logon ne inhein maara — wo Changistaan se the."

He is grieving — and furious at the grief.

His phone lights once on the desk, and goes dark unanswered.

"Maine bhi suna hai — ki ye kisi badi cheez pe kaam kar rahe the."

She holds his eyes.

"Lekin wo kaam kya tha — main nahin jaanti."

Her fingers find her bangles, and still.

She is torn, and must not show it.

*Is bechaare ko to kuch pata bhi nahin hai — ki jis parivaar ko ye apni jaan se zyaada pyaar karta hai — shaayad wahi iske dad ke kaatil hain.*

Her torn has two faces.

The devar weeping.

The husband's roof over the weeping.

The window glass stands empty now.

He took the wet face with him when he turned.

"Main abhi aayi."

She turns for the door — and stops with her hand on it.

Through the half-open door come two voices, low and hurrying.

## The Swap

The corridor stands empty past the makeup rooms. The noon lights burn low.

The makeup-room mirrors throw back nothing but hurry.

"Baat karo — zaroori baat hai."

Parmeet's voice, from the corridor's end.

"Hello — theek hai. Dus minute mein aa raha hoon."

"Jaldi chalo."

Baljeet, under it.

Ahana stands frozen in the cabin doorway.

*Kiske baare mein baat kar rahe hain ye — aur kisse milne aise jaldi mein nikle hain?*

*Mujhe pata karna hoga. Lekin beech mein ye show chhod ke — kahaan se jaaoon — kaise?*

She turns on her heel and goes down the back corridor fast, past the makeup rooms, to where Bharani waits with the spare mic — in a plain grey cotton sari, her keys at her waist.

A clipboard hangs by the makeup-room door, its sheet curling off the clip.

"Bharani — "

She catches her arm.

"Parmeet aur Baljeet ke beech mein kuch to chal raha hai. Aur ye jaanne ke liye — mujhe unke peeche jaana hai. Abhi ke abhi."

The score comes up — quick, urgent.

Bharani's bangle slides once on her wrist, and stills.

"Lekin aap to news nahin padh sakti."

Ahana holds her arm.

"Aage ka kaam ab tumhein karna hoga. Mera jaana bahut zaroori hai."

"Main kaise kar loongi?"

Bharani's voice drops.

"Mujhe poora bharosa hai tum pe. Kuch nahin karna — bas."

She presses her cards into Bharani's hands.

"Lekin in sawaalon ko poochna hai. Lekin haan — tumhein in chaaron ka khaas khayaal rakhna hoga. In chaaron pe kabhi bhi hamla ho sakta hai. To inki suraksha ki zimmedaari tumhaari hi hai."

He trusted her with everything.

She trusts Bharani with the desk.

Trust travels.

Her cards change hands — white paper, black ink, morning's fresh ink thumb-worn by noon — her whole desk in Bharani's palm.

"Theek hai."

Bharani is afraid — and squares her shoulders anyway.

"Kahaan thi, ma'am, aap? Baahar sab log aapko dhoondh rahe hain. Jaldi chaliye. Take shuru hone waala hai. Chalo."

Bharani walks out past him toward the lights — Ahana's walk, Ahana's face, eyes forward.

The mirrors throw her back once — whole, unhurried — and let her go.

## The Locker

The road runs empty past the bank's shut shutters. Parmeet's car stands at the kerb. The afternoon sun beats down on its roof.

"Digital duniya ko humne tyag diya hai."

The manager's voice drifts from the bank's steps, ponsel over his register, his shirt sticking to his back.

"Lekin hum haar nahin maanenge. Hum apni zindagi phir se shuru karenge."

"Oye — kya hua, bhai sahab?"

Baljeet twists in his seat, staring back down the road, a folded token slip gone limp in his hand.

"Aap peeche kya dekh rahe hain?"

"Aisa laga — "

Parmeet's eyes sweep the empty road, his jacket over his arm, sweat dark at his collar.

"Ki saanp — nahin — gira."

"Kuch nahin hai, bhai sahab."

Baljeet waves it off.

"Airul ke paaltu jaanvaron ko dekh-dekh ke aapki aisi haalat ho gayi hai. Jaldi speed badhaaiye. Humein ghar pahunchna hai — jaldi."

The score comes up under the car's idle — watchful.

"Bas — mujhe kuch nahin sunna."

Parmeet gets out.

"Kuch to karna padega aapko — aur wo bhi jaldi."

"Sir — maine aapko pehle hi samjhaaya tha."

The manager folds his hands.

"Ye bank ka protocol hai. Pet se pehle kuch nahin ho sakta. Teen din to aapko intezaar karna hi hoga."

"Arre yaar — koi to raasta hoga."

Baljeet crowds him.

"Koi to loophole hoga."

"Sir — agar koi loophole hota — "

The manager shakes his head.

"To chaubis saal pehle hi bata chuka hota, sir, main. Par nahin hai, sir. Bas baat sirf teen din ki aur hai."

He looks from one face to the other.

"Teen din baad Aryaman ka janamdin hai. Tees saal ka wo poora ho jaayega. Uske baad bank ka locker apne aap khul jaayega. Teen din intezaar karna hi hoga, sir."

He folds his register.

He goes inside.

The shutter rattles down behind him.

"Main chalta hoon."

The words hang in the hot air.

A dog sleeps on in the bank's strip of shade while men plan a murder.

"Us Hindustani ke bachche ko itne saalon se paal rahe hain — "

Parmeet's voice cracks.

"Aur abhi bhi teen din ka intezaar? Ye kaisa torture hai, bhai sahab?"

"Bas, bhai sahab — bas keejiye."

Baljeet's face hardens.

"Aur nahin khel sakte hum ise. Ise thikaane lagaiye."

"Haan."

Parmeet stares at the shut shutter.

"Kuch to karna hi padega."

A breath.

"Maine ise isliye god liya tha — kyonki ye aise scientist ka beta tha — jo ek aisa hathiyaar bana raha tha — jo Changistaan ko tabaah kar sakta tha."

"Dhoondho." His voice drops twenty-four years.

"Yahi kahin hogi wo kitaab. Jaldi."

The office lies ransacked — drawers out, papers everywhere, dust thick in the light — and two men turn it over shelf by shelf.

"Aaraam se."

A small voice, from behind the almirah.

"Haan — ghabrao nahin — ghabrao nahin."

Parmeet goes still.

Then he crouches, slow, and holds out his hand.

"Tum yahaan kyon chhupa hai — nahin — kyon chhupa hai, beta?" His voice goes soft as milk.

"Aaja — aaja — mera bachcha — aaja."

His hand stays out, open and steady.

"Aaja — aaja — aaja — aaja — mere bachche."

"Bas — chup — chup — chup, beta."

Parmeet gathers him in — soft-voiced, dust-handed, lying with his whole body.

"Tumhaare papa ne koi kitaab di tumhein — sambhaal ke rakhne ke liye?"

A pause.

"Daro mat. Hum tumhaare papa ke dost hain. Tumhaare papa ne humein bheja hai — tumhein safe jagah le jaane ke liye. Chal — chaloge na? Haan? Chalo, beta. Aa jaao."

The boy's hand goes into his.

The dust settles over the empty office.

---

"Aur tab se leke aaj tak — "

Parmeet's voice comes back to the hot road.

"Humne us Hindustani ke bachche ko apne ghar ka maan ke isliye paala — taaki jab wo tees saal ka ho jaaye — to humein us locker ki chaabi mil jaaye. Jismein us scientist ne asli file chhupa rakhi hai — jo hum Changistaniyon ke liye bahut zaroori hai."

The score comes up — dark, certain.

"Bas ek baar wo file mere haath mein aa jaaye — "

Parmeet looks at his own hands.

"Is — ise — main apne haath se khatm kar doon."

In the strip of shade, the dog lifts its head.

It looks at them.

It lays its head down again.

He hates the boy.

Behind the shutter's edge, where the lane turns, Ahana stands very still — and hears it all.

Her maroon presses into the shutter's shadow.

Her husband packed his name on paper this morning.

These men sort murder here.

She stands between.

*To yehi thi — Aryaman ko apna beta banaane ki asli wajah.*

Her hands knot at her sides.

*In Changistaniyon ko uske pita ki file chaahiye — aur uske milte hi — *

"Nahin."

The word leaves her mouth without sound.

"Mere jeete-ji aisa kabhi nahin hoga."

Her hand closes round her bangles — the still of the cabin, closed at last.

She is furious — and cold with it.

## The Deflection

The studio floor holds its break-hour quiet under the dimmed lights.

The panel's chairs stand empty.

Aryaman stands alone at the desk with the photograph's afterimage still on his face.

"Aryaman ji — "

Raghav comes up beside him, gentle, spectacles riding low on his nose.

"Aap theek to hain? Wo photo dekhne ke baad — aap kaafi pareshaan lag rahe the."

"Nahin — main theek hoon."

Aryaman straightens.

"Hamaare desh mein khatra hai. Ye soch ke thoda disturb ho gaya tha. That's all."

"Don't worry, Aryaman ji."

Raghav lays a hand on his shoulder.

"Hum poori koshish karenge — ki desh ekdum safe rahe."

Raghav is kind.

"Thank you for that."

Aryaman nods.

"Isi baat pe ek chhota sa break lete hain."

A paper cup lies crushed by the desk's leg.

Morning lifted it.

Noon forgot it.

Break-hour crushed it.

The score breathes once under the empty studio — and goes out.

The red lamp over the door flickers once — and dies with the break.

"Main abhi aaee."

Ahana's voice, from the wing door — back from the wings, bright as ever, giving nothing away.

Giving nothing away.

She has practice.

She keeps his everything.

The door shuts behind her.

Aryaman stands alone at the desk.

On the dark screen behind him, the photograph is gone — but his father's face stays behind on the dark glass.

He holds his grief the way he holds the desk — both hands, no sound.

## The Handover

Evening stands in their room.

The night-lamp burns beside the bed — kept since morning, burning still.

The desk stands empty of its row.

The folder lies shut where he left it — squared, packed, his name inside it.

Downstairs the house eats its dinner without him.

A car stops in the lane.

One door shuts.

Pinky brings the man up — in the same rose-pink suit, the braid gone loose — and leaves him at the door with her eyes on her brother.

The man stands inside with his cap in his hands.

"Inquiry se aaya hoon, sahab. Kagaz — "

"Ye rahe."

Vihaan does not get up from the chair.

"Feed ka kagaz. Licence. Vans ka pass — dastakhat mera hai. Staff cards — chaalees naam, mere haath ke."

The man looks at him — and down at the folder — and takes it up.

"Gin leejiye," Vihaan says. "Yahin. Mere saamne."

The man counts — feed, licence, pass, cards — and nods.

"Bas — yehi chaar?"

"Yehi chaar," Vihaan says. "Jo maanga tha."

The man waits one breath — and does not ask again.

He signs the receipt — and the pen stops.

"Sahab — ek — "

"Kaho."

"Aap khud — kal — pesh honge?"

"Jab bulaaoge," Vihaan says. "Kagaz taiyaar milenge. Main bhi."

The man goes down with the folder under his arm.

Pinky stands in the doorway after him — and her eyes go to the drawer, shut, and back to her brother.

"Haath nahin lagaaya," she says. "Kisine bhi nahin."

"Jaanta hoon," he says.

He sits still in the chair.

Below, the car starts — and goes.

The room ticks round him.

His name has left the house — in his own hand, on their paper now.

The night-lamp burns on.

He lets it burn.
