# Chapter 13 — The Names on the Thali (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 5, "The Hunter Is Sent For" (working title from the track).*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

*The twelve seconds the season plays on every night after its first: the theme, then straight in (`canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` §I). The full forty seconds are written once, at the head of Chapter 1, and are not repeated below it.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## The Constable

The police van stands at the mouth of the lane with one door open and its light still going round, and it is not going round for anything.

Sheshnaag's hood is not shown, but the lane feels like a temple that has forgotten its god.

The constable has his notebook on the bonnet and his cap pushed back, and he has been writing for ten minutes.

"Us bheediye ne mujh pe hamla kiya kal raat."

Nandini says it for the third time, and her voice is steady now the way a voice goes steady after a night of not sleeping.

"Lekin phir uske baad kya hua, ye mujhe nahin pata.", the score sounding under the reporting (m00:53).

"Aapke bhai?"

"Doctors injection dekar gaye hain. Rohan ko abhi tak hosh nahin aaya."

Her hand goes to her own throat with fear.

"Aur meri behen Purvi kahan mil nahin rahi thi. Aur mummy papa ghar pe nahin hain. To please meri help kar dijiye.", the score sounding under the listing (m01:14).

"Mil gayi?" The constable looks up.

"Main yahin hoon."

Purvi stands at the edge of the road with a shawl over her salwar and her hair still wet from the tap, and her spectacles are in her hand and the frame of them is broken at one corner.

The constable looks at her for a while.

"Kisi bheediye ne hamla kiya hai — aap toh yehi keh rahi thi."

He puts the pen down.

"Lekin dekh ke lagta nahin ki aapko gambhir chot hain."

"Nahi."

"Kahan thi aap?"

"Idhar hi."

She points down the lane, at the broken brick and the scrub and the plot where the buildings stop.

"Giri thi."

The constable writes it.

Behind him, in the lane, a dog comes out of a doorway, and looks at the four of them, and goes back into the doorway.

"Aapka number mere paas hai."

He tears a page out and gives it to Nandini.

"Aas-paas ke area mein chhanbeen karke jaldi inform karte hain."

The van goes.

The light stops going round and the lane is only morning again.

## Five or Six Things

The side of the Arora house at eight in the morning, and the tap is still running where somebody left it, and Purvi turns it off, and stands with her hand on the tap.

A bucket stands under the tap with a mug in it, and the mug has a chip out of the rim.

A tray of tea things stands by the door of the house, and the tea in the cups has gone cold.

The washing on the line behind the house is still up from yesterday, and the night's damp is still in it.

Purvi remembers the running.

She remembers the dark going past her on both sides, and the shouting behind her, and her own voice going out ahead of her down the lane.

After that she remembers falling.

"Main bachi kaise — mujhe nahi pata."

She says it to the tap.

"Mujhe itna yaad hai ki main giri. Aur phir kuch ajeeb ajeeb cheezein chadhne lag gayi. Paanch chhe cheezein. Ek saath."

Purvi turns her own wrist over and looks at it.

There is nothing on it.

The ground where she woke is three feet from the tap and it is dry.

The rest of that yard is wet from the night and dark in a patch round the drain, and the patch she woke on is dry, and the dry patch is the size of a girl lying down.

She stands on it and misses it.

"Purvi!"

Her mother, from the kitchen door.

"Andar aa. Naashta thanda ho raha hai. Aur wo chashma kahan hai?"

"Toot gaya."

## What Ananta Said

The Arora house at nine in the morning, with the sun lying in one bar across the floor and the fan going over it.

Ananta sits on the edge of the cot with her feet not touching the floor, and her dupatta over her head, and her hand flat on her own stomach.

The glass of water on the stool beside the cot has not been touched, and the dust has settled in it.

"Us Ravish ke liye faltu itna royi."

Her eyes are dry.

"Itna pareshaan hui. Aur uske liye kya mila?"

"Didi—"

"Mere liye tum log ahmiyat rakhte ho." She says it plainly, with relief, to her sister, in the small room with the fan going.

"Bas. Ab main sirf tum logon ke baare mein—" Her hand is on her stomach.

"Sochungi. Aur apne hone wale bacche ke baare mein."

The fan turns.

The newspaper on the floor lifts at one corner and lies down again.

"Aur us aadmi ka naam is ghar mein nahin."

She gets up.

"Chai banegi?"

## The Sample

The animal's body is on a tarpaulin in the yard behind the Suri house in the morning sun, and the vet has come and gone, and there is a collar on the study desk with a brass plate on it and no name.

"Bhai sahab, doctor ka kehna hai ki iski body mein zeher tha."

The man keeps his eyes on the desk.

"Bahut zyada."

A steel tray stands on the ground with a knife on it, and the knife has been wiped and put down and left.

A bucket of water stands by the wall with a cloth over the top of it.

Parmeet turns the collar over with one finger.

"Johro toh un dono ladkiyon ko khatam karne gaya tha."

The second man says it to the room.

"Aur wapas nahi aaya. Matlab jisne johro ki jaan li hai wo ichchhadhari naagin se bhi takatwar hai."

"Ya phir wo dono ladkiyan ichchhadhari naagin hain."

Parmeet says it without looking up.

"Aur hamare paas ek hi bheji hui jaanwar thi."

Nobody in that room says anything.

"Mahasapere ke paas jao — zeher ka sample le kar."

He puts the collar down.

"Humein jaanna hai kaun hai hamara naya zehreela dushman. Ichchhadhari naagin, ya koi aur."

"Aur agar wo sach mein ichchhadhari naagin hui—"

"To phir duniya dekhegi."

Parmeet stands.

"Ki maut ka khel Parmeet Suri kaise khelta hai.", the score sounding under the vowing (m04:24).

The collar sits on the desk with its brass plate turned up, and the plate catches the tube light once as the door closes.

## Organic Mehendi

The front room of the Suri house at ten in the morning, with the light of the windows on the cushions, and two women on the sofa with their hands out and a girl on a stool between them with a cone.

A tray of mehendi cones stands on the table with one of them split and leaking onto the tray.

"Mujhe hamesha se organic mehendi chahiye thi." Tina turns her hand over.

"Waise ye mehendi lag to badi kamaal ki—" She holds her hand to the light.

"Rahi hai. Dekhiye, kaise lagate hi rang chhod gaya."

The dish of bangles on the floor by the girl's stool has half of them still in their wrappers.

The tin of powder is open on the arm of the sofa with its lid lying on the cushion.

"Haath dhokar aaoon?"

The smell of the mehendi is in the whole room and it is stronger than the powder.

"Haan."

Tina waits until the girl has gone, and then says it to Roma, in a voice pitched for the room.

"You know, tumhare dad ko tumhari stepmother se bada pyaar hai."

"Haan."

"Isliye to wo mujhe batati rehti hai ki meri shaadi pe wo kab, kahan, aur kitne paise kharch kar rahi hai." Her chin comes up.

"Jabki saare paise mere dad ke hi hain." Her voice goes thin.

"And you won't believe it —" She looks at Roma.

"Meri shaadi pe har chhote bade kharch karne se—" Roma says nothing.

"Pehle uski permission mujhe leni padti hai.", the score sounding under the resenting (m05:21).

"I hate her."

"I hate her."

Tina puts her chin back down.

"Dekho, meri mehendi pe bhi comment karengi. Ye design purana hai, ye design mehnga hai."

Roma laughs, and then her phone goes, and she looks at it, and her face changes.

"Kya?"

"Kuch nahin. Office."

Roma puts the phone face down on the cushion.

"Purvi ka message hai. Wo poochh rahi hai ki aaj chhutti milegi ya nahin."

"Hai na?"

Tina is looking at her own palm.

"Uska boss to tum ho."

"Uska boss Aryaman hai."

Roma's hand closes.

"Main sirf uske saath kaam karti hoon."

The girl comes back with the water and the room goes back to being a room with two brides in it and a wedding in two days.

## Kumbh

The lawn is being dressed for a sangeet that is now two days early, and the men on the ladders are working in the dark with a torch propped on a chair.

A drum of cable stands under the ladder with its rope round it, and the rope goes up over the rung.

A sheet with the mandap plan on it lies on a chair with a brick on top of it.

Parmeet walks the length of it with his hands behind his back and his son beside him.

"Kumbh ke plan ke baare mein — Pasha se baat hui?"

"Phone aa chuke hain — teen baar."

Vihaan has his hands in his pockets, hand steady, and he smiles small and proud at the phones, the smile of a man who has made himself necessary.

"Poochh rahe hain ki hum taiyar hain ki nahin."

"Poori tarah se taiyar ho tum?"

"Sau percent."

He stops at the end of the lawn where the marigold goes up the veranda post in a spiral.

"Unke paas land ka paper nahin hai. Unke paas sarkar ka muh hai. Aur hamare paas dono."

Parmeet's face does the thing it does when a figure comes out right.

"Tumhe pata hai ye Kumbh India ka number one tourist destination banne wala hai."

He turns and walks back.

"Duniya bhar ke rais, ministers, businessmen, global icons — sab yahan aane wale hain. Aur jab wo aayenge na, unke desh ke naam ki goonj duniya bhar mein goonjegi.", the score sounding twice over the boasting (m06:24 x2 as one).

"Aur unke saath unke camera aayenge." Vihaan says it to the marigold, smiling small and sharp at the spiral of it, "Jo humare ghar ke andar bhi jhaankenge."

Parmeet stops walking.

He looks at him in the torchlight a moment longer than the sentence needs.

"Chinta mat karo."

He puts his hand on his son's shoulder and takes it off again.

"Ghar ke andar jo hai, wo andar hi rahega."

Above them the man on the ladder ties the last knot and the whole spiral of marigold comes alive in the wind and settles.

## The Door in the Passage

The passage on the first floor of the Suri house at noon, with the sun coming in at the far end of it and the doors on both sides shut.

The shouting starts in the first-floor passage with the band still tuning on the lawn.

"Kholo darwaza! Samir, teri himmat kaise hui meri biwi ke saath?"

The guests in the hall put their glasses down and turn their heads the way guests turn their heads — not towards the noise, but away from it, so that they are not seen to have heard.

Vihaan is up the stairs four at a time.

The passage is four feet wide and there are six people in it. A man in a black waistcoat with his fist on a door and his face gone the colour of old brick with anger.

His wife's sari is on the floor inside the room. A man called Samir stands in the middle of the room with his shirt half buttoned and says "Kuch bhi to nahin" to nobody in particular.

"Samir." Vihaan says it from the top of the stairs, and he says it at a volume that carries no further than the passage.

"Neeche jao. Abhi.", one low string under the sending-down.

"Vihaan, main—"

"Neeche. Bar mein baitho. Main aata hoon."

Samir goes. Vihaan smiles small and cold at his back, the smile of a man who has already decided what he will do with him.

The husband turns round with his fist still up, and Vihaan puts his own body between that fist and the stairs, and takes the man's elbow, and walks him, talking, to the small room at the end where the family keeps the loudspeaker equipment.

"Uncle. Andar. Baithiye. Paani laata hoon."

"Wo meri biwi—"

"Main jaanta hoon, uncle. Andar."

By the time Parmeet comes up the stairs, the passage is empty and the guests are looking at the band.

Parmeet shuts the door of the small room behind himself and stands with his back to it.

"Parmeet."

Rishabh has his hands on his knees.

"Pachaas saal ki dosti hai hamari. Aur ye baat bahar tak jayegi.", the score sounding under the warning (m08:19).

"Rishabh—"

"Main jo kehta hoon wo karta hoon. Aur main ye baat bahar le jaunga. Sabko bataunga. Tere plan ki saari pol khol dunga." He is breathing hard.

"Teri nayi biwi ke peeche tu pagal hai." He allows that much.

"Theek hai. Lekin agar isne gusse mein kisi ko—" Spit flies.

"Bata diya ki hum kahan se aaye hain." He points.

"Ki pachees saal se hum yahan reh rahe hain." The room is quiet.

"Aur doosre desh se hain hum —" He is shouting now.

"To poora plan flop hamara." Nobody answers.

"Aur hum khatre mein aa jayenge.", the score sounding twice over the threatening (m08:59 x2 as one).

The room is quiet.

Outside, the band begins.

"Kya hai yaar."

Parmeet's voice comes out soft and easy and it is the softness that is frightening.

"Itni purani dosti hai hamari."

"Jhooth bolega to pachhtayega."

Parmeet looks at Rishabh for a long moment.

Then he opens the door and calls down the stairs.

"Vihaan. Neeche se Samir ko bhejo. Aur Suvarna ji ko bhi."

## The Girl Who Is Sent For

The passage on the first floor of the Suri house at noon, with the sun coming in at the end of it and the chairs along the wall.

"Uncle, uncle."

Parmeet has his arm round the man's shoulders and is walking him back into the passage.

"Aap jaise soch rahe ho, bilkul waisa nahin hai."

"To kya hai?"

"Girlfriend hai uski."

Parmeet says it with his hand open.

"Wo kisi aur se pyaar karta hai. Deewanon ki tarah. Isne meri naak mein dam kar diya hai."

"Girlfriend?"

The passage has two chairs against the wall where the guests have been sitting out the noise, and one of them is still warm.

"Haan. Office ki ek ladki hai."

He turns to his son, who is standing at the head of the stairs.

"Use yahan bulao. Main use milunga — tabhi maanunga.", the score sounding under the inventing (m09:40).

A fan stands at the end of the passage with its cable taped down along the skirting.

Vihaan goes down the stairs two at a time.

At the bottom, in the hall, the woman who came to the door with her father is standing with her dupatta on her shoulder and her hand on a chair back, and she is looking up at the passage with everybody else.

"Ananta madam?" he asks.

"Haan?"

"Parmeet sir aapse milna chahte hain. Abhi."

Her father is a big man in a white shirt with his spectacles in his hand and his sandals still dusty from the road.

He puts his spectacles on.

"Main bhi chalta hoon."

They go up.

Ananta's father is standing in the passage with his spectacles in his hand, and he looks at the girl in the doorway, and he looks at Parmeet.

"Ye hai wo ladki?"

"Ye hai wo ladki."

Parmeet's hand is out.

"Ananta. Hamare Ravish ki dost."

Ananta's father stops in the middle of the passage.

"Parmeet sahab."

His voice is polite and it is not quiet.

"Aapke bete ne kal jo kiya meri beti ke saath—", the score sounding under the complaining (m10:26).

"Galtfahmi."

Parmeet has both hands up.

"Chhoti si galatfahmi ho gayi thi. Mujhe laga hamare bacche hamari zid ke saamne jhuk jaate hain. Lekin nahi. Aaj ki generation jhukti nahin hai.", the score sounding under the conceding (m10:56).

"Aap mera mazaak uda rahe hain."

"Main aapko apna samdhi bana raha hoon."

Parmeet turns and calls down the stairs, and his voice goes over the band.

"Ravish! Idhar aa. Idhar."

Ravish comes up.

He has a glass in his hand and he puts it on the windowsill before he comes.

"Inke saamne sach-sach bolna."

Parmeet's hand is on his son's back.

"Tune kal mujhe kya bola tha?", the score sounding under the asking (m11:23).

Ravish looks at Ananta, and Ananta looks at the floor.

"Anu se pyaar karta hoon main." He says it to the passage.

"Sachcha pyaar karta hoon." Then, because his father's hand is still on his back, he says the rest of it.

"Aur wo pregnant hai. Aur wo baccha mera hi hai.", the score sounding twice over the swearing (m11:23 x2 as one).

Gurinder Arora takes a step back into the wall.

Ananta's father takes his spectacles off.

"Ananta."

His voice is very low.

"Ye sach hai?"

"Papa."

She has her hand on her own stomach now, in front of all of them, with shame.

"I am really sorry. Mujhe laga nahin tha ki aap allow karenge."

"Tumne mujhe subah bataya bhi nahin tha."

"Ravish."

Parmeet is already talking over the top of it, into the passage, into the house.

"Ravish ne subah mujhe bataya. Aur main maan gaya.", the score sounding under the agreeing (m12:08). He turns to Gurinder with his hands out.

"Dekhiye, Ravish aapki beti se sachcha pyaar karta hai. Isi liye to do din mein shaadi karne ko taiyar ho gaya."

"Do din?"

"Kal sangeet aur mehendi."

Parmeet has his palm flat.

"Aur parson shaadi. Usi mandap mein, Karan ke saath. Ek ki jagah do-do shaadiyan ho jayengi.", the score sounding under the fixing (m12:08).

Ananta's father looks at his daughter.

Then he looks at Parmeet's hand, which is still out.

"Bacchon ke pyaar ke beech mein nahin aana chahiye,"

Parmeet says.

"Aaj Suri parivaar mein double khushi hai."

He takes his own phone out and calls down to the hall for mithai, the score sounding under the celebrating (m12:47).

Ananta stands in the passage with her hand on her stomach and her eyes wet and her mouth open on the beginning of something, and her father puts his hand on the back of her head, once, the way a father does.

"Chal."

He is not looking at her.

"Ghar chalein."

### Continuity and source note

This chapter is **Episode 5**, in the episode's own order: the constable at the mouth of the lane and the girl who says she fell; the animal's body with poison in it and the order that sends a sample to the Mahasapera; the Kumbh and the two men who want it; the locked door in the first-floor passage and the secret that nearly comes out of it; the girl who is sent for and the two weddings fixed in one breath; the twelve kilos of silver with the old names on it; the car and the eyelash; the temple, the crystal and the law that a naagin who loves a man costs that man his life; the nine o'clock floor and its specialist; the sisters on the floor of the front room; and the hunter who comes to the door at nine at night. Beats **B01 to B38** of `canon/E05_BEATS.md`.

**Why the ledger came before the prose, and what it cost.** Episode 5 is the first episode in this workshop cut from the caption track **without a recap beside it** — every line above is the track's, and the track names almost nobody. So the ledger was written first, with the six rulings in §2 of it made and written down before a word of prose, because an attribution error cannot be caught after the scene reads well.

**The readings this chapter declares**

1. **The Kumbh answer is Vihaan's.** The track gives Parmeet the question about Pasha, the three calls and the readiness, and gives the answering voice no name. In this serial the family's London end is his, so the voice is his, and it is his one scene with his father in this episode — and the last thing he says in it is the only thing in the scene that his father does not want to hear: *"Unke saath unke camera aayenge"* — and what those cameras will look at inside the house.
2. **The man the whole world wants is never named.** Purvi's confession at the end of the sisters' scene is canon's own, and canon's referent is Aryaman. Here she comes to love Vihaan, and the line's own wording — *jise poori duniya pasand karti hai* — is the way this adaptation has framed him since his arrival, so the line stays and the name stays out. Nothing on this page says whose face is in her head, and the page does not grade her.
3. **The car, the eyelash and the contempt for love are Aryaman's, and are played as canon plays them.** A boss drives his assistant to work, is rude about love, and does not touch her. Her hand stops two inches short of his face and comes back, and the eyelash never leaves his cheek — the wish is made on nothing, and he makes it worth nothing. That is the scene's whole irony and the page does not add to it.
4. **The locked washroom is the wrong face, and nobody believes her.** Roma says she was locked in while somebody was on the floor; Aryaman says she was beside him the whole time; the floor decides she is joking. It is canon's own grain, and it is the preparation that the wedding night will need (`SITUATIONS.md` §11). The page does not underline it, and Roma herself does not understand it.
5. **The names on the thali are canon's, and so is Gurinder's memory of Attari.** The page adds only this: he says the sentence about the infiltrators **to an empty room**, and his wife has already gone out. Nobody in this adaptation hears the accusation in Episode 5 except the audience.
6. **Ananta's happiness is written straight.** *"Wedding dress jaisi sochi thi wo ho na ho… sirf matter ye karta hai ki meri shaadi usse ho rahi hai jisse main pyaar karti hoon."* The room knows what Ravish is. She does not, and the page does not let the narration know it for her either.
7. **Uttara's law is canon's and it is the season's spine:** *insaan se pyaar karne ka paap kisi naagin ko baksha nahin gaya*, and *us maut ki keemat un ladkon ko apni jaan deke chukani padi*. **Ratiq** is named once, as the track names him, and nothing about him is invented.
8. **The specialist is Aryaman, and the enemy teaches the audience how to kill her.** The spine, the eye that keeps a face, the Naagmani and the born ichchhadhari's twenty-fourth birthday are all said on the floor of her own channel by a man who does not know he is describing the girl with the printout in her hand.
9. **The clock lives in Part 2, though its cue lives in Part 1's window.** The track says the Ardra weather and the forty-eight-to-seventy-two-hours clock early and indoors (02:21); Bharani's question and the keemat come late (35:29–36:13). The page says all four once, together, at the Naag Mandir at eleven in the morning — the clock first and the law after it — set at eleven in the morning, between the morning's scenes and the night's. Nothing is said twice, nothing is dropped, and no beat of either half is omitted.
10. **One beat is not staged, and fourteen lines are trimmed inside staged scenes — all owned here.** B30 (the clothes: sherwanis, the same-team/ishq talk, the support/dimaag warning) has no scene: its report duplicates the fix Part 1 shows directly (l.300-324); its texture is wedding-prep the List, the Notice and the Door already carry; its scandal-talk recaps the passage scene already on the page; and its remaining mouths are unnamed round an incomplete referent (Mr. ki biwi — which Mr., the track never says) with no recap to rule from — E05's ledger-first law forbids inventing them. The 02:21 wolf tail (fifteen against twenty-four, the last fight) is unbeaten in the ledger and withheld with the keeper's history (`S-20`). Trimmed where each scene's beat stands without it: the introduction ceremony (10:26–12:08: minister, milvate, dharampatni — the men simply present, Swarna summoned-not-seen), B19's *pregnant* (the hand on the stomach stays, l.310), the glad/right-track reaction (13:35 — the fixing itself is staged in Part 1, l.318), the reception logistics (14:17), the rishtedaar/fayda tease (14:32), the chupchaap opener (16:31), the newsroom gossip (19:11, 19:40, 20:14), the respect/leave tail (26:35), the maar-diya speculation (29:50 — cut by his own *Main kuch nahin keh raha*), the saas-bahu tail (30:25), and the Lucknow friends (32:24).
11. **The plan-threat is Rishabh's.** The track gives the 08:59 threat no mouth (*Main jo kehta hoon wo karta hoon… bahar le jaunga*), and the ledger's B15 calls it the husband's — but the threat is the plan (twenty-five years, another country), and an outsider husband cannot threaten what he does not know. The page rules the voice Rishabh's: Parmeet's friend, track-named at 12:08 (*mere friend Rishabh*), one of the two who crossed with him, and the only man in the small room with the knowledge to spend. The husband keeps no name of his own (ruling 5); Vihaan's *Uncle* is an address, not an identity.

**Where the episode is not certain, and this chapter does not invent**

- **Samir, and the wife.** The track leaves both half-named and the woman entirely off it. The page uses *Samir*, keeps the wife off the page, and gives the wronged husband no name of his own.
- **What the poison in the animal means.** The men conclude that the killer is stronger than an ichchhadhari naagin. The page records the conclusion and does not confirm it.
- **Who locked the washroom.** Not answered. `S-16` decides nothing here.
- **What the Mahasapera is, and what she carries.** The track gives a woman, a case and a sample. The page gives the door, the light, the shape under the cloth and *"been"* nowhere in her mouth.
- **Whether Purvi knows anything.** She remembers falling and five or six things climbing on her, and that is the whole of what she has. `S-17` is untouched.
