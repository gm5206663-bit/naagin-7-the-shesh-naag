# Chapter 32 — The Guest (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 21, 7 March 2026.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Lead

The night has thinned to its last hour, and one lamp burns on the terrace where the two of them stand.

"Pata chal jaata ki Radhika ne Aryaman se shaadi kyon nahin ki."

Ahana has said it before, and says it again.

"Bharani — mere dimaag se ye baat ja hi nahin pa rahi hai. Kuch aur samajh hi nahin aa raha."

"Isiliye maine pehle hi pata kar liya tha."

Bharani does not tease her.

"Radhika roz ek jagah jaati hai — Paradise Café. Shayad wahan tumhaare sawaalon ke jawaab mil jaayein."

She is impatient to be gone — halfway down the road already.

Her ring turns once on her finger.

Below, a bell counts the hour for no one.

"Par ek vaada tumhein mujhse karna padega."

Bharani holds her eyes.

"Jaise hi tumhein ye pata chal jaayega — ki usne shaadi kyon nahin ki — tum apni saari uljhanein chhod ke mere paas aaogi. Aur meri uljhan suljhaane mein meri madad karogi."

"Ismein koi shak nahin hai, Bharani."

Ahana lifts her chin.

"Desh mere liye pehle hai. Aur mujhe Radhika aur Aryaman ke baare mein isliye bhi jaanna zaroori hai — kyonki wo mera kaam bhi hai."

First.

The country first — before the house, before everything in it.

The vow stands between them, and neither looks away.

Below, the house sleeps through it all.

The lamp stands straight up, and stays standing.

## The Telling

Dawn stands grey at their window, and he is awake at the desk with the drawer open.

She comes in with the night still on her, and stops at what his hands are doing — file after file laid out on the desk in a row, and none of them shut.

"Baitho." Vihaan does not look up.

"Aaj sab khol raha hoon."

She sits on the bed's edge, and waits.

The room ticks.

The grey thins at the glass.

"London."

He lays the first paper flat.

"Terah ki umar mein bheja gaya tha — padhne. School, phir degree. Baaees ka tha jab London end sambhaala — office, property, idhar ka paisa udhar, channel ka foreign side. Do dost, ek flat, ek sair, ek akhbaar — kagaz wala."

"Chhe mahine."

His thumb finds the next file's edge.

"Buniyaad pehle se bani thi — kaam maine kiya. Din-raat. Wo dard tum jaanti ho — uske baad jo bacha tha, wo kaam tha."

He opens the drawer wider, and shows her without handing her — shares ke kagaz, company ke, property ke, naamon mein jo Suri nahin kehte.

"Paper mein hai sab. Cash mein nahin. Yehi aadat hai meri — dikhaava nahin."

Her eyes move over the papers, and do the arithmetic he will never say aloud, and say nothing of the total.

"Ye feed ka kagaz hai — Kumbh ka. Pandrah din."

He touches it once.

"Aur ye licence — jis raat Sangam pe truck, generator, battiyaan khadi ho sakti hain — wo isi kagaz pe. Ye mere paas hai."

"Min-ji." He says the name flat, like an entry.

"Partner hai — London end mein. College se jaanta hoon use — barson se. Hoshiyaar hai — aur mujhe hansa sakti hai, jo shayad hi koi kar sakta hai."

He looks at her at last.

"Wo mujhse pyaar karti hai. Barson se. Main usse nahin karta — kabhi nahin kiya. Ye keh raha hoon taaki baad mein koi kagaz tumhein ye na bataaye."

She does not move.

The name settles between them like one more paper.

Her fingers tighten once on the folded sheet, and loosen.

It is jealousy, and it surprises her more than the papers did.

"Teen vans."

He lays the pass down.

"Kumbh ka raasta — generator van jo kabhi chali nahin. Dastakhat mere hain. Pass pe bhi — call bhi meri."

"Police ki file mein staff card hai — chaalis aadmiyon ke naam, mere haath ke chhape."

He counts it off.

"Tahkhana — khaali kamre ki tasveeren — card meri jeb mein aaya tha, ab drawer mein hai."

"Ek tape hai." His voice does not change.

"Kumbh ki raat ka — generator van ka. Chal raha tha subah se. Dekha nahin kabhi — na maine, na kisi ne. Yehi pada hai."

Something stops in her face for half a breath — the night of that battle, which no camera caught, lying in her husband's drawer on a tape nobody watched — and she swallows it whole, and asks nothing.

"Bas."

He shuts the drawer — his hand slow on it, the way a man shuts a thing he has never opened for anyone.

"Sab."

She gets up, and crosses to him, and covers his slow hand with hers on the shut drawer.

He turns his hand over under hers, and holds it a breath, and lets go.

His low string comes in under the ticking.

Over it, for the one breath their hands touch — the been's second phrase.

A woman's voice, one line, in Hindi.

Woh Do Log.

It goes out on the string it came in on.

"Aaj ghar se kaam karoonga." Vihaan turns to the window's grey.

"Kagaz nikaalne hain — apne."

The question with an address stands up in the room between them — and he does not ask it.

Not this dawn.

His everything lies open; her nothing stays shut; and he asks for nothing.

She looks at his back a moment longer than she should.

Below, a door opens.

The house is waking.

At the window, the grey turns gold.

## The Mark

The morning comes up over the house, and the corridor smells of tea, and a crow calls once from the roof.

Pinky sees it first — over Ahana's collar, small and dark, where no dupatta covers — and her shriek could wake the lane.

"Hai — bhabhi! Ye — ye kya hai!"

Pinky claps both hands to her mouth, too late by a mile.

"Koi kehta hai pehli subah — arre wah!"

Ahana's hand flies to her neck — a breath too late, with the whole corridor watching.

Halfway down, a maid stops with the tea tray, and stares.

"Naashta thanda ho raha hai, Pinky." Vihaan comes up behind his wife with his file under his arm, dry as ever, and does not look at the mark at all.

"Thanda naashta — paap hai."

"Paap!"

Pinky laughs till she shakes.

"Bhai — tum!"

Vikram stands in his doorway down the corridor, and sees the mark, and says nothing at all — and goes back inside, and shuts his door without a sound.

Jealousy shuts the door.

The corridor goes back to its tea and its morning.

Vihaan watches the door shut.

His face does not change.

Only his thumb moves once, slow, along the file's edge.

## The Café

Paradise Café holds its morning crowd. Radhika sits over cold coffee with her laptop shut, and the road goes past the glass.

"One cappuccino."

Ahana sits down across from her.

"Make that two, please."

"Ahana — tum yahan kya kar rahi ho?"

Radhika stares.

"Jaasoosi?"

"Duniya ki sabse badi jaasoos — "

Ahana smiles.

"Biwiyan hi hoti hain na. Matlab tum nahin samjhogi — jaane do."

The café murmurs round them — a cup set down, a spoon, somebody's laugh.

Under it all, the score comes up.

At the next table, a woman looks up once, and looks away.

"Waise — cute photo thi."

Ahana does not smile now.

"Tum aur Aryaman — bilkul made for each other lagte the."

"Ahana — tum jaisa soch rahi ho, aisa kuch bhi nahin hai."

Radhika looks away.

"Tum jaanti ho — Aryaman aur mere beech — this is nothing."

"Wah — coffee ki bhi same choice."

Ahana leans in.

"Dekho — main jaanti hoon mere devar ab bhi tumse pyaar karte hain. Aur clearly tum bhi unhein bhooli nahin ho. To shaadi se peeche hatne ka kaaran kya tha, Radhika?"

"Ahana — wo jo bhi tha, wo past tha."

Radhika shakes her head.

"Mujhe nahin lagta wo jaanna zaroori hai."

"Zaroori hai."

Ahana does not blink.

"Mere liye jaanna zaroori hai. Kyonki tumhara past ab is ghar ka present hai — aur mujhe jaanna hai ki tum jaisi itni achchi ladki ne use chhoda kyon? Kahi Aryaman mein hi koi problem?"

"Nahin nahin, Ahana."

Radhika catches her hand.

"Wo to bahut achche hain — itne achche ki aaj ke zamaane mein dhoondne se bhi na milein. Baahar se thode kathor lagte hain — dil ke bahut pyaare. Itne achche — ki kisi ko pyaar ho jaaye."

"I know."

Ahana looks down.

"Sorry."

The strings go out.

Radhika's hands go still round the cup.

"Aryaman mein koi kami nahin thi, Ahana."

Radhika's voice drops.

"Problem thi — to mere dad ko. Police officer the wo."

"Unhein — unhein kya, Radhika?"

"Unhein lagta tha — ki Aryaman ke parivaar waale galat kaamon mein involved hain."

Radhika's hands shake round the cup.

"Mujhe nahin pata is baat mein kitni sacchaai hai. Lekin mere dad ki aakhri din tak ye shart thi — ki main Aryaman se shaadi na karoon."

"Wo log India se nahin hain."

Her father's voice comes back to her across the table.

"Mere paas saare saboot hain. Tum Aryaman se shaadi nahin kar sakti. Aur agar tumne kuch bhi kiya — to main khud ko kuch kar loonga."

"Aur isiliye — na chaahte hue bhi — mujhe Aryaman ka dil todna pada."

A tear falls into the cold coffee.

"Hum saath nahin ho sakte, Aryaman. I'm sorry."

It is grief — old, and fresh as this morning.

A crow lands on the glass front, looks in once, and drops away.

"Jis din maine apne dad ko khoya — "

Radhika wipes her face.

"Us din unki shart bhi toot gayi. Lekin der ho chuki thi — jo haath maine chhoda tha, wo dobaara thaamne ke liye nahin chhoda tha."

She stands, and picks up her bag, and smiles through the rest of it.

"Khair — jo hua, so hua. Anyway — nice talking to you."

The chair scrapes.

The bell over the door rings her out.

Ahana sits alone over the two coffees long after Radhika has gone.

Yaani Aryaman ka pyaar — uske parivaar ke gunaahon ki bali chadh gaya.

She turns it over once more, and files it where mission-things go — a dead officer's proof against this house, somewhere in the world still.

Outside, the road keeps going past the glass.

## The Shares

The house keeps its afternoon behind shut doors, and three voices run low in Vikram's room.

"O — well played, Vikram bhai."

The first voice laughs.

"Shares. Waise asli game to kal Aryaman ke saath hoga — jab hum apne hisse ke saare shares Vikram ke naam kar denge — aur us Aryaman ko is channel se nikaal denge."

"Phir sab kuch mera — aur Purav ka."

Vikram's voice, sure of itself.

A glass shifts on the table inside — a toast drunk in the dark.

"In fact — agar wo Roma mar gayi hoti na — "

the second voice, Karan's, quick with greed.

"To uske hisse ke shares bhi mere paas aa jaate — matlab aur zyaada power."

"Chill, bro."

Vikram laughs.

"Tu kya marne-maarne ki baatein kar raha hai? Tu Roma ki baat kar raha hai? Roma ke hisse ke paison se kahi zyaada paisa Aryaman ke hisse mein hai. Bas humein ensure karna hai — ki end tak uske paas kuch na bache."

"Par haan — ek katora zaroor de denge."

The first voice again.

"Bheekh maangne mein kaam aayega use. Nice one."

Under the door, low strings come up — pleased with themselves.

Ahana's breath does not change in the corridor.

Down the corridor, a clock strikes the hour for no one.

"Bhai ko kya jhatka lagega na."

Karan breathes it like wine.

"Jis news channel ko usne poore khoon-paseene se banaaya — usi channel pe news chalegi — Aryaman Suri — out. Jisne sab kuch banaaya — wo apna kuch nahin bacha paaya."

Out.

The word hangs in the corridor.

Outside the door that stands a finger ajar, Ahana stands very still in the corridor, and hears it all.

## The Corner

The afternoon corridor lies empty behind her, and the lamp at its turn gutters once for no reason.

Vikram comes out of his room, and stops at the sight of her.

"Ahana."

He smiles, and it does not reach his eyes.

"Sun liya?"

"Vikram — tum Aryaman se chutkaara paana chahte ho."

She says it flat, and does not wait for an answer.

"Ek time aisa tha — ki main ye sapne mein bhi nahin soch sakti thi."

"Bhai maanta tha use."

Vikram steps closer.

"Lekin jo uske ghar ne kiya — meri dulhan chheen ke — uski keemat use chukaani padegi. Aur uske bhai-bandhu — sab mere saath hain. Sab. Kisi ko uski koi parwah nahin hai."

He is close enough for her to hear him breathe.

"Main bahut thak gayi hoon."

Ahana turns.

"Thoda aaraam karna hai."

"Main jaanta hoon, Ahana."

Vikram catches her wrist.

"Tum bhi Aryaman se utni hi nafrat karti ho jitni main. But I promise you — ek baar uske company ke shares chheen ke use channel se nikaal do — phir tumhein bhi us Vihaan se chheen loonga. I swear."

He means it.

His fingers close round the wrist, over her bangles.

She is afraid — for one breath, only afraid.

Footsteps stop at the corridor's turn.

"Chhodo use." Vihaan stands at the corridor's turn with his file still under his arm, and his voice has gone down, not up.

"Abhi."

One low string is struck, and left hanging.

Vikram lets go of her wrist.

Slowly.

"Vihaan — tu — "

Vihaan steps in, and slaps him — one open-handed slap, with the file still under his other arm.

The crack of it goes down the empty corridor.

Vikram's head turns with it.

Slowly, he puts his hand to his cheek — and holds Vihaan's eyes, and walks past them both without another word.

Vihaan does not watch him go.

The file stays under Vihaan's arm through all of it.

Ahana's hand goes to her wrist, to the place of Vikram's fingers.

She looks at her husband — one look — and walks past him to their room, and shuts the door.

At the turn, the lamp burns straight again.

## The Huddle

The study holds its evening lamp, and the house has gone quiet floor by floor.

"Bolo." Vihaan shuts the study door.

"Sab."

"Kal meeting hai."

Ahana does not sit.

"Teenon apne shares Vikram ke naam karenge. Uske baad veto uske paas — game ek tarfa. Vikram aur Purav mil ke — company, channel — sab khatm."

Vihaan opens his file — the shareholding pattern, in his own hand — and runs one finger down it, and stops.

"Veto kal ke baad Vikram ke paas." He says it flat. "Haan. Ye sahi padh rahi ho."

"To roko ise."

She steps closer, desperate.

"Tum rok sakte ho — kagaz tumhaare paas — "

"Nahin."

He shuts the file.

"Rok nahin sakta. Kagaz unka hai — hisse unke hain — dastakhat unke honge. Qanoon ke saamne ye dhokha nahin — haq hai. Galat haq — par haq."

One low string is struck under the lamp, and left hanging.

The lamp gutters once, and steadies.

The lamp burns between them, and neither looks away.

The file lies shut between them.

"Use bata do." Vihaan looks at the shut file, not at her.

"Aryaman ko. Sab. Ladna use hai — faisla uska hai. Mera kaam tha ye padhna — padh liya."

He files the paper shut with the rest, and says nothing of the filing — and the price sits in the room with them both like a third person: a brother walking into a fall no paper can stop.

## The Photograph

The sitting room keeps its evening lamps low, and Aryaman sits alone with the day's papers unread.

"Bhai."

Ahana stops in the doorway.

"Do minute?"

He looks up, surprised out of his tiredness.

"Bhabhi? Aao — baitho."

The day's papers lie unread on the low table, and the lamp throws their shadows long across the rug.

The curtain lifts once at the window, and falls back.

"Kal ek meeting hai."

She sits across from him, and does not waste a breath.

"Jahaan tumhaare saare cousins apne shares Vikram ke naam karne waale hain. Iska matlab veto Vikram ke paas chali jaayegi — matlab game ek tarfa ho jaayega. Aur agar aisa ho gaya na, Aryaman — to Vikram aur Purav dono mil ke — tumhaari company, tumhaara channel — aasaani se khatm kar sakte hain."

"Ye koi chhoti baat nahin hai."

She leans forward.

"Bahut badi baat hai. Tumhaari mehnat — tumhaara channel — sab mitti mein mila denge."

"Technically — "

He almost smiles.

"Tumhein to khush hona chaahiye. Tumhaara Vikram — tumhaare pati ke — "

He stops himself.

"Sorry. Bolo — kyon bata rahi ho?"

"Kyonki galat hai."

Ahana holds his eyes.

"Aur is ghar mein galat ke khilaaf bolna — ab mera bhi kaam hai. Isiliye keh rahi hoon — apne haq ke liye lado. Chhodna nahin — palatvaar karo."

"Kabhi nahin."

Aryaman looks away.

"Mere cousins mujh pe jitne vaar kar lein — main palat ke jawaab nahin doonga. That's from me. Unhein mere sheher chaahiye na? Le lein. Mujhe koi problem nahin hai."

Under it, one tasha knock — a decision, not a feeling.

"Mujhe problem hai."

Ahana stands.

"Tumhaare hisse mein — is ghar ka hissa bhi aata hai. To tum apna haq aise kaise chhod sakte ho — jab tumhein pata hai tumhaare saath galat ho raha hai — dhokha diya jaa raha hai? Lado kyon nahin? Itna mahaan banne ki kya zaroorat hai?"

"Honestly — "

She shakes her head.

"Saint Aryaman."

"Ahana."

He gets up, and goes to the window, and stands there a long moment.

"Jab hum kisi ke ghar mein mehmaan ban ke aate hain na — to hum wahan aaraam se thehar sakte hain. Ghoom-phir sakte hain — khaana kha sakte hain — chain ki neend so sakte hain. Lekin hum unse unki tijori ki chaabi nahin maang sakte. Aisa karna — ya karne ki sochna bhi — galat hai."

"Matlab — "

Ahana stares.

"Main kuch samjhi nahin. Matlab?"

"Matlab — "

Aryaman turns from the window.

"Main Parmeet ka saga beta nahin hoon."

The room goes very quiet.

No score comes up.

The silence is the cue — his face, her face, and the locked frame catching the lamp.

The lamp hums on the side table, and the locked frame catches its light on the shelf.

"Main unka godh-liya beta hoon."

He says it plainly.

"Haan. I am adopted. Isiliye, Ahana — yahan kisi cheez pe mera koi haq nahin hai. Haan — ye baat alag hai ki dad ne mujhe apni taraf se har haq diya hai. Mujhe kabhi kisi cheez ki kami mehsoos nahin hone di. Lekin kami to hai. Hai — aur hamesha rahegi."

He crosses to the shelf, and takes down a photograph in a locked frame, and holds it where she can see.

"Ye."

His voice goes soft.

"Ye mere real dad hain. Jaanti ho — ye ek scientist the. Bahut bade scientist. Poori duniya mein inka naam tha."

"Inki death ke baad mujhe adopt kar liya tha."

He wipes the glass with his thumb.

"Aur tab se — bachpan se — main inki ye photo cupboard mein lock rakhta hoon — taaki kisi ko pata na chale ki main inhein kitna miss karta hoon. Warna Parmeet dad ko bura lagega na."

"Arre — unhone to mujhe apne sage bete se zyaada pyaar kiya hai."

His voice breaks.

"Isiliye, Ahana — main apne bhaiyon ke liye — apne dad ke liye — kuch bhi kar sakta hoon. Mar bhi sakta hoon. Sach mein. You have no idea how much I owe them. Unka karz chukaane ke liye to mujhe doosra janm lena padega."

Tears stand in his eyes, and he does not wipe them, and does not look away from the photograph.

The house keeps its noise back from the door.

His thumb leaves a print on the wiped glass.

"Sorry."

He laughs once, wetly.

"Ye baatein to maine Radhika se bhi share nahin ki. Pata nahin tumse kyon — "

He stops.

"Ye baat — yahin reh jaaye, bhabhi. Please."

He puts the photograph back, and locks the frame, and walks out without another word — and Ahana sits alone in the lamplight with a dead scientist's face burned into her eyes.

## The Selfish

Karan's room smells of last night still, and Tina stands at the window with her back to him.

"Tina — time aa gaya hai."

Karan counts it off on his fingers.

"Main saara paisa lene waala hoon — aur ye sab kuch main Vikram ke naam pe daalne waala hoon. Vikram se meri baat ho gayi hai — kuch dinon baad wo mujhe mera share de dega."

"Par Aryaman to tumhaara bhai hai na."

Tina turns.

"Tumhein apni family se zara bhi pyaar nahin hai?"

"Tina — mujhe pyaar hai meri family se."

Karan shrugs.

"Lekin itna nahin — ki main apne baare mein na sochoon. Unka nuksaan hota hai to ho — mujhe apne faayde ke baare mein sochna hai."

His keys lie where he dropped them, beside an empty glass.

"Kabhi-kabhi na — mujhe lagta hai tum itne selfish ho."

Tina's voice shakes.

"Roma wahan train mein phansi hui thi — aur tum apne doston ke saath terrace pe drinks pee rahe the."

She is hurt, and it comes out as cruelty.

"Tina — I was tensed."

Karan spreads his hands.

"Aur tension mein drink karne ko party karna nahin kehte hain."

A bottle sweats on the table by the window — last night's, unfinished.

The curtain lifts once, and falls back.

"Kal ko agar tumhein kuch ho gaya na — "

Tina turns back to the window.

"To koi tumhein bachaane nahin aayega. Sab ke sab selfish hain."

A train hoots far off, going nowhere she knows.

"No — I don't think so."

Tina shakes her head.

"Aryaman aur Ahana aise log nahin lagte. Par mujhe pata nahin — aisa kyon lag raha hai — ki us din Ahana ne hi meri jaan bachaayi thi."

"Tina — tum kuch bhi sochti ho."

Karan laughs it off.

"Tum itne dinon baad apne friends ke saath party karne gayi thi — bas thodi drink zyaada ho gayi. That's it. Anyway — ye sab soch ke apna mood mat kharab karo."

"Mood sahi karne ka mere paas ek bahut achcha tareeka hai."

He steps closer.

"Try karo — "

"Yah nahin, Karan."

Tina moves past him to the door.

"Just stop it. I'm tired."

She goes out, and shuts the door, and stands a moment in the corridor alone.

The corridor takes her without a sound.

Mujhe aisa kyon lag raha hai — ki wo Ahana hi thi?

## Ramosh

The dark holds them both where no house ear can hear, the lamp behind them turned low, and Bharani's face is all mission now.

"Lagta hai aap Aryaman ka maamla sulajh ke aayi hain."

Bharani studies her.

"Haan — kuch had tak."

"Tum mujh pe hans rahi ho."

Ahana narrows her eyes.

"Nahin — bilkul bhi nahin."

Bharani shakes her head.

"Main kyon aap pe hansoongi? Main to bas chaah rahi hoon — ki Radhika aur Aryaman ka maamla jaldi se sulajh jaaye — taaki hum wo kar paayein jo desh ke liye zyaada zaroori hai."

"Main desh ko leke laaparwaah nahin hoon, Bharani."

Ahana's voice hardens.

"Nahin — main aisa bilkul bhi nahin keh rahi."

Bharani spreads her hands.

"Anant kul Bharat ka hamesha se sabse bada rakshak raha hai — aur aaj bhi humein wahi karna hai. Wo Changistani — jinhone Kumbh pe hamla kiya — Amarnath yaatra ko nishaana banaaya — wo yahan nahin rukenge."

Her bangles do not move while she speaks.

The moon stands white over the garden wall.

"Jaanti hoon."

Ahana looks out into the dark.

"Dushman desh ghaat laga ke baithe honge. Aur ye bhi jaanti hoon — ki wo do hamlon ke baad rukenge nahin. Wo aur bhi ghaatak hamle karenge. Aur unka khaas nishaana hoga — Ramosh."

The dark holds its breath with them — and under the word, a single string holds the threat-note.

"Ramosh?"

Bharani frowns.

"Ye kya hai?"

"Bharat ke chhe honhaar logon ki banaayi ek aisi missile — jo desh ke liye ek suraksha kavach hai."

Ahana counts it off.

"Hamaare dushman desh hazaaron missilon se bhi hum pe hamla karein na — to bhi wo use tehas-nahas kar dega. Ye dekho."

Under the count, the strings come up — the power, at full voice.

"Ye hain wo chhe log."

She holds the photographs where the lamplight finds them.

"Inmein se ek maara gaya, Bharani. Par baaki paanch — hamaari zimmedaari hain. Humein kisi bhi keemat pe inki jaan bachaani hai. Agla hamla in pe kabhi bhi ho sakta hai."

Five faces look up out of the lamplight.

"Magar humne to pehle bhi iski tasveeren dekhi thi."

Bharani bends closer.

"Jis ek insaan ne iski jaan li — wo insaan nahin tha."

Under it, the sting — one knock, and gone.

The photographs lie between them on the bench with their paper sleeves.

A night bird calls once, and does not call again.

"Wahi to samajh nahin aa raha."

Ahana lowers the photographs.

"Kyonki ek dushman to humein dikhaai de raha hai — Changistani — jo saamne se hamla kar raha hai. Lekin ek dushman jo humein dikhaai nahin de raha — jo andhere ki aad mein hamla kar raha hai."

"Wo naagin ka dushman hai."

Her voice drops lower still.
