# Chapter 16 — The Warrant (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 7, first part. The upload carries no title card; the ledger calls the chapter by the thing that comes up the drive at ten in the morning.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Wish

Half past nine in the morning, and the house has been up since five, and the sun is in the passage to the kitchen.

Sheshnaag's hood is not shown, but the house feels like a temple that has forgotten its god.

The women's room at the back of the Suri house has two beds and a mirror and a window that looks at the tent wall, and there is a sari laid out on one bed with its gold side up and a girl sitting on the other bed with her knees drawn in.

The passage to the kitchen has a bucket against the wall and the floor round it is wet.

"Purvi."

Ananta is in the doorway with her hair half done and the pin still in her hand.

"Yahi pasand hai teri," the score sounding under the liking (m00:36), "mujhe pata tha."

Purvi does not look up from the phone in her lap.

"Mat kar ye galti."

Ananta comes in and sits down on the edge of the bed, and the mattress goes down under her.

"Woh tera bhi dil tod dega — woh andar tak toota hua insaan hai."

"Di—"

"Kisi bhi ladki ke liye sahi nahi hai Aryaman."

She says it plainly, the way she says everything at half past nine.

"Main nahi chahti ki tu bhi us dard se guzre jis dard ne us ladke ko aisa bana diya — use nafrat hai pyaar se. Aur auraton se bhi."

The pin goes into her hair.

The mirror has both of them in it, one sitting and one standing.

"Agar meri shaadi wale din mujhe ek wish milti na aaj"—Ananta's hand comes down on her sister's knee—"to mere hisse ki saari khushiyan" She holds her sister's knee.

"tere hisse aa jaayein — yahi maangti main," one low string under the wishing.

"Di, aisa mat bol."

"Ki us Aryaman ke dil mein sirf nafrat hi hai — waise toh mujhe koi doubt nahi hai."

Ananta looks at the mirror and not at her.

"Lekin uske dil mein agar" The mirror fogs.

"itne se bhi gunjaish hai pyaar ki na," She looks away.

"to sirf tere liye ho woh."

"Meri bas yahi wish hai aaj meri shaadi wale din," the score sounding under the rising (m01:15).

She gets up.

"Ab chal. Mera mangalsutra dhoondh. Mummy ne kisi ko diya hai aur koi jaanta nahi kisko."

* * *
## "Ab Aapki Baari"

Downstairs, the hall has been swept and the chairs are in two rows and the fire is lit in its square of brass.

"Chalo guys."

Roma has the microphone and the plates are going round.

"Tina toh meri bhabhi ban chuki hai ab, officially."

The room claps.

Tina has her head down and her dupatta over her wrist and Karan has his hand on the back of her chair.

"Ab aapki baari."

Roma points the microphone at the far end of the row.

"Ravish bhai and Ananta — the bride and the groom, everybody."

Ananta is in the doorway with her sari held up in one hand, and she stops there, and Ravish gets up to come and get her, and the room laughs at him for being quick about it.

"Wah."

Roma turns round.

"Yeh sahi hai."

Tina laughs.

Vihaan stands at the edge of the hall by the fire, his hands in his pockets, and watches the room without moving.
* * *
## The Gate

At the gate at ten in the morning sun, Shyam has the book open on the flat of the wall and the lane behind the flower man's tempo is full.

The guard waits with his cap in his hand and does not speak until he is looked at.

"Chhote sahab."

The guard's cap is in his hand.

"Yeh log poochh rahe hain andar kaun hai."

Vihaan looks down the lane.

Four cars in since seven and two out. The flower man's tempo has blocked the mouth of the lane since eight, and the lane behind it has three cars in it now.

The third one is white and has a light on the roof that nobody has bothered to take off.

There is a fourth behind it with a grille across the back window.

"Kitni der se khadi hain?"

"Pandrah minute."

The guard shifts his weight.

"Woh doosri gaadi mein constable hain, sahab. Uniform mein."

"Naam poochha?"

"Poochhne ki himmat nahi hui."

He takes the pen out of the book's spine.

The book is a school register with a cloth spine, and it is there because of one night in the study, and because of five lines he wrote to Verma at two in the morning: every man who comes into this house, name, work, time, twenty-four hours.

Shyam keeps it.

Shyam has kept it every night since, and in it there are three names from four days ago at half past one in the morning that nobody in this house has explained to him.

"Naam poochh."

He writes the number of the white car on the day's page, and the time, and the number of men he can count in it.

"Aur gate band mat karna."

"Chhote sahab—"

"Band mat karna, Shyam."

He closes the book with the pen inside it.

"Jo darwaza band milta hai, woh todne layak ho jaata hai. Khula chhod do."

Vihaan walks back up the drive, and does not run, and goes straight through the hall where the fire is burning with the two of them at it.

"Papa." He says it at his father's shoulder, quietly.

"Gate pe police hai. Char gaadiyan. Do mein constable hain. Aur ek mein light hai."

Parmeet does not turn round.

"Char?"

"Do lane mein khadi hain. Ek andar aana chahti hai."

He keeps his voice where it is.

"Naam Inspector Dayal likha hai."

The fire goes on burning.

Ravish says something to the pandit and the pandit nods.

"Le aao."

Parmeet says it to the fire.

"Papa." Vihaan does not move.

"Woh warrant le kar aaye hain ya aapko bulane?"

Parmeet turns his head then, and looks at his son for a moment, and the look is the look he gives a bill he has not asked for.

"Le aao," he says again, and turns back to the fire.

* * *
## The Warrant

Inspector Dayal comes in through the hall in the light of the chandelier with his cap under his arm and two men behind him, and the music stops in the middle of a phrase because Vihaan is standing at the sound table with his hand on the fader.

"Parmeet Suri?"

The room turns round.

The white car at the gate has a driver in it with the engine running and the window down.

"Inspector sahab."

Parmeet's hands come up.

"Ruk jao. Aapka phone lagatar baj raha hai subah se. Ab aap khud hi aa gaye. Bataiye."

"Aapne phone kyun nahi uthaya?" Vihaan says it from the side, and it is not loud, and the whole hall hears it.

"Aath baje se lagatar. Aath. Nau. Sawa nau."

The inspector does not look at him.

A tray of tea stands against the wall with every cup on it cold.

"**Parmeet Suri, aapke khilaf arrest warrant hai. Aapko humare saath chalna hoga.**"

The hall makes one sound and then no sound at all.

"Kya?"

Parmeet's hands come down with anger.

"Aapko pata bhi hai aap kisse baat kar rahe hain? Tumhara dimaag theekane pe hai na? Kya bol rahe ho?"

"Aapke office par raid ki humne."

The inspector's voice is flat and unhurried.

"Kal raat, do baje. **Wahan se 200 kilo RDX mila. 12 AK-47. 20 hand grenade.** Yeh hathiyaar wahan kya kar rahe the?"

The hall is very quiet.

"Dad."

Aryaman is on his feet.

"Yeh office kya keh rahe hain?"

"Aap chup rahiye."

The inspector raises one hand without looking at him.

"Dad, aap kuch boliye."

Aryaman is at his father's shoulder now.

"Tauji, aap commissioner sahab ko phone kijiye—"

"**Uski koi zaroorat nahi.**"

Parmeet says it without turning his head.

"Aur bachchiyon ko ghar le jao — tum gaadi bulao."

"Dad—"

"Vihaan."

Parmeet finds him without looking for him.

"In ladkiyon ko apne ghar le jao — main sab sambhal lunga," the score sounding twice over the sending (m02:32 x2 as one).

"Ji."

"Teen minute."

Parmeet has already turned back to the inspector.

"Teen minute mein gaadiyan gate pe."

Vihaan turns and goes to call the cars.

* * *
## The Two Who Stay

The cars come up to the gate in the morning light one after another, and the children go out through the hall with their shoes in their hands.

Upstairs the bags are coming down, and Pinky has two of them against her hip and one on her shoulder and is telling a boy to be careful with the third.

A card slides out of a green handbag and lands face up on the landing.

Pinky puts the bags down and picks it up.

It is a sagai card with a gold border, and the two names on the front are her brother's and his bride's, and inside, on the second line, there is a third name in the same gold script.

**Purvi Arora.**

Pinky stands on the landing and looks at it for a while.

Then she puts it inside her blouse, and not back into the bag, and takes the bags up again.

"Ananta. Purvi."

Dimple has a shawl round her shoulders and her hand out.

"Chalo. Gaadi mein baith jao."

The inspector's hand comes out and stops them at the step.

"**Kahin nahi ja sakti aap dono behenein.**"

"Kya?"

Ananta's sari comes down out of her hand.

"Kyun?"

"**Kyunki complaint ki hai aapke papa ne hi.**"

Purvi's hand is on her sister's arm, and she is not looking at the inspector.

She is looking down the hall, past the fire, at Vihaan standing at the sound table with his hand still on the fader, and her hand is on her sister's arm.

Ananta's fingers close on her wrist.

"Purvi."

"Di."

"Maine tumse kaha tha aaj subah."

Ananta's voice is low and it does not shake.

"Yahi woh ghar hai — ab dekh. Aur tum usko dekh rahi ho."

Purvi's eyes come down to the floor and stay there.

The hall turns again, and this time it turns to Gurinder Arora in the cream kurta standing by the fire with his hands folded in front of him.

"Papa?"

Ananta's voice goes up.

"Aapne usi family ke against complaint kar di? Papa, meri shaadi hai aaj. Ravish se."

Gurinder does not answer her.

"Baith ke bhi to baat kar sakte the na hum."

Her sari is on the floor and she does not pick it up.

"Ek baar baith ke?"

"Bachcha sahi toh keh rahi hai."

Their mother is at the girl's shoulder with her hand up.

"Aur aapne inhi ke against complaint kar di — humari bachche ki shaadi hai. Kyun kar rahe hain aap aisa? Kyun? Boliye na."

The man by the fire says nothing.

"Papa, aap kuch bol kyun nahi rahe hain?"

Ananta is crying now and she does not wipe it.

"Batayiye na. Kyun kiya aapne aisa? Bolo na, Papa. Boliye na," the score sounding twice over the pleading (m03:04 x2 as one).

## "Kyunki Ye Log"

The hall at half past ten in the morning, with the chandelier on and the chairs pushed back against the walls.

"**Kyunki ye log woh nahi hain jo woh dikhte hain.**"

Gurinder says it to his daughter, and then he turns round and says it to the room, and his voice does not shake.

The register in the inspector's hand is open to a page with a carbon under it, and the carbon has gone through to the page beneath.

"Dushman jaise log hain ye — humare jaise nahi hain. Ye Parmeet. Ye Baljeet. Ye mummy."

He does not point at anybody.

"Inki asliyat main jaanta hoon — ye sab jhooth aur nakli hain."

The sweet boxes at the edge of the hall are still tied, and the tray they came on has one corner bent down.

"Gurinder ji."

Dimple's hand is at her throat with fear.

"Aap kya keh rahe hain?"

"Main desh se gaddari nahi kar sakta, aur meri beti ki khushi ke liye bhi nahi."

"Aap saboot dikhaiye."

The inspector has his register out now.

"Aapke paas kya hai?"

"Pakke saboot hain."

Gurinder opens his folder on the low table and does not take anything out of it.

"Ye jo keh rahe hain, woh sach hai ya nahi — lekin main aapke munh se sunna chahta hoon."

Gurinder turns to the family.

"Sirf haan ya na mein jawab dijiye aap."

He waits.

"**Ghus baithe hain aap ya nahi,**" the score sounding under the question (m04:35).

Nobody in that hall answers him, and every one of them who does not answer is an answer.

## Kaun Ho Aap

The hall at eleven in the morning, with the chandelier on and forty people standing where they got up.

"Saare paper jhoothe hain aapke."

The inspector is reading off his own register now, and his finger is on the page.

"Aur ye hain — ye Parmeet Suri." He lifts his head.

"**Toh aap kaun ho,**" the score sounding under the asking (m04:43).

Parmeet stands by the fire with his hands folded.

The tray of tea that came in an hour ago stands against the wall and nobody has taken a cup from it.

The fire in the grate is lit and nobody has put anything on it.

The inspector's cap is under his arm and the badge on it has lost its pin.

"Bolo."

The inspector's voice is still flat.

"Kaun ho aap?"

"I'll tell you."

Parmeet takes his spectacles off and folds them.

"Aap toh humare haan hamesha aate hain. Aaj thoda sahi sawaal poochh liya."

Parmeet looks at his family, one at a time, and he smiles at them, and the smile is the one he uses on the lawn.

"Is Bharat desh ke nivaasi nahi hain hum."

He puts the spectacles in his pocket.

"Hum ghus baithe hain — main, mera bhai, meri behen, aur do dost mere."

The hall has stopped breathing.

The tray of tea that came in an hour ago stands against the wall with every cup on it cold.

"**Chauvees saal pehle, pichhle Kumbh, aaye the hum. Ek mission par.**" He says it slowly, and the slowness is the performance.

"**Kumbh mein tabahi — hamara mission.**"

"Dad—" Aryaman's voice comes out of the front row.

"Changistan — hamara asli desh."

Parmeet's hands are behind his back now.

"India se do sau kilometre door. Wahin se humein bheja gaya tha."

"Papa, please—" he says.

"Main—"

He stops, and he thinks about it, and it is the only time in the whole morning that he stops.

"Mera asli naam—"

"That is not necessary."

The inspector's pen is up.

"Sameer — ye minister ka naam hai."

Parmeet does not hear him.

"Iska naam hai Kareem — mera bhai Daljeet. Aur ye—" He stops again, and this time he does not go on.

"Indian naam toh humne sirf apni pehchaan chhupane ke liye rakhe the — yahi humare asli naam hain."

"News channel se lekar ministry tak, khabrein humne saari ki saari apne desh Changistan bheji."

His chin comes up.

"System mein ghus kar system ko andar se khola karte rahe hum."

"Aur tum"—he looks at the inspector—"ab yahi humara sabse important target hai," the score sounding under the targeting (m06:15), "Kumbh." His finger comes down on the table.

"Kyunki duniya bhar ke saare log" He lifts the finger.

"us din wahan hote hain. Aur tum mein" He looks at the inspector.

"ek baar poore desh mein" He holds the look.

"haahakaar macha dega."

He turns back to his family.

"Tum jaise logon ki zindagi humare desh ke logon ke naseeb mein nahi hoti." He says it pleasantly.

"Faslon se zyada terrorist paida hote hain humare yahan."

* * *
## The Papers

The hall in the chandelier's light is a room with forty people in it and nobody is looking at anybody except the boy who is walking out of it.

Vihaan is gone for four minutes.

When he comes back he has a folder in his hand, and he puts it down on the low table beside Gurinder Arora's, and he opens it where everybody can see.

"Yeh London ke books hain."

He takes them out one at a time and lays them flat.

"Chaar saal. Har mahine ki entry. Mere haath se, mere naam se."

The inspector's hand comes out of his pocket with a card in it, and he turns it over once.

"**Yeh card kisne chhapa tha?**"

"Main." Vihaan does not look at Pinky, who is standing by the door with the bags.

"Poore staff ke liye. Chaalis aadmi. Naam ke saath."

The inspector looks at the second line on the inside of it, and then at the boy, and he puts the card in the file and the file under his arm.

The inspector looks at them and does not touch them.

"Aur ye."

The folder comes down on top.

"B-46. File number. Iska koi naam nahi hai, sirf number hai. Yeh maine banaya tha."

"Beta."

Parmeet's voice from the fire.

"Yeh sab—"

"In papers pe mera naam hai." Vihaan turns round and looks at his father for the first time that morning, and his voice is flat and it does not go anywhere.

"Maine inhein likha hai. Main in jinse paisa aage bhejta hoon. **Toh yeh jhoothe hain.**"

The room is very silent.

"Chaar saal." He says it to the inspector now.

"Aap jo keh rahe hain, woh chaar saal se mere desk se guzar raha hai. Aur mujhe pata nahi tha."

Ananta is standing at the back of the hall with her sari in her hand, and she is looking at the books.

Her purse is under her arm.

In the purse there is a chemist's slip from a night three weeks ago, with a date on it and the name of a medicine and nothing else, and a boy on a cycle who takes no money and says nothing about whose account it is.

*Kisi ko batana nahin.*

The writing on the top book is small and it leans left and the numbers sit flat on the line.

Ananta looks at it for a long time.

Then she looks at her sister, and then at Parmeet, who is standing in front of the inspector with his hands at his sides, and she puts her hand flat on her purse and keeps it there.

The inspector looks at the books, and then at Gurinder Arora by the fire, and then at Parmeet.

"Tumne inhein la kar theek nahi kiya." He says it without kindness.

"Tumne apne aap ko ismein daal diya."

"Pata hai."

"Yeh sab evidence mein jayega."

"Pata hai."

Parmeet Suri has not moved from the fire, and he is looking at his son with a face that has nothing on it at all.

"Tum woh likhte ho jo tumhein diya jaata hai." He says it quietly. "Bas."

"Bas." Vihaan nods once.

"Aaj ke baad main kuch nahi likhunga."

## The Handcuffs

The hall at noon, with the chandelier on and the bride's side of it gone quiet.

"Aapne sab kuch khud hi bata diya — achha hua."

The inspector lifts the handcuffs off his belt and holds them up.

"Parmeet Suri, haath badhaiye — ab main aapko hathkadi pehna sakta hoon," the score sounding under the offering (m06:47).

Parmeet holds out his wrists.

The handcuffs on the inspector's belt have been hanging there long enough to have worn the leather.

The inspector looks at the handcuffs, and then at the room, and then he puts them back on his belt.

"**Par pehnaunga nahi.**"

"Kya?"

Aryaman is on his feet again.

"Inspector sahab, yeh kya—"

"Baithe rahiye."

The inspector turns round, and his voice is the voice of a man who has made up his mind and has enjoyed making it up.

"Lekin main sochne laga, to mere baare mein kaun sochega? Sochne ke liye main bhi desh ke baare mein soch sakta hoon." He puts a hand on his own chest.

"Ab inse wafadari nahi karunga, to kisse karunga? **Parmeet sahab kai saalon se mera ghar chala rahe hain.**"

The hall does not understand him yet.

At the side of the hall a chair has gone over and nobody has picked it up.

"Dhoti-kurta pehna, photo liya, ho gayi deshbhakti — yaar, ye deshbhakti do-teen din ke liye theek lagti hai."

He laughs and the laugh is a real one.

"Ek imaandar aadmi ko kyun badnam karna ab iske liye?"

"Sir."

Gurinder's hand is still on his folder.

"Sir, aap—"

"Inke khilaaf complaint karne ek aadmi aaya tha waise tumse pehle bhi." The inspector says it to the whole hall.

"**Uski girlfriend bhi aayi thi complaint karne — maara gaya bechara.**" He lifts his shoulders.

"**Paisa complaint se bada hota hai** — lekin ye paisa hota hai na."

"Sir, please."

"Chal."

The inspector turns away from him.

"Arre, simple hai yaar. **Jis haath se note milte hain, uske khot nahi ginte.**" He waves a hand at the room.

"Aap bindas raho, sir — Parmeet sahab ke toh sau khoon maaf dikh gaye hain." He puts his cap on.

"Main inko dekh lunga."

The inspector goes.

The door shuts.

The white car stays where it is.

### Continuity and source note

This chapter is the first half of Episode 7, in the episode's own order: the bride's wedding-morning wish to her sister; the pheras of one couple finished and the other couple called; the four cars in the lane; the warrant, the raid's own figures — **200 kilo RDX, 12 AK-47, 20 hand grenades** — and the order to take the girls out of the house; the two sisters whom the inspector will not let go, because their father is the complainant; Gurinder Arora's answer and the one question he asks the room; the confession, in the hall, in Parmeet Suri's own voice; the handcuffs offered and put back on the belt; the inspector's speech about whose house he lives in; the pandit who is not a pandit; the pearl, the Shesh naagin and *"Shesh mein Shesh hai — Ananta"*; the bargain, the refusal, and her father on the floor; the instruction that reverses the season; and the bride standing up in front of the fire and taking her sister's truth for her own. Beats **B02 to B20** of `canon/E07_BEATS.md`.

**The correction this chapter now carries, 2026-11-12.** The first version of it had him refuse the order in front of the hall — *"Nahin"* — and then had his father send him out again at dusk, to Ghaziabad, and gave him a car parked at the gate with a broken contract in it: Meridian, Kessler, three hundred and fifty crore — a figure from another serial, and it is struck out of `OC_VIHAAN.md` §3b on 2026-11-13 and put here at the serial's own scale: six crore and two lakh is the year's biggest contract because the house spends twenty-four lakh a month, and the number that actually bends this story is forty lakh, the price of a bigha at that ghat and the price of the crocodile man's fortnight. Menon waiting three hours in a lobby, Verma on the phone. **All of it is out.** None of it is canon, all of it was business I invented for him, and the worst of it was what the invention was doing to canon's own beat: Parmeet Suri gives that order once, inside the hour after ten, with the warrant already served — B05, 02:32–03:04 on the episode's clock — and the order is *take the girls home*. A man who says no to it does not get a second order from the same father in the same hall; he gets what canon gives him — he goes, and the inspector stops the two sisters at the step, and he is out of the gate with the children and at a hotel by ten past twelve while the house he drove away from turns on the bride that afternoon. He obeys, and the obedience is the whole of the thing that saves him and the whole of the thing that costs him. **The chapter now ends where canon's window ends:** the bride standing in front of the fire with her sister's truth in her mouth, and the room told to listen to her.

**What he carries out of that house is one object.** The folder, and it is his. The guard's book stays where a guard's book stays — open on the flat of the wall at the gate, with the white car's number, the time and the count of men in his hand (Part 1, `## The Gate`; Part 2, `## The Book and the White Car`) — and the instruction not to shut the gate goes out of the house with him, because a shut door is a door worth breaking. That is the whole of his exit: the children, the folder, and the open gate behind him. (This paragraph used to say the book went out with him and that no client waited; both are struck — the book by `SEEN:` and `BF-97`, the client by `RESTORED:`.)

**What canon does to this adaptation in one line.** *"Naagin tum nahi — Purvi hai. Use bachane ke liye humne uska naam tujhe de diya tha."* The family has known since before the first episode. Everything the wedding was for, everything the pearl's name has been doing on Ananta's shoulders, and every scene in which somebody looked at the wrong sister, takes its meaning from that sentence, and it is spoken on the floor of a hall by a man who has just been told he will die.

**B01 is not written, and this is the reason.** The episode opens on a man's voice — *please don't leave me, I love you so much, I'll do anything* — with no face, no room, no addressee, and the episode never returns to it. A page that assigns it would be inventing at the highest price there is, so it stays out and is declared here.

**The name in the track is Rishabh.** Canon has Parmeet order *"Rishabh, in ladkiyon ko apne ghar le jao"*. In this serial the man told is **Vihaan** — `OPEN.md` OPEN-04, `O003` — and he is the one canon makes go. He does not argue with the order and the page does not improve it.
