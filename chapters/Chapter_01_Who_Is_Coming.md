# Chapter 1 — Who Is Coming

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 1, "Naagin Legacy Unleashed" (first part).*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Kumbh

Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh. 2001.

Night at the Kumbh. Lamps burn along both banks, small clay lamps in rows on the wet stone steps, and the water carries their light out into the dark. Orange flags hang over the tents. Somewhere a bell rings and stops. Pilgrims wade in the shallows with their clothes held up in their fists, and the cold comes off the river.

A voice speaks over all of it.

"This is the Kumbh of 2001. The Kumbh of Prayagraj. Lakhs of people come to it, from every province. The gurus of every faith come. The royal priests, the keepers of the law, the heirs, the followers, the stewards."

A fire burns in the middle of an open ground. A great trishul stands lit from below, its three points black against the stars, and a sadhu with his hair knotted high on his head stands under it with his arms folded.

"But this time the Kumbh is different. The gathering of the gurus is to be held here, inside the Kumbh. Every guru and every aghori is being brought together for one purpose."

They come down the lit road in a column. Saffron robes, shaven heads, bare feet on the road. Naga sadhus bare to the waist and grey with ash, their hair matted and long, one of them carrying a golden mace across his shoulder. Old men in white walk with their hands on the shoulders of boys. An aghori comes behind them with a trident, his eyes red from the fire.

Ahead of them a round tent glows red from the inside. The mouth of it stands open, and the light comes out over the ground.

## The Akhara

Inside, nobody is sitting. Monks in maroon and yellow stand in a knot at the back. Sadhus in saffron crowd together under a parasol with a gold fringe. Everyone is talking, and nobody is sitting. They are angry, and they have come a long way to be angry.

"Be calm. Be calm."

"Why have we all been called here in this way? What is the reason?"

"We have always met on the appointed day, in the hills. So why today, in the akhara of the Kumbh?"

"There must be some great reason."

"What calamity has come, that it could not wait?"

"The calamity has not come. Not yet. It is on its way."

The man who says it walks into the open ground at the centre of the tent. He is bearded, in round glasses, with a beige blazer over a black polo-neck. He lifts both hands.

"Sit down, all of you. Please sit."

They sit, a row at a time, the whole tent going down and the noise going with it, until he is the only one standing. Nobody speaks. The anger goes out of them. What is left is fear.

"Forgive me for calling you all here in this way." He turns as he speaks, so that each side of the tent has his face in its turn. "Our faiths may differ. But the country is one. The blood is one. The passion is one. The defence of this country against the enemy countries. Even the blow of a sickness sent from an enemy country. Even massacre. Every calamity that has come on this land, we have found the answer to it. We have fought."

A log settles in the fire outside. Nobody moves.

"Whenever the destroying planets and stars stand together, pralay comes into this world. And whenever the stars have given us that sign, we have met as we are meeting now. Today the same danger has come again."

"What danger?"

"It has not come yet. It is coming. Twenty-four years from now."

"What is it you want to say?"

"I do not say it. The planets say it." He holds up a hand and counts on it, one finger at a time. "When Brihaspati has made his circuit of twelve years and enters Vrishabh, and Surya enters Makar, the Kumbh is held at the sangam of Prayagraj. The Kumbh is held in four places. Allahabad, Nashik, Ujjain and Haridwar. The one that comes to Allahabad after twelve years is called the Mahakumbh. And after twelve Kumbhs, that is, after a hundred and forty-four years, comes the Maha Mahakumbh."

He lets the hand fall.

"It is 2001 now. In 2025 there will be the Maha Mahakumbh. In that hour the destruction begins. There will be an attack. An enemy country will make it."

An old guru in a saffron turban speaks from under the parasol. "The enemy countries have always attacked us."

"Yes. But this attack will be very dangerous."

"An attack that is twenty-four years away. How do you know of it today?"

"I am a professor. For years I have done research into the knowledge and the science of India, and of this world. A person has a kundali. In just the same way a place has a kundali, and a country has one. From the kundali of India I learned this. The enemy countries that burn at our rise and our progress will attack us."

He looks round the circle, from one end of the tent to the other.

"And to prove my research I went to the south, to a Nadi temple. The Nadi Shastra that Rishi Agastya wrote is kept there to this day. My future is written in that book. Yours is. Every person's. The future of India is written in it. The fate of every country was written long ago."

A dark room. Dust in the air, and one lamp. A sage stands bare to the waist, his beard down to his belly, ash striped across his forehead. He holds a palm leaf in both hands, and the leaf is old enough that he holds it from underneath. The professor stands in front of him in a plain white shirt, his glasses pushed up.

"Bharat," the sage reads. He looks up. "It is a strange thing. Your finding is right. By the planets, after two Mahakumbhs, that is, after twenty-four years, a very great attack will fall on India."

The tent again, and the fire red on every face.

"The pandit of the Nadi Shastra told me this. The reckoning, the planets, the stars, the dasha that stood over the war of the Mahabharata will stand again in the year 2025."

"If the danger comes after so many years, why are we meeting now?"

"Because the danger has already set its foot inside our country." He points at the ground between his feet. "For the attack that comes twenty-four years from now, the infiltrators of the enemy country have begun to put down their roots here. Now. Word has come from the border. Infiltrators are crossing our border and coming into our country."

## The Suri House

A house at night behind a white picket fence. The windows are lit. A wooden plate by the gate reads SURI, and under it the same name in Gurmukhi. The lawn is cut. The gate stands open.

The front room is dressed for a birthday. Yellow bunting hangs across the wall in two long loops. Balloons lie on the floor, among the cushions, along the foot of the stairs. On the table stands a cake on a stand, white icing, one candle not yet lit, and a name piped across it in blue: Vihaan. The room smells of fresh flowers; a bowl of them stands beside the cake.

A woman in a pale blue salwar suit stands folding clothes off their hangers into an open case. A bearded man in a lilac kurta-pyjama is down among the balloons, setting the cushions straight and moving them all by an inch.

"O Nimrat! Is your packing-shacking done or not? Then don't go telling me at the Kumbh that you forgot this, you forgot that."

She does not look up. "Parmeet ji, if you do not keep quiet I will forget my own self."

She is tired, and she is enjoying him.

"Oye, be quick." He points at the table. "Your son's cake is still to be cut. And in the morning we have to set out."

"I am doing it!" A kurta flies off its hanger onto the pile.

He laughs and goes back to his cushions. She folds, and shakes her head at the case, and folds again.

The bell rings.

Nimrat's eyes go to the staircase and rest there. No sound comes down. "Who is it, so late at night?"

She is uneasy.

"That will be Doctor sahib. Who comes at this hour." Parmeet is already crossing the room, his slippers loud on the floor. "I'll see."

Men stand at the gate in the dark with bags on their shoulders. One has grey in his beard and a khaki scarf. One is wrapped in a dark shawl, his hand laid on his chest. One stands a little in front of the others: shaggy hair, a heavy moustache, a brown scarf at his throat, the moon behind his head.

Parmeet looks out from the door. "Yes, ji? You people…?"

The man with the moustache looks back at him and does not answer at once.

*With great difficulty we have crossed the border and come into your country. The ruin of your country begins from here.*

Coils of barbed wire under the same moon. Figures come through it one behind another, bent low, a bag passed from hand to hand.

"We are going to the Kumbh," the man with the moustache says. "We are thirsty. Will there be water?"

Parmeet's whole face opens. He is delighted. "O ji, you people are going to the Kumbh? In weather this bad! Come inside. Eat something, drink something, then go. We are leaving for the Kumbh ourselves in the morning." He has the door wide and is waving them up the path. "Come, come, come. Come in, na."

They come through the gate one behind another, and their boots are loud on the gravel.

In the front room they stand among the balloons and look about them: at the bunting, at the balloons, at the cake. A black bag is set down on the floorboards beside a blue pouffe, and its pocket hangs open. Parmeet looks down at it. Dark blue booklets lie in it on a patterned cloth, the size of passports.

He bends and lifts them out.

He stands with one open in his hands. Two of the men have come close on either side of him. He turns a page. He turns another. His eyes go wide. He is frightened now, and not yet angry. He looks up: at the man with the moustache, at the one with the khaki scarf, at a bald one in a red scarf behind them.

"You people are not Hindustani." He raises a finger. "You are from the enemy country—"

An arm comes across his throat from behind.

Nimrat is in the inner doorway. She screams his name. She is terrified.

Upstairs a child begins to cry. The man with the moustache lifts his eyes to the staircase and holds them there.

The house from outside: the fence, the lawn, the lit windows, the cake inside on its stand. The crying carries out over the grass.

White.

## She Has to Come

"The destruction has begun." The professor stands in the red light of the tent. "But the true destruction will come with 2025."

"There must be some answer to this calamity. Who will save us from it?"

"She will save us. She has to come."

"Who is she?"

"Ours is a great country. Whenever Kaal comes, Vishnu and Shiva come too, in one form or another. They save the country. To fight this attack also, they will send her."

"Whom?"

"Her. She has to come." His finger is up. "If the four directions stood in front of her. If the three worlds did. If the universe itself stood there to stop her. Even then she would not stop. She will come."

"Who?"

"Neither woman nor man. Neither beast nor human. Neither devil, nor angel, nor god. But God's greatest devotee, and mighty. Shiva's vardani."

A sadhu grey with ash speaks from the floor. "But the line of Sheshnaag has ended."

"The Sheshnaag on whom Vishnu's hand rests. Whom Shiva keeps beside him. Nageshwar." The professor shakes his head. "The line of that one can never end."

A golden god with many arms sits in burning cloud. A Shivling stands alone in a hall of dark blue pillars, and the floor round it is swept clean.

"Where will we find her?"

The dark room again, and the sage close: the ash stripes, a white mark between the brows. He speaks to the man in the white shirt.

"You will find the Naagrani of the Anant kul. Ananta. Where the northern hills end. The first lane at the edge of the city. The house that has a small Shivling outside it. In that house. There you will find her. Ananta."

The professor does not move.

"You must go to her. You must tell her. The great destruction comes in twenty-four years. Let her make ready to fight it."

The tent. "But who will go to look for her?"

"I will go." The professor looks round the whole circle. "I will find her."

Stone steps climb between broken walls in blue hills. Carved serpents stand on either side of the stair, and one small light burns at the top. Mist sits in the valley below the steps.

A voice goes with the picture. "She is the heartbeat of Naaglok. She has every quality of a Shesh Naagin."

## Papa's Research

A study. Books to the ceiling, a whiteboard, and a wall of papers pinned in rows. The professor is bent over a desk heaped with more books. He wears a navy blazer now, over a red polo-neck, and his glasses are off.

"Papa."

A boy stands among the shelves. He is eight or ten, with a mop of dark curls, and he has come in without knocking.

The professor goes down on his heels in front of him. He puts a book into the boy's arms. It is large, with a metal cover worked all over in raised patterns, and it is heavy; the boy needs both arms to hold it to his chest.

"Keep this safe. This, this is Papa's research. Never give it to anyone." His hand is on the boy's cheek. "It may be that Papa's life is in danger."

"Papa, what are you saying? Where are you going? Don't go."

"I have to go, beta. To Ananta. By my calculation this is the right time. She has to be told that ruin has knocked at the country's door."

The boy's eyes are full. He is frightened, and he is trying not to show it.

"Remember one thing always, beta. The bond with the country is the greatest bond there is. Whatever happens to me, remember only this. That your father was martyred for the country." The hand moves to the boy's head. "And you too. Always keep your country in front of everything."

The boy holds the book and looks up at him. His father is not calm now. He is saying goodbye to his son.

The door of the study stands open behind them. Beyond it is a dark passage, and the rest of a house asleep.

## The First Lane

An old white car on a road at night. The professor drives it himself, both hands on the wheel, his face lit once by each lamp he passes. The sage's voice goes with him.

"Where the northern hills end. The first lane at the edge of the city. The house that has a small Shivling outside it. In that house. There you will find her. Ananta."

A television stands on a dark cabinet against an ochre wall, the picture red and blue, the sound low. A strip along the bottom says BREAKING NEWS, and above the words a worn gold "SN".

"Infiltrators have begun to enter our country. Many people from the enemy country are coming in, killing our people, and taking their places."

A room at the head of a staircase. Yellow bunting shows below, down the stairs. A small boy stands in a cot with both hands on the rail. A paper cap has slipped to the back of his head. His face is wet, and the crying has worn down to a croak. Round his cot the room is dark, and the door stands open on to the landing.

The man with the moustache comes up the last stair and stops in the door. He looks at the boy for a long moment. The boy looks back at him and goes quiet.

The man bends and lifts him out. The boy's fist closes on the brown scarf.

"Be careful," the television says. "Stay alert."

A young man in spectacles and a red check shirt sits on a patterned sofa in front of it, a cup of tea going cold on the arm. Across the room a woman in a pink salwar suit sits by the lamp, her plait over her shoulder.

"Shalini. Parmeet and Nimrat bhabhi are not picking up the phone. I went to their house. There is a lock on it." He shakes his head. "Who knows where that whole Suri family has vanished to. All at once. Without telling anyone. You be careful too. Watch yourself."

He is worried.

She points at the screen. "Look at this, ji. Look what they keep telling us on the news, again and again."

A knock at the door.

"Who has come at this hour?"

"I'll see. Wait." Shalini goes to the door and stops short of it. "Who is it?"

The professor stands outside under the street lamps. "I am Tushar Sinha. I am a scientist. Please open the door. I have to speak to you. It is important."

"No, no. We will not open the door."

"Look, please. I have very urgent business with you. Please, open the door."

Her husband gets up. "Let us see."

Shalini puts her back against the door and spreads her arms across it. She is frightened of the man outside. "No, no, don't open it. Who knows who this is?"

"I'll see. Wait. Leave it."

"Please open the door."

He opens it. A white car stands in the lane behind the man on the step with its door still open, and the engine running.

"O ji. What is your business?"

"I have to meet Ananta."

"Which Ananta?"

"Ananta of the Anant kul."

"Anant kul?" The husband looks at his wife and back. "O ji, you go. Nobody of that kind lives here."

"Look, one, one minute. One minute." Tushar Sinha lays a hand on his own chest. "This, this is the address. She, she lives here. Call her. I have to speak to her. She has to know that, that only she can save this country. Only she."

The husband turns to Shalini. "Now what is this man talking about?"

"Look, try to understand. The infiltrators of the enemy country have entered this, this country. And only she can kill them. She can save all of us. The enemies of the country want to destroy it, and she is the one, the only one, who can finish them for ever."

"O brother, forgive us. You go from here. Go now."

"Please listen to me. Please."

The door shuts. The bolt goes across.

They stand in the middle of the room.

"I told you, didn't I? Don't open it." Shalini turns on him. "Who is he? Whom is he talking about? Who is going to save the country? Which Ananta was he talking about?"

"Ji, how would I know?"

From the other side of the door: "Look, listen to me, please."

A car stands in the lane with its lights off. The man with the moustache sits in it, looking back over his shoulder, and he does not move while the professor begs.

## The Woman at the Door

Another knock.

"Now that will be him again. The scientist." The husband goes to the door. "I don't know why he has come back. These scientists too." He pulls it open. "Arre bhai, we told you, na, there is nobody here—"

Storm cloud rolls across the night sky, going over the lamps.

A woman stands on the step with a grey shawl over her head. There is blood on her forehead at the hairline, and dust on her feet. Her hands are joined.

"I need help."

"Na ji. We do not help strangers."

Shalini comes past his shoulder and looks. "She is with child, ji. I think her time has come." She is not frightened of her. She is frightened for her. She has the woman by the arm. "Come, ji. Quickly, come. Come inside."

Inside the door the woman's head goes back and she cries out.

"There. There, there, there." Shalini holds her up with both arms, the shawl slipping off her head. "Gently. I am bringing water. Gently, gently."

They lay her on the divan under the shelf of files and cover her with a dark blanket. A shadow moves across the ochre wall, thrown by the lamp, and the shadow of Shalini's hands moves with it. The woman's face is wet, the eyes shut, the head pressed back, and she holds the blanket in both fists.

Then Shalini lifts a child wrapped in white.

"It is a daughter."

She lays the child against the woman's side.

"You, you don't worry," Shalini says. "We will not let anything happen to you."

She is in great pain, and she is calm.

"I know." The woman's eyes are on the child. "I know I will not live. But you have to save her. You have to look after her."

"Ji, we… but why us?" the husband says.

"Because He, He wants it. It is His wish."

"Who is He?"

She raises one finger toward the roof.

"He, Vikral. He, Akal. He, Mahakal."

The child begins to cry.

"Many will come to kill her. But you have to save her. As I have saved her today."

## Sarvashreshth

Mountains at night, blue under the moon. Snow stands on the far peaks. The woman walks alone along a ridge with the grey shawl drawn round her, the wind pulling at it, one hand under her belly. Her voice goes with her.

"I was known as the Sarvashreshth Naagin. I took three births. First Pratha."

A woman in a mustard kurta stands with a cloth bag on her shoulder. Beside her the hood of a great cobra is spread over moonlit water.

"Then Prarthana."

A crowned naagin stands in a green forest in warm light. Gold light pours out of her hands.

"And then Pragati. Three births, and human in each. Yet each time the birth was the Sarvashreshth Naagin's."

She climbs between the rocks, and the shawl pulls, and she stops to breathe.

"A naagin may change her form as often as she pleases. She may live as many lives as she pleases. But the greatest moment for her is the moment her God chooses her. I was chosen for a purpose. To give birth to you."

Snow peaks stand behind her.

"You are a naagin greater than the greatest. An ichchhadhari naagin is made by a hundred years of tapasya. You will be a naagin without those hundred years. To bring you into this world may be the greatest work of my life."

She stops and lays the hand flat.

"You have been sent with Vishnu's boon. From the line of Sheshnaag. With Shiva's amrit. You have been sent to save this country, India, from the great danger that is coming. You will come as the new heir of the all-powerful Naagmani, and all-powerful yourself. You are of the Anant kul. Fate has chosen you. Creation has chosen you. To save this world from ruin, it has chosen you."

The shawl flies out behind her.

"Today I am going to give birth to the great heir, the all-powerful heir, of all the seven worlds. I am leaving your sister, who is three years old, with her family. Because this is the command of Shiva and of Vishnu." She walks on. "Come. Let me take you toward your fate."

She stops.

"You will not listen."

The head of a wolf fills the dark, the lips drawn off the teeth. Then two more on the slope above her. Then the pack.

She is frightened. She does not run.

She stands on the valley floor, small, and they are all round her.

She turns on them. Her hair flies across her face. Her hand is up, the fingers spread.

Then she is low among the stones with her hair hanging over her face.

## Ananta

The ochre room. She lies on the divan with the child against her. Her eyes are open. There is blood on her face and on her neck, and a bangle shines on the wrist of the hand that holds the child.

"My daughter." Her hand rests on the child. "Adbhut. Alaukik. Akhand. Anant."

Shalini's hand comes down to steady the child.

"Ananta."

Shalini turns to her husband. "That man was saying this very name. Ananta."

He frowns down through his spectacles and says nothing.

The woman lies still under the dark blanket. Husband and wife stand over her and look at each other, and neither of them says anything at all.

A ruined room, lit blue. Roots hang from the roof. There is one wooden chair, and a window with no glass. Tushar Sinha is dragged down between two men and put on the floor. The bald one and the man with the moustache have him by the throat.

"Speak! Who is coming to destroy us? Tell us! Speak, speak!"

He lies on his side in the dust with his glasses still on and blood at his mouth. His mouth opens wide. He is not afraid of them. He is in pain.

"Not one blow. Strike a thousand. Tomorrow she will come, and she will answer every blow of yours with her own."

"Who will come?"

"You people can kill me. You can make an end of me. She has no end." He gets the words out between breaths. "This is Bharat. The land of Kaal Bhairav. The land of Mahakal. Whenever enemies of your kind have come, she has come."

"Who is she? Tell us!"

Four men stand over him now, and the blue light moves on their shoulders.

"Neither woman nor man. Neither beast nor human. Neither angel nor devil, nor yet a god. And God's greatest devotee, and mighty. Shiv vardani. Queen of Naaglok. Naagrani of the Anant kul. Ichchhadhari naagin. Shesh Naagin."

The ochre room.

"Shalini. What will we do with this little girl?"

She has the child up against her shoulder, wrapped in white. "What will we do with the child? I mean, we cannot just leave her, can we?" She turns so that he can see. She is crying, and she is smiling. "Look at her, ji. See how lovely she is, this Ananta. Look at her face."

Tushar Sinha's cheek is against the floor. His lips still move.

"She on whom Vishnu's grace has no end. She who is the garland at Shiva's throat."

Shalini lifts the child in both hands in front of her face and shuts her eyes. "Mata Rani. Protect her."

"Who is coming? Speak! Speak, who is coming?"

One of the four bends and strikes downward.

Tushar Sinha's face lies against the floor. Behind the glasses his eyes are closed.

"Who is coming?"

In the ochre room the girl lies in Shalini's two hands. Her fist is closed.

### Continuity and source note

This chapter is the 2001 part of Episode 1, in the episode's own order: the Kumbh, the gathering, the Suri house, the prophecy, the professor and his son, the two knocks at the Aroras' door, the birth, the mother's account, the naming, and the death of Tushar Sinha. Of the forty-one cue blocks the track carries for that stretch, thirty-three are rendered whole and two in part: one holds the narration that opens 2025, which belongs to Chapter 2, and one begins with three words that cannot be made out. Six are not rendered at all: three cues of the title song, and three cues of a word or two.

The chapter is written to be seen and heard, because that is what the author asked for: the episode's places, its weather, its objects, its faces, and what its faces show.

Canon gives the Suris' son one line, "your son's cake", and a room dressed for his birthday. It never shows him. Four things are added here and nothing else: the cake with its candle and his name; Nimrat's look at the stairs; a child's cry after her scream; and one picture under the newsreader's words, the man with the moustache lifting the boy out of his cot. Nothing on the page says why the boy was kept.

The husband in the ochre room is not named in this episode, so he is not named here. The map that carries the episode to 2025 opens Chapter 2.
