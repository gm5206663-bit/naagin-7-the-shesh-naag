# Chapter 3 — The Award Night

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 1, "Naagin Legacy Unleashed" (last part).*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Newsroom

Nine o'clock. The channel's building stands at the edge of the city with the towers still lit behind it. The logo on its roof is lit too, and the letters stand out over the road.

At the security desk a guard writes down names in a ledger. Visitors' badges lie in a tray. A lift opens and lets out three young men in jackets, laughing, and they go up the marble with their hands in their pockets.

Inside, the newsroom is full of screens and the sound of a dozen conversations. The ticker runs along under the ceiling. A woman at the makeup mirror clips an earring on and reads her script at the same time.

On air, a woman reads the bulletin. "The eyes of all of India are on our channel today. Soon we will announce the winner of the 2025 Journalist of the Year award. For the third time in a row, it is Aryaman Suri."

In the newsroom the staff clap. The screens behind them show his name in gold, and the ticker under it, and the phones on twenty desks start ringing at once and nobody picks them up.

A man comes out of a corridor with his jacket over his shoulder and his tie already loose. Aryaman Suri is tall and beard-trimmed, and he walks through the clapping without looking at any of it.

"Congratulations, sir." His assistant is at his elbow with a folder. "This time, sir, care to the bloody point."

"Say something important."

"Sir, you have a call from the Home Minister's office, from the Chief Minister's office, and from the State Secretary's office. The Head of State has sent you a message. And two businessmen have called."

"I said something important."

"Something life-changing, sir."

"Life-changing?" He stops. "Something life-changing is happening to me. I am getting married."

"That is horrible." Aryaman looks at her with real concern. "I mean, you are killing yourself. That is sad. Very sad."

"No, sir, I am very happy. Since childhood I had one dream, that I would get married."

"So you have been suicidal since childhood." He starts walking again. "Look, to each their own. But may your soul find peace. I will pray for you."

Two of the staff watch him go.

"He is a strange mad man, Aryaman Suri. On a happy occasion, who says a thing like that?"

"He talks without a filter. He drinks all day. And he still never makes a mistake in his work. Unbelievable, sir."

## The Diet

Aryaman's cabin stands behind glass at the corner of the floor. Three phones on the desk, a bottle of water with no glass, a framed front page on the wall, and a chair that nobody sits in but him.

A young man leans in the doorway with his hands together. "Sir. The more we praise you, the less it is. And the less of you, the more it is."

"The less of me, the more it is?"

"The more it is, sir."

"Say what you came to say."

"Sir, I was saying that we should announce your award in the nine o'clock news."

"Did your wife make garlic chutney for you last night? Shall we report that too?" Aryaman puts down his pen. "Our work is to show important things. Relevant things. Not the ordinary thing that somebody has won an award. Anybody can win an award. I am on a diet."

"Sir, it looks like your diet is struggling more than you."

"These days I am getting thinner."

"I can see that, sir."

"Everybody can see that." He signs a page without reading it. "Go and find me something that matters."

The young man goes. At the far end of the floor the night desk is starting its shift: the screens racked from floor to ceiling, the ticker running under them, a woman at makeup, a boy carrying three cups of tea on a tray.

## The Mandap

The lift opens on the news floor, and the news floor is a wedding.

White and gold cloth is looped from the ceiling in long swags. Flowers stand in tall vases along the desks, and their water is already going cloudy. A low platform has been built in the middle of the room and covered in white, and there are lamps on it, and petals on the floor, and a row of chairs facing it, and somebody has hung a garland across the biggest screen. The screens go on playing with nobody watching them.

Pinky and Nisha stand in the middle of it with their hands on their hips and their friends behind them, and a dozen people turn to look at the man in the doorway. One of the friends has a garland ready in both hands and nowhere to put it. A tray of glasses stands on a desk beside a keyboard.

Aryaman stands and looks. "My good newsroom. You have made it a wedding mandap."

"And what else would we do, bhai?" Pinky spreads her arms at the room. "You never go out to meet our friends. So all our friends came here to meet you. Brotherhood."

"Brotherhood!"

A young man in an orange jacket pushes through with his arms wide. "Congratulations! Seeing your success, I am really feeling good. What happiness is this, brother?"

"And this is Arjun." Nisha takes the young man's arm. "And Karan. Your own brother. Our cousin."

"Arjun is not like you at all," Aryaman says. "At least Arjun listens to us. And he is close to me." He looks at the other cousin, who stands with his arms folded. "And honestly, Karan gives me Duryodhan vibes. He does not like you much either."

"I am not a pizza that everybody will like."

"No, no, bhai. Karan bhai likes you. He only thinks you are a little arrogant."

"Arrogant?" Aryaman turns on the whole room. "I am allergic to stupidity. To stupid people. And especially to a crowd of stupid people. That is why I am not in the crowd."

"That is so mean, bhai. We arranged a party for you with so much love, and you behave like this." Nisha puts her hand on Pinky's shoulder. "And brother, you are calling our arrangement wedding decoration. We did so much LED decoration."

"I know, I know. You are going to say that I do not like girls generally. But we are the sisters. How can you hate us? Say I love you."

"I love you." He says it at once, and it is not a joke. "But I do not like show at all. And all of your friends do nothing but show." He turns his folder over in his hand. "I have a lot of work. This party is a useless thing. You all do it. I am not coming out."

His assistant leans in. "Sir. Parmeet sir and Baljeet sir have arrived."

Aryaman closes his eyes. "Now I have to go out."

At the back of the room, by the tall screen, a young man stands on a stool with a coil of flex in his hand. He has been holding the ladder for an hour and he is not holding it now. He steps down, and puts the coil on a chair, and goes to stand with his sisters.

## The Two Brothers

The corridor behind the party, past the pantry, where the wall is glass and the city is a field of small lights. A kettle is boiling in the pantry and nobody turns it off. A poster on the wall says **THE TRUTH, FIRST**, and somebody has pinned a party hat to it.

Aryaman comes down it with his jacket still over his shoulder. Vihaan comes behind him with his hands in his pockets.

"You came from London."

"I did."

"For this." Aryaman stops at the glass and turns. "An award is an ordinary thing. I said it downstairs and I will say it again."

"You said it four times."

"Three years in a row." Aryaman looks at his own reflection. "They are calling it three."

"Someone in the room was saying four."

"Someone in the room is a fool." He is quiet a moment. "Why did you come back?"

"Because you were getting an award."

"You could have sent a card."

"I could have." Vihaan looks at the lights with him. "I have a desk here from Monday. Papa has decided. I am to learn the business."

"From the ground up, I hope."

"From the tea."

Aryaman laughs once, unexpectedly, and it changes his whole face. Then it goes.

"Look at this room. A mandap. My staff cannot say one important thing in a day, and they can build a wedding in an afternoon." He puts his jacket on properly. "You are twenty-four and you have been away six years. This family is a circus. You will see it by Friday."

"I know what this family is."

"Do you."

"I know the house." Vihaan says it simply. "It is the only house I know."

Aryaman looks at him for a second longer than he needs to. Then he puts a hand flat on his brother's shoulder and walks back down the corridor.

## Father and Son

The decorated floor, the mandap, the friends, and two older men coming through them: one in a dark bandhgala, one behind him with a smile for the room.

"Congratulations."

"Thank you, Dad."

The second man spreads his hands. "Will nobody talk to me too?"

"Baljeet uncle, you have been talking since you came up in the lift." Aryaman turns and sees his brother at the edge of the crowd with his hands in his pockets. "And you. Do you want an invitation as well?"

"I came for the ladder," Vihaan says.

"Then go and hold it." Aryaman turns back to the two older men. "Dad. Ten minutes and then I am going back to work."

Parmeet laughs and puts an arm round his son. The photographers' flashes go off across the white cloth, and the sisters pull the cousins in for a photograph, and the newsroom full of screens goes on playing the same bulletin behind them all.

## The Road

A car on a road out of the city. Ananta drives. Purvi sits beside her with her hands in her lap, and a jumper, a jacket and a muffler on top of each other. The blue card hangs at her neck on its lanyard, and the inhaler sits in her bag, and the bag sits on her knees.

The city goes by in glass and light. Then the flyover, and the last of the hoardings, and a petrol pump with nobody at it, and a man asleep on a handcart at the side of the road with his arm over his face.

"Oh god, Purvi. I knew you would bring east and west together." Ananta reaches over and pulls at her sister's sleeve. "Take those socks off."

"Thank you."

"And this lovely jacket. And this muffler. Take it off too."

"No, di, I will be cold."

"Purvi, you will feel cold. Do as much as I say."

"Please, na."

Ananta sighs and drives. Her bangles knock on the wheel. Purvi watches the road and says nothing about being cold, and after a while she puts her hands inside her sleeves.

The road goes out of the city between dark trees. The street lamps thin and then stop. The shops stop. The headlights find the trunks of trees, and the white line, and once a dog that stands and watches them go past, and the dark comes up close behind the car.

Then the car coughs once and dies.

Ananta turns the key. The engine turns and does not catch. She hits the wheel with the flat of her hand.

"That damn thing. It had to stop now." She gets out. The night air comes in through the open door. Purvi gets out on her side and stands in her layers, holding herself.

"There is nobody anywhere," Ananta says. "Now what do we do?"

## The Naag Mandir

A voice comes out of the dark ahead of them, calm and glad.

"So you have come. You have come where you were meant to come. This is your Naag Mandir, and you are the Naagrani of our line. Come. We have been waiting for you to come and rule from your throne."

Ananta stops. The dark opens into a courtyard of carved stone. There is a gate of black iron standing open, and a bell rope, and a threshold worn low in the middle by feet.

Beyond the courtyard a hall goes up on pillars, and the pillars are carved from the floor to the dark with snakes in coils. In the hall a lamp burns under the idol. The idol is gold in the lamplight, a naagin with her hood spread and her hands folded, and the stone around her is old and black and wet in places where the roof has let the water in. The stone is wet at the edge of the steps. Something moves in the dark above the pillars and is only a bird.

Two women come down the steps towards her. One is older, in a dark shawl with her grey hair drawn back. The other is young, in a blue blouse and a sari, and she is looking at Ananta and crying.

"Uttara Maa." The young one wipes her face and turns. "In the world of us ichchhadhari naagins there are many, many who have been given the boon after a hundred years of tapasya. But who are the six great naagins? The six great ichchhadhari naagins because of whom people still want to hear us?"

"Tell her."

"I will tell." Uttara takes Ananta's hand and walks her into the hall, and the pillars go up into the dark above them. "Because of these six naagins, the world knows our story. They saved the universe and India together. And now it is the turn of our next naagin."

She stops in front of the idol, and the lamp finds her face.

"For her, the doors of Naaglok opened for the first time.

"Shivangi, who received her mother's power. She destroyed her mother's killers.

"A pair of ichchhadhari naag and naagin. Do you know who she was? Bela, who came in another form, and took her revenge.

"Fate played such a game, so poisonous. She was the ichchhadhari naagin Brinda.

"From creation she was given her own name. The Adi Naagin of Naaglok, who fell in love with her own enemy, the eagle. Who wrote with her enemy the eagle a strange and unforgettable story of love.

"The asur came as a plague. It struck the country. It tore the breast of the earth for its own soil, and a naagin came on the earth: Shesh. The Sarvashreshth Naagin, the Shesh Naagin, Pratha.

"And this is the greatest naagin of all. Our Maharani. The Shesh Naagin of the Anant kul. Pragati."

The young one is weeping now, without a sound.

"Her time to come has come. The Naagrani of the Anant kul. Ananta."

## The Swap

A bedroom in the Arora house, a lamp on the side table, the bed turned down, the sari folded over a chair, and the photographs on the wall in their frames: a school function, a wedding, a girl in a paper crown. Shalini sits on the edge of the bed. Her husband stands in front of her, still in his kurta.

"Sunil ji, we are making ready to go to the Kumbh. And Purvi's twenty-fourth birthday is coming too."

She looks at the floor, and then at him, and it comes out of her the way a thing comes out that has been buried for years.

"For years I have sat with a secret buried in my chest. We gave our own daughter the name Ananta. And we gave Ananta our daughter's name. Purvi."

He looks back at her and does not speak.

In the temple, the lamp burns in front of the idol, and the great Naag Mandir goes up into the dark, and the two naagins stand in front of the girl with the folded hands.

"This is our Naagrani. The queen of Naaglok. We all bow to her."

"Anant kul ki Naagrani. Ananta."

At the Arora house the hall clock ticks. The television is off. On the painted panel, Shiva's trident catches the last of the lamp.

At the Suri house, on the roof, a young man stands with his hands in his pockets and looks out over the city. The water tank behind him is still warm from the day. The music comes up through the floor of the roof, and the laughter comes up with it, and the lights of the city go on to the edge of the dark.

A car comes down the road below and turns in at the gate. Two old men get out of the back of it, and the gate shuts behind them, and the guard salutes nobody in particular.

He stays on the roof a while longer.
