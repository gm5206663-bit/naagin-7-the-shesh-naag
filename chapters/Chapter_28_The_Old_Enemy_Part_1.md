## After Dinner

The stairwell keeps the night in one bulb and cooling walls, and the dinner's noise dies below by degrees.

Aryaman sits three steps up with his elbows on his knees.

Vihaan comes up behind him and stops a step below.

"Neend nahin aa rahi?" he says.

Aryaman shakes his head once.

A sound that is neither yes nor no.

His phone stays dark in Vihaan's pocket.

Somebody's keys chime far below, and still.

Vihaan sits down on the step beside his brother.

"Main yahin hoon," he says.

A door shuts somewhere below, soft, and the stairwell settles.

He stays till the house goes quiet — one low string under the stair-sitting.

## The Silent File

The study keeps the small hours in one lamp and shut shelves, and the stairs have gone quiet behind him.

The Verma file lies where he left it under the ledger, the owed morning long past and no answer in it.

Vihaan draws it out and opens it on the blotter.

The request stands in his own hand — Purvi, missing, by eight — and the silence under it has outlasted the owed morning.

His brother's voice comes back with the page: called four-five times, never answered.

Alive enough to be called.

Gone enough never to answer.

He takes up the pen and writes one line in the margin under the date — Rishabh ke chaar-paanch phone, koi response nahin — and blots it.

The phone waits beside the blotter, Verma's number one touch down.

His hand settles on it a breath, and lifts away.

Nothing new to give the man tonight but a brother's restlessness, with the house asleep round him.

The Ghaziabad deal file sits squared with the sale-papers at the desk's edge; his palm flattens on it once, and leaves it shut.

Verma's lift-contract file rests read and docketed at the stack's foot, the nine-o'clock order kept.

He slides the Verma file back under the ledger, turns the lamp down, and goes.

"Anantveer. Mera saathi. Chehra — jab aap denge."
