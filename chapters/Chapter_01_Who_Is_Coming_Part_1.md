A match goes to a brass lamp and the flame takes, and the room it stands in fills with gold.

An eye opens in that gold with no face around it, and the pupil tightens on whatever it is that has woken it.

A man's hand comes down flat on the head of a drum in a lane at night, and the talking in that lane stops, and the drum does not start.

A wrist turns over in the lamplight and the skin at the edge of it lifts, one corner, the way paper lifts.

A shoulder fills a doorway, and two men get up out of their chairs before either of them knows why he has stood.

Rain falls on a tin roof; underneath the roof, at the edge of the water that comes through, the border of a sari goes red.

Stone pillars go by at the pace of a man walking, and each pillar carries a hooded serpent cut above it.

Behind water, in shadow, a hood moves, and nothing in the picture says what it is joined to.

A girl walks away down a corridor and is out of focus, and then she is in it. At the far end of the same corridor a man has stopped walking to let her pass, and neither of them speaks, and the corridor is long enough for the frame to have time to look at both of them.

A mirror stands in an empty room with a bed and a wall in it, and the room holds four seconds longer than it can bear.

The title comes over thunder with something red falling through it, too slowly to be rain.

The eye, again, with gold at the edge of it now. Then a face that is not hers, one second, unlit, looking past the camera.

Over all of it a man says four words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

And where the been was, there is one low string, struck and left hanging. It does not resolve, and it is the last thing the season tells anybody before it begins.

A drum, once. Three seconds of dark. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## The Kumbh

Night on the ghat at Prayagraj, and the dark full of lamps. The river takes their light and carries it out into the middle of itself, and the crowds stand in it on both banks, and the tents stand behind the crowds with their ropes going up into the dark.

The lamps burn on the water and on the steps. Strings of light go across the crowd wherever it thickens, and the faces turn toward the river and away again, and nobody in the picture is in a hurry.

The card comes up over the lamps: *Prayagraj (Uttar Pradesh 2001)* — and under it the same in Hindi.

Cards come after it, one by one, purple on the dark, and each holds long enough to be read. Then the crowd again, sadhus among it, saffron moving through the ordinary clothes.

A voice comes over all of it, slow, a man who has told this before and knows where it goes.

"Ye hai 2001 ka Kumbh — Prayagraj ka Kumbh. Is Kumbh mein lakhon log, har prant ke log aate hain. Har dharm ke dharmik guru. Rajguru. Dharm adhikari. Uttaradhikari. Pakshdharta. Kartadharta aate hain."

"Lekin is baar ye Kumbh alag hai. Kyunki jo dharmik guruon ka sangam ho raha hai, wo is baar Kumbh mein hone ja raha hai. Saare guruon, aghoriyon sabko ek khaas maksad ke liye ekatrit kiya ja raha hai."

## The Akhara

The round tent stands lit at night at the edge of the ground, and inside it is a hall. Red pillars go up into the lamplight, and the gathering sits in a circle on the floor, saffron and white and maroon, and a gold chhatra stands over the seat at the centre. A whiteboard stands to one side with nothing on it yet, and a blackboard behind it carries the moon in chalk, phase by phase.

Nobody is sitting still. The men are on their feet between the rows, turning, asking, and the noise goes round the circle and comes back.

"Shant raho! Shant raho!"

"Hum sabhi ko yahan is tarah kyun bulaya gaya hai? Kya wajah hai?"

"Hum sab tay tithi par hamesha pahadi ilakon mein milte the na. To aaj is Kumbh ke akhand akhade mein kyun? Koi to badi wajah hogi."

"Aisi bhi kya musibat aa gayi?"

"Musibat aayi nahin hai." The voice comes from the back of the hall. "Abhi tak aane wali hai."

The circle turns. He walks out into the open ground at the centre. Bearded, round glasses, a beige blazer over a black polo-neck. He lifts both hands.

"Baith jaiye. Aap sabhi log. Baith jaiye."

They sit, a row at a time, and the noise goes down with them. Nobody speaks.

The lamps burn low over the circle, and the gold chhatra throws its shadow across the empty seat.

"Mujhe maaf kijiyega ki maine aap sabhi ko is tarah yahan par bulaya." Music goes under the apology and is gone. He turns as he speaks, so that each side of the circle has his face in its turn. "Hamare dharm bhale hi alag-alag ho, par desh ek, khoon ek, junoon ek hai. Desh ki raksha, dushman deshon se raksha. Yahan tak ki dushman desh se aayi beemari ka waar, narsanhaar. Is desh par aayi har musibat ka tod nikala hai humne. Lade hain hum."

His spectacles catch the lamplight. The gold chhatra does not move. The circle sits inside its own silence.

"Jab jab vinaashkari grah-nakshatra saath hote hain, tab-tab is duniya mein pralay aati hai. Aur jab jab sitaron ne hamein ye sanket diye hain, hum isi tarah mile hain." He looks round the circle. "Aaj phir wahi sankat aaya hai."

"Kaisa sankat?"

"Sankat abhi aaya nahin hai. Aane wala hai." A beat. "Chaubees saal baad."

A guru is on his feet with his finger up.

"Aap kehna kya chahte hain?"

"Main nahin." The professor goes to the blackboard. "Grah-nakshatra kehte hain. Brihaspati jab baarah saal ki parikrama ke baad Vrishabh raashi mein pravesh karte hain, aur Surya Makar raashi mein pravesh karte hain — tab Prayagraj ke sangam pe Kumbh lagta hai. Kumbh chaar jagahon par lagta hai. Allahabad, Nashik, Ujjain aur Haridwar. Aur Allahabad mein baarah saal ke baad jo Kumbh lagta hai, use Mahakumbh kehte hain. Aur baarah Kumbh ke baad — yaani ke ek sau chawalis saalon ke baad — aata hai Maha Mahakumbh."

He lets the chalk down.

"Abhi 2001 hai. Aur 2025 mein Maha Mahakumbh hoga. Usi waqt vinaash shuru hoga. Hamla hoga. Dushman desh karega."

"Dushman desh to hamesha se hi hamla karte aaye hain."

"Haan. Par wo hamla bahut hi khatarnak hoga."

"Jo hamla chaubees saal baad hoga, uska aapko aaj kaise pata?"

"Ek professor hone ke naate — Bharat desh aur is duniya ke gyaan-vigyaan par maine saalon se research ki hai. Jis tarah logon ki kundali hoti hai, bilkul usi tarah jagah aur desh ki bhi kundali hoti hai. Bharat desh ki kundali se mujhe pata chala ki jo dushman desh hamare desh ki unnati aur pragati se jalte hain, wo karenge hamare desh par hamla."

The door of the hall stands open on the night, and nobody comes through it. Every face stays on him.

"Aur apni research siddh karne ke liye main Dakshin Bharat ke vishesh Nadi mandir mein gaya. Jahan Rishi Agastya ka likha Nadi Shastra granth aaj bhi hai. Us granth mein mera, aapka, har insaan ka bhavishya likha hai. Bharat ka bhavishya likha hai. Har desh ka bhagya pehle se likha ja chuka hai."

A dark room. A sage stands bare to the waist, his beard down to his belly, ash striped across his forehead. He holds palm leaves in both hands, from underneath, and the leaves are old enough to go to powder at a breath.

"Bharat." The sage looks up. "Ajeeb baat hai. Aapki khoj sahi nikli. Grahon ke hisaab se, do Maha Kumbh ke baad, yaani chaubees saal ke baad, Bharat pe koi bahut bada hamla hoga."

The hall again. The lamplight on every face.

"Nadi Shastra ke pandit ne kaha ki 2025 mein jo gan, jo ganana, jo grah, jo nakshatra, jo dasha Mahabharat ke yuddh mein thi, wahi banegi."

"To sankat aayega itne saalon baad, to hum abhi kyun mile?"

"Kyunki sankat hamare desh mein kadam rakh chuka hai." He points at the ground between his own feet. "Jo hamla, jo aakraman chaubees saal baad hone wala hai — uske liye dushman desh ke ghuspaithiyon ne apni jadein is desh mein abhi se banani shuru kar di hain. Border se khabrein aa chuki hain. Ghuspaithiye hamare border se hamare desh mein ghus rahe hain."

## The Suri House

The Suri house at night behind its white picket fence, and every window lit. A plate on the gate reads SURI, and under it the same in Gurmukhi.

Inside, the front room has been got ready for a birthday. Yellow bunting hangs across the wall in long loops, and balloons lie on the floor and stand against the walls, and the staircase goes up behind them into the dark. On a table there is a cake: one candle in it, and a name across the top. Vihaan. One candle, because there is one year to count.

Nimrat, in a pale blue salwar suit, folds clothes off a hanger onto a pile. Parmeet, bearded, in a lilac kurta-pyjama, is at the decorations. An open case lies packed half-full beside the pile. Music plays under them, and they work on inside it without looking at each other.

"O Nimrat!" He says it to the cushions. "Teri packing-shacking hui ki nahin? Phir wahan Kumbh mein jaake mat bolna ki ye bhool gayi, wo bhool gayi."

"Parmeet ji, tu chup na hue na—" A kurta comes off its hanger and goes onto the pile. "—to main apne aap ko bhi bhool jaaungi."

"Oye, sheti kar." She does not look up. "Tere puttar da cake bhi to kaatna hai. Aur phir subah room mein bhi to nikalna hai."

"Kar toh rahi hoon!"

She shakes her head at the case, and folds, and looks up at the cake, and her hands stop. She looks at the name for a while, small and private, and then looks away and goes on folding.

The bell rings.

"Itni raat ko kaun hai?"

"Wo doctor janaab honge." Parmeet is already up. "Khota itni late. Main dekhta hoon."

"Parmeet ji—"

He is at the door with his hand on it. Four men stand at the gate with bags. The one who does the talking has shaggy hair, a heavy moustache and a brown scarf. With him: a bearded man in a dark shawl, and a bald, bearded man in a red scarf.

"Ji?" Parmeet looks out. "Aap log…?"

The camera holds the moustached man's face. Figures pass through coils of barbed wire.

"Badi mushkil se sarhad paar karke tumhare desh mein aaye hain." The aside goes under the wire. "Tumhare desh ki barbadi yahin se shuru karte hain."

"Hum Kumbh ja rahe hain." The talker smiles with his mouth only. "Pyaas lagi hai. Paani milega?"

"Mauji! Tum ja rahe ho, aap log?" Parmeet waves them in with the door still in his hand. "Itna mausam kharab hai. Andar aaiye. Kuch kha-pi kar jaiye. Hum log bhi subah Kumbh mein ja rahe hain. Aaiye, aaiye, aaiye — aaiye na."

A black bag sits on the wooden floor. A hand lifts booklets out of it. Parmeet stands reading one. His face changes.

"Tum log Hindustani nahin ho." His voice has gone flat and loud, and his finger is up. "Tum dushman desh ke—"

A man seizes him from behind, an arm across his throat. Nimrat, in a doorway, cries out his name. The shot cuts to the outside of the house, and the screen goes white.

## The Keeping

The lane is a lane again under its lamps — dark between them, and the Suri gate standing open. Inside, the birthday is still up. The bunting hangs, the balloons lie where they fell, and the cake stands on the table with its one candle unlit and the name drying across the top.

The cot stands against the wall of the inner room. The boy in it is awake. He has cried himself out; his face is wet, and his fists are shut, and he looks at the lamp.

The keeper comes in with the brown scarf still round his neck. He lifts the boy out of the cot and turns him to the landing light, checking the face the way a man checks a seal. The boy's fist closes on the scarf and will not open.

"Bachcha?"

The keeper holds him a moment longer than he needs to.

"Zinda—" He looks down at the face on his shoulder. "Qabr ko mohalla yaad rakhta hai. Jeete ghar ko nahin. Ye ghar jiyega. Ye ladka — is ghar ka saboot hai. Bachcha jiyega."

He puts the boy down in the cot and folds a paper cap and sets it beside the rail. Then he goes out through the rooms, turning the lamps off one by one, and locks the gate with his own keys, and the lane keeps the dark.

Morning finds the house locked and the family vanished, and the neighbours phone and come and find the lock. What is inside the lock is a kept thing: Vihaan, a boy of one, asleep at last, with the paper cap beside the cot.

## She Has to Come

The hall at night, and the lamps burning low over the circle. The gold chhatra stands over the empty seat, and the professor stands in the open ground at the centre, and every face is lifted to him.

The circle sits without a sound. The lamplight moves on the gold chhatra when the air moves.

"Tabahi ki shuruaat ho chuki hai. Lekin asli tabahi lekar aayega 2025."

"Lekin is musibat ka koi hal to hoga? Usse hamein kaun bachayega?"

"Wo bachayegi." He says it quietly, and the hall goes quiet to hear it. "Use aana hi hoga. Kaun hai wo?"

No one answers. The lamps burn, and the circle waits, and the professor lets the silence stand.

"Hamara desh bahut mahaan hai. Jab-jab Kaal aata hai, tab-tab Vishnu aur Shiv kisi-na-kisi roop mein aa jaate hain. Desh ko bachate hain. Us hamle se ladne ke liye bhi wahi bhejenge use."

The circle does not stir. A monk in maroon draws his shawl closer, and the whiteboard stands blank at the edge of the light.

"Kise?"

The finger stays up. The lamplight runs along it.

"Wo." His finger is up. "Use aana hoga. Chaaron dishaayein. Teenon lok. Yahan tak ki khud Brahmand bhi uske saamne khada ho jaye use rokne ke liye — wo tab bhi nahin rukegi. Wo aayegi. Kaun?"

The words go round the circle. Nobody looks away from him.

"Na naari, na nar." Music comes up under him. "Na jaanwar, na insaan. Na shaitaan, na farishta. Na Bhagwan. Par Bhagwan ki sabse badi bhakt. Sashakt. Shiv ki vardani."

The hall is still. The lamps burn on, and nobody speaks.

"Aur Sheshnaag ke is kul ka to ant ho chuka hai."

"Jis Shesh Naag par Vishnu ji ka haath ho. Shiv ji ka saath ho. Nageshwar." He lets it sit. "Uske kul ka ant kabhi nahin ho sakta."

The answer settles over the circle. A lamp gutters beside the seat, and a monk's hand steadies it.

"Us kul ka vaaris — kaun?"

"Naagrani bankar aayegi." The professor lifts one finger. "Anant kul ki. Ananta."

"Aur uske saath?"

"Nagaraj bankar aayega." The second finger joins the first. "Anant kul ka. Anantveer."

His spectacles catch the lamplight. Nobody in the circle moves.

"Hamein wo milegi kahan?"

"Anantpur ki Naagrani, Ananta. Aapko milegi." He turns the leaf in his hands. "Uttari pahadiyon ke khatm hone ke baad, shehar ki sarhad ki pehli gali. Jis ghar ke bahar ek chhota Shivling hai. Us ghar mein. Wahin wo milegi. Ananta."

The leaf is still in his hands. The circle waits for the rest.

"Aur uska Nagaraj?"

"Uska naam patte par hai. Uska pata nahin."

"Tumhein uske paas jaana hoga, aur batana hoga — chaubees saal baad jo Mahavinash aane wala hai. Usse ladne ke liye use taiyaar karna hoga."

"Par use dhoondhne jaayega kaun?"

"Main jaaunga." He looks round the whole circle. "Dhoondhunga use. Wo Naaglok ki dhadkan hai. Shesh Naagin ke saare gunon se sampann hai wo."

## Papa's Research

A study at night with one lamp on the desk, and the rest of the room standing in the dark round it: books to the ceiling, a whiteboard, a wall of papers pinned in rows. The professor is bent over the desk, a navy blazer now over a red polo-neck.

"Papa."

The boy stands in the door. The professor turns, and his face changes, and he waves him in. On the desk lies a large book with an ornate embossed metal cover.

"Ise sambhal ke rakhna." His hand is flat on the metal. "Ye, ye Papa ki research hai. Ise kabhi kisi ko mat dena. Ho sakta hai ki Papa ki jaan ko khatra ho."

The boy hugs the book to his chest. The papers on the wall do not move.

"Papa, ye kya bol rahe ho? Aap kahan ja rahe ho? Mat jaiye."

"Mujhe jaana padega, beta, Ananta ke paas." He fixes the boy's collar without looking at it. "Meri calculation ke hisaab se yahi sahi waqt hai. Use batana padega ki tabahi desh ke darwaze par dastak de chuki hai."

"Ek baat hamesha yaad rakhna, beta." His hand stays on the boy's head. "Desh ka rishta sabse bada rishta hota hai. Mujhe kuch bhi ho jaye — bas ek baat yaad rakhna, ki tere pita desh ke liye shaheed ho gaye. Aur tu bhi hamesha sabse aage apne desh ko rakhna."

Music comes up, and holds while neither of them moves.
