# Chapter 32 — The Guest (Part 2)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 21, 7 March 2026.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Breakdown

The road runs empty under a dead street lamp, and their car sits crooked across it with its doors open.

"Andar mujh pe chhod do, Bharani."

Ahana checks the road both ways.

"RAW chief keh rahe the — train incident mein kisi ne unki jaan bachayi thi. Lekin wo jo bhi tha — saamne nahin aaya."

"Pata nahin kaun tha wo."

Bharani keeps her voice low.

"Ab sab log baar-baar adrishya shakti aur ichchhadhari naag-naagin ke baare mein baat karenge — to in saare incidents ko unhi se related karenge."

Her dupatta snaps once in the night wind, and she pins it down.

"Lekin RAW chief hai ki in saari baaton ko maante hi nahin."

Ahana shakes her head.

"Wo to in saari baaton ko man-gadhant aur andhvishwas kehte hain."

A car climbs the rise at the road's end.

Under the wind, the score comes up.

"Kya ho gaya? Sir — sir — wo meri gaadi kharab ho gayi hai. Aap please help kar dijiye na. Dekhiye — itni raat ho chuki hai, aur upar se itni sunsaan road hai. Door-door tak koi nazar bhi nahin aa raha hai."

"Sir please — dekhiye. Sir — check kar lijiye na ek baari. Dekh lijiye na — kya dikkat hai."

Bharani folds her hands.

"Dekho — kya problem hai."

"Haan — main dekhta hoon."

He is kind — and kindness is the door they walk through.

The men get down, and one of them leaves his door swinging.

"Battery to bilkul theek hai."

"Sir — mujhe lagta hai shayad gaadi ke andar se koi wire nikal gaya hai. Aap please andar jaake ek baar check kar sakte hain?"

"Dekho — shayad taar-vaar ka kuch lafda hua hai andar se. Chalo."

Hands move in the dark — and both of them are out cold on the roadside.

The score stays on over them — one note, held.

Their car ticks as it cools, and its keys hang in the lock.

"Ab ye dono kuch der tak behosh hi rahenge."

Ahana looks up at the moon.

"Aaj poornima ki raat hai — isliye saari jaankaari lekar fauran lautna padega. Chalo."

## The False Stone

The yard lies open under the full moon, and every face turns up to it.

"Aaj poornima ki raat hai."

The voice shakes.

"Aaj pata chal jaayega — ki ye naagmani asli hai ya nakli."

"Is naagmani ko chaand ki roshni mein rang badalna tha."

A hand holds the stone up to the moon.

"Magar ye to aisi ki aisi hai. Iska matlab ye hua — ki ye naagmani nahin hai. Ye to sirf ek chamkeela patthar hai."

The stone lies dull on the cloth, moonlight running off it unchanged.

The dark growls — and a scream tears out of it.

"Baba aa gaya!"

The whole yard is afraid.

The score comes up under the screaming.

"Pasha — aaj naagmani ne rang nahin badla kyonki chandra grahan laga hua hai."

Laughter breaks over the fear.

"Are — "

"Tantra-gyaan tha — tantra-gyaan tha. Kisi ne to rang nahin badla."

Breathless laughter shakes the yard.

"Bach gaye — phir se bach gaye. Kya kismat hai tumhaari."

The lamp in the niche jumps, and shadows run up the wall.

"Sirf ek mahina hai tumhaare paas — aur ye aakhri ek mahina hai. Iske baad ye samajh lena — ki kaal tumhaare peeche tumhaara grahan lagaane aane hi waala hai."

"Aur agar tumne kuch bhi galat kiya — to ye soch lo — hamaara bachcha is baar tum pe rehem nahin karega. Aur phir hamaare ye Baba se tumhein koi nahin bacha sakta."

The score comes up under it — low, and in no hurry.

The growls settle.

The stone does not change.

## The Doctrine

The war room hums under its tube lights, and two chairs stand empty at the table.

One tube light flickers over the empty chairs.

"Mujhe Ana se baat karni hogi."

An officer looks up from his phone.

"Aap theek to hain?"

"Haan — fine. Ye dono kahan reh gaye?"

"Sorry — humein thodi der ho gayi."

The pair comes in.

"Koi baat nahin — aap dono baithiye. Hum log aap hi ka wait kar rahe the."

A glass of water stands untouched at every place, and the files lie closed down the table's middle.

"As we all know — ki hum sab yahan kyon ikattha hue hain."

The senior officer looks round the table.

"Aur hamaare secret ki wajah se kitne saare dushman humein jaan se maarna chahte hain."

Under the table, the score comes up.

"Desh ka Ramosh ban raha hai."

A scientist takes it up.

"Ek aisa equipment — jo Rambaan ki tarah kaam karega. Aur dushman desh ke hone waale har baarudi hamle ko dhwast kar dega."

"Haan — Ramosh Bharat ka ek anokha abhedya suraksha kavach hai. Dushman se hamla hote hi ye turant activate hota hai. Missile ko detect karta hai — aur use maar giraata hai."

Under it, the strings come up — the power, at full voice.

A pen moves down the margin of the open page.

"Ye poora system bees din mein taiyaar ho jaayega. And I wish — ki hamaare group ka chhatha member bhi hamaare saath hota — aur is Ramosh missile defence system ko safal hote dekh paata. Lekin use desh ke dushmanon ne maar diya."

"Khair — is samay hum sab ki jaan ko khatra hai. Kyonki hum sab ke paas khud ki alag-alag ek aisi technique aur parts hain — jisko mila ke hi Ramosh ko banaaya jaa sakta hai. Am I right, officer?"

"Yes."

Fear goes round the table.

Outside, on the roadside, the two left behind open their eyes.

"Are — ye kya hua tha? Wo achanak se behosh kaise ho gaye? Wo ladkiyaan kahan gayi?"

"Pata nahin kya hua. Ab humein der nahin karni chaahiye. Humein meeting mein pahunchna hai — turant. Haan — chalo."

Their car starts at the second turn of the keys.

"Are — sorry-sorry — hum log thoda late ho gaye."

They burst through the doors.

"Hum log ladkiyon ki car start kara rahe the — aur pata nahin achanak se kaise behosh ho gaye. Isliye meeting miss ho gayi."

"Miss ho gayi? But aap log to andar the na?"

"Ye sab baatein hum baad mein karenge. Abhi humein der ho rahi hai. Chalen — chalo."

Under the confusion, quick strings.

The doors shut behind them, and the question stays standing in the corridor.

## The Giants

The file room sleeps under one bulb, and the wall holds one photograph bigger than the rest.

"Ye mere real dad hain."

Aryaman stops before the big photograph.

"Ye na ek scientist the. Bahut bade scientist. Poori duniya mein inka naam tha."

Ahana knows the face before he finishes — the cupboard-kept face, carried in her eyes since his room.

But here — on this wall — how?

Under it, the sting — one knock, and gone.

Shock goes through her like a current.

"Chalo."

Aryaman is already moving.

"Kahan?"

"Maaf karna — aaj mera pehla din hai yahan."

The peon freezes with the broken frame in his hands.

"Dekho na — safai kar raha tha. Hawa se ye photo gir ke toot gaya."

"Koi baat nahin."

A staffer waves it off.

"Main theek karwa deta hoon."

"Shukriya."

"Vaise ye hai kaun? Inki itni badi photo yahan lagi thi."

"Are — badi photo to lagegi na? Ye scientist bhi bahut bade the. Vishal Puri sahab. Hamaare jo babu log hain na — inhi ki koi file dhoondh rahe hain. Agar inki wo file mil jaati — to in logon ka bahut kaam aasaan ho jaata."

The broken glass winks from the frame in the peon's hands.

Yaani Parmeet Suri ne ek anaath bachche ko god nahin liya — balki Hindustan ke ek bahut bade scientist ke bete ko god liya tha.

Par ek Hindustani bachche ko god lene ke peeche un Changistaniyon ka maksad ho kya sakta hai?

Kyonki kuch to aisa zaroor hai — jo wo Aryaman se ab bhi chaahte hain.

Magar kya?

Shayad is baat ki khabar Aryaman ko bhi nahin — Aryaman apne aas-paas ke maujood khatre se bekhabar — nahin, main aisa nahin hone de sakti.

Mujhe Aryaman ko bachana hoga — kisi bhi haal mein.

Low strings go under it all.

Duty says it — her house's, nothing else.

The broken frame goes back on its nail, glass and all.

## The Diamond

The living room burns its lamps late, and the whole house crowds around the hurt man.

"Jaldi se first-aid box lekar aao. Jaao."

"Ahana beta — "

"Bhai sahab — ek minute. Wo lekar aa rahi hai — bas do minute."

Vihaan stands at his father's shoulder, and moves the lamp closer without being told.

"Dad — kya hua aapko?"

Ahana kneels with the box.

"Are — kuch nahin."

Parmeet waves it off.

"Tum kahan the, beta?"

Parmeet catches Aryaman's sleeve.

"Maine kahan-kahan nahin dhoondha tumhein — kab se wait kar raha tha tumhaara."

"Haan — aur tumhaare intezaar mein wo doctor ke paas bhi nahin gaye."

"Ye kisne maara?"

"Are — kiski himmat jo mujhe maare?"

Parmeet laughs it off.

"Thoda accident ho gaya — are, chhodo na, beta."

"Ji — aap mujhe ye batao."

Aryaman turns on her.

"Wo jo aapne nayaab heera mujhe diya tha — wo asli tha na?"

"Kyon — ye kya hua?"

"Nahin-nahin — kuch nahin. Wo valuation ke liye main jeweller ke paas le gaya tha — to use zara shak tha."

"Dad — aap keh rahe hain ki main aapse jhooth bol rahi hoon?"

Ahana's voice rises.

"Maa — aap mujh pe shak kar rahe hain — ki maine aapko nakli heera diya? Seriously? Maine — Ahana Dhawan ne — inhein nakli heera diya — nahin!"

"Nahin-nahin, beta — "

Parmeet backpedals with both hands up.

"Main — main to wo — "

"Aap theek karein — are, wo jhooth bola hai. Wo Ujjwal ne jhooth bola tha."

"Dad — what the hell — mujhe abhi batao — ye kya — "

"Ye kya hua — ye Pinky — mujhe de — are-are — Dad, aap baithiye-baithiye, please."

The room holds its breath through the shouting.

"Vihaan — roshni idhar."

Ahana holds out her hand, and he sets the lamp steady beside her.

"Are beta — main theek hoon."

Parmeet tries to rise.

"Nahin, Dad — aap theek nahin hain. Aur aap na — please careful kariye. Aap jaante hain na — aapko zara si bhi takleef hoti hai to mera kya haal hota hai."

"Achcha — itni gehri chot lagi hai? Dikhaaiye."

"Bhai sahab — bas-bas-bas — ek aur baar — ek aur baar — bas-bas-bas."

The first-aid box clicks shut between dressings.

"Aaram se, Dad," Vihaan says, and steadies the lamp.

"Are — unhone to mujhe apne sage bete se zyaada pyaar kiya hai."

Aryaman does not look up from the bandage.

"Isliye — main apne bhaiyon ke liye — apne Dad ke liye — kuch bhi kar sakta hoon. Main mar bhi sakta hoon."

Nobody answers the debt.

The lamp burns on between them.

Pity rises in her — and has nowhere to go.

Ahana watches how he loves his Dad — how he loves the whole family — while all of them use him, and the poor thing does not even know.

Vihaan's hand rests once on his father's shoulder — careful, the way he is with him — and lifts.

## The Road

The car runs dark between sleeping fields, and the road throws nothing back.

"Ajeeb baat hai."

The elder of the two stares out.

"Jab humne apne group mein bataaya ki hum late hain — to kisi ko koi fark nahin pada. Aur sab log normal the."

"Haan — mujhe bhi kuch theek nahin lag raha hai. Hum dono achanak se gaadi mein behosh ho gaye. Aur jab hosh aaya — to dono ladkiyaan gaayab thi. Aur upar se hamaare advisor ka behad behrahmi se murder ho gaya. Mujhe to pakka yakeen hai — ki hum dono pe bhi koi nazar rakh raha hai."

"Sahi keh rahe ho. Humein aur bhi zyaada alert rehna padega."

"Yes — you are right."

The rear-view mirror shows nothing but the empty road behind.

Dread rides in the back seat.

"Ye kya ho gaya? Suno — main tumhein thodi der mein call karta hoon."

Screams tear the dark open ahead — bachao, koi bachao, koi hai — and cut off into nothing.

The car door slams, and the dark gives back no answer.

Mera andaaza galat nahin ho sakta.

In Changistaniyon ne zaroor kisi bade maksad ke liye Aryaman ko god liya hai.

Aryaman ke dad ek scientist the — jo ek bahut bade mission pe kaam kar rahe the.

Par kaun se?

Aur aisa kya hai Aryaman ke paas — jise paane ke liye Parmeet ne use itne pyaar se paala?

Kuch to hai.

Shayad is baat ki khabar Aryaman ko bhi nahin.

Mujhe use bachana hoga — kisi bhi haal mein.

Duty says it — the house's.

Nothing else.

PMO officer ki rahasyamayi maut.

The car radio breaks in.

"Ji haan — kal raat ke andhere mein PMO officer ki hatya kar di gayi. Is ghatna ko kisne anjaam diya? Na koi gawah — na koi saboot. Ye chal kya raha hai?"

Pehle advisor — phir PMO officer.

Aise jaan jaana — ye sab koi ittefaq nahin lagta.

## The Telecast

The night presses at her window, and her phone lights up on the table.

"Bolo. Kya kar rahi thi?"

"Kuch soch rahi thi."

"Mere baare mein?"

"Mere itne bure din bhi nahin aaye."

"Relax. Mere baare mein sochne ke liye bhi appointment lena padta hai."

"Ye bataane ke liye phone kiya hai? Nahin — bulaane ke liye. Chal — office aa ja, fauran."

"Kyon? Ek bahut zaroori kaam hai. Tu aa — bataata hoon."

Her keys are in her hand before the call ends.

"Ab — kahan lost ho? Ab kahin nahin — wo kuch nahin. Tumne mujhe yahan kyon bulaaya? News read karne ke liye? News read — main? Aur main ye kyon karoongi?"

"Kyonki train haadse pe — I mean, jo haadsa thankfully hote-hote bach gaya — us pe jo tumne news reading ki thi — uski maximum ratings aayi hain. Aur isliye main chaahata hoon — ki ab tum mere saath ek bahut important piece pe kaam karo."

The ratings lie printed on paper on his desk.

"Come on — are you sure? Kyonki us din main sirf 20 second ke liye aayi thi. Wo bhi tumhaare paas koi anchor nahin tha — backup option. Aur tum achche se jaante ho — ki main bahut rich hoon — aur ye sab karne ki mujhe vaise bhi zaroorat nahin hai."

"Rich hona koi achievement nahin hota, Ahana. Apne talent ka sahi istemaal karna achievement hota hai. Okay — don't believe me. Dekhte hain — social media tumhaare baare mein kya kehta hai. Come on — oh wow — ise dekho. Ye lo — top comment — very hot girl."

"Kaun hai ye? Aur uske baad — wow."

He scrolls.

"Aur ye dekho — mujhe inke saath business karne mein koi problem nahin hai. Lekin tumhaare business partner ne iski photo nahin lagaayi. Gumnaam admirer lagta hai."

He turns the screen so she can see.

"You know what? Yahi problem hai tum ladkon ka. Do din baad Women's Day aa raha hai — tab dekhna — hazaaron post karenge — ki ladkiyaan kitni mahaan hoti hain, unki kitni respect karni chaahiye. Par baaki din — unhone kya pehna hai — good looks — inhi sab pe comment karenge. Right?"

"Nahin — ismein problem kya hai? Agar koi respectfully tumhaari taareef kar raha hai — to unka compliment le lene mein kya harj hai? Kya matlab?"

"Matlab ye dekho — isne likha hai ki ab se orange iska favourite colour hai. Kyonki us din tumne orange pehna tha. So there you go — isne tumhaari pasand ko apni pasand bana liya. I think that's supposed to be sweet."

"You are right. Tumhaara favourite colour kaun sa hai?"

"Orange. Pehle se tha — hamesha se tha."

She almost smiles, and does not.

Her reflection listens from the glass.

"Tum mere cabin mein aao. Look — main chaahata hoon tum mere saath — "

"Main chaahata hoon tum mere saath ye host karo. Meri final telecast."

"Final telecast?"

The cabin's glass wall throws their reflections back at them.

"Hmm. Tumne mujhse kaha tha na — ye log mujhe yahan se hataana chaahte hain. Aur ab mujhe bhi realise ho gaya hai — ki Purav ke aane aur Vikram ke mujhse itni nafrat karne ke baad — yahan pe koi meri side pe nahin hai. Jaise hi mere cousins ko shares milenge — wo mujhe is channel se hata denge. And that's fine. Agle 48 ghante mein ek board meeting hai — aur us board meeting ke khatm hote hi — "

Pity — she has read this verdict already.

"Main yahan se out ho jaaoonga. Aur isiliye main ye feature karna chaahata hoon. Chhe log India ko bachaane ke liye kuch bana rahe hain. Lekin unmein se do logon ki — under mysterious circumstances — death ho gayi."

"Ye sab information tumhein kisne di?"

"Journalist hoon. Baaki sources hain mere paas."

The newsroom hums through the glass.

"Lekin mujhe pata lagaana hai — ki wo log kaun hain jo mere Bharat ka bura chaahte hain. In dushmanon ko to mujhe bhi khatm karna hai."

"Kya? Kya kaha tumne?"

"Ab — main — main bhi yahi chaahati hoon — ki desh ke dushman khatm ho jaayein. Aur sorry — tum kya bata rahe the wo?"

"Chhe log the — jo India ko bachaane ke liye kuch bana rahe the. Ab chaar bache hain. Aur main chaahata hoon — ki wo agle saat din mein is cheez ko bana dein — kyonki ye actually India ke liye ek bahut badi strength hogi. Main Changistaan pe ek feature karna chaahata hoon. Main agle 48 ghante ke liye ise ek sansanikhez news banaana chaahata hoon. Aur isliye main chaahata hoon — ki tum — tum ise mere saath host karo. Kyonki ye mera final — "

Under it, one tasha knock — a decision, not a feeling.

"Telecast hoga — aur ye telecast dhamaakedaar hoga. Aur isiliye iska host bhi dhamaakedaar hona chaahiye."

"Yes."

The yes is the work's.

Nothing else is in the room.

## The Friend

The room sits low under its lamps, and the news has reached it before them.

"Ye jo kuch scientists wahan kaam kar rahe hain — kisi ne in pe hamla kiya hai."

Airul looks round the room.

"Lekin ye hamla to humne nahin kiya. Kumbh — Amarnath yatra — in dono pe humne hamla kiya. Lekin ye hamla humne nahin kiya."

A glass sweats untouched on the table.

"To phir kisne kiya? Are — kaun hai wo — jo hamaara kaam hamaare liye kar raha hai?"

Airul turns from the wall.

"Kaun hai wo — hamaara gumnaam dost — jo Hindustan se mohabbat karne waalon ko maut ke ghaat utaarna chaahta hai? Hamaari tarah — "

Something in the room is uneasy.

Nobody finishes it.

The lamp burns on over the unsigned papers.

## The Moon

Their room holds the poornima moon in its window, and the lamp burns low beside the bed.

She comes in from the night, and the door shuts soft behind her.

Vihaan sleeps with the file shut under his hand — her husband, whole and breathing, owed nothing tonight.

"Main aa gayi."

She says it to the dark, and does not need him to hear.

She lies down beside him, and the day lets go of her by inches.

In his sleep his arm finds her, the way sleep knows and he does not.

His breathing slows, and hers slows beneath it.

He shields.

Nothing in him wakes to claim it.

She is safe — and knows it.

The moon-spent body stands down its half-brace.

The shed-night's tenderness settles.

The ache quiets.

She sleeps through.

The lamp steadies.

The wind stills at the window.

The moon keeps its watch.

They will wake whole, and remember nothing.

The night helped — that is all.
