# Chapter 2 — The Year of the Mahakumbh

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 1, "Naagin Legacy Unleashed" (second part).*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## 2025

The year 2025. The Maha Mahakumbh has come, after a full hundred and forty-four years.

The sangam at Prayagraj under a wide bright sky. Bridges of pontoons across the water, and crowds on them in every direction, and tents going back to the horizon. Flags. A helicopter crossing high above the gathering. The water is brown and wide, and the boats on it are low and crowded. A voice speaks over all of it.

"The whole world will bow to India. It will congratulate India. India will hold the keys of the world. Atithi devo bhava, do we not say it in our country? This is not only a Mahakumbh. It is the crown on India's head. Prayagraj is calling the whole world."

## The Aroras' House

A house in Delhi. Brick arches, and a flight of stairs going up from the hall, and photographs in frames along the wall.

The newspaper lies folded on the arm of the sofa. The tea is in a glass. A brass tray stands on the low table with two biscuits on it. 

A big painted panel stands where everyone can see it. It is a portrait of Shiva, with a trident worked in silver across it.

An old man sits on the sofa with his tea. He is grey now, in a white kurta and a mustard waistcoat. Across from him his wife sits in a dark printed sari, her glasses on, a shawl over one shoulder.

"So much has changed. We have come from Amritsar to Delhi."

"Hmm."

"But you have not changed. The tickets for Prayagraj are still not booked."

"What do you think? Will somebody come from the Kumbh and take us along with them?"

"Arre Shalini ji, do not worry about that. I have made the whole arrangement." He sets his cup down. "By the way, there has been no sound of anything breaking since morning. Where are all my dolls?"

"Ananta!"

"Both of them are not at home."

"Not at home? Where have the two of them gone?"

"Shopping. Your Ananta cannot swallow a bite until she has been to the market." She turns a page. "Arre, our Ananta is a menace."

He laughs into his tea, and she says it again. The clock on the wall ticks. The newspaper slides off the arm of the sofa and he catches it without looking.

## A Son Comes Home

A gate with a nameplate on the pillar, SURI in English and the same name under it in Gurmukhi. A car stops outside and the driver gets out to open the back door.

The man who gets out is twenty-four. He is taller than the driver, in a dark shirt with the sleeves rolled and no jacket over it. He has one bag.

He stands on the pavement a moment and looks at the house. Then he puts his hand flat on the nameplate as he goes past it. A man does that who has been away a long time.

Inside, the hall is full of flowers and work. The floor is marble and the flowers are in buckets on the newspapers. Two women are arguing over a list. A caterer's boy carries a crate of glasses through, breathing hard. Somebody has moved every chair. A man stands on a stool with a garland in both hands. He is not tall enough for the hook.

Pinky sees him first and screams. "Vihaan!"

Nisha drops the list. "You said tomorrow!"

"I said I would come. I did not say when." He puts his bag down. Both of them hug him at once, and he stands it without moving his feet. "The flight was early. Nobody was at the airport, so I took a taxi."

"Nobody was at the airport?" Pinky's hands go to her cheeks. "I told Driver to go! I told him at six!"

"He went. To the old terminal." He is looking past her at the hall. At the flowers. At the crate of glasses. At the panel being carried out through the other door. "What is all this?"

"The party. Tonight." Nisha is already pulling at his sleeve. "Aryaman bhai is getting Journalist of the Year. Third time. He does not know we know. We have done his whole newsroom up like a mandap, and he will shout at us, and then he will come out with us, because Papa and Baljeet uncle are coming to the channel."

"Does he know that?"

"He will find out." Pinky takes his other arm. "You are coming with us. We need a man. There are lights to be hung and nobody to hold the ladder."

"I have been in a plane for nine hours."

"You have been in London for years, bhai. Nine hours is nothing."

He looks at the two of them, and at the hall, and he does not argue. "Where is Papa?"

"On the phone since morning. Everybody in Delhi is calling him about the Kumbh." Nisha waves at the stairs, where a door stands shut. "Go and put your bag in your room. Then come down, and we will go."

He picks up the bag. 

On the wall by the stairs there are photographs in frames. Two boys at a cricket match. A family at a wedding. A big house with a lawn.

He goes up, and the house goes on shouting under him. 

His room is at the end of the corridor. The door has his initials on a brass plate. The bed inside is made up and has never been slept in.

## The Bazaar

The market is a wall of colour. Shopfronts stacked with bangles and dupattas. Racks of saris out on the pavement. A row of mirrors where the women stand trying earrings. A fruit cart. A boy with a tray of chai. A loudspeaker playing a song nobody listens to.

A snake charmer sits on the ground with his basket on a square of cloth. The basket is woven of green cane. There are coins in the cloth already. His flute is long and dark. He wears an orange kurta, a black cloth tied round his head, and beads at his throat. His hair is long and thick and tied back. He lifts the lid and puts his flute to his lips.

"Naagraj, come out."

The crowd backs off and then leans in. A woman in a green sari puts her hand on her mouth. A boy is pushed forward and pulled back again.

The cobra comes out of the basket and crosses the stones with its head up. A woman in the crowd screams and holds her shopping to her chest. The flute goes on, and the crowd goes back a step and then comes forward two.

"Madam, put this snake in the basket quickly!"

Ananta stands in the middle of the crowd in a dark pink blouse and sari. Her hair is open, and her earrings catch the light. She does not move back.

"Sheshnaag carries the whole world on his hood. And you want to shut this one in a basket?"

The woman will not be quiet. "Madam, if you do not shut it up, it will bite somebody. It will bite somebody."

"The snake is Shiva's favourite." Ananta's voice carries over the crowd, and the crowd goes quiet to hear her. "They have no voice of their own. They see what nobody else can see. If you keep them with you, then look after them."

She goes down on her heels in front of the cobra and holds out her hand. The snake comes up out of the stones and puts its hood into her palm. It rises. The crowd pulls back with one breath. The flute stops.

"Har Har Mahadev."

"Har Har Mahadev. Har Har Mahadev. Har Har Mahadev."

The charmer is on his feet, bowing with his hands together. The snake is up in her hand, its hood spread against the light. Ananta smiles at it. The whole bazaar is watching her.

A stout woman in a yellow salwar suit pushes through to her side. "Ananta, whatever you know, it is all magic. Today you have done magic on a snake too. You are not a girl at all, you are a magic wand. A wand!"

"Thank you." Ananta sets the snake back in its basket herself, and the charmer shuts the lid and bows again. "Where is Purvi? She did not come with me today."

"Your sister?" The woman looks around. "Which sister? She was standing here just now."

## The Jhumkas

At the jewellery stall a girl is bent over a tray with her glasses almost on the glass. The tray is green velvet. The earrings standing in it are silver and heavy. They swing when the stall takes an elbow from the crowd. She wears large dark spectacles, two plaits, a brown cardigan over a green muffler, and thick socks. A blue card hangs from a lanyard round her neck.

The shopkeeper calls after her. "Arre, Purvi! You have given the money. Are you going to go off without the earrings?"

"Sorry. Sorry. Sorry."

"You are lucky I saw you." He puts the jhumkas into her hand. "Here. These were what you wanted, na?"

Ananta comes up beside her. "These are the ones I have been looking for since morning. I will wear these to the party tonight."

"Look at them carefully first." Purvi holds one up. "Careful. All right. Keep them here."

"I swear, Purvi. Let me once find those boys who tripped you and threw you down. I will not let them go."

Purvi puts the jhumkas away in her bag and shuts it. "Leave all that. You tell me instead why my interview call has not come from your news channel yet."

"Why do you get so troubled about everything? I have told you, na, it will all happen. Do not worry."

"It will happen." Purvi's mouth goes crooked. "It will happen."

Ananta puts an arm round her sister's shoulders and walks her out of the crowd. Behind them the stout woman watches them go and shakes her head.

"Your sister is colder than the snows of Kailash. In the age of the BMW she is an auto-rickshaw." She points down the street. "Look, there goes your auto."

## Home: The Tickets

The Arora hall in the afternoon. Lamps lit on the side tables. The painted panel of Shiva over the stairs. Candles stand in a row on the dining table, not yet lit. The whole house smells of the flowers in the brass pot at the foot of it. The family comes in one behind another, and the old man is at the door before anybody.

"Purvi. Why are you walking like that?"

"Papa, nothing has happened." Purvi is limping, and she tries to walk straight. "I got a small hurt. I fell in the market."

"She fell in the market." Ananta has her by the arm. "Some boys pushed her."

"Accha, listen to me." Shalini sits down with her sari pleated over her knee. "You booked the tickets for Prayagraj for the Mahakumbh, na? It was your job."

"Ji." Purvi's hand goes to the blue card at her neck. "One minute."

The old man cannot keep still. "There. Now we have had the darshan of the Mahakumbh. That is why nobody booked the tickets."

"Look, beta, tell me. The calendar you go about with round your neck. There will be something written in it, na?"

"Hanh, hanh, one minute, I am telling you." She turns the card over and reads it. Her face falls. "What happened?"

"You forgot to write it."

"Sorry." Her voice goes small. "I forgot to write it."

"So tell me now, how did we get the darshan of the Mahakumbh?"

A young man comes down the last steps with his phone in his hand and a hood over his head. "Arre, it will be done. Ticket, ticket, ticket, Papa ji. Ticket, I am your darling child. I am not like this Purvi of yours. Ghajini, who forgets one thing while she is saying the next." He grins round the room. "A dimwit like this, and she wants to be a journalist."

Purvi's eyes fill. "Mummy."

"Mummy, mummy, mummy. What has happened?" Shalini looks up. "Have you got a cold?"

"I am crying."

Ananta turns on her brother. He is already going up the stairs, laughing. She follows him with her finger up at his back, and the old man calls after both of them, and the lamps burn on the tables.

Ananta comes back and sits down beside Purvi and puts an arm round her. "Sorry. You are not a dimwit. You are a first-class sticky-pudding." She pushes Purvi's plait back over her shoulder. "You are a first-class sticky-pudding, and you are my jaan."

## Two Women

Evening. The gate of the Arora house, the street outside, a car parked a little way along the road with its lights off. The street lamp comes on while they are standing there. A dog crosses and goes under the car and does not come out.

Ananta stands at the gate and looks down the street. "Purvi. That boy is a nuisance, always troubling you. But those two women. Who are they? They have been standing and looking at our house for a long time."

"Which women?" Purvi comes up beside her. "They were standing right here just now. Where have they gone?"

The street is empty. The car is empty.

Two women walk away round the corner, one old in a pale sari, one young. Their pace is unhurried, and they do not look back.

"We have been keeping watch on our Maharani for so many months. But today she has seen us." The older one's voice is warm, and her eyes are wet with something that is not grief. "2025 has come. The time has come when the great war will be fought, and she does not know how large a part of that war she will be. She will be the great warrior of it. Yes. And she will save this country."

The younger one stops and turns and folds her hands to the house behind her.

"To the great naagin of the Anant kul, the salutation of us ichchhadhari naagins."

## The Inhaler

Ananta's room in the evening. A mirror with bulbs round it. A dressing table with the creams and the bottles in a row. A lamp with a beaded shade. A framed photograph turned a little towards the bed. A phone face-down among the creams, and a velvet tray of earrings open beside it.

A sari is laid out on the bed, lemon-yellow, with the fall of it over the edge.

"Bey, open the door! I need the inhaler."

The door opens. Ananta is in a lemon-yellow sari, a jhumka in her hand.

"The door is open, the door is open. Since when have you been looking for the inhaler? I cannot find the inhaler. You are not opening the door." Purvi is going through the drawers. "Where have I put it down? Oh, here it is."

"You are going somewhere?"

"Oh ho, Purvi. You forget everything too." Ananta turns back to the mirror and puts the jhumka to her ear. "I told you, na, there is a party in my office today. My boss is getting the biggest award of journalism for a sting operation on a minister. It has not been announced yet, but all of us in the office are sure the award is going to him. So all the arrangements for the party were made long ago."

Purvi sits on the end of the bed with the inhaler in her hand. She watches her sister in the mirror.

"Your life is so pretty, na." She says it without any bitterness. "You yourself are so pretty. Actually, you are a genius, smart, all of it. You run the whole house. You are the one everybody keeps looking at." She turns the inhaler round in her fingers. "And then there is me, who nobody looks at."

Ananta comes and sits down beside her on the bed, in her yellow sari, her earrings swinging.

"You are very sweet. And you are my jaan, and you are so pretty that I cannot tell you how much." She takes Purvi's chin in her hand. "Truly. Now come on, get ready quickly. You are coming to the party with me."

"Where? What will I do at a party?"

"You want it, na? You want the job at my news channel, na? I will get you that job."

"But how will you get me a job? You are a fashion designer. You make the anchors' clothes. Who is going to give you importance?"

"Importance will be given. You want the job, na? I will get it for you. You sit and get ready."

"You are talking as if you will do magic."

"I do not know about magic." Ananta gets up, and her sari swings, and she picks up her phone. "But I have got some superpowers."

Purvi laughs, and wipes her eyes, and stays where she is on the bed.

Downstairs a door bangs. Nisha's voice comes up the stairwell of the Suri house, in another part of the city: "Vihaan! We are leaving! Vihaan!"

A car starts in the road outside the Arora gate.
