# Chapter 31 — The Farishta (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 20, 1 March 2026.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Message

The dark speaks first in the empty room, and it does not ask who listens.

"Insaanon ne bahut bada bewakoofi wala kaam kiya hai."

The voice comes from nowhere her eyes can find.

"Ab tum jaaogi — aur bachaaogi."

Ahana stands very still in the empty room.

Her phone lies face-down, its screen black.

"Tumhein pata to chal hi gaya hoga."

The voice turns, slow as smoke.

"Ye jo Amarnath yaatra pe attack hua hai — wo maine nahin kiya."

Her breath catches.

Fear, cold and complete.

"Par jab main waar karoonga — tum par, aur tumhaare desh par — to tum use bacha nahin paaogi."

A pause the length of a held knife.

"Jaao. Waqt aa gaya hai tumhaara. Is desh ko bachaane ka — insaanon ke waar se."

"Hindustan ko tum insaanon ke waar se phir bhi bacha logi."

The voice drops lower.

"Par mere waar se nahin. Kyonki main insaan nahin hoon."

Silence.

Then, almost gentle:

"Naagin ka dushman insaan nahin hai. Kaun hoon main?"

"Jiske saath Naagin ki dushmani yugon puraani hai."

The dark fills with old names.

"Hamaari aur tumhaari dushmani sadiyon puraani hai. Aaj is sadiyon puraani dushmani par — main tumhaare apne khoon ka gehra laal rang chadha doongi."

Under it, the score comes up — low, and old.

A second shape moves in the voice.

"Doosra dushman hai nevla — jo Naagin ka sabse khatarnaak shikaari maana jaata hai."

The door stands shut behind her.

"Naagin ka sabse bada dushman — jo Naaginon se kam nahin, balki unhi ka lahu hai."

The voice bares itself.

"Dragon. Naagin ka dushman hai."

"Dagaaba."

The voice turns on her.

"Paas aa — maine tujh par jo taras khaaya tha, wo meri sabse badi galti thi."

"Jo aasmaan se waar karta hai — aur dharti pe Naagin ki taakat ko chunauti phenkta hai."

The last words hang.

"Bujho to jaano — main jo kuch bhi hoon, mere waar se koi shikaar bach nahin paata."

The room empties.

Only her breathing stays.

Two enemies now.

One she can see coming.

One she cannot.

## The Dread

The passage lamp burns low outside Aryaman's room.

Aryaman holds the phone too tight.

"Haan — meri baat ho gayi hai. Thankfully nuclear facility safe hai."

Vihaan crosses the passage outside, awake with the night's file still turning, and slows at the open door.

"Yes. Lekin jiska kabhi tala nahin hai — call karo."

Aryaman stares at nothing.

"Unbelievable. Ye Changistani sudhrenge nahin. Ye alag-alag tareekon se India ko todne ki koshish karte rahenge."

He listens, and his face hardens.

"Aur ye Amarnath Express hai. Logon ne kitna wait kiya hai iska."

His voice drops.

"Aaj agar train pe chadhe logon ko kuch ho gaya — to dahshat phail jaayegi. Amarnath jaane se log darne lagenge. Damn it. Yahi to ye Changistani chaahte hain."

Dread fills the room.

Vihaan does not move from the door's edge.

"Lagta hai unhein ki Hindustan ko mitaana hoga — to Hinduon ko mita do."

Aryaman turns, and looks back at him from the doorway.

"Itni dahshat machaa do — ki Hindustan mein log Hindu hone se darne lage. Yahi plan hai inka."

Under it, low strings.

"Office."

Aryaman grabs his keys and goes.

"Khabar dena." Vihaan says it to his brother's back.

Below, Aryaman's car starts.

The sound thins down the lane, and the house sleeps on.

Vihaan turns back down the passage.

## The Chant

Night presses at the bogie window, and the lamps hum over the seats.

One lamp gutters.

Roma keeps her voice low in the rocking bogie.

"Aryaman — yahaan kuch gadbad hai. Is bogie mein. I can feel it."

Under it, the score comes up.

"Camera on rakhna."

Her whisper sharpens.

"Lekin hoshiyaari se."

The compartment door slams.

"Bolo Changistan Zindabad."

The man fills the doorway, gun first.

"Bolo. Changistan Zindabad — bolo."

Passengers shrink into their seats.

"Bolo."

The gun sweeps the rows.

"Khaao India ke liye apna pyaar. Sab ke sab chithde chithde ho jaaoge."

A woman starts to cry.

A child hides its face.

"Please — humein mat — "

The pleading rises and breaks.

"Sabke chithde udenge — sabke — "

A phone buzzes among the men.

"Ji bhai. Media wale yahaan ki hamaari khabar le rahe hain."

"Capture."

The order cracks down the bogie.

"Dhoondo saale ko. Kaun kaun hai media wala. Kaun hai — kaun hai media wala — bol."

Roma slides behind a seat, phone pressed flat to her palm.

She is terrified, and thinking anyway.

"Camera."

A small voice pipes up, clear and fearless.

"Mujhe ye camera chaahiye. Uncle — mujhe bhi camera do na, please. Please, uncle — de do na."

"Goli mat — goli mat — "

The shot fills the bogie like a slammed door.

Roma runs.

Behind seats, past frozen faces, down the rocking corridor — and the bathroom door shuts behind her with a click like a held breath.

"Roma — phone kyon nahin lag raha hai."

Far away, a voice she cannot hear.

"Phone utha lo."

"Kaun hai iske saath."

The killer stands over the fallen cameraman.

"Ae — kaun hai iske saath?"

Under it, the sting — one knock, and gone.

## The Kitchen

The kitchen holds its evening glow, and steam rises off the stove.

"Chai banao."

Dimple does not look up from the stove.

"Arey — ek cup mere liye bhi rakhna."

Arjun leans in the kitchen doorway, watching her.

"Haan."

"Waise aap aaj bahut zyada glow kar rahe ho."

He tilts his head.

"Haan."

"Hmm."

She almost smiles.

"Ye sab mere Korean face pack ka kamaal hai. Waise tu yahaan kya kar raha hai?"

"Arey wo tauji aur papa gaye hue hain na channel."

Arjun keeps his voice easy.

"Wo sab sambhaal lenge. Wo tauji kuch Roma ke baare mein bata rahe the na?"

Dimple's spoon stops moving.

"Arey haan."

She lowers her voice.

"Roma to unke jaal mein fans hi gayi hai."

"Roma fans gayi — matlab plan yahi tha."

Arjun steps closer.

"Haan?"

"Haan — aur nahin to kya."

The words spill now, like chai from a cracked cup.

"Train mein hi to terrorist hain — apne desh ki."

Arjun's face does not change.

Only his eyes go somewhere else.

Fury — iced over.

"Ek ghanta."

Dimple stares into the steam.

"Ek ghanta aur. Iske baad — India — "

Arjun walks out of the kitchen with his phone already in his hand.

## The Last Call

The office floor never emptied this night.

It only thinned, and waited.

One dead tube buzzes over the empty desks.

"Un aatankiyon ne train ko nishaana banaaya hai."

Aryaman stands at the desk with the source-word still hot in his mouth.

"Haan — mujhe bhi abhi-abhi pata chala hai. Aur sabse pehle — wo usi RAW officer ko khatm kar dena chaahte hain — jo train mein hai."

Under it, the score comes up.

"Kya?"

Dinesh goes white.

Dinesh reaches for the desk pad.

The phone on Aryaman's desk screams.

He snatches it.

"Hello — hello — "

"Aryaman — Aryaman — "

Roma's voice comes thin through the bathroom door and the rocking dark.

"In logon ne Ajay ko maar diya. Usne — usne sirf goli maar di. Mujhe to maar de — Aryaman — please — please kuch karo."

"Roma — tum meri baat suno — "

"I think in aatankiyon ko hamaare baare mein koi to khabar de raha hai."

Her whisper breaks.

"Koi to hai — jo tumhaari taraf se gaddaari kar raha hai. Aryaman — mujhe nahin pata ki main khud ko kitni der aur chhupa ke rakh paaoongi. Shayad ye mera aakhri call hai."

"Roma — "

"Please. Dhoondo — wo gaddaar kaun hai."

The line dies.

"Roma. Roma — "

"Tumhaari taraf se gaddaari."

Aryaman says it aloud to the room, and Dinesh writes it on the pad, underlining once.

"Koi to hai."

Aryaman stares at the dead phone.

Then at the pad.

Then at the men around the desk.

Rage, cold and counting.

## The Terrace

Cold wind moves over the terrace, and the city lights burn far below.

"Do you understand what just happened?"

Tina's voice shakes on the office floor.

"Kya maara? Kya matlab — kya maara hai? Meri behen hai us train mein. Haan."

Dinesh tries to steady her.

"Kis berahmi se maara — mere desh ke logon ko? Kaisi aafat aa gayi hai mere desh ke upar."

"Haan — bhai sahab yahi keh rahe hain."

Tina twists her hands.

"Aur ye bhi — ki Roma ko kuch nahin hoga. Mujhe nahin pata. Fikr ho rahi hai mujhe."

She is worried sick.

Aryaman turns on the room.

"Koi wahaan unhein train pe information de raha hai. Hamaari taraf se. Aisa kaun kar sakta hai?"

The question lands on the room, and no one picks it up.

Aryaman looks from face to face.

No face answers.

On the train, the tannoy crackles through the rocking dark.

"Ab hum Anantnag pahunchne waale hain — bas pahunch rahe hain."

Above them, the terrace wind cuts cold.

"Kya hai yaar, Karan? Neeche itni tension chal rahi hai — aur tu upar leke kyon aa gaya?"

A glass rings against its neighbour on the table.

Under it, the score comes up — light, and heartless.

"Arey tension lene ke — "

Karan laughs with his friends around the glasses.

"Let's have some fun, guys. Chal yaar — drink peete hain. Chalo."

Karan's keys lie forgotten by the bottle.

"Excuse me."

Tina stands in the terrace doorway, staring at the glasses.

"Hello."

Karan does not look up.

## The Blackout

The lamps hum over the office floor, and the city burns beyond the window.

"Koi hoshiyaari nahin hai."

The train's voice is far from here.

"Sab log shaant raho."

Here, in the city's humming heart, Ahana makes her decision standing up.

"Mujhe kuch na kuch to karna padega."

"Ab kya karengi aap?"

Bharani watches her face.

"Wahi jo main hamesha karti hoon."

Her voice does not shake.

"Insaaf. Par uske liye mujhe yahaan se nikalna hoga."

Under it, the score comes up.

Her phone stays dark in her fist.

She is ready.

"Main bhi aapke saath aati hoon."

"Nahin, Bharani."

She stops her with a look.

"Tumhein mera ek zaroori kaam karna hai. Dhyaan se suno."

The office floor hums under tube-light.

Then —

"Ye light kyon chali gayi?"

Dinesh spins in his chair.

"Ye light ko kya hua?"

Dark swallows the room whole.

Somewhere a chair scrapes.

Somewhere a woman gasps.

The lamp in the corner gutters and dies.

"Ruko — koi hilega nahin."

Aryaman's voice cuts through the black from the desk's far side.

"Apni jagah. Sab apni jagah."

And under the dark, where no office ear can hear, Bharani smiles into the humming mantra-night.

"Ab hamaari Naagrani ko koi nahin dekh paayega."

Her whisper joins the old syllables rising somewhere below.

"Maine ab poori ki poori Delhi ki light band kar di hai. Karpoor gauram — karunaavataaram — "

## The Dark Floor

The dark has a shape on the office floor, and Aryaman learns it with his hands.

"Battery lamp."

He finds the desk's edge, then Dinesh's shoulder in the black.

"Woh kone waala — haan, wahi. Jalaao."

The lamp flares.

Faces swim up out of the dark — Dinesh, two juniors, the police-line operator with his headset still on.

His keys ring faint at his belt.

"Line mat kaatna."

Aryaman carries the lamp to the desk himself and sets it down where its light falls on the pad.

"Roma ki line — jo bhi ho — ye line khuli rahegi."

The paper waits under the lamplight.

"Sir — poori Delhi ki light gayi hai."

The operator's voice shakes.

"Generator — "

"Generator aayega jab aayega."

Aryaman plants himself at the desk's head.

"Tab tak ye floor khada rahega. Dinesh — paani. Sabko. Aur tum — "

He looks at the youngest junior.

"Darwaaze pe khade raho. Jo aaye — naam poochh ke andar aane do."

Dinesh watches him move through the lamplight, giving the dark its orders, and something in the boy's shoulders settles.

Relief.

"Ye floor khada rahega."

Aryaman says it once more, quieter, and it is not hope.

It is a held desk in a dead city — held because he stands at its head.

## The Dark House

The house lies dark from gate to terrace, and the city's hum is gone.

The silence wakes him before the dark does.

Vihaan lies still a moment, listening to nothing.

Then he gets up, and closes the night's file.

Aryaman's door stands open on an empty room.

Ahana's door is open across the corridor, and her room gives back nothing.

He tries her number.

Nothing answers.

From the stair window, the drive holds only its own dark — his brother's car is gone.

Dimple stands at the stair-foot, a candle shaking in her hand.

She looks up the stair, and finds him coming down.

"Vihaan? Ye light — "

"Light chali gayi hai." Vihaan comes down the last steps toward her.

"Bas light hai. So jaaiye — main hoon."

"Aur Aryaman — ?"

"Office gaya hai." Vihaan walks her to her door.

She takes his word, and goes back into the dark.

He lights the lamp at the stair-foot and leaves it burning.

He puts the phone away, and sits down on the stair beside the lamp.

He is worried — and files it till morning.

The question can wait for morning.

## The Beating

The lamp swings over the bogie wall as the train rocks.

"Nahin karne doongi."

Roma stands in the bogie's rocking aisle with her back to the passengers.

"Main tumhein kisi ko kuch nahin karne doongi. Kisi ko kuch nahin hoga."

The man's phone glows.

Far away, a voice gives the order.

"Meri baat dhyaan se suno."

The words crawl down the line.

"Wahaan pe ek RAW officer bhi hai. Wo kisi bhi haalat mein bachna nahin chaahiye. Main tumhein photograph bhej raha hoon uski."

"Okay."

The photograph flares on the small screen.

The man looks up — scans the rows — and stops.

The bathroom door holds shut.

"Aur RAW officer to yehi hai."

His smile shows teeth.

"Isko to pakka khatm karna hai."

"What — "

Roma steps between.

The slap turns her head sideways.

"Ruko — mat maaro — ise maaro — "

The compartment erupts.

"Reham — reham karo — mat maaro — mat maaro — reham karo — "

Hands plead from every seat.

The beating goes on.

Pity, with its hands tied.

"Ye chal kya raha hai?"

The man squints into the thickening dark.

"Kuch bhi saaf nazar nahin aa raha hai."
