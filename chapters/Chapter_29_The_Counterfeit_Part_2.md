## The Delivery

The den floor keeps its dark behind black water, and the crocodiles lie along the far bank like drowned logs, and the score sounds once over the dark water (m14:57).

"Pasha ji — wo dono waapas aaye hain."

The announcer's voice comes thin off the water, the score striking under the summons (m15:13).

"To bulaao — bulaao unhein. Suno — agar ye dono khaali haath aaye — nigal le. Chhodna mat."

A lamp gutters on the dais steps, and the shadows climb the wet wall.

A ripple breaks against the stone steps and settles.

Airul spreads his arms wide on the dais.

"Aao — aao — aao — aao."

"Humko to laga ki tum naagmani laane waale the."

"Ye kya hai, Pasha?"

"Aap ise pehniye to — jo aap dhoondh rahe hain na — wo aapko dikhne lagegi. Dikh jaayega."

"Haan — dikh jaayega."

Airul takes the stone in both hands.

"Dekhte hain."

The crocodiles sink without a sound at the stone's first flash.

Parmeet's phone stays dead in his pocket — no signal under the earth.

Airul screams it at the roof and dances where he stands, mad with it, the stone over his head.

"Naagmani! Mil gayi — mil gayi!"

"Pasha — ab to naagmani aapke haath aa gayi na."

Parmeet presses his palms together.

"To kya hum apne desh Changistaan waapas aa sakte hain? Bharat mein rehte-rehte oob gaye hain. Bahut saaf-suthra desh hai. Humein gandagi ki aadat hai. Humein please waapas bula lo na."

"Haan, Pasha — wahaan jeena bahut hi aasaan hai."

Baljeet bows with him.

"Humein jaddojehad chaahiye. Hum naram padte jaa rahe hain. Humein waapas aana hai."

"Naagmani hai ye."

Airul stops dancing.

"Tum logon ka kaam ab tak khatm nahin hua. Tumhein ye mandir dhoondhna hai."

"Mandir?"

Parmeet blinks.

"Humein iska pata chaahiye. Par ye mandir hai kahaan?"

"Tum dono nasha karke aaye ho."

Airul's voice drops.

"Are — humein pata hota to kya hum tumhein dhoondhne ke liye kehte? Pehle naagmani dhoondhne mein tumne itna samay lagaaya — aur ab aisi bevakoofi jaisi baatein. Kisi gupt jagah par — usi gupt jagah par ye naagmani rehti thi. Dhoondho us jagah ko. Aur jab tak tum log wo jagah dhoondh nahin lete — tum yahaan waapas nahin aa sakte."

"Ye jagah dhoondhna hamaare liye bahut zaroori hai."

He holds the stone to the torch.

"Ab tumne naagmani to dhoondh hi li hai. Ye jagah bhi tum dhoondh hi loge."

"Are, Pasha — jab tak mani is mandir mein thi to mandir ki zaroorat thi."

Parmeet finds his courage.

"Ab to mani aapke paas hai — to is mandir ki kya zaroorat? Is mani ko bechiye — paisa lijiye — aur humein bhi ghar aane dijiye. Pasha — aap hi ne to kaha tha na ki naagmani mil gayi to hum yahaan waapas aa sakte hain. Ab naagmani mil gayi to aap mandir beech mein le aaye."

"Aur nahin to kya?"

Baljeet dares it.

"Is mani ko khareedne ke liye achche se achche khareedaar baithe hue hain. Aur — "

"Aap apna ayyar-pana dikha rahe hain."

Parmeet finishes it.

"Are — koi kam-dimaag waala hi aisa kar sakta hai."

Airul is off the dais and above them, the score striking with the crushing (m17:17).

"Do kaudi ke aadmi — zabaan sambhaal ke baat karo."

"Apni aukaat mein — zameen ke keede ho tum log — aur mere ye pair tumhein kuchal sakte hain. Dobaara — dhyaan rahe — aisi badtameezi sahi nahin jaayegi."

"Nahin — nahin karenge — nahin karenge."

They fold to the floor together.

"Ye mani aur wo mandir — humko ye dono chaahiye."

Airul turns the stone in the light.

"Kyunki hamaari manzil ka raasta wahin se guzarta hai."

He holds out a folded map between two fingers, and Parmeet takes it with both hands.

A rope ladder drips from the dark over the far steps.

"Samajhte kya hai ye apne aap ko?"

Parmeet mutters it into his collar on the way out.

"Hum sab partners hain. Main to inki madad hi to kar raha tha."

The car drives the dark road back with the map on the seat between them.

"Ye Airul bhi ajeeb hai."

Baljeet stares out at nothing.

"Mandir ka na ata na pata. Rahasyamay mandir dhoondho. Are — kahaan se dhoondhenge? Pagal ho gaye hum log. Kabhi naagmani — kabhi mandir dhoondho. Bhai sahab — kuch karo."

## The Price

Pammi's room keeps its lamps low past midnight, and the insult sits in it like a third person.

"Us Dawan ne jo mujhe bataaya — maine unko bata diya."

Pammi bolts the door and leans on it, the score striking under the venting (m18:02).

"Ab mujhe kya pata us buddhe ko bhoolne ki beemaari hai. Is Ahana ki wajah se aaj meri itni beizzati hui. Lekin is Ahana se gussa karke main karoongi bhi kya? Itne bade diamond merchant ki beti hai. Jeb bhi jhaadegi — to heere hi girenge."

"Bua ji — aapko lag raha hoga ki main aapse upset hoon."

Ahana stands in the doorway, calm.

"Par aisa bilkul nahin hai."

She comes in.

"Misunderstandings to har kisi mein ho jaati hai na."

The mirror on the wall gives Pammi her face back, lined under the lamps.

Pammi turns, already repairing.

"Haan, beta."

"Mujhse galti ho gayi. Lekin main kya karti? Tumhaare Daddy ko bhoolne ki aadat thi. Lekin main tum pe poora bharosa karti hoon. Poora. You are like a daughter to me. Tumne mujhe maaf kar diya na?"

"Of course, Bua ji. It's okay."

Pammi catches her hands.

"Nahin — aise nahin."

"Tum mujhe agar latest model ki luxurious car dilaogi — tab main maanoongi ki tumne mujhe maaf kar diya."

"Hmm — to agar main aapko best car dilwaoongi — to phir aap uske baad maan lengi ki maine aapko maaf kar diya?"

"Haan — ye to simple hai."

Pammi smiles for the first time all night.

"Main Dad se boloongi."

Ahana meets the smile with a level one.

"Aur phir to wo aapki baat taalenge nahin."

"Oh no — aapke chehre pe ye wrinkles."

Ahana tilts her head.

"Meri — wrinkles?"

Pammi's hand flies to her face.

"Haan. Oh God."

"Sab negativity ki wajah se. Haan — aur kya? But don't worry. Main hoon na. Main aapki help karoongi. Idhar aaiye. Main aapko ek jaadui nuskha deti hoon."

"Achcha."

"Ye potion, Bua ji — main London se lekar aayi hoon."

Ahana holds up a small cut-glass vial, the score running under the potion (m19:34).

"Ye saari negativity ko cleanse kar deta hai — aur positivity laata hai. Haan — maine khud try ki hai. Try karo aap. Achcha. Try — try."

Pammi laughs, lighter than before, the score sounding under the delivery (m20:05).

"Very good. I am feeling good. Thank you. Thank you so much. Any time."

"Aapko luxurious car chaahiye thi na?"

Ahana opens the door.

"Come with me. Sambhaal ke. Bua ji — aapke liye."

The basement keeps its tube light cold over the covered car, and the cover comes away like a held breath let go.

The keys came on her husband's card.

Vihaan's name sits behind the slip.

Pammi circles the car, glowing, the score striking under the greed (m20:42).

"God. Tumne itni jaldi sab arrange kar diya. Main bahut khush hoon. Tum meri sabse best best best bahu ho."

"I told you about that girl na — jisse Ravish ki shaadi hone wali thi."

Pammi runs a hand along the bonnet.

"Ek number ki kangaal thi wo. Un logon ne aap logon ke ghar ko loot liya tha na?"

Her voice drops, confiding.

"Nahin — humne us Ananta ko aur uske poore parivaar ko maar daala. Pata hai kaise?"

Ahana hears the two halves of it apart.

Ananta stood in her own room on her wedding night, alive and plain — so half the boast is a lie told to sound big.

But the other half — the family — is a confession, and it comes out of Pammi's own mouth, smiling beside a gifted car.

"Haan."

Pammi laughs.

"Aise. Are — ye kya kar rahi ho? Kya kar rahi ho? Aa jaao — theek hai."

"Ye gaadi nahin hai."

Pammi's laugh thins into horror.

"Iske paas hai. Bachaao — bachaao — bachaao."

## The Sentence

The basement room goes still enough to hear the tube light hum, and the snake lifts off the leather where Pammi sits frozen.

Pammi presses back against the door.

"Nahin — nahin — nahin. Mujhe mat maaro."

"Maaf kar do mujhe. Nahin — maaf kar do. Bachaao — koi hai?"

"Koi hoga bhi to tere badle hi khud marne nahin aayega."

Ahana's eyes have gone serpent-gold, and the scales begin at her temples.

"Nahin — nahin — nahin."

Pammi sobs it at the floor.

"Aaj maut khud tujhe lene aayi hai."

She comes forward through the cold light.

"Tujhe hi lekar jaayegi."

"Nahin — mujhe mat maaro."

Pammi's voice breaks.

"Sharm khaao mujh pe."

"Aaj ek naagin sirf inteqaam nahin."

Ahana stands over her in half-form, terrible and exact.

"Us raat ke har gunaah ka faisla hi bankar aayi hai."

"Main Purvi hoon."

The name lands between them like a struck coin.

"Aur Pammi ki maut bankar aayi hoon."

She strikes — and the basement keeps no record but the swaying tube-light.

It is over before the next plea finishes.

"Tere gunaahon ko uska anjaam hi mil chuka hai. Aaj ek aur hatyaara maara gaya."

Her bangle ticks once against the car door, loud in the stillness.

The scales sink back from her temples.

Her eyes turn dark again.

Ahana straightens her dupatta, steps past the car, and is gone up the stairs before the tube-light steadies.

## The Walk

The road outside the house keeps its midnight dark and empty to both gates, and her breath smokes once in the cold.

A gate lamp burns over the empty road, and no one passes under it.

"Ahana."

Parmeet leans from the car, Baljeet beside him.

"Beta ji — is waqt aap yahaan akele? Sab theek to hai?"

"Shaayad inhone kuch nahin dekha."

The aside goes low to Baljeet.

"Actually — Dad — thoda suffocated feel ho raha tha — to night walk karne baahar aa gayi thi. Par by the way — aap dono baahar kaise hain?"

Parmeet laughs down the road.

"Beta — jab bahu itni fit ho to sasur ko bhi fit ho rehna chaahiye na."

Sasur nahin — asur ho tum.

The suspicion crosses behind her eyes and finds no mouth.

"Kuch kaha, beta?"

"Nahin — nahin — nahin. Kuch bhi nahin. Chalein. Haan — theek hai. Chalo — aao. Bahut sardi ho rahi hai baahar. Aur andar aao. Chalo — aaiye."

The door opens on lamplight and Dimple's worried face, the score sounding under the cold return (m23:10).

"Achcha — wahaan aap log aa gaye."

Dimple wrings her hands, the score running under the news (m23:50).

"Pammi di ne kab se apne aap ko kamre mein band kar rakha hai — na jawaab de rahi hai — na neeche aa rahi hai. Aapko unhein itna nahin sunaana chaahiye tha, bhai sahab. What is happening, guys?"

"Wo — Bua ne apne ko kamre mein band kar liya hai — aur wo kisi ko jawaab nahin de rahi."

A young voice carries it inside.

"Oh no — really? Yes."

Vaise ab wo kabhi tumhaari kisi baat ka jawaab nahin degi.

Ahana knows it behind her eyes like a stone in a shut fist, and none of it reaches her face.

"Andar aao sab. Sardi hai." Vihaan stands at the stair-foot in the lamplight, awake, his eyes finding his wife's for one held beat.

He files the lateness of the hour, the steadiness of her face, and the bank's hour-stamped amount still unopened on his phone — the amount gladly, all of it for morning, none of it for this doorway — and steps back to let them in.

## The Exit

The hall keeps its small-hours lamps on for the suitcase by the door, and Bharani wears Pammi's face down the stairs like a borrowed shawl.

The phone waits silent on the side table through it all.

"Pe — kahaan ja rahe ho?"

Dimple stares, the score striking under the suitcase (m24:28).

"Samaan leke kahaan ja rahe ho? Are Pammi — ye kya kar rahi ho?"

"Ye suitcase — purse leke kahaan ja rahi ho?"

Dhoondhenge zaroor.

Behind the borrowed face the thought passes cold and finds no mouth — they will search for what they cannot find, and the searching is already spent, the score sounding under the cold thought (m24:39).

Bharani — wo kuch — chhod dijiye.

She steadies the worn mouth on the thought, unspoken.

"Mujhe nahin lagta aapko ab meri koi zaroorat hai. Aapne meri sabke saamne insult ki. Matlab — jo mann mein aaya kaha — to theek hai. Mere mann mein bhi jo aa hi raha hai — main kar rahi hoon. Main kuch hafton ke liye London jaa rahi hoon."

"Are, Mummy — tum mujhe jaante nahin kya?"

Parmeet spreads his hands.

"Are — main gusse mein kuch bhi bol jaata hoon. Pammi — wo ye mean nahin kar rahe the."

"Lekin mean to the na."

The suitcase handle tightens in her grip.

"Isiliye mujhe kisi ki baat nahin sunni. Main inhein maaf nahin karoongi."

"Ab jaane dijiye na, Pammi di. Baat ko kyun badhaana?"

Dimple pleads.

"Dimple — meri beizzati hui hai. Tum imagine bhi nahin kar sakti main is waqt kya feel kar rahi hoon."

The hall lamp hums over the stairs she will not climb again.

"Pammi Bua — ek baar baat to — "

A young voice tries.

"Nahin sunoongi. Keh diya so keh diya."

The worn mouth does not turn.

Her bag strap slides off her shoulder, and she hitches it back.

Baljeet murmurs it at Parmeet's shoulder.

"Jaane dijiye, bhai sahab."

"Humein mandir khojne mein focus karna hai. Yahaan nahin rahegi hi to shaanti bani rahegi. Samjho."

Parmeet nods slowly.

"Theek hai, Pam."

"Apna dhyaan rakhna. Aur hamesha yaad rakhna — humein tumhaari zaroorat rahegi. We will miss you. Aur jab dil kare — waapas aa jaana."

"Sochoongi."

The door opens on the cold road.

"Aaiye. Take care."

A young voice looks round the thinned hall.

"Yaar — ye Aryaman bhai party se kahaan chale gaye?"

"Pata nahin, yaar. Saara phone to miss hi kar diya. Haan."

"Main Aryaman ko dekhta hoon." Vihaan is already moving for the phone.

"Party se nikla tha — ghar nahin aaya."

He dials, listens to the unanswered ringing, and sets the receiver down.

"Miss kar gaya. Main jaag raha hoon — wo aayega to mujhe milega."

## The Seeking

Radhika's lane keeps its night behind shut shutters, and one door stands open on lamplight.

"Mujhe usse hi baat karni hai."

Aryaman stops at the gate with his hands in fists, the score striking under the need (m26:10).

"Mujhe jawaab chaahiye. Usne mujhe kyun chhoda tha? Kyun meri zindagi se chali gayi? Wo meri zindagi saath le gayi."

"Radhika Kothari."

He says her name at the open door — and stops.

Inside, Radhika bends over Purav's injury, dressing it with careful hands, her whole attention narrowed to the wound.

The lamp throws both their shadows long on the wall.

Aryaman watches, shattering, one breath longer than a man should — then turns on his heel and walks back down the lane without calling again, and the open door clicks shut somewhere behind him.

## The Confiding

The sitting room keeps one lamp burning past two, and Vihaan looks up from the sofa as his brother comes through the door like a spent storm.

Vihaan moves over and makes the space beside him.

"Aa jaa. Baith. Main jaag raha tha."

His phone lies face-down on the sofa arm, the unanswered call cooling in it.

"Aryaman — mujhe tumse baat karni thi."

Ahana rises from the chair by the lamp, her voice low.

"Actually — main — "

"Can you imagine?"

Aryaman sinks down between them and stares at his hands, the score running under the confiding (m26:27).

"Can you imagine ki — koi ek aisa insaan ho — jise tum itna pyaar karte ho. Lekin wo kisi aur ke liye bana ho. Wo kisi aur ko dekhta ho — kisi aur ke liye — ta ho. Kitna dard hota hai."

His wrecked eyes turn to her.

"Soch sakti ho tum. Tu imagine tak nahin kar sakti."

"Main kar sakta hoon." Vihaan answers it for the room, steady.

"Bol. Main sun raha hoon."

"Usne mujhe hamaari shaadi ki raat hi chhod diya."

Aryaman's voice thins to the wound, the score sounding under it (m27:00).

"Bina wajah bataaye chhod diya. Aur wajah bhi nahin bataayi aisa karne ki. Main sawaal ke saath chhod diya. Sawaal bana ke chhod diya."

"Main tumhein itne dard mein nahin dekh sakti hoon."

Ahana's hands fold tight in her lap, the score striking under the resolve (m27:26).

"Aryaman — main pata laga ke rahoongi — ki agar Radhika se tum itna pyaar karte the — aur wo bhi tumse utna hi pyaar karti thi — to usne tumhaara dil kyun toda? Kyun chhoda tumhein?"

Vihaan hears the resolve land beside his brother's bowed head, and files it with the night's others — his wife hunting answers for his brother — and says nothing against it.

"Soch mat abhi." Vihaan's hand settles heavy on his brother's shoulder.

"So jaa. Main hoon."

Aryaman's head sinks back against the sofa within the minute, and the shaking evens out into exhausted sleep.

Vihaan stays where he is, keeping the watch — one low string under the brother-watch — and Ahana leaves the brothers to the one lamp without another word.

## The Valentine

The veranda keeps its one lamp against the last-dark, and the cold comes in under the door behind her.

Ahana steps out with her shawl pulled close, and stops one step down.

Vihaan catches the door before it latches.

"Ahana."

She turns with one hand on the rail.

"It is Valentine's Day." He says it like an account long kept.

"And all day I hoped for a gift from you."

Her hands stay where they are, empty inside the shawl, and she gives him no answer because she has none.

"Then I take my own."

He turns her face up with one finger under her chin — and takes the kiss, brief and certain, there under the lamp.

She does not pull away.

Her fingers tighten once in his sleeve, and for one breath the night lets her be only his wife — one low string under the authored kiss.

"I saw the bank's message." His voice stays low enough for only her.

"Spend — what is mine is yours. I am glad you reached for it."

Something crosses behind her eyes at that — there and gone — and her hand finds the doorframe instead of his sleeve.

"And this —"

He takes the small wrapped box from his pocket.

"I prepared it before the party. For you."

The box holds a gold pendant on a fine chain, simple as a held breath.

"Turn."

He clasps it at her nape himself, his fingers warm against her cold skin.

She looks down at it where it lies, and up at him, and says nothing — but she does not take it off.

"Go careful in that dark."

He steps back into the lamplight.

"Come back to me."

She goes down the steps with his pendant at her throat, and the lane takes her.

He stands in the lamp's edge till her shawl is gone from it, then turns back to the brother asleep by the one lamp inside.

## The Refusal

Radhika's door keeps its lamp lit past three, and the raised voices reach the lane before Ahana reaches it, the score striking twice over the refusal (m27:48 x2 as one).

"Kya kar rahe ho, Purav? Kya kar raha hoon matlab? Beewi ho meri — mat. Kya kar rahe ho? Stop it, Purav. Please — stay away."

"Radhika — main pati hi hoon tumhaara."

"Koi beewi to nahin hoon main tumhaari, Purav."

Radhika's voice goes iron.

"Duniya ki nazar mein bhale hi pati-patni hain — lekin hum to sach jaante hain na. Sirf hum — sirf isiliye kar rahe hain kyunki hamaare — tumhein kyun explanation ki zaroorat ho rahi hai, Purav? Tum to sab sach jaante ho na. Aainda se apni had mat paar karna. Jaao yahaan se. Please leave."

The door opens and Purav goes out past the gate without looking up, his shoulders high with the refusal.

Ahana steps back into the lane's shadow and lets him pass.

Iska matlab Radhika Purav ki beewi nahin hai — inki shaadi nahin hui hai.

The realisation lands behind her eyes whole, and carries her through the still-open door.

Somewhere across the sleeping city, Bharani turns down another empty lane, seeking.

"Naagrani — kahaan chali gayi hai aise?"

Itna bada sach kyun chhupaaya Radhika ne?

The question sits behind her eyes while Radhika's defiance fills the room.

"Koi beewi to nahin hoon main tumhaari, Purav."

Ahana gives Radhika's own words back to her across the room.

"Please move on kar lo."

"Tum kar paayi ho?"

Radhika turns on her, sudden.

"Move on. Nahin — sawaal."

Her eyes narrow.

"Tum bhi aurat ho na? To batao. Kyun karti hai aurat aisa? Jawaab do. Are — bolo na. Give me answer."

Ahana holds the pushback without flinching.

"Ghar mein sab kehte hain — Aryaman bhai ki girlfriend ne unhein shaadi ke din hi dump kar diya tha."

A beat.

"Jab Radhika ne Purav se shaadi hi nahin to — Aryaman ka dil kyun toda? Kyun use bina wajah diye shaadi waale din chali gayi? Aaj tak mujhe nahin di. Are Radhika — Radhika — main tumse — "

She leaves the last question standing in the lamplight between them, unanswered, the score running under the grilling (m28:46).

Outside, past her sight-line, Purav stops under the lane's dead lamp — and his face comes away like a borrowed shawl, and Ahana's own face looks out of the dark in its place.

She does not see it.

The night keeps it unwitnessed, and the mystery files itself shut.

## The Warning

The empty road keeps its last-dark before dawn, and Bharani steps out of it into Ahana's path in her own face, urgent.

A shutter rattles down over a closed shop somewhere.

Her phone stays dark in her pocket, and no one calls.

"Mujhe Naagrani ko hi jaldi dhoondhna hoga."

The words come out with her breath.

"Aur bataana hoga ki desh par hi bahut bada sankat aane waala hai — desh khatre mein hai."

Ahana stops mid-step.

The warning lands between them with no one else on the road to hear the word Naagrani spoken aloud.

## The Landing

The arrival gate keeps its night in sea-salt and landing-lights, and the eastbound flight sinks through it toward the lit strip.

Min-ji comes down the aerobridge with one bag and no one sent to receive her, unannounced by her own design.

The accounts told her everything the family never would — a wedding moves through money before it moves through mouths — and the arithmetic of what he has just agreed to sits filed behind her eyes, accurate and unshared.

The glass wall throws the runway lights back at her.

"To ye hai tumhaari Mumbai."

She says it low to the glass, to the sea beyond the glass, to no one.

The city opens before her, unknowing, and she sees it first — before it, before anyone, sees her.
