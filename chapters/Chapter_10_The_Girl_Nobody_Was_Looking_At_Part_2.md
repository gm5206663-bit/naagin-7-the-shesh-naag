# Chapter 10 — The Girl Nobody Was Looking At (Part 2)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 4 (first part).*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

*The twelve seconds the season plays on every night after its first: the theme, then straight in (`canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` §I). The full forty seconds are written once, at the head of Chapter 1, and are not repeated below it.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.



## The Box Left Open

The lane behind the out-house at midnight, with one bulb over the generator and the bins standing in a row under it, and the dark going on past them. Sheshnaag's hood is not shown, but the lane feels like a temple that has forgotten its god.

The lane behind the out-house is where the house keeps its noise.

The generator, the bins, the dark past them.

"Aur ye— chal mere saath. Kya hua — Purvi? Purvi?"

Vihaan's voice, from the lane behind her, low enough that she turns and looks at him.

His jaw is tight, and his hand is at his side, clenched once and let go.

The generator's noise goes on and does not change, and the light over it does not change either.

Purvi stops at the corner with the key in her hand, because a man is talking on the other side of the wall in a voice that is being kept down and is not being kept down enough.

She is smiling, small and sad, at the gravel.

"Tumhein pata hai, basement ki lift ka secret button box khula reh gaya hai."

That is Parmeet.

"Kaise? Ye mujhe kaise pata? Ye baat tumhein pata honi chahiye na?"

"Bhai sahab, Rishabh aur Sameer ke saath hum log saath nikle the. Uske baad tum scientist ke saath DNA ka injection leke gaye the."

"Bhai sahab, wo uske pehle ya uske baad hua hoga."

"Uske baad!"

The voice comes off the wall.

"Mujhe kitni baar chilla-chilla ke samjhana padega ki zara si bhi laparwahi humein mehngi pad sakti hai? Kisi kaam ke nahi ho tum. Useless ho."

"Bhai sahab, I am sorry."

Parmeet comes round the corner of the out-house with his hand still up, and Baljeet comes behind him with his head down.

Purvi is standing in the middle of the path with a key in her hand.

"What the hell?"

He stops.

"Tum dono yahan kya kar rahe ho?"

She opens her mouth, and he does not wait for it.

"Saaman leke wahan jao. Jao."

Purvi goes.

She is fifteen steps away when two women come out of the dark behind her, and they do not know how far fifteen steps is, and one of them says, to the dark: "Wo aadmi itna gusse mein kyun tha?"

"Le, dekh kar aa. Humein dono ko roop badal ke pick karna chahiye."

"Tum aisa kuch nahi karogi, Bharani. Magar yahan kuch to hai jo hum naaginon ke liye khatra ban sakta hai."

"Mujhe bhi ye mehsoos ho raha hai."

At *hum naaginon* Purvi's step falters, with shock. She does not turn. She walks on.

Vihaan steps forward from where he stood back out of the light.

Purvi looks at him once and keeps walking. He smiles at her, small and private, and she does not smile back.

"Parmeet," he says. "Basement ki lift. Secret button box." He says it quietly, so the dark takes it. His throat works, with fear. "Kya kar rahe ho tum dono?"

Purvi walks on with the key in her hand and her eyes on the gravel.

He does not call after her. His hand stays at his side, open and closed and open again, with unease.

* * *
## Meri Marzi

The side of the house has a veranda that the party does not use, and a swing, and a bulb on a wire that has been switched off.

A pair of slippers stands at the foot of the swing, and a dupatta is hanging over the swing's chain where somebody left it.

Purvi comes round the corner with the empty bags folded under her arm, and stops.

Ravish is against the wall with his mouth on a woman's mouth and his hand in her hair.

The woman's back is to the corner, and her bangles are on the step below her feet where she has put them so they will not make a sound.

Purvi does not move for a long moment, with disbelief. Then she says it.

"Ravish sir, kaun thi wo ladki?"

Purvi's voice is quiet and it does not shake, with anger.

"Hum aise kaise kar sakte hain meri behen ke saath?"

The woman's head comes up.

She is not young, and her face is not surprised, and she looks at the girl in the salwar with the folded bags and does not say anything at all.

Ravish straightens his shirt.

"Aap—"

She takes a step.

"Maine apni aankhon se dekha. Aap ek ladki ko kiss kar rahe the. Aap kisi ke saath the."

"Haan."

He says it without heat.

"Kaam hai."

"Ye meri life hai."

He comes down the veranda steps and past her, and he does not stop, and his shoulder goes by hers without touching it.

"Meri marzi."

The woman picks up her bangles from the step, and puts them on, one by one, and follows him round the corner into the light.

* * *
## Down

The lawn at midnight, between the side veranda and the flowerbeds, with the party's light coming out of the house in long shapes across the grass.

The music is still going when Purvi's knees give.

It is not the falling.

There is no height in it and no rail and no child — she is on the lawn between the side veranda and the flowerbeds, and her legs go, and she is on the grass on her side with her arm under her and the folded bags in the dirt.

In the hall, the door stands open, and Vihaan stands in it watching her. He does not come out. He does not call.

He sees her on the grass, and the hall's light falls on the back of his neck, and he stays where he is. His jaw is tight, and his hand is on the door frame, and his breath is steady and slow. Then he draws the door to behind him, and the hall's light narrows off the grass.

A tray of glasses stands on the grass where somebody set it down, and the glasses in it have not been touched.

The grass is wet where the sprinklers have been on, and the wet shows dark on the hem of her salwar.

"Purvi?"

Ananta's voice, from a long way off, and then not a long way off.

"Purvi! Purvi, kya hua?"

Hands on her shoulder, turning her.

"Neeche kaise gir gayi? Chakkar aa gaya kya? Kya ho gaya? Arey bol na, kya hua hai?"

"Dard ho raha hai."

Purvi's teeth are together, with pain.

"Chot lagi hai."

"Kya hua? Kahan dard ho raha hai?"

She puts her hand flat on her chest, under her collarbone, where there is no mark.

"Bahut dard ho raha hai."

"Kyun?"

Ananta's voice comes apart, with panic.

"Purvi, kyun?"

"Kyunki koi meri behen ko chot de raha hai."

Purvi's eyes are open and she is looking at the lit windows of the house, at the marigolds, at the cake on the table inside.

"Saaf-saaf bol na."

"Mera Ravish sir itna achha insaan nahi hai."

She gets her elbow under her.

"Wo ek number ke gire hue insaan hai. Maine apni aankhon se dekha. Wo ek ladki ko kiss kar rahe the."

The song inside ends, and the guests clap, and someone laughs at something.

Ananta's hand goes to her mouth, with horror.

The lawn is full of music from the hall and nobody comes out into it.

Ananta does not speak after that.

She puts her hand on Purvi's shoulder and helps her up, and they stand together on the wet grass while the music starts again inside.
