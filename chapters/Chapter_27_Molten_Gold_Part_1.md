## The Assurance

The serpent shrine holds the late night in one lamp's circle, and the temple crowd's noise stays behind at the court.

Vihaan stands at the court's edge with the apple in his hand, retrieved from the courtyard floor, and his wife stands at the shrine's step with the dizziness still moving behind her eyes.

Neither of them has spoken since the courtyard.

The priest comes forward with his hands raised, calm.

"Are ghabraaiye mat."

His voice carries past her to the family, the score sounding under the assurance (m00:08).

"Naag-naagin kabhi kisi Shiv-bhakt ko haani nahin pahunchaate. Shiv ji hamesha naagon ke saath rehte hain. Aur ye unke rakhwaale hote hain. Inko ek vishesh kripa praapt hai."

Vihaan watches his wife's face while the priest speaks, and files the stillness in it.

Her thali waits on the shrine's step with its diya burned low.

"Kaha jaata hai,"

the priest says, "ke Shiv ji ne apni Naagmani apni shreshth naagin ko sambhaalne ke liye de rakhi hai. Aur Shivratri ke samay mein sabhi naag-naaginon mein ek alag hi taakat utpann ho jaati hai."

A pause.

"Agar koi Shiv ji ka sandesh ho — to naagin use apne sapne mein dekhti hai."

Something changes in her stance at that — a small thing, a breath's worth.

His thumb stops on the apple's skin.

"Iska matlab,"

she breathes, "Mahadev mujhe kuch sandesh dena chahte hain. Isiliye mujhe chakkar aa rahe hain. Aur kuch ajeeb sa dikhaai de raha hai."

The lamp burns steady at the shrine.

Behind them, the temple bells ring on.

Vihaan crosses to her side and holds the apple out.

"Ye tumhaara tha." His voice is quiet.

"Court mein gira tha."

She takes it without looking at him.

Her eyes are on the priest, and far past him.

## The Accusation

The temple courtyard keeps the night in lamp-rows and marigold smoke, and the family clusters near the steps.

The inspector comes in fast with his cap under his arm, and goes straight to Parmeet where he stands with the baskets.

"Sahi samay pe aaya hai ye. Sir — mujhe aapse ek baat kehni thi."

Parmeet turns.

"Tum mere saath aao. Idhar aa."

A woman's thali trembles at the steps, and its diya bows in the draft.

Vihaan stands three paces off with his hands in his pockets, and watches the inspector's face, and files the shine on it.

"Kahan hai wo naagin jo roop badal leti hai?"

Parmeet asks.

"Ichchhadhari naagin — kidhar dekhi toone?"

"Sir, maine use yahi dekha tha."

The inspector's voice carries.

"Wo ichchhadhari naagin hi hai, sir. Maine apni aankhon se dekha use roop badalte hue."

"Mujhe bhi dikhaana,"

Parmeet says, "jo naagin roop badalti hai."

"Sir, wo ichchhadhari naagin hai."

The inspector points — past the baskets, past the steps, at Ahana where she stands with the apple in her hand.

"Tera dimaag kharab ho gaya? Wo meri bahu Ahana hai."

The courtyard goes quiet round them.

Vihaan is already moving — three strides, and he stands between the pointing finger and his wife.

"Bas." His voice cuts flat across the courtyard — one low string under the shutdown.

"Ek shabd aur meri patni ke khilaaf — saboot ke saath, ya bilkul nahin."

He holds the inspector's eyes.

"Naam lete ho to saboot rakhte ho," he says. "Nahin rakh sakte — to ungli neeche karo."

The pointing finger comes down.

The inspector's eyes drop from Vihaan's face — and when he speaks again, it is to Parmeet past Vihaan's shoulder, the bluster in it suddenly borrowed.

"Sir, wo aapki bahu hai — par wahi to ichchhadhari naagin hai, sir."

The inspector unbuttons his holster.

"Tu jhooth bol raha hai na? Agar jhooth bolun na, sir — meri revolver se mujhe maar dena. Isme chhe goliyan hain, sir. Chhe goliyan utaar dena, sir. Main sach keh raha hoon, sir. Wahi naagin hai."

Six bullets.

Vihaan files the number with the pointing finger, and shuts the file.

"Mujhe Baljeet se milna hoga,"

Parmeet says, and turns away with the inspector at his heels.

Behind them, Ahana's fingers tighten once on the apple — and loosen.

She says nothing at all.

## Mannat Talk

The temple courtyard's edge holds the waiting hour in drifting smoke, and the women sit round a pillar with their thalis in their laps.

"Main kisi reeti-riwaz ko nahin maanti."

The voice is young, carrying.

"Lekin haan — agar mannatein poori hoti hain to main chaahoongi mujhe dher saara paisa, mehengi car aur lavish lifestyle mile."

Laughter, and someone answers through it: "Kami to abhi bhi kahaan hai aapke paas, mummy ji."

"Waise dimaag ki kami to tum dono mein bhi hai."

The retort comes back quick, and the laughter rises again.

Thalis pass hand to hand along the pillar, each diya swimming in ghee.

Vihaan passes the pillar on his way to the steps, and the laughter follows him two paces and drops.

A man's voice cuts across from the steps, sharp: "Are! Kaam pe focus karne ke bajaay tum log faaltu ki baatein kiye ja rahe ho. Are humein naya heera chaahiye Ahana se. Uske baare mein socho."

Someone's phone glows face-up on the stone, ringing unanswered.

"Tum mere saath chalo."

Another voice, low.

"Mujhe zaroor baat karni hai."

Near the sanctum steps, half-lost under the bells, three voices trade one line each: "Ji haan — wo ichchhadhari naagin hi hai."

— "Kaun, bhai sahab?"

— "Kaha na, ichchhadhari naagin hai."

— "Ichchhadhari naagin to aap bhi ho."

Vihaan stops on the steps.

He looks back once at the pillar, at the steps, at nothing he can place.

He files the shape of it — accusation loose in the air like smoke — and goes on.

## The Resolve

The veranda shadow behind the puja baskets holds two women close, and no one else near enough to hear.

Bharani's voice is low.

"Mujhe usi din shak ho gaya tha ke koi mujhe dekh hi raha tha."

A clay diya sweats oil onto the stone beside them.

Ahana's eyes are on the courtyard where the inspector went with Parmeet, the score striking under the resolve (m03:23).

"Ab uska kya karen?"

Her voice drops.

"Socha tha ise tadpa-tadpa ke maarungi."

A breath.

"Par ab lagta hai ke Bholenath bhi yahi chahte hain — ke iska ant aaj hi ho."

A cold thali leans against a pillar, the puja long done.

The marigold stirs in the baskets.

The bells cover the rest.

## The Lure

The temple court lies past the precinct line in thin dark, and the crowd's noise stays inside with the lamps.

"Thank you. Hum sab sambhaal lenge. Tum jaao."

The inspector does not go.

"Lekin sir — mera wo inaam."

"Inaam yahan nahin de sakte hum."

"Sir, wo inaam — I am sorry, I am sorry — sorry, sorry."

He wrings his cap.

"Ye Parmeet sahab aise kyun kar rahe hain? Sorry, sorry, sorry, sorry."

"Kahin ye ichchhadhari naagin roop badal de to — kya hua?"

"Mujhe koi inaam nahin chaahiye."

"Sir — inaam dena hai to mandir ke andar hi deejiye."

## Molten Gold

The temple chamber holds its breath in the lamp glow and gold, and the chant comes thin through the stone from the rite above, the score striking twice over the chant (m06:39 x2 as one).

"Om Namah Shivaya. Har Har Mahadev. Om Namah Shivaya."

"Jis naagin par poore Bharat ki suraksha ka bhaar ho — tu maarega use?"

She takes one step toward him.

"Sheshnaag — jinhone poori prithvi ko apne phan pe uthaaya — us naag ke vanshajon ko chhalega tu?"

His revolver comes up.

It fires — once, twice — and nothing touches her.

The sound goes flat against the gold and dies.

"Geeta mein Bhagwan Shri Krishna ne kaha tha ke dhaatuon mein jo sona hai — wo main hi hoon."

She does not hurry.

"Par imaandaari se kamaaye sone mein unka vaas hota hai. Beimaani se kamaaye hue sone mein Kalyug ka vaas hota hai. Jo mati bhrasht kar deta hai."

A beat.

"Jaise teri — galti hai."

"Jis haath se note milte hain uske khot nahin ginte hum."

He backs against the gold.

"Parmeet sahab ke to sau khoon maaf."

"Tu saari zindagi paisa-paisa karta raha."

Her voice does not rise.

"Jis paise ke liye toone apna desh bech diya, imaan bech diya. Aur ab dekh — yahi paisa teri jaan bhi nahin bachaa pa raha."

She lifts her hand, and the gold near her loosens and runs like water past the lamp, shining.

"Mukhe hiranyam niksham tatha tulsi dal vimishram, evam sanskaar prabhute Vishnu lokmat sajj."

The shloka hangs in the chamber.

He stares at her mouth.

"Jaanti hoon teri is moti buddhi mein is shlok ka arth ghusa hi nahin hoga."

She translates it the way a judge reads a sentence.

"Isliye main samjhaati hoon. Mritak ke mukh aur saaton chhidron mein sona rakha jaata hai. Tulsi aur Gangajal se sanskaar hota hai — taaki ek paapi ko bhi moksh ki praapti ho sake."

A pause.

"Par tu — jisne paison ke liye apna desh bech diya, imaan bech diya aur begunahon ki jaan li."

Her eyes hold his.

"Tujhe main moksh nahin de sakti."

A breath.

"Par haan — wo zaroor de sakti hoon jo tujhe zindagi bhar chaahiye."

The gold rises.

He screams.

"Nahin, nahin — chhod do mujhe. Chhod do."

The chamber door stands shut.

Nobody comes.

When it opens again, the lamp burns on over gold, and silence, and nothing else at all.

"Afsoos."

Bharani's own voice now, soft, to the empty air.

"Ke ye shubh kaam main pehle hi kar chuki hoon na."

A spent diya smokes in its saucer on the floor.

The lamps lean.

The corridor keeps its counsel.

## The Confusion

The temple courtyard burns lamplit and restless, and the night search has the whole family in it now.

"Ye inspector ka bachcha kahan reh gaya?"

The voices overlap.

"Phone hi nahin utha. Are bhai sahab, aap bhi kahan inspector ki baaton mein aate ho? Mujhe to lagta hai usne vaapas chalaani hi shuru kar di."

"Parmeet bhai sahab, aapne inspector ko kahin dekha?"

The wife pushes through.

"Wo mujhe mil nahin rahe kahin."

"Usi ko to hum dhoondh rahe hain."

Parmeet turns.

"Are kam se kam tumhein dikhe to — humein to mile bhi nahin."

Vihaan stands by the steps with his hands in his pockets, watching his father's face.

The thoughtfulness there is new.

He files it.

"Ye lo. Achhe lage. Ye lo."

The prasad passes hand to hand.

"Aa gaye chief minister sahab. Idhar aao."

The inspector comes up the steps with his wife at his heels — or the shape of him does.

"Kahan hai wo ichchhadhari naagin? Sir, wo yahi hai. Maine kaha tha na — wo ichchhadhari naagin hai. Aur aaj yahan zaroor aayegi."

"Bahut zehreeli hai wo. Bahut zehreeli."

He looks round.

"Are lekin hai kahan? Main abhi aapko bataata hoon kaun hai wo."

He points.

"Ye rahi wo ichchhadhari naagin."

"Paagal ho gaye ho kya?"

The wife-shape turns on him.

"Inaam ke laalach mein kuch bhi bol rahe ho? Main naagin hoon."

She hauls at his arm.

"Chalo tum — aaj tum chalo. Aaj tumhaari granny."

"Ye inspector aaj agar biwi ke haath se nahin mara na — to ise main jaan se maar doonga."

"Are bhai sahab, ghar ka masla hoga."

Baljeet waves it off.

"Aur mujhe lagta hai isne bahut zyaada peeni shuru kar di hai. Aap aao — ab yahan bhi."

The pair goes, scolding and scolded, into the dark past the prasad tray.

The courtyard laughs, uneasy.

Vihaan does not laugh.

His father's eyes follow the pair past the lamps, and the thoughtfulness does not lift.

He files that too — doubt planted, and something unsatisfied under it — and shuts the file.

## The Call

Their room keeps the small hours behind a shut door, and the lamp burns low on the dresser.

Vihaan lays her on the bed and draws the sheet up, and sits on the bed's edge till her breathing evens.

When her eyes open, he is still there.

"Ye mujhe kya hi dikhaai de raha tha?"

She stares at the ceiling.

"He Bholenath — aap mujhe kya bataana chah rahe hain? Aaj Shivratri hai. Ye kaun si jagah hai? Ye kya hai? Kaun sa mandir?"

Her voice thins.

"Ye jagah maine pehle kabhi nahin dekhi."

"So jaao." His voice is gentle.

"Subah dekhenge."

Her phone lights on the side table.

He rises, and goes out past the curtain to give her the call's privacy, and the door stands ajar a finger's width behind him.

"Hello, baby — tum theek to ho na?"

Vikram's voice comes thin through the speaker.

"Haan, main theek hoon. Don't worry."

Vihaan stands in the passage with his back to the door, and does not mean to listen, and listens.

"Tumhein pata hai, baby — us din mujhe tumhein uske saath bedroom mein dekhkar bahut gussa aaya tha."

A pause.

"Par mujhe pata hai ke tum dono ke beech kuch bhi hua hoga — to uski zabardasti ki vajah se hua hoga. Tumhaari izaazat se nahin."

A breath.

"And I know that."

Vihaan's jaw moves once, without his leave.

The accusation wears his room, his bed, his blank morning — and the truth of that morning is undecided still, her laugh the only answer on record.

He files Vikram's certainty for what it is: a lover's belief, not a witness's.

The file shuts hard.

"Ab tumhein uske saath rehna nahin padega."

Vikram's voice warms with its secret.

"Main kal hi use office mein ek special surprise dene waala hoon. Tum zaroor aana."

A beat.

"Main chahta hoon — us surprise ko dekh ke uske chehre ke jo rang udte hain — wo tum zaroor dekhna."

The call ends.

Vihaan steps back inside, and her eyes meet his over the sheet, and neither of them says what the call cost.

"Haan."

"Ye nahin pata chala."
