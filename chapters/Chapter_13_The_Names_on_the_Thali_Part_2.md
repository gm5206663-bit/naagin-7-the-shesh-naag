## The Thali

The car is at the gate in the morning light, and Gurinder is on the drive with his keys in his hand and his spectacles in the other, and he is in no hurry to go, and the house knows it.

Gurinder is looking at the side table by the door where the gifts have been stacked, and there is a plate on the top of the stack, and it is silver, and there is a name cut into the rim of it.

"Ise maine pehle kahin dekha hai — ye naam."

He turns the plate over.

"Kora hai yaar?", the score sounding under the recognizing (m12:54).

"Pushtaini hain, sir."

The boy at the door has his hands behind his back.

"Bhai sahab ke khaandaan ke. Sab ke naam khudwe hue hain."

Gurinder looks at the plate for a long moment longer than a plate is worth, with suspicion.

Then he puts it back on the stack, square, so that nobody can tell it has been moved.

"Chalo."

He gets into his car.

"Arre, gaadi. Chalo."

The boy watches the car go down the drive and then he goes back to his work, and the plate sits on the stack in the sun.

## The Names on the Thali

The gifts go out of the Suri house at eleven in the morning and come into the Arora house at noon.

Three men carry them.

There are cards in a stack, and sweets in boxes, and cloth in paper, and under it all, wrapped in a bedsheet and tied with tape, twelve kilos of silver.

"Gurinder ji."

The driver has the last of it in his arms.

"Bhai sahab ne ye khaas bheja hai. Ye khaandani aur pushtaini bartan hain."

Gurinder unwraps the sheet on the floor of the front room, and sits back on his heels, and looks at what is inside.

Twelve pieces of silver, plates and bowls and a covered pot, hammered, and on the rim of every one of them the same four names cut into the metal.

"Maine ise pehle kahin dekha hai — ye naam." He says it to himself.

"Oye, Kora hai yaar."

His wife is already putting her hand in the box of sweets.

"Kya naam?"

"Parveen Suri." He turns the plate.

"Baljeet Suri." He turns the plate again.

"Dimple Suri. Pammi Suri."

The room goes quiet.

The fan turns.

"Shalini."

He gets up with the plate in both hands.

"Attari mein humare padosi the. Wahi Suri parivaar. Main is plate ko saalon pehle Parveen bhai sahab ke ghar dekha tha. Aur unse uske kisse bhi sune the."

"Aap kya kehna chahte hain?"

"Main kuch nahin keh raha." He puts the plate down very carefully and does not let go of it.

"Pachees saal pehle har channel pe khabar aa rahi thi. Hamare padosi desh ke jaasoos humare desh mein ghuspeth kar gaye. Aur hamare us Attari ilake mein unke chhupe hone ke chance bahut zyada the. Phir ek khabar aayi ki poora Suri parivaar achanak gayab ho gaya." He looks at his wife.

"Kisi ne kaha, maargaye. Kyunki uske baad unhein kisi ne dekha hi nahin."

"Gurinder."

"Aur ab ye ek parivaar. Praveen Suri. Aur family. Wahi jagah, wahi naam."

His hands are flat on the table.

"Ye ittefaq nahin ho sakta, Shalini."

"Aap apne crime wale serials dekhna band kar dijiye, please."

His wife takes the plate out of his hands and puts it with the others, and then, because she is a kind woman, she says the rest of it kindly.

"Aap aise acchhe logon pe shak rakhna band kijiye. Main ja rahi hoon. Subah se list bhi milani hai.", the score sounding under the going-out (m30:25).

Shalini goes out with the fan turning behind her.

Gurinder stands in the middle of his own front room with twelve kilos of silver at his feet and the names on it.

"Kya ye wahi ghuspethiya jaasoos hain," he says, to the empty room, with dread, "jinhone asli Suri family ko khatam karke khud unki jagah le li?"

The tube light in the front room flickers once, and holds.
