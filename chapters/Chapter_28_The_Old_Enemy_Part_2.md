## The Masks

The Suri hall holds the party evening in bustle and perfume, and half the house crosses it dressed for Vikram's night.

"Haan, dad, main bilkul theek hoon."

Dimple holds the phone close, glowing.

"Aryaman ke saath bahut khush hoon."

Her voice drops.

"Dad — wo aapne promise kiya tha — naayaab heera — uska — what?"

The glow goes out of her face.

"Kya hua, Dimple ji? Papa ne kya kaha?"

— "Mana kar diya."

A set of keys turns in the hall door behind the crush.

"Jaaiye, aajkal in dono ke paas Ahana ke peeche ghoomne ke alaawa aur koi kaam hi nahin hai."

The jibe follows two boys across the hall.

"Mujhe to bahut kaam hai, Dimple chachi. Main bahut excited hoon Vikram ki party ke liye. Main to sabse hot lagoongi wahaan par."

Vihaan comes down with two masks in his hand — his and his wife's — and takes the stairs' side out of the bustle's way.

What she wears tonight is the card's first spending — his gift, carried silent into Vikram's hall.

"Itna taiyaar ho ke to kahaan jaa rahe ho tum log?"

"Taau ji, wo Vikram ki masquerade party hai na — bas wahi." Vihaan answers past the masks in his hands.

"Nahin."

## The Oldest Foe

Her room keeps the evening behind a shut door, and the party bustle stays below with the stairs between.

The voice reaches her out of nowhere — old, amused, close as breath.

"Ahana."

A pause.

"Ya phir tumhein Ananta kahoon? Ya phir kahoon Anant-kul ki Naagrani?"

The amusement deepens, the score striking under the taunt (m18:49).

"Main kaun hoon? Kahan se aaya hoon? Roop-rang kaisa hai?"

A beat.

"Waise hoon to main tumhaara sabse puraana dushman."

Her mask lies on the bed, forgotten.

The lamp on the dresser burns a shade too blue.

"Naagin ke paanch dushman hain."

The voice settles into the telling.

"Wo dushman jo sadiyon se — karodon varshon se — uske peechhe pade hain. Naagin ka sabse pehla dushman mor — jiske saath Naagin ki dushmani yugon puraani hai."

"Doosra dushman hai nevla."

The lamp flame leans, the score running under the lore (m19:40).

"Jo Naagin ka sabse khatarnaak shikaari maana jaata hai. Naagin ka teesra dushman hai cheel. Jo aasmaan se waar karta hai — aur dharti par Naagin ki taakat ko chunauti deta hai. Chautha dushman — barfeele pahaadon ka raaja — Yeti."

A pause.

"Sadiyon pehle ek Yeti ke pairon tale achaanak ek naag aa gaya — aur kuchal kar maara gaya. Gusse mein uske saathi naagin ne us Yeti ko das liya — aur wo wahin dher ho gaya. Jab doosre Yeti ne apne saathi ki laash dekhi — uske shareer pe Naagin ke daanton ke nishaan dekhe — to wo bekaabu ho gaya."

The mirror on the wall gives back only lamplight and her own listening face.

The voice stops.

The fifth name never comes.

## The Book and the Mask

The study keeps the evening in one lamp and stacked files, and the party hour presses closer with every bell from below.

Ahana turns the book over in her hands.

"Tum is book ke saath kya kar rahi ho?"

Aryaman stops in the doorway.

"Oh my god — tumhein bhi fantasies pasand hain? I love fantasy. Aur ye waali to bahut hi cool hai. Indian mythology — naag, naagin, naaglok. Oh my god. So cool hai na?"

"Ye book to office mein thi."

She looks up, the score sounding under the book (m20:41).

"Ye yahaan kaise aa gayi?"

A file slides from the stack and slaps the floor.

"Mujhe kuch yaad kyun nahin aa raha hai?"

Aryaman rubs his forehead, the score striking under the wiped day (m21:18).

"Aise lag raha hai jaise meri life ka ek din mit gaya ho. Total wipe out."

Her bookmark slides from the pages to the floor.

"Shaayad aajkal tum kaam ki zyaada tension lene lage ho. Isliye."

She bends for the bookmark.

— "Maybe. Kuch yaad nahin rehta."

The desk clock ticks past the party hour.

"Sir — sorry, sorry. Wo seriously — whatever."

A peon's voice fades past the study door, the score sounding under the tail (m22:24).

"Taiyaar rehna. Hum fauran niklenge."

Their room keeps the party hour in lamplight and open wardrobe doors, and her mask waits on the bed where the evening left it.

Vihaan comes in with the second mask in his hand — and stops.

She stands at the mirror with the book's lore still in her eyes, and the dread sits on her undisguised.

"Oh ya."

He straightens.

"Mere saath party mein chalogi na?"

— "Date ke liye poochh rahe ho?"

— "Relax. Date bol ke apni expectations mat badhaao. Party hai — koi proposal nahin. Aur waise tumhaara Vikram bhi hoga wahaan. Usi ki party hai."

— "Oh, don't worry about me. Awkward situations — they are my favorite. And I think hum saath mein kaafi cool lagenge."

"Ye tumhaare liye," he says, gentle, the score sounding under the invite kept (m21:58).

She takes it.

He settles it on her face himself, and turns her to the mirror with his hands light on her shoulders — one low string under the mask-giving.

"Dekho," he says. "Achha lag raha hai."

"Ek dance." He says it to her in the mirror, quiet.

"Mere saath. Tonight."

"Pakki."

She looks.

And for one breath the mirror gives her back her other face — the Naagin whole and crowned in the glass — and with the crown, faceless out of the dark behind it, the marked back: paanch phan, sunehra; the locked name.

Anantveer.

Her saathi — her destiny, her equal — shown by Mahadev's own hand and sought since dawn without a face.

And here, human-warm on her shoulders, his hands.

The tear goes through her like the crown's own weight — and panic grips her throat, and her fingers whiten on the mirror's edge.

Then it is gone.

Only her own masked face looks back, and her secret sits safe behind it, and her breathing evens by inches.

She does not step out from under his hands.

He saw nothing but the paling.

His hands steady on her shoulders till it passes, and he asks nothing at all.

## The Leaving

Their room holds its breath after the mirror lets her go, and the party hour presses up from below.

"Mujhe ek kaam hai — party se pehle."

The excuse is thin and she knows it, and her eyes don't leave his.

Vihaan nods once, and lets her go with it.

His file takes the thinness and keeps it.

"Vikram's," he says. "I'll find you there."

Her hand finds his for one breath at the door — and then the stairs take her, and the room keeps only him and the lamplight.

## The Cave

The cave floor keeps the night in ice-blue dark and old bones, and the wind moves through it like something warned.

Her bangles ring once on the ice, and still.

She comes in fast and fearless, and strikes first, and the cave shudders with it.

"Ye aakhiri warning hai."

Her voice fills the ice, the score covering the warning twice over (m23:53+m23:59 as one).

"Mere desh ke khilaaf kaam band karo. Gaddaari maaf nahin hogi."

The wind lifts a cloth strip tied to a bone, and drops it.

The dark settles.

She is gone before it stills.

## The Court

His den keeps the night in low fire and kneeling men against the stone wall, and the air tastes of old smoke and newer fear.

"Nahin mili Naagmani."

Airul's voice comes out of the fire-shadow.

"Kahan hai meri Naagmani? Sawaal seedha hai. Lekin galat jawaab."

A slow clap, mocking.

"Oh — achha hai. Achha hai. Achha hai. Achha hai. Haan — kamaal hai. Kamaal hai. Dhoti faad ke rumaal hai."

The clapping stops.

"Waah — kamaal hai. Kya baat hai. Mazaa nahin aaya. Mujhe tumhaari ye nakli taaliyan pasand nahin hain. Humein pasand hai asli khauf."

Parmeet kneels with his forehead near the floor.

Baljeet kneels beside him, shaking.

A brass plate of offerings cools untouched by the wall.

"Hmm — jaao. Tum jaao."

— "Are nahin — aaj ka hamaara nishaana tum ho."

— "Nahin, bhai sahab. Are bhai sahab — nahin — are are bhai sahab — are bhai sahab — are are bhai sahab — are bhai sahab — are bhai sahab — bhai sahab — "

"Heh."

The fire-shadow shifts.

"Airul ko sirf Airul ki aawaaz pasand aati hai."

Airul leans forward into the light.

"Tum gadhon ko maine sirf teen kaam diye the. Ek — Kumbh mein tabaahi machaana. Doosra — wo hazaaron saal puraana dastavej khoj kar hamaare paas lekar aana. Aur teesra — teesra sabse zaroori kaam. Wo Naagmani ko tumko mere paas lekar aana tha."

A beat.

"Lekin ye teenon kaam mein se tumne ek kaam bhi poora nahin kiya. Ek kaam ko tumne anjaam nahin diya."

A coal breaks in the brazier and sparks climb.

"Hum — agar hum nishaana lagaana chaahein — to hum aankh band karke machhli ki aankh bhi phod sakte hain."

"Waise tumhaare wo do khachchar nazar nahin aa rahe. Kahan bhaag gaye?"

— "Wo to hinhinaate aur bhaag gaye."

— "Bhaagne waale ko lagta hai ke wo Airul se bach jayega."

The voice drops to silk, the score striking under the fled two (m26:34).

"Lekin Airul ki di hui maut se kya koi bach sakta hai? Airul se gaddaari karne waala kutte ki maut nahin marta."

The silk tears.

"Sukh ki maut marte hain. Nahin, Airul — meri — meri Naagmani nahin deni padi unko mujhe. Isiliye wo logon ne mujhse gaddaari ki aur bhaag gaye na."

A breath.

"Wo dono to gaye. Aur ab tum — "

The cellar door groans once in the draft.

"Chaar din ki mohlat maangi thi tum dono ne."

Airul rises out of the fire-shadow.

"Mohlat khatm. Tum dono to khatm."

"Pakka mil jaani chaahiye."

Parmeet's voice cracks upward.

"Bhai sahab — pakka — "

## The Party

Vikram's hall holds the night in masks and music and money's perfume, and Delhi's glittering crowd turns as one when they enter.

"Bhai sahab — Karan bhai — aaj karenge party. Welcome, guys."

The host's friends sweep them in.

"Are Vikram bhai — thanks for inviting."

— "Yahaan — guys, please — welcome home. Thanks, bro, for this classy Valentine's mask — great party."

"You know," he says to the host's friends, "you know, shaadi ke baad kaam hi kaam chal raha tha. Ahana ko romance ka chance hi nahin mila. But now — thanks to you — sab balance ho jayega."

A tray of untouched glasses floats past on a waiter's palm.

"Jitna udna hai abhi ud le, Anand."

The taunt threads the music, meant for one pair of ears.

"Thodi der mein hawa ka rukh badalne wala hai. Big B is here."

"Wow."

A laugh.

"You are wish — in fact Big Babi ki life ka sabse bada upgrade yahi to hai."

Her ring catches the light as she lifts her hand to the crowd.

What the card bought tonight takes the floor's eyes and keeps them.

"Tumhaari aankhen." Vihaan says it low, for her only, through the noise.

"Bahut khoobsurat lag rahi hain."

"Taareefein sunkar main hairaan nahin hoti."

Her smile does not slip.

"Tell me something I don't know. And guys — kaisi chal rahi hai party?"

— "Are badhiya. Superb, yaar. Superb. Nice."

— "Tum dono ke glass kahan hain?"

A young guest lifts his glass toward them, grinning.

"Jab aankhon ka nashaa itna strong ho — to uske saamne meri drink bhi fail ho jaati hai. Phir kuch yaad nahin rehta."

"Main baaki sab check karke aata hoon."

The host's man melts into the crowd.

"Enjoy the party. Alright, everyone. Mask on. Welcome to the Valentine mascot dance floor."

## The Dance Floor

The dance floor holds the midnight in turning light and bass, and the masks blur shining past each other.

"Welcome home, guys."

Vikram's voice rides the music.

"Thank you so much for inviting us. Hello, guys — tumhaara mask kahan hai? Perfect. Guys, come — please, help yourself."

A laugh.

"Main abhi aaya. Hey — "

The crowd parts at the stairs, the score striking three times over the entry (m29:59 x3 as one).

Purav and Radhika come down together, masked and composed, and Vikram goes to meet them with both arms wide.

"Welcome home."

Across the floor, Aryaman's glass stops halfway to his mouth.

He does not move again till they have passed.

The glass sweats forgotten in his hand.

Vihaan watches his brother's face from the floor's edge, and files the stillness.

His eyes go back to Vikram through the turning light, and stay there, and the party-night promise from the boardroom corridor holds its breath in him.

Across the turning light the compliments find her again, and a guest's glass rises toward them both.

Jealousy answers in him before thought can — bodiless and certain, as it answered at the vow — and under it, harder tonight than nature allows and for no reason he can put his hand on, the pull.

His eyes go back to her as breathing goes back to air.

Fear files itself with the rest — the paling, the thin excuse, the door — and the watch holds.

Mid-turn the floor tilts under her — one breath, gone — and her steps steady on nothing she can name.

His hand is at her elbow before the thought to move; too fast, and filed unsaid.

His eyes hold her a breath longer than the dance allows — the grey morning, the possible, unanswered — and the music covers them both.

The music slows under the turning light, and his hand never left her elbow.

"Meri dance," he says.

"Ab."

She turns into his arms as the floor turns round them, and the masks blur past, and for one song the watching floor gets nothing of either of them — one low string under the claimed dance.

Closer than the dance allows, and neither moves back.

Her breath comes too fast for a slow song; his hand at her waist forgets the floor entirely.

The bass keeps time for what neither of them says.

When the song ends she slips from his arms with the music still on her.

## The Letter

The washroom keeps the night behind a latched door in humming tube-light, and the mirror runs wall to wall over marble basins.

She goes in to breathe.

The mirror gives her back her masked face — and for one breath, behind it, a queen's crowned shadow where her face should be.

There.

Gone.

Then the writing crawls across the glass.

"Is baar apne aap ko aaine mein dekh ke sharminda ho lena — kyunki tumhaara andaazaa galat nikla hai, Naagrani."

Her breath stops.

More words bleed up the walls round her.

"Main Yeti nahin — koi aur hoon. Kya hoon? Kaun hoon? Socho, Naagrani. Tumhaara puraana dushman main hi to hoon."

A last line, slowest of all.

"Par ye hai kaun?"

She stands alone among the words, shaken past hiding, and the question has no mouth to answer it.

## London

The London office holds the evening in glass and river-light, and the city burns patient beyond the windows.

Min-ji stands at the desk with the day's last post unopened, and the screen wakes for Delhi before she touches it.

A wedding-week outflow, flagged and held a week — and under it, fresh tonight, a card's first spending, his gift moving through the dark.

She reads the figures twice.

Weddings move through accounts before families, and this one has moved twice now — the marriage in the outflows, the husband in tonight's spend.

The arithmetic of what he has just agreed to does itself, exact and unsparing.

"Oh, Vihaan."

She says it to the empty office, soft, and smiles the smile nobody in Delhi has ever been given.

She books the dawn flight east before the screen sleeps.

No one sends for her.

No one has to.
