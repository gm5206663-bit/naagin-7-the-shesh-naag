# Chapter 15 — Two Weddings, One Day (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 6, second part. The episode ends where this chapter ends: on a crate, on a sentence about a blood-red wedding, and on twelve hours.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

*The twelve seconds the season plays on every night after its first: the theme, then straight in (`canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` §I). The full forty seconds are written once, at the head of Chapter 1, and are not repeated below it.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## The Same Day, Twenty-Four Years Later

One in the morning, at the Arora house, and the terrace is the only place in it with the light off. Sheshnaag's hood is not shown, but the terrace feels like a temple that has forgotten its god.

Gurinder Arora stands at the parapet with his phone in his hand and his spectacles in the other, and he is not talking to anybody.

Twenty-four years. He does the sum again, because the sum is the part that does not fit, and fear sits cold in his chest beside the sum.

A family that comes out of nowhere with a surname half the district shares. A channel that owns a basement. A boy who prints cards in the middle of the night.

And a wedding in the morning that is the only reason any of them sit in the same house.

"Mahakumbh mein bahut badi dahshat phailana chahte hain kya yeh log?" He says it aloud.

"Jab yeh log aaye the pichhli baar" The paper crackles.

"to Mahakumbh tha." He looks at the date.

"Aur aaj, chaubees saal baad, wahi din."

The wind comes over the parapet and turns the edge of his kurta.

"Kuch bahut bada plan kar rahe hain shayad yeh log."

He looks out at the roof across the street, where a water tank sits and nothing else happens.

"Nahin."

He puts his spectacles on.

"Kisi bhi haal mein nahin — main inhein inka maksad kaamyaab nahi hone doonga."

The door to the terrace opens.

Ananta stands in it with her dupatta round her shoulders.

"Papa? Aap yahan?"

"Yes, main sahi tha." He says it to her, and then to the stairwell, where his wife is coming up.

"Mujhe uske saboot khojne honge — Ananta, Purvi ke office mein zaroor kuch ajeeb chal raha hai."

"Papa, abhi?"

"Abhi."

He takes his keys off the parapet.

"Tum dono so jao."

## The Sisters' Room

The girls' room has two beds in it and one of them is covered in clothes. The dressing table has a tin of powder open and a lamp angled wrong, and the ceiling fan makes a sound on the second turn every time.

Ananta is on the near bed with her knees up and her hands folded on them.

Purvi is at the dressing table with her back to the room and her hair down.

"Kya hota hai jab pyaar ho jaye?" Purvi asks it of the mirror, the score sounding four times over the asking (m23:15 x4 as one).

"Kya?"

"Jab pyaar ho jaye to kya hota hai, di?"

Ananta sits up.

"Ek second." She points.

"Tum yeh sab kyun poochh rahi ho, madam? Meri behen ko pyaar to nahi ho gaya?"

"Pata nahi."

Purvi turns round.

"Jab se usey dekha hai, dil kahin kho sa gaya hai. Shayad yeh pyaar hi hai jo mujhe usse pehli nazar mein ho gaya."

The fan goes round twice.

The tin of powder on the dressing table is open and there is a print of a hand in it.

On the far bed the clothes are piled high enough to slide off the edge, and one bangle has already gone to the floor.

"Kahin tere is dil ka chor Vihaan to nahi?"

"Nahin."

Purvi says it at once.

"Woh kyun hoga?"

"Purvi."

Ananta comes forward.

"Yeh sirf dil todna jaante hain — camera-man type ke ladke na, are terrible. Tujhe pata hai kaisa ladka chahiye?", the score sounding under the warning (m23:57).

"Kaisa?"

"Pyaar karne wala. Koi bahut seedha-saadha. Pyaar nibhane wala banda. Bahut hi achha sa aadmi."

Ananta's chin comes up.

"Vihaan jaisa nahi."

"Toh kaisa?"

Purvi puts the comb down.

"Aisa ladka Dilli mein kahan milega, di?"

"Dilli mein nahin milega."

Ananta sits up against the wall with her arms round her knees.

"Isliye to main keh rahi hoon."

"Kya keh rahi ho?"

"Ki jo mile, use dekh ke samajhna."

Ananta's chin stays up.

"Aaj raat sangeet mein mera gaana chal raha tha. Beech mein band ho gaya."

The fan goes round.

The window is open on the lane and a scooter goes past and does not slow down.

"Di, woh—"

"Poora hall chup ho gaya. Do sau aadmi. Aur woh aadmi sound table pe khada tha, haath fader pe."

Ananta says it slowly.

"Usne band karwaya. Kisi ne poochha nahin. Kisi ne kuch nahin kaha."

Purvi holds her sister's look, and the score holds the room with them.

The dressing table mirror has a photograph pushed into the corner of its frame, and the photograph faces the wall.

The fan's cord hangs down over the bed and the switch on it is at the bottom of the swing.

"Uske baad maine dekha."

Ananta's voice stays where it is.

"Tu kaanch mein ghutnon pe thi. Tere paer ke neeche toota hua glass. Aur Parmeet Suri ka beta teen sau logon ke saamne ghutnon pe baith kar tujhe uthata hai. Apne baap ke ghar mein. Apne baap ke mehmaanon ke saamne."

"Woh—"

"Aur ek bhi aadmi usse rokne ke liye nahin uthta. Ek bhi nahin."

Ananta's hand comes down flat on the bed with anger.

"Yeh ghar ka badmaash nahin hai, Purvi. Yeh ghar ka chhota maalik hai. Aur jo us ghar ka maalik hai, woh Papa se aise baat karta hai jaise naukar se."

"Di, bas—"

"Usne tera naam liya."

Ananta does not look away.

"Teen sau logon ne suna. Main ne suna."

Purvi's hand comes off the powder tin and leaves the print of it in the powder.

The bedside lamp is on and its shade has a scorch mark on one side.

"Woh mujhe naam se bulata hai."

She says it quietly, to the tin.

"Yeh galat hai?"

The fan goes round twice.

Ananta looks at her sister for a long moment, and then she lies back down and puts her arm over her eyes.

"Main tumhe kaam se nahin rokti."

She says it to the ceiling.

"Main tumhe us ghar se rokti hoon. Kal main usi parivar mein ja rahi hoon. Main jaanti hoon main kya keh rahi hoon."

"Of course, di."

Purvi picks her comb up.

"Aur mujhe bhala Vihaan se kyun pyaar hoga?", the score sounding under the denying (m23:57).

"Bas."

Ananta's arm stays over her eyes.

"Yeh baatein raat ko nahi karni chahiye. So jao."

"Ruk na."

Purvi opens the slim diary beside the lamp and turns it to the page the list is on, and reads it down with her finger.

"Do Bareilly ke liye cars," Her finger moves down.

"teen Kapurthala ke liye," Ananta groans.

"aur agar na woh Chachi aa rahi hongi" The lamp flickers.

"to unhein zaroor bolungi" She turns the page.

"ki sattu zaroor leke aaye woh."

"Sattu kyun padi hai tumhein?"

"Dekho."

Purvi turns the page round.

"Sab same hai — yeh poori list hai unke naam ki."

She waits, and then she says it.

"Jo hamare desh mein chori-chhupe ghusse hue hain — iska matlab yeh log ghuspaitiye hain. Mahakumbh ki cuttings bhi dekhi hai maine unke kamre mein."

Ananta looks at the page for a while.

Down in the house somebody is running water into a bucket and the noise of it comes up the stairwell.

"Aap so jaao."

She says it gently.

"Kyunki kal shaadi hai aur pheron ke liye aapko raat tak jaagna hai — mujhe lagta hai aapko so jana chahiye.", the score sounding under the sending-off (m24:26).

Purvi closes the diary and puts the light out, and in the dark, from the other bed, her sister says one more thing.

Outside the window the lane's lamp goes out and comes back and stays on.

"Purvi."

"Hmm."

"Kal Mummy ke saamne main kuch nahin bolungi."

"Kuch hua hi nahin, di."

"Haan."

The fan goes round twice in the dark.

"Wahi to."

## The Cake

Half past one, and the house has gone quiet the way a house does after a party, with one light on in the passage and the kitchen still smelling of the catering.

"Chalo."

Ananta is at the door of their room with her hand on the switch.

"Ab so jaana chahiye. Kal pheras hain, raat tak jaagna hai."

"So to jaana chahiye."

Purvi is sitting on the bed with her knees drawn up.

"Kaise so jaaun lekin — apni behen ko birthday wish kiye bina?"

Ananta stops with her hand on the switch.

On the table the plates are stacked and the fork on top of them has been licked and put back.

"Aapko yaad tha?"

"Haan."

Ananta comes and sits down.

"Main bhool gayi — tumhein laga?"

The door opens without a knock and the man of the house comes in with a cake box in both hands.

The box in his hands has a ribbon on it that has been tied badly and tied again.

"Hello, girls. Aa jao."

"Arre, hello ke bachche."

Purvi has her hand on the mirror.

"Knock karke aaya jaata hai ladkiyon ke room mein."

"Exactly."

Aryaman puts the box down on the bed.

"Aur specially tab jab cake mere paise ka aaya ho — yeh sab formality main nahi karta.", the score sounding twice over the coming-in (m24:57 x2 as one).

"Mummy, Papa, aa jao."

Ananta is at the door with her hands cupped round her mouth.

"Purvi ka cake kaatna hai ab."

"Kitchen mein thi — yeh rahi."

Their mother comes in with the knife and the plates and her dupatta over her shoulder.

"Thank you."

"Purvi."

Aryaman sits on the floor by the bed.

"Tere birthday ke liye maine tera naya naam rakha hai — Baby Penguin. Tera birthday hai na?"

"Phir se bakwaas kiya tune na?"

"Aaja, aaja, birthday girl."

"Arre mummy."

Gurinder is in the doorway with his arms out.

"Lekin akal iske ghutnon mein hai abhi bhi —" His arms stay out.

"itna bada ho gaya na toh." The room laughs.

"Yeh dekho, sab gira diya."

Purvi goes down on her knees with the plates and picks them up and says, to the plates, "Main theek hoon. Sab theek hai."

A slice of the cake goes over on the plate and nobody rights it.

"Ek beti ka birthday aur ek beti ki shaadi."

Their mother says it with her hand on the back of Purvi's head, and then again, because she likes the sound of it.

"Dono ek din."

"Sahi baat boli aapne."

Aryaman is laughing now.

"Agle saal se dono ki party ek hi din mein nipata denge — humare kitne paise bach gaye."

"Ek minute."

Ananta holds her phone up.

"Ek selfie le lein cake katne se pehle.", the score sounding under the selfie (m25:50).

They get in together, four heads and a cake, and the flash goes, the score sounding twice over the singing (m25:50 x2 as one), and nobody in that photograph is looking at the thing that is not in it.

The camera is on the bed between them when the flash goes, and it stays there afterwards.

"Chalo."

Gurinder is at the door again.

"Chalo, jaldi so jaate hain — abhi ho gaya na sab kuch.", the score sounding under the winding-up (m26:28).

"Arre suniye."

Their mother catches his sleeve.

"Itni jaldi mein aap kahan ja rahe ho?"

"Zara bahar kaam hai mujhe."

"Bahar is waqt?"

"Tum sahi keh rahi thi."

He has his spectacles in his hand.

"To socha ki extra card chhapwa ke baant denge — humne kitne logon ko cards nahi diye hain na? Main cards print karke le aata hoon."

The door shuts behind him.

In the room the four of them sit on the floor round a cut cake, holding the quiet between them, and the fan makes its sound on the second turn.

The knife lies on the cake box with the icing still on it.

The window in that room is shut and the room has gone warm with the four of them in it.

## A Man Who Is Not In It

Two in the morning, and the hall comes down around the last of the family.

Chairs on their sides. The sound table gone to the van.

A boy going along the wall with a bin bag and the marigold going into it.

Vihaan is on the lawn with the light-and-sound vendor's final bill in one hand and his phone in the other, walking the line of the tent.

A man comes past with a coil of cable over his shoulder.

"Chhote sahab."

He stops.

"Woh log aa gaye hain. Gate pe gaadi khadi hai."

"Kaun log?"

"Woh."

The man nods at the drive.

At the gate a car stands with its lights on, and four people are getting into it, and one of them has a case on her shoulder and one of them is being helped in by the elbow.

"Yeh sangeet mein aaye the?" Vihaan looks up from the bill.

"Ji. Subah se."

The man shifts the coil.

"Aapke Tauji ke mehmaan."

"Mehmaan." Vihaan looks at the gate.

"Kis kaam ke?"

"Pata nahi, sahab."

The man goes.

"Hum kaam waale hain. Humse kaun batata hai."

Vihaan stands with the bill in his hand and watches the car go out through the gate and turn left, and holds the watching till the tail-lights are gone.

"Ek baat." He says it before the man with the coil is out of hearing.

"Tauji ke mehmaan teen din se hain. Khaana kahan khate hain?"

"Chhote sahab, woh apna khud ka laate hain."

The man stops.

"Aur, sahab—"

"Bolo."

"Humare ladke terrace pe nahin jaate raat ko. Do saap hain. Kale. Ek to kal raat se wahin hai, tanki ke neeche."

The man shifts the coil to his other shoulder.

"Maine Parmeet sahab ko bola tha. Unhone kaha jo hai so hai."

Vihaan looks up the side of the house at the terrace and the water tank against the sky.

"Kisi ko kuch mat bolna."

He turns back to the bill.

"Aur kisi ko bhi terrace pe mat bhejna. Main dekh lunga."

The man nods and goes.

"Oye."

One of the uncles comes out of the hall with a bottle in one hand and two glasses hooked in the other, and he is the cheerful one at every function in that house.

"Chhote, aa. Aaj to peeni padegi. Kal tera bhai shaadi kar raha hai."

"Main nahin peeta, chacha."

"Tu nahin peeta?"

The uncle puts the glasses down on the wall.

"Poora London, poora London, aur tu nahin peeta? Kya karta hai phir tu wahan, doodh?"

"Kaam." Vihaan puts the phone in his pocket.

"Aur chacha, aapke do glass hain."

"Ek tera tha."

"Ek mera nahin hai."

He picks one glass up and puts it back on the tray of the boy going past with the crates.

"Aap apna peeiye. Main andar hoon."

"Arre, ruk."

The uncle is laughing now, and it is the laugh of a man who has had four.

"Sun. London mein ladkiyan bhi hain? Ya wahan bhi tu kaam hi karta hai? Kyunki teri umar mein—"

"Chacha." Vihaan stops at the step with his back to him.

"Aapke bete ne aapko bataya nahin?"

"Kya?"

"Kuch nahin."

He goes up the steps.

"Subah chaar baje uthna hai. Aapko bhi."

The uncle stands with his bottle and looks after him, and says something to nobody that the lawn takes away.

Inside, the hall is empty and the marigold is down off the chandelier and somebody's dupatta is over the back of a chair by the far wall, and he looks at it for a second and does not go near it, and goes up the stairs past it.

In the morning the small book that the gate keeps has four names in it in Shyam's slow hand, and all four are crossed out, and beside them the time of the going: ten past two.

## The Guests Are Cancelled

Two in the morning, and the Suri front room is full of people who are not guests.

"Mummy."

Tina is in her robe with her hair half done.

"Sab mehmaanon ko cancel kyun kiya hai? Ab bataogi bhi?"

"Baby—"

"Tina ki wedding ko leke main kitna excited thi, you know na."

She has her phone in her hand and it is going.

"Ki is shaadi mein poora shehar aane wala hai — infact mujhe to laga tha."

"Arre baby, kya karein?" Vihaan comes in from the veranda in the middle of it with his coffee.

"Aur tum, tum kya kar rahe ho? Tumhare bhai ki shaadi hai."

"Main dekh raha hoon, Mummy."

He does not raise his voice.

"Sab dekh raha hoon."

Dimple has her hand on her own forehead.

"Woh ladki ultiyan par ultiyan kiye jaa rahi thi — us ladki Ananta ki haalat dekhi thi na aapne sagai mein?"

"Mummy—"

"To hamari bahut badnami hoti — agar logon ko pata chal jaata ki woh pregnant hai isliye shaadi ho rahi hai."

She looks round the room.

"Ab call karke mana karna padega — maine Bollywood ke saare bade producers aur actors ko invite kar diya tha."

"Please, relax."

Roma has her arm round her from the other side.

"Come, sit here."

She sits, and she looks up at all of them, and her chin comes up.

The tray of tea that has been brought in stands on the table with every cup on it full, and nobody reaches for one.

The chairs in the front room have been pulled into a half-circle and not put back.

"Aap logon se pehle hi keh rahi hoon main."

Her voice carries.

"Yeh sab nahi chalega mere Vikram aur Pinky ki shaadi mein."

"Baby."

The elder of the house is at the sideboard.

"Bilkul waisa hoga — aap jaisa chaahoge."

"Aap chinta mat kijiye, bhabhi."

Roma's hands are up.

"Usmein jise chahe bula lein — hum log bada reception karenge."

"Filhaal."

Dimple's hand comes down on the arm of the chair.

"Humein guest cancel karne padenge filhaal.", the score sounding under the cancelling (m27:51).

On the lawn the workers are taking the chairs down off the stacks and putting them back up again in a different order, because there are three hundred chairs and forty people coming.

Out on the drive a stack of chairs goes over and nobody picks it up.

## The Print Room

Ten minutes to two in the morning, and the channel's office has four windows with light in them and the rest of it dark.

The month-end is on the fourth floor, and the month-end is his.

Two screens, a printout with a ruler across it, a cup with the tea gone cold at the bottom, and the whole floor dark behind him except the strip that the lift throws up the wall.

He is on the phone with the London end when the lift comes up and Shyam brings a man down the corridor.

"Sahab."

The guard's cap is in his hand.

"Yeh Ananta ji ke papa hain. Cards print karwane aaye hain."

Gurinder Arora is in the same cream kurta, and he has a folder under his arm, and he is tired the way a man is tired at the end of the day his daughter marries.

"Excuse me."

He stops a step short, because the boy is on the phone.

"Namaste ji."

He puts his hands together.

"Kal meri beti ki shaadi hai, Ravish Suri ke saath — main woh Ananta ka father."

"Main jaanta hoon." Vihaan puts the phone down on the desk and stands up.

"Vihaan."

"Haan ji."

Gurinder looks at him for a second longer than the introduction needs.

"To print karwane the — cards kam pad gaye the."

He shifts the carton.

"Parmeet ji ne kaha" The corridor hums.

"ki office mein yahin print karwa lo," He lowers his voice.

"kyunki ab is waqt" Nobody passes.

"koi shop khuli nahi milegi kahin."

"Printing machine hai hamare paas." Vihaan comes round the desk.

"Yeh aap mujhe de dijiye."

He takes the folder, and he does not go.

He turns the first page up and looks at it.

"Ek minute."

He puts it down again.

"Aap yahin rukiye. Main kar ke laata hoon."

"Thank you."

The fourth floor is dark and the machine is two floors down, in the room behind the print desk, and the room is locked, and the key is in the drawer of the empty desk outside it, and that takes four minutes.

Gurinder Arora waits where he is left.

Then he walks a little way along the corridor, because a man who has been standing all day will walk a little way along any corridor.

The board at the end of it has the sangeet prints pinned across it in rows, still up because nobody has taken them down: the hall, the aunts, the child with the garland, Tina's father asleep in a good chair.

And in the third row, one picture of a pair dancing with a lamp behind them, and the girl's face half turned away, and the man's hand flat on her back.

Gurinder takes his spectacles out of his kurta and puts them on and stands in front of that picture for a while.

"Ji."

The guard is at the end of the corridor.

"Woh chhote sahab neeche gaye hain. Chaar minute."

"Haan."

He folds the spectacles.

"Yeh ladka kaun hai?"

"Vihaan sahab. Chhote sahab."

The guard says it easily.

"Parmeet sahab ke chhote bete."

Gurinder looks at the picture again, at the eyes and the set of the shoulders, and he puts the spectacles away and does not say anything else about it.

When he comes back up with the carton, the corridor is empty and the print desk has a page on it that has not been there before, and the page is a list of names.

He stands at the desk and reads it, and his hand is flat on the counter.

"Chhote sahab."

The guard is at the end of the corridor.

"Woh sahab chale gaye. Bola ki subah aayenge."

"Kitne bajay gaye?"

"Abhi. Do minute pehle."

Vihaan looks down the corridor at the lift.

The indicator above it is not on 4.

It is on **B**, and it stays on B for a long moment, and then it goes out.

Then he takes the pen off the print desk, and he opens the visitors' book, and he writes the time in the margin, and under it, in his own hand, one line: *lift B — barah baje ke baad — Ananta ji ke father gaye nahin, doosri gaadi se gaye.*, one low string under the writing.

Vihaan closes the book.

He puts the pen back in the holder and turns it so that it lies the way it lay.
