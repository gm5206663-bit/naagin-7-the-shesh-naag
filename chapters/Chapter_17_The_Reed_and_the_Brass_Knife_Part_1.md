# Chapter 17 — The Reed and the Brass Knife (Part 1)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 7, second part. The ledger calls it by the two things the men say out loud before they do them.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

*The twelve seconds the season plays on every night after its first: the theme, then straight in (`canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` §I). The full forty seconds are written once, at the head of Chapter 1, and are not repeated below it.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.

## The Reed

The hall at one in the afternoon, with the chandelier on and the tent's canvas going up outside the windows.

"Ek minute."

There is a chair in the second row with two coils of the sound men's cable on the seat of it and nobody sitting in it, and when one of Tina's uncles asks where *chhote sahab* is, Parmeet says *Ghaziabad* and does not turn his head.

Parmeet Suri stands up out of the chair by the fire and puts both his hands behind his back, and the hall waits for him the way a hall waits for a man who has not yet spoken the useful thing.

The tray of the wedding's last tea stands on the sideboard with the cups against each other.

"Bahu rani ne kaha ki woh naagin hai."

He says it without any triumph in it at all.

"Toh phir main bhi ek baat bata doon."

Parmeet turns round to the men who came with him.

The been lies across the knees of the man who carries it, and the case for it is at his feet with the lid open.

"**Maarunga reedh ki haddi pe na,**"

He touches the back of his own neck, lightly, to show them where.

"**to mar jayegi us second mein. Bardasht nahi kar payegi.**"

He taps the spot.

"**Reed ki haddi pe [maar],**"

He looks round at them.

"**aur uska kaam tamaam — kamzori hoti hai naagin ki.**"

"Papa."

Ravish's voice.

"Papa, aap kya—"

"Beta, tu chup reh."

Parmeet does not look at his son.

"Aaj tera din nahi hai."

"Yeh galat hai."

Ravish takes a step forward.

"Papa, yeh galat hai."

Parmeet's hand comes up, not touching him, stopping him.

"Chup reh, beta. Tera din nahi hai."

"Yeh uski behen hai."

Ravish's voice is lower now.

"Papa, yeh uski behen hai."

Ananta stands in front of the fire with her hands at her sides and does not turn round.

* * *
## "Been Bajao"

The hall at half past one, with the chandelier on and the two rows of chairs filled to the walls.

"**Koi nahi hilega.**"

Parmeet says it to the room and not to the men, and the men move anyway.

The tray of tea stands at the side of the hall with the cups against each other and nobody has taken one.

"Main sabka yahi haal kar dunga — koi nahi hilega."

He walks the length of the fire and comes back.

"Wahan se mera been lao. **Humein ise naagin roop mein laana padega — isse naagin ko paane ke liye.**"

"Papa."

Ravish's voice from near the door.

"Papa, yeh mat karo. Woh hamari apni hai."

Parmeet does not turn.

"Jao yahan se, Ravish."

"Nahin jaunga."

Ravish takes a step forward.

"Yeh galat hai, Papa. Bahut galat hai."

In Parmeet's pocket the phone begins to ring against the cloth, and he puts his hand flat over it and does not take it out, and the name on the screen is Vihaan's, and it rings until it stops, and then it begins again.

The man at the passage does not move.

The tent's canvas comes in and goes out at the window and the light through it moves on the floor.

A glass stands on the sideboard with the water in it and nobody has touched it.

"Been bajao," the score sounding twice over the playing (m15:37 x2 as one).

Ravish does not move.

He stands there, looking at his father, and the hall waits with him.

* * *
## The Sound From the West

It comes from the west side of the house, past the tent wall and the store room and the small room at the back where the case has been standing open on the floor since before dawn.

Two notes.

Then a third, and then the fourth, and the fourth is the one that stays.

The case on the floor of the small room has its cloth turned back, and the reed in it is not there.

In the hall the men put their hands over their ears and the women at the back stand up, and Ananta's shoulders come down half an inch, and nothing else in that room happens at all.

Purvi's hands go to the sides of her own head.

"Ise band karo."

She says it to nobody.

"Kisi ne— ise band karo."

"Purvi."

Her mother has her by the arm.

"Purvi, baith ja. Baith ja, meri bachche."

The been goes on, the score sounding six times over the playing (m16:19+m16:24+m16:31+m16:38+m16:49+m16:56 as one).

It is not loud.

It is the wrong kind of quiet.

* * *
## Outside the Wall

At the west wall, on the other side of the tent, in the dark, an old woman in a sari and a younger one with her dupatta drawn over her head stand with their hands folded and their faces turned to the sound.

"**Aa rahi hai bahut badi aafat,**" the score sounding under the coming (m17:03).

Uttara's voice is not steady with fear.

"**Mujhe andar jaana hoga.**"

"Aapke saath chalungi main."

"**Nahin, Bharani.**"

Uttara's hand comes out and stops her.

"**Wahan se khatra aaye to tumhein sambhalna hai — tum yahin ruko.**"

"Ma."

"Yahin ruko."

The old woman looks at the gate, and then at the wall, and then at the girl.

"**Is dhun se in sabke saamne aa jayega Purvi ka sach,**" the score sounding under the knowing (m16:02).

Uttara puts her hand flat on the wall.

The old woman's stick stands against the wall beside her and she does not lean on it.

The tent's rope is tied to a peg at their feet and the peg is going out of the ground.

Beyond the wall the lane is empty and the lamp at the corner is on.

"In logon ka dhyan bhatkana hoga mujhe."

She says it to the brick.

"Purvi ko bachana hoga."

"Main aapke saath jaungi."

Bharani's hand comes out, and this time Uttara does not stop it.

"Main jaungi, Maa."

Uttara looks at her for a moment.

"Nahin. Tum yahin raho."

* * *
## "Yeh To Naagin Roop Mein Aa Hi Nahi Rahi"

The been stops on a note that does not resolve, the score sounding twice over the stopping (m17:21+m17:26 as one), and Maa Sapela's man comes into the hall in the chandelier's light with it in his hands, and Parmeet looks at his daughter-in-law.

Ananta stands where she stood.

Her sari is on the floor.

Her hands are loose.

Her chin is up.

Nothing about her has changed except the breathing.

"Papa."

Ananta's voice is a thread.

"Papa, yeh kya kar rahe ho?"

The kechuli lies on the floor by the bride's chair where it came off her.

A pen lies open on the register by the fire with its cap off.

"Chup."

Parmeet does not look at her.

"Chup reh, bahu."

"**Naagin roop mein to aa hi nahi rahi yeh.**"

The man plays again, four bars, and the room watches a woman stand still through it.

"Aayegi."

Parmeet says it with his eyes on her.

"Aur apne naagin roop mein aa jayegi."

He lifts the been.

"Meri been ki dhun ko sun kar"

His fingers find the holes.

"bardsht nahi kar payegi."

Parmeet takes one step towards her.

"Aur naagmani humein de de — **maan ja, ichchhadhari naagin.**"

Ananta does not move.

"Naagmani ko nahi."

His voice drops.

"Apne aap ko bacha. De de."

Ananta looks at him, and then past him, at her sister, and she says nothing at all.

* * *
## "Naagrani Se Door Hat"

The hall at two in the afternoon, with the chandelier on and the canvas beating at the windows.

The service door comes off its hinge, the score sounding under the breaking (m18:28).

The service door's hinge has torn the frame, and the splinters are on the floor.

It comes off because a woman puts her shoulder into it and walks through, and she is old, and her sari is dark, and she is across the floor before the two men nearest the door have decided to move.

"**Naagrani se door hat.**"

"Kaun hai yeh?"

Parmeet turns round.

"Kahan se aayi yeh? Pakdo ise."

The old woman's hand comes up and the fire goes out.

The hall is dark and then it is not: the two lamps at the far end come on with a sound, and the room stands where it stood, and the men have not moved, and the woman is between the fire and the bride.

"**Yeh kaun hai?**" Ravish's voice, at the back.

"**Is desh ki khasiyat,**" the score sounding under the naming (m18:42).

The man with the been says it, and he does not have to think about it.

"**Ichchhadhari naagin.**"

* * *
## The Order

The hall at two in the afternoon, with the chandelier on and the floor cleared back to the pillars.

"**Reed tod do is naagin ki,**" the score sounding under the order (m18:42).

Parmeet's voice is not loud and the whole hall hears it, because he has spoken the sentence once already today.

A register lies open on the table by the fire and the pen on it has rolled to the edge.

Then, more slowly, the way a man says a thing he wants written down:

"**Aur uske baad**"

He raises the knife.

"**peetal ke khanjar se waar karo iske seene pe — aur mar jayegi ye naagin.**"

The old woman's hands are up.

"**Ye naagin nahi bachegi jab tak meri been bachti rahegi.**"

Somebody puts the brass knife into the hand of the man nearest the fire.

The knife is old and it has no edge on it.

An edge is not what it is for.

Then the hall moves, all at once.

The old woman is not where the knife is meant to go.

The bride is standing in front of her, and the bride does not move out of the way, and what happens in the next twenty seconds happens to a woman in a wedding sari.

* * *
## The Strike

Purvi is held by two men at the wall, the score sounding under the holding (m19:12), in the chandelier's light and she does not see it.

What she hears, the score sounding under the hearing (m19:19), is a sound she will be able to describe for the rest of her life and never does: a short, flat thing, and a long one after it.

Her mother is on the floor with her hands over her head.

The reed is on the floor in two pieces, and one of them has gone under the chairs.

The lamps go out again.

The brass knife stands on the tray at the side of the hall with nothing else on the tray.

When they come back on, the old woman is down on one knee by the fire, and the bride is on her side on the floor with her sari under her and one arm out, and the brass knife is standing in the ash at the edge of the fire with the handle up.

"Ananta!"

Purvi's voice comes out of her at a pitch that is not her own.

"Didi—"

"Chhodo ise."

Parmeet says it to nobody in particular, and the men let go of Purvi's arms.

"Iske paas kuch nahi hai. Ise utha lo."

* * *
## Twenty Minutes

The inspector is at the door, the score sounding under the waiting (m19:48), in the afternoon light with his cap in his hand and his car running in the drive.

"Sir."

His constable is at his shoulder.

"Jo bhi karna hai, jaldi kijiye — **bees minute mein police ki doosri van aa jayegi.** Phir main kuch cover-up nahi kar paunga."

"Mujhe naagmani nahi mili hai abhi tak, lekin."

Parmeet's voice comes from behind him, from the passage, where he has been standing for a while.

"**Bhai sahab, naagmani to hum baad mein bhi le lenge,**" the score sounding twice over the dying (m19:56 x2 as one).

The constable says it to both of them.

"**Agar ye zinda rahega na, hum apna kaam kabhi poora nahi kar payenge — lekin usse pehle inka marna zaroori hai.**"

The inspector looks at his watch, and then at the hall, and then at the drive.

A pen lies open on the register at the door and the ink on the nib has dried.

The inspector's cap has a badge on it with the pin lost, and it sits crooked on him.

"Chal."

He puts his cap on.

"Kya koi dekh raha hai?"

"Koi nahi."

The car goes out through the gate.

The drive has the car's dust all over the flagstones and the gate is open wide, and the gate's register lies open on its table with the pen on its string.

* * *
## Ananta Sweet

Ravish comes round the fire in the hall, the score sounding under the coming (m20:24), and stands over his bride with his hands in his pockets.

"**Ananta sweet.**"

He crouches down.

"Kahan jaa rahi ho tum?"

Her eyes find him.

It costs her something to do it.

A tray stands at the side of the hall with the sweet boxes on it still tied.

The chairs by the wall are pushed out of line and one of them is turned to face the wall.

"Maa banne wali thi na mere bachche ki?"

He smiles.

"Mujhe mere dad ne — hum kitna pareshaan kiya."

He looks over his own shoulder at his father.

"Tumse chhutkara mil jayega — lekin ab afsos. Finally."

"Ravish."

Ananta's voice is a thread.

"Seedhi si baat hai."

He puts his hand out.

"Hum suna nahi tumne? **Naagmani mujhe de do, warna maari jaaogi.**"

Ravish waits.

A glass of water stands on the floor by the bride's chair with a straw in it, untasted.

"I said, naagmani de."

* * *
## What She Says Instead

Purvi is on the floor beside her sister in the chandelier's light, with her sister's head in her lap and the two men have stopped holding her, because there is no longer any reason to.

"Didi."

She is wiping blood off a face with her own dupatta, and she is not doing it well.

"Didi, phir se utho. Didi—"

Ananta's hand comes up and finds her wrist.

The kechuli and the torn sari are on the floor within reach of her hand, and nobody has taken them up.

"Papa ne kaha tha."

Her voice is very quiet and very clear.

"Ichchha nahi — naagin. Tu naagin hai."

"Didi, chup karo—"

"**Tujhe chuna gaya. Tu hai,**" one low string under the choosing.

Ananta's eyes do not close.

"**Main nahi — isliye lagta [hai].**"

Her fingers tighten.

"Papa ne kaha tha," She breathes.

"mujhe tujhe bachana hai."

"Didi."

"**Mujhe maaf karna.**"

Her hand comes down off the wrist with grief.

"**Tujhe bacha nahi payi main.**"

"Nahin nahin."

Purvi's hands are on her sister's face and her sister's face has gone the way faces go.

"Didi, didi, phir se utho. **Didi, phir se utho,**" the score sounding twice over the waking (m21:43 x2 as one).

The hall is quiet.

Somebody has put the fire out and nobody has lit it.

The tray of water stands by the wall with the glasses still full.

* * *
## Neeche

Purvi comes down the drive at a run, the score sounding twice over the running (m22:16+m22:24 as one), in the afternoon light, with her dupatta gone and her hands red, and the inspector's car is halfway out of the gate, and she puts both her hands flat on its bonnet.

"Sir."

She is out of breath and she does not stop.

"Sir, rukiye. **Bacha— bas bacha hua sir.**"

"Madam—"

"**Kya aapke ghar waale mere ghar waalon ko maar rahe hain woh neeche?**"

She says it to the windscreen.

"Meri mummy. Papa. **Please. Please rok lo. Bacha lo, sir.** Jaan-jaan se. Woh phool se khoon mein…"

The window comes down four inches.

The gate's book lies open on its string with the day's page wet at the corner, and the last line on it is in Shyam's hand — *chhote sahab, gaadi, gyarah baje* — and there is no line under it.

The gate's boom is up and the dust hangs in the afternoon light behind the car.

A file lies on the car's seat with the window down on it.

"**Kaun maara hai?**"

"Woh log."

"Kya bol rahi ho tum?"

The inspector's face does not change.

"Arre, Purvi. Purvi, tum woh dekho—"

"**Ye is desh ke nahi hain,** sir."

Her hands are still on the bonnet.

"Par aap unmein se nahi ho. Please. Please bata dijiye. Meri family ko zaroor mujhse maar denge. Woh jaan se maar denge. Zaroor, sir. Please."

The window goes back up.

Her sandal has come off on the drive and lies where it came off.

A tray stands on the gate's table with the visitor cards on it and the pen beside them.

The car goes out through the gate and turns left, and its light goes down the lane and is gone, and behind her the tent wall blows in the wind once and settles.

* * *
## Upstairs

The inspector's car is out of the gate in the afternoon light, and Purvi goes back up the steps and through the front room and into the passage where the phone is, and her hands are shaking so much that she gets the receiver off the stand and has to hold it with both of them.

There is no dial tone.

The phone's cradle is still rocking on the stand, and the cord has been pulled out straight.

At that hour the man she is trying to call is on a kerb outside a sweet shop in Ghaziabad with three sleeping children in the car behind him, telling a boy at his own assignment desk to pull a tape.

The passage light is off.

The light from the hall comes as far as the first step of the stairs and stops.

Then Vihaan is standing at the top of the stairs.

"Tum."

Purvi puts the receiver down on the stand and it misses and hangs by its cord, swinging.

"Tum aa gaye. Tum—" Purvi smiles small and broken at him, the smile of a girl who has just seen the one person she does not dare to hope for.

Vihaan comes down the stairs slowly, in his shirtsleeves, with his hair dry and his cuffs buttoned.

"Suno."

He takes her by both arms, jaw tight, hands steady, thumb brushing her sleeve through the cotton, "Suno."

His hands are cold through her sleeves, and she does not notice it.

"Kisi ko kuch nahi hone dunga main." He says it evenly, in the flat way a man says a thing he has spoken before.

"Especially tumhein. Trust me."

"Woh log phool le kar aaye the—"

"Trust me."

His eyes do not go past her face at all.

"Kisi ko kuch nahi hone dunga main."

And then Vihaan laughs.

It starts small and private, then turns small and cold.

It is not a big laugh.

It comes out of him at the end of the sentence and it goes on a little longer than a laugh should, and it is one note lower than the man she knows.

"Tumhein kya laga?"

He lets go of her arms and steps back on the step.

"Ki main kuch jaanta hoon?"

"Kya—"

"Main London ka hisaab dekhta hoon."

He puts his hands in his pockets.

"Tumhare ghar ka nahin. Is ghar ka nahin. Aur tumne mujhe kis liye bula liya?"

"Kyunki tumne kaha tha."

Her voice is very small in that passage.

"Kal raat. Lobby mein. Naam list mein likhwao."

"Koi list nahin hai."

He smiles small and broken at nothing, then smiles small and cold at her.

"Koi Manoj nahin hai. Koi aath baje nahin hai."

The receiver swings on its cord and touches the wall and swings back.

"Tumhare ghar waale mar rahe hain"—he says it lightly, and he is looking at her face while he says it—"aur tum apne premi ko dhoondh rahi ho."

Her hand goes out and hits him in the chest, and he takes half a step back and puts his own hand where she hit him.

Vihaan smiles small and broken after the slap, the smile of a man who has been hit by the only person he would let hit him.

"Anh."

He straightens his collar.

"Zor se lag gayi. Dard ho raha hai."

Her fist has something in it when it comes away.

One button, off the placket of his shirt, and the thread on it is not a shirt's thread, and she closes her hand on it.

Then Vihaan takes her by the shoulder, hand steady on her shoulder, and turns her, and the knife is in his other hand, and it goes into her back just to the left of her spine, and it is quick enough that she does not shout.

"Tumne insaan chun liya." He says it with his mouth at her ear, his breath steady against her neck.

"Woh tumhara nahin tha kabhi."

Purvi goes down onto the third step with her hands in front of her and her chin on the wood.

Silent tears come down her face, and she does not wipe them away.

There is a hand flat on the side of her head, holding it up so that her mouth is not in the dust.

It is warm and it does not let go until her breathing has come back to something that will keep going.

The passage light goes on.

There is nobody on the stairs.

The receiver is still swinging on its cord, and her blood is going down between the third step and the second in a thin line and stopping at the carpet where the carpet starts.

Purvi is still awake.

Purvi stays awake for a long time.

### Continuity and source note

This chapter is the second half of Episode 7, in the episode's own order: the weakness said out loud in front of the woman it is for; the been brought and played; the instrument that finds no naagin in the room; Uttara at the wall and then through the service door; the order — the reed, then the brass knife; the two sisters on the floor; the inspector counting his twenty minutes; Ravish asking a dying woman for a pearl she does not have; what Ananta says instead; Purvi on the drive with her hands red; Aryaman's promise and Aryaman's laugh; the corpses and the final plan; the dragon; and a hall of stone where the lamps stand over a body and the instrument's own question goes unanswered. Beats **B21 to B41** of `canon/E07_BEATS.md`.

**The one reading this chapter takes, and it is reversible.** The track is ambiguous for about ninety seconds. A second naagin comes in (B26), the men are told to break *"is naagin"*'s reed (B27), and ninety seconds later Ravish is crouched over **Ananta** asking for the pearl, and it is **Ananta** who says *"mujhe maaf karna, main tujhe bacha nahi payi."* This page reads the death as Ananta's: she claimed the pearl's name in Chapter 16 to save her sister, and the men kill the woman who claimed it. Uttara is driven off, not killed. The reading is declared in `canon/E07_BEATS.md` §2 ruling 5 and can be reversed without touching anything else in the chapter except one paragraph.

**What the been is for, and why it fails.** Canon orders it played (B22) and canon's own man says what it would do — *"is dhun se Purvi ka sach in sabke saamne aa jayega"* — and then the instrument finds nothing, because the woman standing in front of the fire is not a naagin and never was. **`S-16` is answered by canon here, not by the workshop**: the compulsion was to be *prepared, not played*, and canon plays it, and it fails, and nothing has to be added.

**The instrument stays off the page.** Canon never says who plays it. It is Maa Sapela's been, in her case, in the small room at the back of that house since before dawn — and the page keeps her out of the hall, keeps the sound four bars long and audible from the west side of the house, and lets the family hear the thing it brought into its own wedding without ever learning what it was.
