# Chapter 17 — The Reed and the Brass Knife (Part 2)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 7, second part. The ledger calls it by the two things the men say out loud before they do them.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Newsroom

Two in the afternoon, and the car is standing under the shade of a peepal outside a sweet shop on the Ghaziabad road with three children asleep in it and one awake and asking for a cold drink.

Vihaan is on the kerb with his elbows on his knees and the phone at his ear.

"Rukh, rukh, rukh."

He puts a hand over his other ear.

"Yeh bulletin kaun likh raha hai?"

"Sir, woh subah se ready hai."

The boy at the other end is at the assignment desk of his own channel, and he is twenty-three, and he sounds pleased.

"Aapke ghar ka case hai na. Do dulhan, jewellery, aur poora parivar thaga hua. Do bajey ki khabar mein chala."

"Do bajey?" Vihaan looks at the road.

"Kitni baar chala?"

"Sir, ek baar two o'clock, aur repeat teen bajey."

"Repeat rok do."

"Sir—"

"Roko." He says it with no heat at all and the boy stops talking.

"Aur teen bajey ka tape mere paas rakho. Kisi ko mat dena. Samjhe?"

"Ji, sir. Sir, ek baat—"

"Kya?"

"Yeh story hamare paas aayi kahan se, woh poochhne pe"—the boy's voice goes down—"Parmeet sir ka office. Bola ki unki taraf se hai."

Vihaan sits on the kerb with the phone at his ear and does not say anything for a moment.

"Sir?"

"Tape sambhal ke rakho."

He stands up.

"Aur mujhe do minute mein assignment desk ka number message kar do. Main khud baat karunga."

"Ji, sir."

Vihaan puts the phone in his pocket.

Then he takes the slip of paper out of his wallet and opens it on his knee and reads the thing he asked to have brought to his desk at noon and has been carrying around since ten.

**Three numbers, in Verma's hand, and a fourth line under them in a hand that is not Verma's** — an amount, and the word *advance* against it, and the date.

The date is the day before the wedding.

The three numbers are the buses.

The fourth is a different kind of number, and it belongs to the same man.

"Kessler ko call karo," he says into the phone, standing.

"Abhi. Aur jo bhi uske office mein mere naam pe rakha hai — Bidari ke saath jo draft banaya tha, woh bhi. Do minute."

He waits on the kerb with the children asleep in the car and the sweet-shop flypaper turning above the door.

"Sir, line pe hai."

Vihaan takes the slip of paper into his left hand and looks at it while he talks, and it is the only paper he looks at for the length of that call.

"Mr Kessler. Vihaan Suri."

He puts a hand over his other ear.

"Main jaanta hoon aap nikal gaye. Main aaj subah aapke conference room mein nahin tha, aur wajah main aapko khud bata raha hoon, kyunki woh aapko shaam tak kisi aur se sunni hogi."

He listens.

"Ghar mein police aayi thi. Meri family ke khilaaf warrant tha. Yeh khabar aapke desh ke akhbaar mein kal subah hogi."

The awake child watches him through the glass.

"Isliye main aapko woh de raha hoon jo aapko chahiye." His voice does not change.

"Mahamahakumbh ka jo feed mera channel dega, woh feed do hafte pehle aapki platform pe jaayega, aur sirf aapki platform pe. Aapko content chahiye, mujhe signature. Aaj shaam chhe baje, hotel wahin."

A pause.

"Nahin. Main aapse kuch chhupa nahin raha. Main aapse theek woh baat kar raha hoon jo aapko kal akhbaar mein milegi, aur usse pehle."

Vihaan puts the phone away and stands a moment with his hand on the roof of the car.

"Chhote bhaiya."

The awake child has the window down.

"Cold drink?"

"Ek minute."

He opens the door.

"Pehle ek zaroori kaam."

Vihaan takes his phone out again and calls his own house, and it rings four times and goes to nothing.

Vihaan calls Shyam's phone, and it rings and rings.

On the third ring somebody picks it up, and it is not Shyam, and the line is bad, and whoever it is does not speak.

"Hello? Shyam?" Vihaan's hand is flat on the roof of the car.

"Hello. Kaun hai?"

The line goes dead.

Vihaan stands in the road with the phone in his hand for a long moment.

Then Vihaan opens the door and gets in and starts the car, and pulls out into the road the wrong way for the turn, and a scooter honks and Vihaan does not notice.

## The Final Plan

By three the hall has been swept and washed and the windows are open on the afternoon light, and the two rows of chairs are standing in it the way they stand at the end of a wedding.

"**Dafna do yeh saari laashein.**"

Parmeet is at the sideboard with his spectacles on.

"**Saboot koi nahi milna chahiye.**"

"Tauji."

Karan is at the table with the register open.

"Purvi?"

"Baad mein."

Parmeet does not look up.

"Pehle mitti."

"Ji."

"**Humare final plan ko anjaam dene ka waqt ab aa gaya hai,**" the score sounding under the hour (m24:41).

"Kaun sa final plan, Tauji?"

Karan has his feet up.

"Wohi Kumbh wala?"

"Wohi."

"**Saara Kumbh pahunchega — saara RDX, ek-ek hathiyaar.**"

Parmeet takes his spectacles off.

"Pichhle das-pandrah saalon se India ne Kumbh ke infrastructure par bahut kharch kiya hai. Aur is baar ka Kumbh to **Mahamahakumbh** hai."

The register goes on being written in.

The chairs are in two rows with the aisle down the middle and the marigold on the posts going brown.

The tent's canvas is down at the west side and the poles stand in the grass.

"Is baar is desh ki tabahi pakki hai — yeh mauka nahi chukna chahiye."

He looks round his family.

"Isliye security ko poore high alert pe rakha gaya hai — mahamahakumbh mein is saal duniya bhar ke CEOs, global investors aur bade-bade political leaders pahunch rahe hain."

"Toh phir?"

Karan's pen stops.

"**RDX ka dhamaka hoga kal shahi snan ke din, tum ke beechon-beech.**"

Parmeet says it the way he says a date.

"Phir koi bhi videshi palat kar yahan nahi aayega — **laakhon laashein bik jayengi.** Aur ek saath itni mauten is desh ki kamar todne ke liye kaafi hongi."

Parmeet sits down.

A bucket stands at the side of the hall with a cloth over the top of it.

The electrician's ladder is against the wall with its cable still coiled on the rung.

"**Yahi plan banaya tha humne chauvees saal pehle.**"

"Kaise jaayega?"

Karan's pen stops.

"Pichhle saal sangam pe ek truck bhi nahin ghusi bina pass ke."

"Teen OB vans."

Parmeet puts his finger on the register.

"Channel ki teen gaadiyan, pontoon pe parking pass ke saath, chaubees ghante ke liye. Aur ek generator van jo kabhi nahin chalti."

The fan turns over the table.

The register for the evening stands open on the table by the fire with the pen beside it.

"Pass pe signature kiska hai?"

Karan's voice is careful.

"Chhote sahab ka."

Parmeet takes his spectacles off and polishes them on his sleeve.

"Woh Kumbh ka feed London bech raha hai. Uske pass pe teen gaadiyan hain, aur ek generator jo kabhi nahin chalti. Woh apne hisaab mein woh teen gaadiyan dekhega, aur hisaab mein jhooth nahin likha hoga."

"Aur agar woh gaadi khol ke dekhe?"

Ravish sets his glass down.

"**Jo humein office mein mile the — ye tabahi aap unhi hathiyaaron aur RDX se karoge na?**"

"Haan."

"**Par Tauji, agar hum pakde gaye to?**"

Parmeet looks at Karan for a moment and then he smiles.

The pandit's things are on a tray by the fire, and the brass of it has been polished.

"Isliye India security high rakhti hai, aur wahan pe koi gadbad nahi ho sakti — kumbh ki security ko halke mein nahi le sakte. Kumbh bahut bade level pe hota hai."

He puts his spectacles back on.

"**Mere paas plan bhi hai toh phir uske liye.**"

## The Dragon

The small room at the back of the Suri house at four in the afternoon, with the table cleared and one lamp on it.

Parmeet sends for the file and it comes, and it is not a file, it is a folder of plates and a single sheet, and he puts the sheet down on the table with the register.

"Sadiyon pehle darbaar mein charchayein hoti thi yeh dragon."

Ravish puts his feet down.

The folder of plates lies open on the table with one plate face down.

"Ye dragon naagon jaisa hi hota hai, kehte the."

Parmeet's finger goes along the top line of the sheet.

"Aur tez — lekin usse laakhon guna shaktishali."

"Dad—"

"Devtaon ka vardaan mila tha naagon ko."

He does not look up.

"Padosi desh ki dharti par paida hue ye, panpe," the score sounding under the birthing (m26:14).

His finger holds the line.

"aur yahi sochte rahe nafrat ki aag mein jalte hue"

He turns the sheet.

"ki naagon ko vardaan kyun mila, inhein kyun nahi"

He looks up at last.

"lekin dragon ko nahi."

The men in the room have stopped moving.

The lamp on the table has its shade crooked and nobody has straightened it.

"Aur apni poori duniya mein raaj karna chahta tha — apne munh se aag ugalta."

He turns the sheet over.

"Inka ant ho gaya lekin dheere-dheere."

"Toh phir—"

"**Apne mission ke liye dragon chahiye the magar humein.**"

Parmeet looks up.

"Vinashakari shaktiyan chahiye thi iski."

"Tauji, woh mar gaya—"

"**Humne iski gene badal di,**"

His hand lifts.

"**ise phir se paida kar diya isliye.**"

He puts his hand flat on the sheet.

"Jo is Bharat desh par kahar banke tootega — **humara dragon,**" the score sounding under the breaking (m27:05).

The lamps over the table do not move.

Outside, in the drive, a man is hosing the gravel down.

A tin of the powder the plates are kept in stands open at his elbow.

## What Will Not Wake

The house at dusk, with the lights coming on in the front rooms one after another.

It is not in the house.

The bed in the small room at the back is turned down and nobody has been in it, and a bottle of water stands on the stool beside it.

It is in the hall of stone, where the lamps are lit and the cloth is off the seat, and where a woman's body lies on a slab with her sari arranged and her hands folded and her face washed, and where the two of them stand over her and cannot make themselves stop.

"**Aapko kuch nahi ho sakta.**"

Uttara's voice is going.

"**Aapko kuch nahi ho sakta — aapka is tarah se ant nahi ho sakta. Aapko aapki saari shaktiyan milne wali thi. Aaj aap apne poorn naagin avatar mein aane wali thi.**"

The younger one puts her hand out and touches the dead woman's hair and takes her hand back.

"Ma."

Bharani's voice is very small with fear.

"Ma, woh—"

"Chup."

"Ma, suniye."

She turns round on her knees.

"Kuch to galat hai. **Yeh dragon toh abhi tak so raha hai.**"

Uttara looks at her.

"Jo khud jaag nahi raha hai — hum is dragon ko attack karne ke liye bhej rahe hain."

"Woh nahi jagega."

Uttara says it to the body.

"**Jab tak ichchhadhari naagin ki [garn] nahi aayegi, woh nahi jagega,**" the score sounding under the sleeping (m28:02).

"Ma."

Bharani's hands come together.

"Ichchhadhari naagin kahan se laoge ab," the score sounding under the asking (m28:27).

Her hands tighten.

"**woh to mar gayi.**"

The hall of stone is quiet.

The lamps stand.

"**Mahadev, aisa anyay nahi ho sakta.**"

Uttara's hands go up.

"Unki saari shaktiyan jagrit hone ka tha — aaj ka din ant ka nahi, aarambh ka tha. **Humari Naagrani nahi mar sakti,**" one low string under the refusing.

"Ma, woh mar gayi hai."

"**Bani thi woh har baar ko sehne ke liye.**"

Uttara kneels down beside the slab.

"Aur taiyaar bhi thi woh is cheez ke liye — yeh baat woh achhe se jaanti thi. **Amrit ko paane ke liye vish peena padta hai.**"

Uttara puts her forehead down on the edge of the slab with grief.

"Unki jaan kyun chali gayi phir?"

Her voice is not steady any more.

"**Aapka vardaan, aapki kripa jo aapne hum naaginon ko di — kahan hai woh saari shaktiyan? Kahan hai?**"

The lamps do not answer her, and neither does the woman on the slab, and outside the storm that has been over the city since the morning goes on being a storm.

## The Hall at Night

Half past nine, and the last car comes up the drive in the porch light with four sleeping children in it and one man who has been on the Ring Road since four.

The gate is open and the white police car is not there.

The chairs are stacked against the wall and the marigold on the posts has gone brown.

Vihaan stops the car where the wedding cars stood, and sits with the engine off and his hands on the wheel and looks at the house through the windscreen.

The hall is dark.

The lawn is dark.

There is one light on in the front room and one in the passage, and the marigold is still up on the poles.

The fire at the side of the hall has been lit and the room smells of it.

Vihaan gets out.

"Chhote sahab."

Shyam comes across the gravel with his cap in his hands and he does not look at Vihaan.

"Bhai sahab kahe the ki aap aate hi unse milne."

"Theek hai." Vihaan opens the back door and takes the smallest of the children up in his arms.

The child's shoes come off and stay on the seat.

"Shyam. Aaj dopahar maine phone kiya tha."

"Ji, sahab."

"Do baar."

He walks towards the house.

"Ek baar kisi ne uthaya aur kuch bola nahi."

"Network ki dikkat thi, sahab."

Shyam's voice is flat.

"Aap andar chaliye."

In the hall the floor is still wet in one corner and the wet is a clean wet and the whole hall smells of phenyl.

Vihaan stands in the middle of it with a sleeping child on his shoulder.

"Aaj yahan kya hua?" Vihaan says it to the empty hall, and then, when Shyam does not answer, Vihaan says it again, without any force in it at all.

"Yeh floor kyun dhoya hai?"

"Bhai sahab kahe the."

Shyam's cap is in his hands.

"Shaadi ke baad sab saaf."

"Sab saaf."

"Ji."

Vihaan walks to the passage where the phone is, and the receiver is hanging off its stand on its cord, and he puts it back, and it rings once in his hand and stops.

The light in the passage is on.

At the third step the carpet starts, and where the carpet starts there is a dark place in the pile that has dried brown.

Vihaan stands and looks at it for a moment, and then he goes down on one knee and puts two fingers on it and looks at them.

"Shyam." He says it without turning round.

"Yeh kya hai?"

"Sahab"—Shyam is at the end of the passage with his cap in his hands—"bhai sahab kahe the ki safai kal subah hogi."

"Bhabhi kahan hai?"

"Woh—"

Shyam stops.

"Woh dono chali gayin, sahab."

Vihaan looks at him for a moment.

"Kahan?"

"Bhai sahab batayenge."

Shyam goes out to the car.

Vihaan goes up the stairs and puts the boy in his bed and puts the sheet over him, and then he stands in the corridor for a moment, and then he goes down again past the hall with the wet corner in it, past the passage, to the lift.

The lift is standing on 1 with its door open.

The small hinged box in the wall beside it is hanging out on its hinge, and the key is in the lock, and the grey paint on the edge of the box has been broken off in a fresh line.

Vihaan takes his wallet out and takes a folded slip of paper out of it, and opens it, and looks at it under the corridor light.

The fold has gone soft.

His own hand, small and flat, from the accounts office at half past one in the morning: *chaar stop, paanch ka bill, poochho kyun.*

Vihaan asked why.

Vihaan turns the key.

The fifth button comes up under the row of four.

The basement is empty.

Not stripped, not broken into: emptied.

The crates are gone and the stains where they stood are clean.

The map is off the wall and the nail is still in it.

The clips are gone from the shelf.

The straw is swept into one corner in a little pile.

Vihaan stands in the door of it with the corridor light behind him, and then he takes the camera out of its case.

Vihaan shoots the room the way he shoots a site: from the door, from the two corners, close on the nail, close on the marks where the crates stood, and the last one standing in the middle of the floor while the flash goes up the walls and puts his own shadow on all four of them.

The flash is the first thing that has happened in that room in a long time.

Vihaan looks at the small screen, one frame at a time, and takes the card out and puts it in his shirt pocket and puts a fresh one in.

"Chaar saal," he says to nobody, at last, in Hindi, quietly.

"Chaar saal."

Vihaan turns the key back.

The fifth button goes out.

Vihaan closes the box and puts the key in his pocket, and the slip of paper goes into his shirt pocket and not back into the wallet.

In the passage he takes out his phone and writes to Verma, standing up, at that hour: *Lift ka contract, 1998 se aaj tak. Sab bill. Sab revision. Aur jo aadmi woh lift service karta hai, uska naam aur address. Kal subah nau baje file mere desk pe.*

Then he adds one more line, and this one takes him longer than the rest: *Aur kisi ko mat batana ki maine poochha.*

Upstairs in his own room he puts the card in the drawer with the folder that has *bina vivaran* written twice in its margin and the file with no name on it, and turns the key, and then he sits down and opens the laptop and answers Verma's eleven messages in four lines.

The last thing he writes, at one in the morning, goes to a man in Bombay: **Chhe baje nahin pahunch saka. Ghar mein kuch hua hai. Kal subah aath baje, main khud aata hoon.**

### Continuity and source note

This chapter is the second half of Episode 7, in the episode's own order: the weakness said out loud in front of the woman it is for; the been brought and played; the instrument that finds no naagin in the room; Uttara at the wall and then through the service door; the order — the reed, then the brass knife; the two sisters on the floor; the inspector counting his twenty minutes; Ravish asking a dying woman for a pearl she does not have; what Ananta says instead; Purvi on the drive with her hands red; Aryaman's promise and Aryaman's laugh; the corpses and the final plan; the dragon; and a hall of stone where the lamps stand over a body and the instrument's own question goes unanswered. A second naagin comes in (B26), the men are told to break *"is naagin"*'s reed (B27), and ninety seconds later Ravish is crouched over **Ananta** asking for the pearl, and it is **Ananta** who says *"mujhe maaf karna, main tujhe bacha nahi payi."* This page reads the death as Ananta's: she claimed the pearl's name in Chapter 16 to save her sister, and the men kill the woman who claimed it. **What the been is for, and why it fails.** Canon orders it played (B22) and canon's own man says what it would do — *"is dhun se Purvi ka sach in sabke saamne aa jayega"* — and then the instrument finds nothing, because the woman standing in front of the fire is not a naagin and never was. **`S-16` is answered by canon here, not by the workshop**: the compulsion was to be *prepared, not played*, and canon plays it, and it fails, and nothing has to be added.

**The instrument stays off the page.** Canon never says who plays it. It is Maa Sapela's been, in her case, in the small room at the back of that house since before dawn — and the page keeps her out of the hall, keeps the sound four bars long and audible from the west side of the house, and lets the family hear the thing it brought into its own wedding without ever learning what it was.
