## Possible

The curtain burns orange at its edge, and the room holds its breath between sleeping and waking.

## The Message

The outer court of the temple holds the late night in deep quiet, and one lamp burns at the serpent shrine beyond the crowd's noise.

Ahana stands at the shrine's edge with the cool air on her face and the dizziness still moving behind her eyes — and stops.

There, on the shrine's step, raised and watching, a snake holds its head high in the lamplight.

A gasp goes up from the family behind her.

Someone steps back.

Someone's bangle chimes against stone.

"Naag-naagin kabhi bhi kisi Shiv-bhakt ko haani nahin pahunchaate."

The priest comes forward with his hands raised, calm.

"Shiv ji hamesha naagon ke saath rehte hain. Aur ye unke rakhwaale hote hain. Inko ek vishesh kripa praapt hai."

The snake lowers its head.

It flows down the step and away between the stones, unhurried, and is gone — and Ahana stands looking after it with the priest's words settling into her like stones into water.

*Iska matlab — Mahadev mujhe kuch sandesh dena chaahte hain.*

The knowing comes whole.

*Isiliye mujhe chakkar aa rahe hain. Aur kuch ajeeb sa dikhaai de raha hai.*

The dizziness parts behind her eyes like a curtain drawn — and she sees.

A man's back she has never seen, bare, lamplit out of darkness.

Between the shoulder blades a naag rears in gold, five hoods fanned wide — the ichchhadhari's own mark, worn on skin.

The back never turns.

No face is given.

And with the seeing, whole, the name she already carries locks onto it — Bharani's name, given without a face: *Anantveer.*

Her saathi, named again — by Mahadev Himself this time — and still without a face.

She turns.

At the court's edge, half in shadow, Vihaan stands watching her — the apple in his hand, retrieved from the courtyard floor — and neither of them speaks, and the name burns behind her eyes belonging to no face she knows, and the temple bells ring on over them both, and the episode ends.
