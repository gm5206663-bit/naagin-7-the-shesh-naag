# Chapter 15 — Two Weddings, One Day (Part 2)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 6, second part. The episode ends where this chapter ends: on a crate, on a sentence about a blood-red wedding, and on twelve hours.*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

### TITLES

*The twelve seconds the season plays on every night after its first: the theme, then straight in (`canon/TITLES_SEQUENCE.md` §I). The full forty seconds are written once, at the head of Chapter 1, and are not repeated below it.*

The logotype comes up over thundercloud, with something red falling through the frame too slowly to be rain.

A man's voice asks two words in the pitch of a been: *"Kaun hai woh."* A choir of women answers underneath him and leaves the answer unfinished.

The been stops. One low string is struck and left hanging over the black.

A drum, once. Mahadev's trishul is not shown, but the thunder is his. Then the story, and no music under it until the story asks.


## The Basement

The lift stands in the corridor in the dark with its door open and its button panel lit, and the panel has four floors on it and no fifth button under them.

Gurinder puts his spectacles on and looks at it properly.

"Bhai."

He has his phone at his ear.

"Basement to jaati hi nahi hai, bhai.", the score sounding twice over the refusing (m28:38 x2 as one).

He presses what there is to press. The doors stay open.

He steps back, and his heel catches on the skirting, and the small hinged box set into the wall beside the lift — the one the painter has painted over twice — swings out on its hinge.

Inside it there is a keyhole and a key, the score sounding under the finding (m28:53).

"Chaabi mili hai us nagri param ke roop se."

He takes the key out and turns it over in his fingers.

"Lekin lagti kahan hai yeh chaabi?", the score sounding under the key (m29:02).

He puts it in.

The panel goes dark, and then a fifth button comes up under the row of four, lit, with nothing written on it, the score sounding under the fifth button (m29:27).

"Sirf kaun sa button hai ismein?"

He presses it with one finger.

The door closes on him.

The floor numbers count down to 1 and stop, and the lift goes on going.

The basement is a store room the size of the channel's whole floor, and it is not a store room.

There are crates along the far wall with a generator's name stencilled on them and a generator's weight written on the side in a shipping line's hand.

There is a bench with four empty clips on a shelf above it.

There is a map of the city on the wall with three of the Kumbh's ghats marked in red chinagraph and a fourth mark that is not a ghat.

And at the end of the bench, in an open crate with the straw pulled back, there are sticks of RDX, packed the way people pack something they mean to use.

"Yaar abba." He says it to the crates.

"Yeh yeh sab yahan—"

Gurinder has his phone out.

He is photographing the crates, the map, the bench, the sticks, one after another, his hand not quite steady with fear.

"Yeh sab main—" He says it to nobody.

"Yeh sab saboot mein—", the score sounding under the proof (m29:57).

A voice comes down the lift shaft from the corridor above, thin and far away, and it is not a voice he knows.

"Bhai sahab — aap log hain kahan?", the score sounding under the voice (m30:09).

The lift gives its chime.

He gets in and holds the fifth button down with his thumb until the door shuts on the basement, and he does not breathe until the numbers come back up.

## The Thana

Twelve minutes past two, and the car stands at the side of the sector road under the one lamp that is working, with the engine off and the phone face-up on his knee.

"Haan Shalini, bolo."

"Kahan reh gaye aap?"

His wife's voice is small and close.

"Itni raat ho gayi hai."

"Thode se cards bache hue hain bas."

Gurinder looks at the folder on the seat beside him.

"Baant ke aa raha hoon unhein.", the score sounding under the cover (m30:28).

"Cards."

A pause.

"Aap ghar aa jaiye. Subah sab kuch hai."

"Main aa raha hoon."

Gurinder puts the phone in his pocket and sits for a moment with both hands on the wheel.

Then he gets out and walks to the gate of the thana, which is lit, and goes in.

The road is empty in both directions, and the car's light is the only one on it, the score sounding under the going-in (m30:43).

Three in the morning, and the police station at the corner of the sector road has two men in it awake.

The duty officer is behind the desk with his register open and his spectacles on the end of his nose, and the man in the cream kurta is on the stool in front of it with his hands on his knees.

"Sir."

Gurinder's voice is steady.

"Complaint likhwani hai mujhe."

"Kiske khilaaf?"

"Parmeet Suri." He says the name and then the rest of it.

"Uski poori family ke khilaaf, sir."

The officer's pen stops.

The register on the desk is open and the carbon under the page has gone through to the page beneath it.

"Parmeet Suri?"

He takes his spectacles off.

"Aapko pata hai na woh kitne izzatdaar log hain? Kahin aapko galatfahmi to nahi hui? Woh business tycoon, jiska khud ka news channel bhi hai?"

"Woh to is desh ke bhi nahi hai, sir."

Gurinder leans forward.

"Woh bahut badhiya hai — chaubees saal pehle humare padosi desh Changistan se aaye hue jaasoos hain woh log. Sir, yahan apna naam aur pehchaan badal ke reh rahe hain. Humare desh ke khilaaf bahut bada plan kar rahe hain, sir."

"Beta—"

"Ek minute."

Gurinder opens his folder on the desk.

"Maine news channel ke basement mein yeh sab dekha — yeh dekhiye.", the score sounding under the showing (m31:19).

The officer looks at the page.

Then he turns it over, and turns it back.

"Aur maine Mahakumbh ki cuttings bhi dekhi, sir."

Gurinder's hand is flat on the desk now.

"Sir, yakeen maaniye, woh log kuch bahut bada plan kar rahe hain."

The officer sits back in his chair and looks at him for a while.

Behind him the board on the wall has three notices pinned to it and one of them is hanging by a corner.

"Agar yeh sach hai," he says slowly, "to raid daalna hoga humein."

"Uske liye itni raat ko apply nahi kar sakte, sir."

Gurinder is up.

"Kal subah sabse pehle warrant nikaalo. Kal subah hi yeh kaam ho paayega."

"Kal subah."

"Sir, please."

He puts both hands together.

"Kal us ghar mein meri beti ki shaadi hai, sir — please, unhein arrest kar lijiye."

The officer says nothing.

"Lekin koi rishta bada nahi hota desh ke saamne."

Gurinder's chin is up and his eyes are wet with grief and his voice does not go.

"Woh shaadi rok doonga main, sir."

"Is shaadi ko mat rokiye aap." The officer picks his pen up again.

"Kyunki wajah jaanna chahenge woh log," He uncaps it.

"aur alert ho jayenge." The form waits.

"Humein raid daalne dijiye kal sabse pehle —" He writes.

"aur office pe bhi, unke ghar pe bhi." He stamps it.

"Phir apni beti bhi bacha lenge aap," He looks up.

"aur bacha payenge desh bhi.", the score sounding twice over the promise (m31:51 x2 as one).

"Lekin sir."

Gurinder's hands come down on the desk.

"Warna bahut bada anand ho jayega — rasmon ke pehle aa jaiye please."

"Aap nishchint rahiye."

The register shuts.

The man in the cream kurta goes out into the dark of the sector road and stands on the pavement for a moment with his hand on the bonnet of his car.

## The Storm

The sky over the city at four in the morning is the colour of a bruise, and the only light in it is the sodium glow off the road, and the wind has changed.

In the hall of stone the lamps are lying over on their sides and the bell rope is standing out from the door.

"Bharani, yeh toofan dekh rahi ho?" Uttara has her shawl round her shoulders with both hands.

"Koi saadharan toofan nahi hai yeh." The shawl slips.

"Anhooni hone ka sanket hai bahut badi." She catches it.

"Warna is tarah hawaon ka rukh nahi badalta."

The young one says nothing, and dread keeps her silent.

The bell's rope lies across the floor of the stone hall, still swinging at the end.

"Humari Mahanaagrani ka janmadin shuru ho gaya hai aaj."

Uttara turns from the door.

"Poore chaubees baras ki ho gayi woh. Bhavishyavani aaj sach hone ka waqt aa gaya hai."

"Andar aaiye."

"Humari Mahanaagrani par bahut bhaari hain agle kuch ghante."

The wind comes through the hall and the lamps do not go out.

## Two Weddings, One Day

The morning arrives with dust in it, and the light comes up over the wall of the Arora house.

At the Arora house the cars are at the gate and the bags are on the roof and the women are coming down the steps one at a time, and the street is full of the wedding's noise before it is properly light.

The gate stands open on the lane and the neighbour's dog has come in and is sitting in the middle of the drive.

"Purvi."

Ananta is at the foot of the stairs with her sari over one arm.

"Chal, chal, chal."

"Ruk na—"

"Arre, sun."

Their mother is at the door with a steel box in her hands.

"Yeh dabba le ja. Khaali wapas mat laana."

"Tum logon ne taiyaar hone mein itna time laga diya."

Gurinder is at the gate with the keys in his hand.

"Humari wait kar rahe honge na woh log."

"Chal, chal."

He opens the first car's door himself.

"Main baaki ko doosri gaadi mein leke aata hoon — arre ek kaam karo, aap log ek gaadi mein jao."

"Tu aaj bada responsible ho raha hai."

Ananta says it over the roof of the car, and it is the first time she has teased her father in years.

"Tu bhi ban ja."

"Mamma, help karo na."

Purvi is on the step with her arms out.

"Bahut bhaari-bharak hai."

"Kitna ajeeb sa mausam ho raha hai, oh god." Ananta has both hands up at the sky.

"Poore fashion ki aisi ki taisi kar deti hai — tu bhi na," the score sounding under the teasing (m32:46).

Ananta is beside her, pulling at her sleeve.

"Pehna hai itna pyaara sa suit. Uske oopar yeh sweater aur jacket pehen liye hain. Jao, utaar dena."

"Par yeh mausam—"

Purvi looks up at the sky with her arms out.

"Mujhe to bahut achha lag raha hai — it is lovely.", the score sounding under the loving (m33:17).

"Purvi—"

"Mausam bhi utna hi excited hai meri shaadi ke liye, jitna tum log — aisa lag raha hai."

Her mother's hand comes down on her shoulder.

The steel box for the jewellery stands open on the bed with the cloth laid out beside it.

"Jaa, jaa."

She pushes her towards the car.

"Utaar dena."

Ananta is at the gate with her own bag, and she stops there and looks up.

The lane behind her is empty and the milk float has gone and left its bottle crate at the corner.

"Aap yeh Delhi ka mausam dekh rahe ho?"

She says it to the street.

"Kaisi aandhi-toofan chal rahi hai — lag raha hai cyclone aane wala hai."

"Aasman gir jaye, toofan aaye." Purvi takes her by the wrist.

"I don't care." Her wrist turns.

"Mera pyaar mil raha hai mujhe," She laughs.

"aur itni khush hoon main" The wind lifts.

"ki shaadi se bhi ab mujhe" She means it.

"dar nahi lag raha."

"Main bhi khush hoon."

Ananta's hand comes over hers.

"Bahut khush."

"Purvi."

Their mother is on the step with the steel box.

"Gaadi."

"Aryaman sir ko de dein hum ye file."

Purvi has the folder under her arm.

"Main jaldi se aayi hoon — aap thodi der baitho."

"Jaldi aana."

At the Suri gate the marigold is up and the chairs are out and the pandit is standing on the step with his bag of books under his arm, and the sky behind him is going grey and then yellow and then the colour of the inside of a shell.

The pandal's poles are wrapped in cloth and the cloth has come loose on the second one from the gate.

"Gurinder ji."

He comes down the steps.

"Chaliye, baithiye — kya soch rahe hain aap?"

In the second car, Ananta is holding the steel box on her lap, and she turns her head to the gate, and then she turns it to her sister.

The cars stand nose to tail with their engines running and their boots open on the drive.

"Purvi."

"Hmm."

"Woh."

Ananta's chin points, and her hand comes down on Purvi's wrist.

"Kal raat ki baat bhool ja."

"Koi baat nahin hui thi."

"Hui thi."

Ananta does not look at her.

"Kal raat maine tujhe teen cheezein yaad dilayi thi. Gaana, kaanch, aur naam. Ab dekh. Woh gate pe khada hai, aur tumhari nazar wahin hai. Subah aath baje. Apni behen ki shaadi ke din."

Purvi looks out of the window and does not answer her, and her hand stays where her sister put it.

The road goes past with the market on it and the shutters down and the fruit off the front of the shops.

At the side of the step, Vihaan comes round the corner of the house with the vendor's supervisor beside him and the morning's delivery sheet under his arm, and he stops on the gravel and looks at the gate, because the guard is there with his book open and the wedding's cars coming in.

Vihaan walks down and puts his hand flat on the book.

The visitors' book lies open on the guard's table with the pen on its string and the string worn thin.

*"Yeh khula rakho."*

The guard nods and turns the book round so that the page faces the road, one low string under the ordering.

"Chhote sahab."

The supervisor has his cap in his hand.

"Phool doosri gaadi se aayega, saade nau baje."

"Tab tak tent khali nahin dikhna chahiye." Vihaan turns back to the house with him.

"Chalo. Main dikhata hoon kahan."

"Waah."

Gurinder looks at the lawn.

"Ghar ko kaafi achhi tarah decorate kiya hai. Bahut sundar, aunty.", the score sounding under the arriving (m33:17).

"Sab ready hai. All set," the score sounding under the all-set (m33:52).

In the house the two brides are in two rooms, and neither of them is being dressed, and the makeup woman has her hands full and her mouth fuller.

The corridor between the two rooms has a folded chair against the wall and a tray of bangles on the floor.

"Ab pandit ji." The elder of the house is in the passage with his hands together.

"Shaadi ke mantra shuru kijiye aap jaldi-jaldi se." His hands stay together.

"Karan aur Tina ke phere pehle," The passage fills.

"Ravish ki shaadi uske baad.", the score sounding twice over the beginning (m34:04 x2 as one).

"Ji, jaisi aapki ichchha."

"Aap log guest room mein aaram kar lijiye tab tak."

## What Is This Nonsense

At the gate in the morning, a car with a dented door comes in too fast and stops, and the boy from the channel gets out with his laptop under his arm and one hand up.

Vihaan comes down the last three steps of the lawn while the boy is still talking.

"Sir."

He is talking before he is through the gate.

"Sir, yeh wise happen—", the score sounding under the stammering (m35:07).

"What is this nonsense?"

Aryaman turns round in the middle of the lawn with anger.

"Tumhare paas dimaag nahi hai? Yeh level hai tumhare kaam ka? Yeh bakwaas broadcast karunga main time-time pe?", the score sounding under the shouting (m35:07).

"Sir—"

The boy's hands come up.

"Shaadi ka ghar hai na, bhai, bhai, bhai. Galtiyan ho jaati hain — kaam ka pressure hai. It's okay na?"

Aryaman looks at him for a moment.

The boy's laptop is under his arm and the strap of the bag on his shoulder has given at one end.

"You come with me."

He puts a hand on the boy's shoulder.

"Come. Don't mind."

Aryaman walks him towards the house, and at the step he stops and turns and looks at the grey sky and the chairs and the pandal, and for a second his face holds still under the sky.

"Aaj aise hi rehne wala hai bhai ka mood thoda."

One of the cousins is at Karan's ear behind him, loud enough for four people.

"Actually Aryaman bhai yeh shaadi-waadi ke functions attend nahi karte. Past ki kadwi yaadein judi hain.", the score sounding under the excusing (m35:07).

"Uski girlfriend ne"

He leans in.

"dump kar diya tha usey shaadi ke din hi." The other cousin's hand is up.

"Don't mind."

Aryaman does not turn round.

He goes up the step and through the door.

## Twelve Hours

At the side of the house in the dark, where the pandal meets the wall and the drain runs, Maa Sapela is sitting on an upturned crate with her case between her feet.

The elder comes round the corner and stands over her.

Her case lies open beside her with a cloth over the top of it, and the case has been rained on.

A tray stands on the edge of the drain with three bangles in it and a red thread tied round the tray.

The pandal's edge goes up and comes down again in the wind, and a tin lies on its side in the drain.

"Bas thodi der aur." She does not look up.

"Aurat ho ya naagin," The wind waits.

"qurbani zaroor deti hai apne suhaag ke khatir." Her voice drops.

"Apni naagmani zaroor degi yeh bhi."

"Aur usne na maani to?"

"Lekin agar isne hamari baat nahi maani—"

Her hand goes flat on the case.

"Hum apni manmaani karenge to phir.", the score sounding under the threatening (m35:58).

She looks up.

"Agar isne hamari baat nahi maani, to apni shaadi khooni-laal shaadi ban jayegi."

The wind takes the edge of the pandal and lets it go.

The gutter runs and the water in it is the only noise at that side of the house.

*Ab shuru hota hai naagin banne ka chautha aur aakhri padaav. Agle barah ghanton mein na, us rani ki zindagi badalne wali hai.*
