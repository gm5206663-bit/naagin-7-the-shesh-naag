# Chapter 27 — Molten Gold (Part 2)

*Episode reference: Naagin, season 7, Episode 16, "Ahana Performs Shiv Tandav".*

*Draft; not yet accepted.*

## The Office

The channel's tenth floor holds the morning in tube-light and ringing phones, and the boardroom door stands open at the corridor's end.

Vihaan stands at his brother's office door with a sale-sheet in his hand, come up for the board meeting's money papers and the share-transfer arithmetic the morning needs.

Inside, Aryaman stands at the window with his phone at his ear.

"No, Sharma ji."

Aryaman's voice is flat.

"Maine aapse pehle bhi kaha tha — main us advertiser ko apne channel pe nahin loonga."

Vihaan waits in the corridor.

The file-man files the refusal's shape and stays out of its substance.

A peon's tray clinks past with the morning tea.

"Magar kyun, sir?"

The phone gives back static.

"Kyunki ye mera faisla hai."

Aryaman turns from the window — and stops.

Ahana stands in the corridor behind Vihaan, festival-pale, her thali-hand empty.

"Hi."

She looks past Vihaan to her husband's brother, then back.

"Tum — tum yahan kya kar rahi ho?"

"Wife ho tumhaari."

She says it to Vihaan, steady, with the corridor listening.

"Tum jahan — main wahin. Ya right?"

Vihaan looks at her for a long moment — the night's faint still grey under her eyes, the temple still in her hair.

"But I don't think tum mere matlab ya maksad ke kuch bhi karti ho." His voice stays level.

"So batao. Kyun aayi ho yahan?"

"Vikram ne bulaaya hai."

She meets his eyes.

"Board meeting. Surprise — uske liye."

A nod toward Aryaman's door.

Something in his face eases a fraction, the score sounding under the softening (m15:35).

"Achha hua tum aa gayi."

Quiet, for her only.

"Tumhaare bina kaam pe man nahin lagta mera."

"Oh Vikram — baat karni hai."

Vikram's voice comes down the corridor, bright.

"Board meeting rakhi hai. Tum aa jaao."

Vihaan offers his wife his arm.

She takes it.

They go in together.

## The Board

The meeting room holds the morning in a long table and shut blinds, and the company seal stands inked on the table's head.

Parmeet sits at the head with the agenda open.

"Good morning."

The room settles, the score sounding under the greeting (m16:20).

His eyes find Ahana at Vihaan's side, and he beams.

"Beta ji — main bahut khush hoonga agar future mein aap humein join kar lo."

Vihaan takes the finance chair with his papers squared, and says nothing, and misses nothing.

"Hello?"

Vikram stands at the window with his phone up.

"Wo aa gaye hain."

A grin.

"Great. Send them."

He turns to the room.

"Maine apne hisse ke shares unhe de diye hain. Meri taraf se ab wo handle karenge."

A pause for it to land.

"Ek top journalist hai — media company ke — jinhein main apne shares ki power of attorney de raha hoon."

He spreads his hands, the score running under the intro (m16:44).

"Let me introduce them."

The boardroom door opens, the score sounding under the opening door (m17:09).

Vihaan looks up from his papers with everyone else — and files, first of all, his brother's face: Aryaman has gone absolutely still.

## The Stakeholders

The boardroom door stands open on the corridor's light, and two people come through it into the morning.

"Meet your new stakeholders — Mr. Purav Kothari sahab."

Vikram beams, the score striking twice over the reveal (m17:17 x2 as one).

"Good morning, everyone."

The man nods round, assured.

"Please — bhool jaao mujhe."

A small laugh goes round the table, uncertain.

Vihaan notes the laugh's thinness over his papers.

His eyes find the woman in the doorway — Radhika Kothari, first sight — and something cold moves under his skin.

No reason, no memory, nothing he can name; only instinct lifting its head, the way a body knows a storm before the sky does.

It is not softening.

Nothing in him softens for any face but one.

He looks away first, and his eyes go back to Aryaman, who has not moved at all.

"I love you, Radhika."

The whisper comes from Aryaman's mouth before he knows it is coming — old, buried, surfacing whole.

"Ye naam to suna-suna sa — oh to ye — "

Vikram's grin widens.

"Aaj se aap is channel ke 25% stakeholders hain. Mere 25% shares ko aap sambhaalenge."

He turns to the room.

"We are looking forward to your experience and — Aryaman, tu to jaanta hoga na inhein. Mrs. Radhika Kothari ko."

A beat.

"After all — ek time pe tum dono kaafi achhe dost the. Like really close friends."

The silence that follows has edges.

Radhika Kothari stands composed in the doorway, calm as morning water, and looks at no one in particular.

A glass sweats untouched at the table's head.

"Aryaman." Vihaan says his brother's name once, low, from the finance chair.

Aryaman does not hear him.

He is already on his feet, and the chair goes back behind him, and he walks out of the boardroom without another word, the score sounding three times over the walkout (m18:26+m18:34+m18:41 as one).

Vihaan rises.

He follows his brother out past the staring table, past his wife's unreadable face, into the corridor.

## Payback

The corridor outside the boardroom holds the morning in echoing tile, and the board's murmur stays shut behind the door.

"Aryaman, bro — kya hua?"

Vikram comes after him, easy.

"Tu to become their — "

Aryaman turns on him.

"Tu sab jaanta hai — phir bhi toone ye kiya. Ye deal nahin hoga. Shares mere hain."

"Tu deal kar ya mat kar — par mere shares yahi handle karenge."

Vikram's ease does not crack.

"Ab tu partnership dissolve kar — ya phir mere partners ko board mein accept kar."

A shrug, the score striking under the ultimatum (m18:59).

"That's completely your choice."

"Teri — tere shares main khareed loonga."

Aryaman's voice shakes.

"Lekin ye partners nahin ban sakte. Vikram — please don't do this."

Vihaan stands three paces down the corridor with his hands in his pockets.

His brother's choice is his brother's — dissolve it, accept it, buy it out, none of it his to touch.

But the plea hangs in the corridor air — *please don't do this*

— and Vihaan knows exactly what this is costing Aryaman.

So does Vikram.

That is the part Vihaan does not file away.

"Kya yaar, Aryaman."

Vikram's voice turns.

"Kal tu hi to bol raha tha — duniya mein sab kuch mil jaata hai. Paisa. Fame. Status. Yahan tak ke ladki bhi."

A beat.

"Par badi kismat waale to wo hote hain jinhein sachchi dosti milti hai. Humein ek doosre ki dosti mili hai. Right?"

The smirk surfaces, the score running three times under the dosti thrown back (m19:29 x3 as one).

"Aur phir tu aaj aise react kar raha hai — wo bhi itni chhoti si baat pe? Chhoti si baat?"

"Mujhe tujhse ye ummeed nahin thi."

"Ummeed to mujhe bhi nahin thi tujhse, Aryaman."

The smirk stays.

"Ke tu dekhta rahega — jab tere ghar ne meri dulhan chheen li — aur phir mujhe dosti sikhaayega."

Vikram steps closer.

"Dosti mein dagha ki hai to sazaa to milegi na."

Aryaman stares at him.

The corridor holds its breath, the score sounding under the held breath (m20:09).

A wall clock ticks loud in the hush.

"No."

Aryaman shakes his head, the score sounding twice under the refusal (m20:20 x2 as one).

"Maine pehle bhi kaha hai — this is not right. No, yaar. This is not done."

He walks away down the corridor without looking back.

Vikram watches him go, smirking, and turns for the boardroom — and Vihaan is in his path, the sale sheet crackling in his fist.

"Use gawah banaake laaya yahan — mere bhai ka gala kaatte hue dikhaane ke liye." Vihaan's voice is quiet — one low string under the answering — and the corridor tile gives it back.

"Meri biwi mohra nahin hai, Vikram."

A step closer.

"Aur mera bhai tera hisaab-kitaab nahin hai. Tu jaanta hai ye uske liye kya hai — tu sab jaanta hai, phir bhi."

A beat.

"Is baar aasaan nahin hoga."

The smirk does not survive it.

Vikram's mouth flattens; his eyes flick past Vihaan's shoulder to the boardroom door, and for one breath the ease is gone and something smaller looks out.

Then the grin is rebuilt — thinner, borrowed — and he goes past Vihaan into the boardroom.

Vihaan stands alone in the corridor for one breath.

Then he goes after his brother.

"Andar chal."

He falls into step beside Aryaman at the corridor's end.

"Board baaki hai," he says.

Aryaman walks on without answering.

Vihaan walks with him, and says nothing more.

## Black Coffee

The office pantry holds the afternoon in a humming fridge and one high window, and the corridor's noise stays outside.

Aryaman stands at the counter with his head in his hands and an untouched glass beside him.

The door opens soft behind him.

"Black coffee pee lo."

Radhika sets the cup down in front of him.

"Sar dard hoga na phir. Aur tum jaante ho ke ek baar tumhaara sar dard shuru hota hai — phir jaldi khatm nahin hota hai."

A pause.

"Chaliye, Aryaman."

He looks up at her.

"Tum jaanti ho ke hamaare beech mein jo bhi kuch tha — wo khatm ho chuka hai."

His voice rasps.

"Please — move on kar lo."

She holds his eyes, calm, the score sounding twice over the return (m21:20+m21:38 as one).

"Tum kar paayi ho?"

A breath.

"Move on?"

The cup steams on between them, untouched.

He has no answer.

She leaves the coffee steaming on the counter, and goes.

From the corridor's bend, past the pantry's open door, Ahana stands with her back to the wall — come looking for her husband's brother with a message she no longer remembers — and watches the whole small exchange in confusion, a stranger to whatever stands between them, a witness to something private that is none of hers.

She turns away before either of them sees her, and walks back down the corridor with the message unsaid.

## The Vent

The empty edit bay holds the evening in dead screens and one lit panel, and the door stands ajar on the corridor.

Aryaman sits in the operator's chair with a bottle at his elbow, and Ahana stands in the doorway with nowhere else she has to be.

"Tum bhi aurat ho na?"

He looks up at her.

"To batao — kyun karti hain aurtein aisa? Jawaab do. Are bolo na. Give me an answer. Damn it. Bolo."

His voice rises.

"Kya mazaa aata hai kisi ke dil ke saath khelne mein? Kya mazaa aata hai tum logon ko? No."

She stays in the doorway.

She says nothing at all.

"Are yahi to problem hai ke main Radhika ko bhool hi nahin paata hoon."

His voice breaks.

"Nahin bhool paata hoon main use. Radhika ki vajah se mujhe apni life ka sabse bada regret mila tha."

He stares at the dead screens.

"Mera bhai Ravish apni life ke iklaute pyaar se shaadi kar raha tha. Lekin mujhe shaadiyon se nafrat hai."

The panel hums.

She does not move.

"Mujhe trigger karti hain — kyunki meri Radhika hamaari shaadi ki raat mujhe chhod ke chali gayi thi."

The words come faster now, the score running twice under the trigger (m22:29 x2 as one).

"Aur uske baad mujhe har shaadi saamne waale ki barbaadi lagti hai. Ravish ki shaadi mein bhi yahi hua. Main itna disturb tha — aur main itna high ho gaya, itna high ho gaya ke main shaadi attend hi nahin kar paaya."

A laugh like glass breaking.

"Pyaar karne ki sazaa milti hai — movie. Aur tumhein bhi wahi sazaa milegi. Sazaaye — miss."

He sags.

"Yahin pada tha — behosh."

"Phir tum jaanti ho kya hua? Kya hua?"

He answers himself.

"Wo ladki — uski family humein loot ke chale gaye."

"Looteri dulhan thi wo."

He spits it.

"Kya naam tha uska? Ahana."

A beat.

"No — sorry. Wo to tumhaara naam hai. Ananta."

His voice hardens, the score sounding twice under the looteri belief (m23:13 x2 as one).

"Ananta naam tha uska. Humein loot ke chali gayi apni family ke saath. And I was in there."

A pause.

"Lekin agar main wahan hota na — to aisa main kabhi nahin hone deta."

The edit bay hums.

She turns, and walks away down the corridor, and leaves him to his bottle and his wrong, certain history.

## The Refusal

The corridor keeps the evening in long shadows, and Aryaman's office door stands open on lamplight.

"Yes. Nahin."

His voice comes through the door, flat.

"Maine kaha na — hum us advertiser ke saath kaam nahin karenge. Kyunki wo Changistaan se hain."

A pause.

"Changistaan kab se hamaare desh ko barbaad karne ki koshish kar raha hai. Chupchaap hamaare desh mein ghuspaithiye bhejte hain — jo hamaare beech mein rehte hain aur hamaari secrets dushman tak pahunchaate hain."

His voice hardens, the score striking twice over the stand (m23:42 x2 as one).

"Maine un pe research ki hai. Lekin wo ab tak pakde nahin gaye hain."

The call ends.

His steps go down the corridor and away.

Ahana slips into the empty office past the open door, and opens the file drawer, and reads.

The advertiser's file lies on top — the offer, the figures, the refusal marked in his own hand with the reason written out beside it: enemy money, no compromise, the nation's secrets not for sale.

She closes the drawer soft.

She stands in his empty office with the lamplight on the files, and sees the patriot in him standing clear of the wounds for the first time — a man who drowns his nights and holds his country's line by day — and says nothing to anyone, and goes.
